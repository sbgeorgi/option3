Inspirock Trip Planner Alternative: 5 Apps Compared

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android). It is one option here and a narrower one than the other five. Facts checked on 13 November 2026. Updated 13 November 2026.

The right Inspirock trip planner alternative depends on whether you want an itinerary generated for you or a plan you build yourself. Layla, Stippl and Tripomatic generate one, Wanderlog helps you build one on a map, and I built NomadSwipe (Android) for choosing places by swiping.

Key facts

  • Inspirock, as TechCrunch described it in 2015, took a destination, an age range and dates, let you pick categories such as gardens or landmarks, and built a schedule you could trim by deleting or saving suggestions.
  • I found no Inspirock app. On 13 November 2026 a Google Play search for inspirock returned 11 apps and none was Inspirock, and the search on Apple's U.S. store returned 8 results and none was Inspirock. Its status is covered in what happened to Inspirock, so I do not repeat it here.
  • On Google Play the closest generated-itinerary options are Stippl (3.7 from 2,200 ratings), Tripomatic (4.2 from 18,419) and Layla (4.7 from 542). Wanderlog has the most ratings (4.7 from 37,944, 1M+ downloads).
  • The Stippl, Tripomatic and Wanderlog Play listings show in-app purchases, and Stippl's and Tripomatic's put AI planning under Pro or Premium. Layla's listing shows none and says it is free to download, with prices confirmed before you pay for booked travel. So "free" here means free to start.

Inspirock trip planner alternative: five apps, one at a time

Layla: the AI agent that also books

The Layla Play listing calls it a personal AI travel agent: you describe the trip, Layla asks what it still needs and builds the plan, and you can swap a hotel or move a day and the rest is reworked. A human travel advisor can finalise bigger trips. The Layla site says using it to plan is free with no subscription or planning fee, and that you pay for the travel arrangements in a booking. It also recommends a minimum budget of €100 per person per night for its tailor-made trips, so it is aimed at trips you intend to book through it. Its Play listing says it is free to download and shows 10K+ downloads, the smallest of the four listings I read. More in Layla alternatives.

Stippl: closest to Inspirock's route-and-days shape

The Stippl site says its AI turns a described trip into a day-by-day itinerary with budget, stays and bookings. The Play listing adds routes across several cities and countries, transport between stops, hour-by-hour days, expense splitting and a journal, and labels its AI trip planner Pro. It has the lowest Play rating here (3.7 from 2,200), which says little without the review text, and I did not read the reviews. See Stippl alternatives and is Stippl worth it.

Tripomatic: day plans with travel times

Tripomatic is the new name of Sygic Travel, per its Play listing. The listing describes an AI trip planner, day-by-day itineraries with estimated travel times and walking distances, collaboration with friends and cost tracking, and says offline maps need Premium and that Premium unlocks AI travel planning. The Tripomatic site adds that it is free to use without a credit card, runs on web, iOS and Android, and exports plans to PDF, GPX or KML. See Sygic Travel alternatives.

Wanderlog: build it yourself on a map

The Wanderlog pages I read describe building the plan by hand rather than generating it from a form. The Play listing describes adding things to do from top guides in one click, pinning each place on a map, importing reservations by forwarded email or Gmail, and editing together in real time. It also says offline access is Pro. If you liked trimming Inspirock's suggestions yourself, this is the nearest workflow. See Wanderlog alternatives and how to use Wanderlog.

Mindtrip: an AI assistant (Apple listing only)

The Mindtrip listing on Apple's store shows a free app with a 4.7 average from 871 ratings, updated 10 November 2026. It describes a travel assistant you talk to by text or voice, places on an interactive map, price watching on flights and hotels, booking on Mindtrip, and pulling places out of a video, article or Google Maps pin you share. I did not read a Google Play listing for it, so I cannot say whether an Android app exists. See Mindtrip alternatives.

Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

For choosing the places first

I built NomadSwipe, so weigh this with that in mind. It is an Android app: pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time, swipe right or tap Save to keep one, and your saved places are pinned on a trip map so you can see which ones sit near each other and plan each day yourself. Cards show category, rating, distance and opening status, and the list sorts by rating, distance or A to Z. The Play listing says its screenshots show a sample trip: a long weekend in New York with 16 places, all picked by swiping.

It is the opposite of Inspirock's generated schedule: it does not write an itinerary from a form or an AI prompt, and it does not book anything. The plan without payment has limits, and Premium is optional. iPhone is coming soon.

A workable split: choose places in NomadSwipe, then lay them out by day, in a spreadsheet or in one of the planners above. How to pick, in five steps

This is the order I would work through it, based only on the pages above.

  1. Decide whether you want to be handed a plan or to make one. If you used Inspirock to get a ready schedule, stay with the first three apps in the table. If you liked deleting and saving suggestions, look at Wanderlog or NomadSwipe.
  2. If you want to book through the planner, Layla and Mindtrip both describe booking in the app. Layla describes paying for the whole trip or only parts, and its site says its tailor-made trips assume a minimum budget.
  3. If your trip crosses several cities or countries, Stippl and Wanderlog describe multi-stop routes most directly.
  4. If you need maps without a connection, read the offline line first: Tripomatic's listing says offline maps need Premium, and Wanderlog's listing marks offline access as Pro but also says plans are stored offline, so I could not tell which applies.
  5. Check the store price on the day, because three of the four Play listings show in-app purchases and Layla charges for booked travel, and test with one real trip before you rely on it.

FAQ

What is a good Inspirock trip planner alternative?

It depends on how you used Inspirock. Layla, Stippl and Tripomatic generate a day-by-day itinerary from a description of your trip, Wanderlog is the closest if you want to build the plan yourself on a map, and Mindtrip is an AI assistant I could only read on Apple’s listing. I built NomadSwipe, which is narrower: it helps you choose places by swiping.

Is there an Inspirock app?

I did not find one. On 13 November 2026 a Google Play search for inspirock returned 11 apps, none of them named Inspirock, and the search on the U.S. Apple store returned 8 results with none named Inspirock. For the site itself, see what happened to Inspirock.

Which Inspirock alternative is closest to the original?

Stippl and Tripomatic are the closest in shape. TechCrunch described Inspirock in 2015 as a site where you enter a destination and dates, pick interest categories and get a customised itinerary. Stippl says its AI builds a day-by-day itinerary from a description, and Tripomatic lists an AI trip planner with day-by-day plans and travel times.

Is there a free Inspirock alternative?

Several are free to start. Tripomatic says it is free to use, Stippl calls itself a free AI travel planner, Wanderlog calls itself completely free on Google Play and Layla says planning is free. Stippl, Tripomatic and Wanderlog also show paid parts, such as in-app purchases or a Pro or Premium plan, and the Stippl and Tripomatic Play listings put AI planning under Pro or Premium. Layla charges for the travel you book through it, so check the current listing.

Is there an Inspirock alternative for Android?

Yes. Wanderlog, Layla, Stippl and Tripomatic all have Google Play listings, and I built NomadSwipe, which is on Google Play too. I did not read a Google Play listing for Mindtrip, so I cannot say whether it has an Android app.

Is NomadSwipe an Inspirock alternative?

Only for one job. I built NomadSwipe, an Android app where you swipe real places in a city, save the ones you like, see them on a map and build a trip or day plan. It does not generate an itinerary from a form and it does not book anything.
