Organic Maps Alternative Routes: What It Shows, and 3 Other Apps

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android). It is not a navigation app, so it is not one of the three apps compared, but I say where it fits. Facts checked on 13 November 2026. Updated 13 November 2026.

Organic Maps alternative routes exist: since its June 2026 update the app shows the shortest route beside the fastest, and the September update improved them. If you want another app for routing, CoMaps, OsmAnd and Google Maps offline are the three compared here. I built NomadSwipe (Android), which is not a navigation app.

Key facts

  • The Organic Maps news page dates the feature: a 15 June 2026 beta, then the 29 June update (the shortest route "in addition to the fastest"), a 23 July recap (alternate routes for driving, hiking and cycling) and a 29 September update (improved alternative routes and arrival times).
  • Avoid options (tolls, unpaved roads, motorways, ferries) are a settings toggle, and the route how-to says they apply to car routes. A route takes up to 100 intermediate points, and navigation only starts from your current location.
  • The sources disagree on public transport, install counts and review counts. I list each difference below with who said what.
  • Google Maps offline is the weakest of the four for this query: its help page says no alternate routes when driving offline, and no walking, cycling or transit directions.
  • NomadSwipe is not a navigation app, and its Google Play listing describes no downloadable maps. It says up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online.

What Organic Maps does for routing today

The Organic Maps site describes a privacy-focused offline maps app with GPS navigation, track recording and route planning for hiking, cycling, running and driving, built on OpenStreetMap data and "absolutely free" with no ads. Its feature list adds a choice between fastest and shortest routes, multi-stop planning with automatic optimisation of stop order, turn-by-turn walking, cycling and car navigation with voice guidance, and CarPlay and Android Auto support. The Google Play listing shows 4.1 from 24.3K reviews and 5M+ downloads, updated 29 September 2026, with a Data safety section that says no data is collected or shared.

The routing history is the most useful thing the news page gives you for this query. It is a short timeline, not a review.

Two things the posts do not say: how many routes the app draws beyond fastest and shortest, and where on the screen you switch between them. The how-to page I read does not describe that either, so test it before a trip.

Creating a route, step by step

These steps come from the Organic Maps route how-to, which I read on 13 November 2026. I did not follow them in the app.

  1. Wait until your location is found on the map.
  2. Pick a destination: tap the search button, tap the bookmarks button, or tap any place on the map.
  3. Press "Route to". The app builds the route and shows distance and estimated travel time.
  4. Change the route type with the Car, Pedestrian, Subway, Bike or Ruler icon at the top of the screen.
  5. Tap "Start" to follow the route. To finish, press the arrow in the lower right corner and tap Stop.

Three limits sit in the same article. "Route From" lets you preview a route from another start, but navigation is only available from your current location. You can add up to 100 intermediate points, by building the route and then tapping a point, picking a bookmark or searching, and pressing "Add stop". And the avoid options live in settings under Routing options, for car routes.

Where the sources disagree

Reading the official pages side by side turned up four mismatches. None is a scandal, but each changes what you can safely say.

  1. Public transport. The site's feature list says public transport routing by bus, tram, subway and light rail. The Play listing says live schedules and routing are "currently in development". The news page explains most of it: routing arrived on 29 June 2026, and live schedules are what is still in development. The Play wording may simply be older; I did not test any city.
  2. Install counts. The site says 12M+ installs in October 2026 and the 3 November news post says the same. The GitHub README says "over 6 million". The Play listing shows 5M+ for Play alone. These are three different measures, not three versions of one number.
  3. Review counts. The Play header showed 24.3K reviews while the ratings block lower on the same page showed 23.5K. I quote the header figure and say Google Play showed it. My sibling page on apps like Maps.ME read 4.0 on 11 November, and the listing showed 4.1 today.
  4. "Free and open source". The F-Droid page lists the Apache License 2.0 and flags a non-free asset: the compiled map data files (.mwm) under a non-FOSS licence. The GitHub README says binary data files, including .mwm map files, are provided under a separate licence. So the app code is Apache-licensed, and the map files are not.

One more observation from the same session: the Play listing was last updated on 29 September 2026, while the GitHub page's latest release tag read 2026.11.11-39-android and the F-Droid page's newest version was 2026.09.29-32. I did not compare their contents, so I do not claim Play is behind.

Organic Maps alternatives for routing, one at a time

CoMaps: the closest relative

The CoMaps site says the project is a fork of Organic Maps and Maps.Me, driven by an open-source community, with offline search and routing and turn-by-turn instructions by foot, bike or car including voice announcements. Its Play listing adds subway transit directions, says all app functions are designed to work offline, and shows 4.1 from 1.72K reviews with 100K+ downloads. I found no mention of alternative routes, avoid options or stop limits on either page, so I cannot compare it feature by feature. It shows far fewer installs on Play than Organic Maps (100K+ against 5M+), which is a size difference, not a quality verdict, and I did not compare the two apps in use. More context in apps like Maps.ME and whether Maps.ME is worth it.

OsmAnd: the most routing controls

The OsmAnd site lists the longest routing section of the pages I read: offline navigation, voice guidance, automatic re-routing when you leave the route, intermediate points, profiles for car, truck, motorcycle, bicycle, walking, horse riding, skiing, boat, train and more, fine-tuning to avoid specific roads or unpaved roads and to set vehicle size and weight, and routing modes that include offline OsmAnd routing, BRouter, online routing and straight line. The Play listing shows 4.5 from 255K reviews and 10M+ downloads, and lists unlimited map downloads under paid Maps+ and several extras under OsmAnd Pro. The pages I read do not mention alternative routes by name, so if that is your reason for leaving Organic Maps, check it in the app. The price of Maps+ and Pro is not on the pages I read.

Google Maps with a downloaded area

This is the option most people already have. The help page says to download an area while connected and signed in, and that you can store it on the phone or an SD card. Offline it "can guide you to your destination as long as the entire route is within the offline map". The same page says transit, bicycling and walking directions are unavailable offline, and that driving offline gives no traffic info or alternate routes. It also warns that some countries and regions cannot be downloaded. For the narrow question of alternative routes, it is the one app here whose own page says they are missing offline. Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

For choosing where to go, not how to get there

I built NomadSwipe, so weigh this with that in mind. It is an Android app, not a navigation app, and its listing describes no route options, so it gives you nothing for the alternative-routes question. The listing does say you can open a place and start directions, but I did not test what that does. What it does: pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time, swipe right or tap Save to keep one, and your saved places are pinned on a trip map where you build a trip. Cards show category, rating, distance and opening status, and the list sorts by rating, distance or A to Z. The listing's sample trip is a long weekend in New York with 16 places, all picked by swiping.

Offline support, per the listing, is narrow: up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online, and the offline copies expire. The listing does not describe downloadable maps. A workable split is to choose the places in NomadSwipe, then add them as stops in an offline map app: Organic Maps takes up to 100 intermediate points by search, bookmark or tap, according to its how-to. Which app for which routing need

  • Fastest and shortest options, free and offline: Organic Maps, per its June 2026 news.
  • Avoiding tolls, ferries or unpaved roads on a drive: Organic Maps names all four of tolls, unpaved roads, motorways and ferries as settings toggles; OsmAnd lists avoiding specific roads and unpaved roads, plus vehicle size and weight.
  • A vehicle profile such as truck, boat or horse: OsmAnd; the others name none of these.
  • A community-run fork of Organic Maps: CoMaps.
  • Walking or cycling directions with no signal: not Google Maps, which says they are unavailable offline.
  • Choosing which places to visit before you route: NomadSwipe, alongside one of the above.

FAQ

Does Organic Maps show alternative routes?

Yes, since its June 2026 update. The Organic Maps news page says the app now shows the shortest route in addition to the fastest, and a July post lists alternate routes for driving, hiking and cycling. The September 2026 update improved them and their estimated arrival times. I did not open the app, so I cannot say how many routes it draws.

How do I avoid tolls or ferries in Organic Maps?

Open the app settings, go to Routing options and toggle the road types to avoid: tolls, unpaved roads, motorways and ferries. The route how-to says these settings apply to car routes, and that avoid options are also shown when a route is built if changing them could change the route.

Does Organic Maps do public transport routing?

The news page says public transport routing by subway, light rail, bus and tram arrived in the June 2026 update, and that live schedules are still in development. The route how-to lists a Subway route type. The Google Play listing words this more cautiously, so test your city before you rely on it.

What is a good alternative to Organic Maps for routing?

CoMaps is the closest, a community fork of Organic Maps and Maps.Me. OsmAnd lists the most routing controls on its pages: profiles for car, truck, bicycle, walking and more, avoided roads and several routing modes. Google Maps with a downloaded area is the mainstream option, with the limits its help page lists.

Does Google Maps give alternate routes offline?

Not for driving offline, according to the Google Maps help page: it says that if you drive offline you cannot get traffic info or alternate routes, and that transit, bicycling and walking directions are unavailable offline. Offline navigation only works when the whole route is inside the downloaded area.

Is NomadSwipe a navigation app?

No. I built NomadSwipe, an Android app where you swipe real places in a city, save the ones you like, see them on a map and build a trip. It is not a navigation app and its listing describes no downloadable maps, so it does not replace Organic Maps, CoMaps, OsmAnd or Google Maps for getting somewhere.

Does NomadSwipe work offline?

Partly. Its Google Play listing says up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online, and the copies expire. The listing does not describe downloadable maps.
