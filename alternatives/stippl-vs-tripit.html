Stippl vs TripIt: Which Trip Planner App Fits You?

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), which appears once below as a third option. Facts about Stippl and TripIt checked on 14 November 2026. Updated 14 November 2026.

Stippl vs TripIt: choose TripIt if the trip is already booked and you want the confirmation emails turned into one itinerary, with flight alerts on its paid tier; choose Stippl if you want to build the trip itself, with route, day plan, budget and journal in one app. Both are free to download and both sell a paid tier.

Key facts

  • On Google Play on 14 November 2026, TripIt showed 4.7 from 96.8K reviews and 5M+ downloads; Stippl showed 3.7 from 2.2K reviews and 100K+.
  • TripIt Pro is $49 a year on its Pro page, with a 30-day free trial. Stippl Pro is EUR 4.99 a month on its Pro page.
  • Forwarding booking emails is on TripIt's free plan but is a Pro feature in Stippl, per their pricing pages.
  • Real-time flight alerts are TripIt Pro. Budget tracking, a travel journal and AI itinerary generation are Stippl, and the AI part is Pro.
  • Stippl's own site quotes a Google Play rating that differs from the Play page itself, covered below.

Five questions that decide it

I built this walk-through from the pages above, in the order I would ask the questions. Each step names the app the pages favour.

  1. Is the trip already booked? TripIt's help article gives three ways to add plans: forward confirmations to plans@tripit.com, set up inbox sync, or add plans by hand. Its pricing page lists inbox import on the free plan. Stippl's Pro page puts forwarding reservation emails under Pro. TripIt.
  2. Do you want alerts on the day of travel? TripIt's Pro page lists real-time flight status, a check-in reminder 24 hours ahead, a Go Now prompt for leaving, terminal and gate updates and baggage claim info. I found none of this on the Stippl pages. TripIt, if you pay for Pro.
  3. Are you building the route and the days yourself? Stippl's Play listing describes adding cities and stops across countries, transport between them and an hour-by-hour schedule, and its free column lists unlimited destinations and activities. TripIt's pages describe adding plans and mapping them, not designing a route. Stippl.
  4. Are you splitting costs with a group? Stippl's Pro page lists unlimited expenses, viewing and settling expenses and a smart balance calculation on the free plan, plus unlimited co-planners. TripIt's pages list sharing plans, but no expense tracking that I could find. Stippl.
  5. How long will you pay? TripIt Pro is one annual price, $49. Stippl Pro is quoted monthly at EUR 4.99, which is EUR 59.88 over twelve months if you stay that long; the currencies differ, so this is not a like-for-like price. TripIt's Pro page also offers a 30-day free trial, so for a single short trip the trial may cover it; I did not check what the trial includes.

Where the numbers and pages disagree

Stippl's home page shows "4.7 App Store" and "4.4 Google Play". The Play page I read the same day shows 3.7 from 2.2K reviews, and the U.S. App Store page shows 4.6 from 488 ratings, while the home page says 4.7 across more than 8,000 ratings and does not say which countries that covers. I would trust the store pages over a marketing page, and I would not read a 3.7 against TripIt's 4.7 as a verdict on features, because the two apps do different jobs.

Reviews are worth a look before you pay. One Play review dated 7 November 2026 says saved locations vanished after Stippl's latest update, and the developer's reply dated 13 November says the redesign moved them: your lists sit under the compass icon, and each trip's places sit behind the pin icon at the top of that trip. I did not check that in the app. TripIt's Play listing shows "Bug fixes and performance improvements" as its latest note.

Size claims are also self-reported: Stippl says 1.5 million travelers, TripIt says nearly 20 million. Neither number was verified here.

Who each fits

TripIt

TripIt suits someone who books flights, hotels and cars across several sites and wants one itinerary built from the emails, with calendar sync on the free plan and flight alerts if Pro is worth $49 a year to them. Its own pricing page frames the free plan as organizing, and Pro as the alerts layer. It fits frequent flyers and anyone who dislikes typing plans in by hand. For how it works in practice, see how to use TripIt and TripIt alternatives.

Stippl

Stippl suits someone who starts from an empty trip and wants to shape it: cities on a route, a timeline for each day, a shared budget and a journal afterwards. Much of that sits on its free plan, while email forwarding, calendar sync, PDF export and unlimited AI plans are Pro. If it is the app you are weighing, see is Stippl worth it and Stippl alternatives.

Neither

If you do not have a trip yet and the hard part is deciding what to see in one city, neither page leads with that. Both start once you have a destination or a booking.

FAQ

Is Stippl or TripIt better?

Neither wins outright. TripIt is the stronger fit if your trip is already booked and you want the confirmations turned into one itinerary, with flight alerts on its paid tier. Stippl is the stronger fit if you want to build the trip inside the app: route, day plan, budget and journal. I did not test either app, so this is based on what their own pages say.

Is TripIt free?

It is free to sign up and download. TripIt's pricing page lists email-forwarded itineraries, calendar sync and three documents per trip on the free plan. TripIt Pro costs $49 a year according to its Pro page, which also offers a 30-day free trial, and real-time flight alerts are a Pro feature.

Is Stippl free?

It is free to download, and Stippl's Pro page lists unlimited trips, co-planners, expenses and journal entries on the free plan. Stippl Pro is €4.99 a month on that page and adds unlimited AI itineraries, PDF export, calendar sync and forwarding of reservation emails.

Can Stippl import booking emails like TripIt?

Yes, but only on its paid tier. Stippl's Pro page lists forwarding reservation emails as a Pro feature. TripIt lists adding plans from your inbox on its free plan, and its help center describes forwarding confirmations to plans@tripit.com plus an Inbox Sync option for Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft accounts.

Which one works offline?

Both mention it, in different words. Stippl's Play listing says routes, itinerary, bookings and maps are available offline. TripIt's Play listing says reservation details can be found even when you are offline. I did not test either app, so check before relying on it abroad.

Is NomadSwipe a Stippl or TripIt alternative?

Only for one job. I built NomadSwipe, an Android app where you swipe real places in a city, save the ones you like, see them on a map and build a trip. It does not read booking emails the way TripIt does, and it does not track budgets, journals or reels the way Stippl does.
