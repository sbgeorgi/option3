How many days do you need in Porto? 48 sights counted

How many days do you need in Porto? The NomadSwipe app's Porto data has 19 well-rated sights (4.0+ with 100+ reviews) within 6 km. At five a day that is 3.8 days, so I would allow four, or three if you stay central: 15 of the 19 sit within 2 km. It is a counting rule, not a measured fact.

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), and I wrote this page. Every number comes from the NomadSwipe app's stored Porto place data, read with read-only queries on 9 November 2026. I used no outside source, so there are no visit times, ticket prices or fares here.

The counting rule

The data has no visit times, so I cannot measure how long a sight takes. What I can do is count, and I state the rule so you can change it. I use the same rule as on my how many days in Lisbon and how many days in Budapest pages: about five sights a day, with the rest of a day's stops going to food and drink. The rule is days = sights worth seeing ÷ sights per day. It is my own choice, and the sensitivity table below shows what happens at four and six a day. My Porto 3-day itinerary is a little slower than five: it holds 13 sights in three days, about 4.3 a day.

Which sights count is also a choice. My sight list is the stored types museum, art museum, history museum, tourist attraction, historical landmark, landmark, monument, sculpture or statue, park, beach, bridge, church and winery: 13 types, 48 cards. "Well-rated" means a stored rating of 4.0 or higher with 100 or more stored reviews, which gives 19 of the 48.

Who needs fewer days, who needs more

  • Two days is enough if you want the best-known sights only. The first ten by reviews cover 10 of the 19 well-rated ones, and 9 of those 10 sit within 1.4 km of the centre point. My Porto 3-day guide says days one and two stand alone as a 2-day plan.
  • Three days is the default if you stay central: 15 of the 19 well-rated sights are within 2 km, and 15 is three days at five a day.
  • Four days if you want all 19, you walk slowly (4 a day makes it 4.8), or you like museums. The deck holds 26 museum cards, and several are small ones such as a dollhouse museum, a puppet museum and an Egyptian-themed one.
  • The port cellars do not add a day. The deck has three winery cards (Caves Ferreira, Cockburn's and Caves Cálem) plus Sandeman stored as a tourist attraction, all within 1.4 km of the centre point and all with Vila Nova de Gaia addresses. That is four stops, or about 0.8 of a day at five a day.
  • Food and nightlife do not change the number. Within 6 km the deck has 388 places to eat and drink plus 58 bars, 167 of them rated 4.0+ with 100+ reviews. At about three food stops a day, four days use 12 of them.
  • Add your own pins and you add days. Searching card titles found no sight card for the Torre dos Clérigos, Serralves, São Bento station or the Bolhão Market. Each one you add is another sight, so four added places is about 0.8 of a day.

What this count cannot tell you

  • How long a sight takes. The data has no visit times, so five a day is my assumption, not a measurement. A cellar tasting or a museum you love can take half a day.
  • Prices, tickets and getting around. I did not read them for this page, so check each place's own site and a transit map. Porto is hilly and I have no gradient data.
  • Opening days. 32 of the 48 sights have stored hours, and they are a snapshot from 2 October 2026. Check the day you plan to go.
  • Sights the deck lacks. A deck holds only the places the app has, so a famous sight with no card is not counted.

Questions about how many days to spend in Porto

Is 3 days enough in Porto?

By my counting rule, yes for the centre and the river. The Porto deck has 15 sights rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews within 2 km of its centre point, and three days at five sights a day is exactly 15 slots. All 19 well-rated sights within 6 km need 3.8 days, so three days leaves out the last four by review count: Praia do Carneiro, the Atkinson Museum, the Monumento aos Heróis da Guerra Peninsular and the dollhouse museum.

Is 2 days enough in Porto?

Two days holds 10 sights at five a day, which covers 10 of the 19 well-rated ones, and 9 of those 10 sit within 1.4 km of the centre point. Push to six sights a day and the 19 need 3.2 days, so two days is a trip of the best-known sights only: the Luís I Bridge, the Palácio da Bolsa, the cathedral, the Praça da Liberdade and the port cellars across the river.

How many days do you need in Porto to see everything?

Nobody sees everything, but the count is large. The app's Porto data has 48 sight cards within 6 km, which is 9.6 days at five a day, and 26 of them are museums. Of the 48, 19 are rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews, 18 have between 1 and 99 reviews and 9 have no review count, so beyond day 4 the data gives you far less quality signal.

Do the port wine cellars need an extra day?

Not by this count. The deck has three cards typed as wineries, Caves Ferreira, Cockburn's and Caves Cálem, plus Sandeman stored as a tourist attraction. That is four cellar stops, all within 1.4 km of the centre point and all stored with Vila Nova de Gaia addresses, so at five stops a day they use about 0.8 of one day. The app stores no tour lengths, so a long tasting can change that.

Does the number of restaurants change how many days I need in Porto?

Not in this data. Within 6 km the deck has 388 places to eat and drink and 58 bars, and 155 of the food places are rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews. At about three food stops a day, four days use 12 of them, so sights set the day count and food never does.
