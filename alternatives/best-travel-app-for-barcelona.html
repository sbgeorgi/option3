What changes in Barcelona itself

These are the local facts that decide which app you open, each from an official city, transit or sight page.

Language: Catalan and Spanish, with English common

The city council's welcome site says the two official languages are Catalan and Spanish, with Catalan recognised as the city's own language, that the vast majority of people from Barcelona consider themselves fully bilingual, and that many also have a strong command of English. In practice I saw it on the pages themselves: TMB's English app page left several paragraphs in Catalan, and Citymapper's Barcelona page showed rail service notices in Catalan. None of that blocks you, but a transit notice may arrive in Catalan, so a translate button helps.

Transit: TMB fares and T-mobilitat

TMB's fares page lists what a visitor chooses between for metro and bus: a €2.90 single ticket valid for metro, bus and the Montjuïc funicular, a €5.90 airport ticket, a 10-journey T-casual from €13 (not valid at the Aeroport T1 and T2 stations on L9 Sud), and the Hola Barcelona Travel Card from €12.50, with the return metro trips to and from the airport included. T-mobilitat, which TMB describes as its new contactless ticketing and validation system, is managed in TMB App. The TMB home page also carried the L2 closure (La Pau to Gorg, until 3 December inclusive) on the day I read it, so check the planner before you leave.

Sights: timed tickets and online-only sales

Two of the city's biggest Gaudí sights are ticket-first. The basilica's individual ticket page lists €26 for the visit, €30 with a guided tour and €40 with a guided tour and a tower. Park Güell's official site says its tickets are online only, that you must reach the access control at the assigned time, that you have 30 minutes after the time on your ticket to enter, and that once you leave you cannot re-enter. It also lists 12 hectares of monumental and green areas with panoramic views of the city. Plan those two before you plan anything loose.

Eating hours: late

Turisme de Barcelona's tips page says lunch is usually around 2 pm and dinner tends to start from 9 pm onwards. That matters for any app that shows whether a place is open: an early dinner plan can land on an empty kitchen.

Passes: Barcelona Card, Travel Card and sight tickets

Passes are not apps, but they decide what your apps need to do. Turisme de Barcelona's Barcelona Card page lists 48, 72, 96 and 120 hour cards, with a 48 hour adult card at €39.00 listed at €35.10 with the 10% online discount, and a 72 hour adult card at €59.00, listed at €53.10 online. It says the card includes admission to more than 25 museums and cultural venues, unlimited public transport only in the option that includes it, and more than 70 discounts. A cheaper "Essential" version without transport is listed, at €29.00 for 72 hours for an adult. I did not read the list of covered museums, so I cannot say which sights it covers.

The Hola Barcelona Travel Card is transport only. The sources disagree on its length and price: TMB's fares page says "up to 5 days" from €12.50, the Hola Barcelona site lists 1 day (24 h) to 5 days (120 h), and Turisme de Barcelona's getting-around page lists 48, 72, 96 or 120 consecutive hours from €17.76 with a 5% discount. I do not know which duration the €12.50 belongs to, and the three pages may simply sell different durations or channels.

To compare: the sight tickets above are priced separately from either pass, so add up what you actually plan to buy (for example the basilica at €26 plus your transit days) before choosing a card.

The apps, one at a time

TMB App: the operator's own, for metro and bus

The Google Play listing is published by Transports Metropolitans de Barcelona and describes planning trips that combine bus, metro and shared bikes from Bicing, AMBici, Donkey Republic, Cooltra and Bolt, saving favourite places, routes and stops, real-time updates with personalised alerts, and a scanner for ddTag timetables at stops. It says you pay with GPay and Apple Pay; TMB's own app page lists Visa, Mastercard, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Bizum, so the two pages differ on payment methods. The listing was updated 27 October 2026 and showed 2.9 from 11.1K reviews with 1M+ downloads. I did not open the app, so I cannot say what drives the score.

Hola Barcelona: tickets and passes for visitors

TMB's visit page lists "App Hola Barcelona" as its visitor app, and the Play listing shows the same developer, Transports Metropolitans de Barcelona. It calls itself the official shop for travel cards, tourist bus and cable car passes and tour tickets, stores them in an in-app wallet, and offers downloadable routes for 2, 3, 4 and 5 days that name Sagrada Familia, Park Güell, Casa Batlló, Las Ramblas, La Pedrera, Port Olímpic, Montjuïc Castle and the Magic Fountain. Listing: 4.1 from 993 reviews, 100K+ downloads, updated 30 September 2026. It is a seller as well as a guide, so judge its suggestions with that in mind.

Sagrada Familia Official: one building

The basilica's app page says you can buy tickets ahead of time or the same day, save them and use the app to enter, and download an audioguide. The Play listing adds an audioguide in 19 languages, AR, a map of the basilica, an AI assistant and sign-language guides, 2.4 from 1.91K reviews and 1M+ downloads, updated 4 November 2026. Its Data safety section says data can't be deleted. It helps with one stop, not the rest of the city.

Citymapper: a transit planner

The Citymapper Barcelona page shows nearby Bus, Metro, FGC, Rodalies, TRAM and bike options and a list of line notices, with an app for iPhone and Android. The Play listing describes combining bus, subway, train, ferry, bike share, e-scooters and walking, and names US cities first with "many more worldwide", so I cannot confirm Barcelona from the Play text alone and the web page is my evidence. It was updated 4 November 2026. If you want more options in this class, see apps like Citymapper.

Google Maps: the default planner

The Play listing describes directions for driving, walking, cycling and public transport, custom lists of favourite saved places and photos and reviews, and adds that some features are not available in all countries or cities. Its help page says that when you are offline, transit, bicycling or walking directions are unavailable, which matters for a metro-and-walking city. I did not test transit in Barcelona.

Who should pick which

  • You will ride the metro and buses most days: TMB App for its own ticket system, with Citymapper or Google Maps as the planner. TMB's L2 closure is the kind of notice to look for there.
  • You do not know what to see yet: NomadSwipe to pick places, next to one of the transit apps above. I make no claim that its saved list moves into them automatically.
  • You want to buy a transport card and tourist bus tickets in one place before you land: Hola Barcelona, keeping in mind it is a seller.
  • Sagrada Família is the priority: the basilica's own app or website for tickets. Park Güell is online-only, so book it before you arrive.
  • You walk a lot and want one familiar app: Google Maps, with the help page's limits for offline use in mind.
  • You have only a day or two and a fixed list of famous sights: you may need no new app beyond tickets and a map.

  • The sources disagree on the Hola Barcelona Travel Card's length and starting price, and on TMB App's payment methods.

FAQ

What is the best travel app for Barcelona?

There is no single best one, because a Barcelona visit needs several jobs done: moving around, buying tickets and choosing places. TMB App covers metro and bus per its Google Play listing, the Sagrada Família Official app covers that basilica's tickets, and Google Maps or Citymapper cover journey planning. I built NomadSwipe (Android), which only helps with choosing and saving places. I installed none of these apps, so this is what their pages say.

Which app do I need for the metro and bus in Barcelona?

TMB App is the operator's own app. Its Google Play listing says you can combine bus, metro and shared bikes, and buy, top up and validate T-mobilitat cards. TMB's fares page also lists a single ticket for metro, bus and the Montjuïc funicular at €2.90, and the Hola Barcelona Travel Card from €12.50. Google Maps and Citymapper are the planners that are not run by the operator.

Do I need the Sagrada Família app to visit?

The basilica's site calls its app the official one: you can buy tickets in it, link them to the app and use them to enter, and it includes an audioguide. The basilica's site also sells tickets, so the app is a convenience I did not test, not a stated requirement. Its Google Play listing showed 2.4 from 1.91K reviews on 15 November 2026.

Is the Barcelona Card or the Hola Barcelona Travel Card better for a visit?

They are different products. The Hola Barcelona Travel Card is public transport, with the metro trip to and from the airport included per TMB. The Barcelona Card, per Turisme de Barcelona's ticket page, adds admission to more than 25 museums and cultural venues, with unlimited transport only in the option that includes it. I did not check which sights each covers, so add up the tickets you plan to buy before choosing.

Do I need Catalan or Spanish to use travel apps in Barcelona?

No, but expect both. The city council's welcome site says the two official languages are Catalan and Spanish, that the vast majority of people from Barcelona consider themselves fully bilingual, and that many also have a strong command of English. The apps and pages I read were in English, but some text on TMB's English app page and Citymapper's service notices was in Catalan.
