Swipe travel apps for finding places to visit in a city, compared

Updated 9 November 2026. Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, which is Android only for now.

Swipe travel apps show places one card at a time so you can save the ones you like. NomadSwipe, which I built, swipes real places in one city on Android and saves them to a trip map. The four other apps below are all listed as iPhone only.

A search on 9 November 2026 for swipe apps for restaurants, things to do and places to visit turned up these, each read from its store listing: Swipe to Discover, Tabical, stuff2do and Honua. Flavr is left out, because its listing does not mention swiping. This page says where each one is the better pick, including where it beats NomadSwipe.

NomadSwipe

Best for: choosing places in one city by swiping

NomadSwipe is an Android trip planner for finding places. You pick a city and it shows real places one card at a time. Each card shows the category, rating, distance and whether the place is open. Save a place to keep it, or skip it. Saved places are pinned on a map for that trip, and the list can be sorted by rating, distance or A to Z.

  • Strength: a shortlist of places you chose yourself, before you have a plan, saved to a map.
  • Limit: it does not build an itinerary. You plan each day yourself around the map.
  • Limit: it is Android only for now. An iPhone version is coming soon.

The free plan includes 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip a month, 2 custom pins a month and a daily limit on saves. Premium (Monthly or Lifetime, bought in the app) removes those limits. Up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online; offline copies expire.

Swipe to Discover

Best for: saving places and turning them into a day-by-day itinerary on iPhone

Its App Store listing (the Italian storefront, so prices are in euros) describes swiping right to save a place and left to skip it. Its Trip Mode, listed as a premium feature, turns saved places into a day-by-day itinerary, can auto-plan it in one tap and can export the route to Google Maps. Offline access to saved spots is also listed as premium. The app is free to download with in-app purchases, at 5.99 euro monthly or 44.99 euro yearly there. The listing carries an 18+ age rating and says it contains ads.

  • Strength: it goes further than NomadSwipe after the swiping, because it lists a day-by-day itinerary and auto-planning.
  • Limit: the listing is for iPhone only, and Android is not listed.
  • Limit: the trip and offline features are listed as premium.

Tabical

Best for: short outings of a few stops in a handful of US cities, on iPhone

Its App Store listing describes each card as a complete micro-itinerary for your city, with 2 to 4 stops that show photos, ratings, hours, price level, category and vibe tags. Swipe right to save a card and left to pass. It names eight cities: New York City, Washington DC, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Atlanta, Boston, Seattle and Chicago, and says more are coming. It is listed as free and mentions no in-app purchases, and it requires iOS 18.0 or later.

  • Strength: ready-made outings of a few stops, so there is less to put together yourself.
  • Limit: iPhone only, and limited to the eight listed cities for now.
  • Limit: each card is a pre-built plan, so it is a weaker fit if you want to choose individual places for a trip.

stuff2do

Best for: picking one thing to do tonight, on iPhone

Its App Store listing describes a 50-second bracket game. You pick one of six categories, swipe right or left on ten activity cards that show a one-line description and no name, and the game ends with one winner. Then you scratch the card to reveal the name, photos, rating and directions. The listing says the free version gives 3 games a day and contains advertising, and that Pro removes ads and allows unlimited games at $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year.

  • Strength: a quick way to settle on one activity near you when you cannot decide.
  • Limit: it ends with one winner, so it is not built for saving a shortlist of places to a trip map.
  • Limit: its listing is for iPhone only.

Honua

Best for: browsing photos of places around the world and saving favorites on a map

Honua's App Store listing says you swipe through photos of locations around the world, add the ones you like to your favorites, and see those favorites saved on a map. It also says the app relies on a network of photographers who chose to share their pictures. The listing shows the price as Free and does not mention in-app purchases.

  • Strength: a map of the places you saved, on iPhone, at no listed price.
  • Limit: its App Store listing is for iPhone, and it does not mention Android.
  • Limit: it is built around photos of locations worldwide, so it is a weaker fit for shortlisting one city's places.

FAQ

Is there a swipe travel app for Android?

NomadSwipe is on Google Play, and I built it. The listings for Swipe to Discover, Tabical, stuff2do and Honua are all for iPhone only, so this page does not claim an Android version of any of them.

Which swipe app saves places to a map?

NomadSwipe pins the places you save on a map for each trip. Honua's listing says your favorites are saved on a map. The Tabical, stuff2do and Swipe to Discover listings do not describe a map of saved places, so check the app before you rely on it.

Is NomadSwipe free?

NomadSwipe has a free plan with 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip a month, 2 custom pins a month and a daily limit on saves. Premium (Monthly or Lifetime) is bought in the app, and its price is shown in Google Play before you pay.

Is Honua on Android?

Its App Store listing does not mention Android, so this page does not claim an Android version.

Is NomadSwipe on iPhone?

Not yet. NomadSwipe is Android only today, and an iPhone version is coming soon.

Does NomadSwipe work offline?

Up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online. Offline copies expire, according to the Google Play listing.

Does NomadSwipe build an itinerary for me?

No. It gives you a shortlist of real places and a map for each trip, and you plan each day yourself around that map.
