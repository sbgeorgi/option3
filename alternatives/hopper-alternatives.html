What Hopper is today

Hopper is a mobile app for booking flights, hotels, homes and rental cars, and it is still maintained: its Google Play listing showed an update on 18 November 2026, 4.5 stars from 151K reviews and 10M+ downloads. The listing says you can "watch a trip" and get a notification "when it's the best time to buy", recommending you "book now, or maybe wait just a little longer". It also describes a color-coded deals calendar for finding the cheapest dates, "Cancel and Change for Any Reason" plans, a Flight Disruption Guarantee and 24/7 access to customer support.

Hopper's price prediction page says it "analyzes trillions of data points" to give guidance on whether to book now or wait, and that the feature is "exclusively available in the Hopper app". The homepage has Stays, Flights and Cars tabs and invites you to "join 120 million travelers", while the Play listing says the app has helped "over 100 million travelers"; the two pages do not give the same figure, and I did not try to reconcile them. So the job people mean by "Hopper" is the one on those pages: watch a trip, get told when to buy, then book it in the app.

Why people look for Hopper alternatives

I read no reviews or forums, so I do not say why anyone leaves Hopper. These are the places where the pages leave a question, which is usually why a comparison like this gets opened:

  • Prediction is one feature of one app. The page says price prediction is exclusive to the Hopper app. If you want to track prices without it, the other pages below describe alerts, but only Hopper's pages promise a "book now or wait" call.
  • Where you pay differs. Hopper books in its own app. Skyscanner and KAYAK describe comparing other sites. Which is better depends on whether you want one app or one comparison.
  • The job may be hotels, not flights. Hopper's price watching is described for trips, while Booking.com's listing is stay-first and its text mentions no price tracking.
  • Ratings are not like for like. Hopper shows 4.5 on Google Play and the others 4.8, but each store page counts its own reviewers, and I did not read them.

Google Flights: tracking and price insights, flights only

The Google Flights page says it helps you "find and book cheap flights worldwide and track prices", with a date grid and price graph, and "price history and trend data" to show the best time to book. Its help article on tracking says you turn on "Track prices" for a route, or for a specific flight, and get email updates when the price changes significantly. It adds that you also get an email when prices on a route are likely to go up, with an estimate of how much and a confidence level. That is the nearest thing I read to Hopper's "buy now or wait", though the article describes emails, not an in-app recommendation. It is a website feature, so there is no Play rating to compare, and I did not test it.

Skyscanner: compare first, no booking fee per its listing

The Skyscanner listing says it compares flight prices across airlines, online travel agencies and booking platforms, and that you "book directly with airlines, hotels, and travel agencies". It describes an "Everywhere" search for ideas, filters, a calendar view of the cheapest dates in a month, a Price Alert for a flight price change, a saved feature with hearts, hotel search and rental cars. It says it charges no booking fees on flights, hotels or cars. Skyscanner is the closest to Hopper if what you want is the deals calendar and alerts but you are happy to finish the booking elsewhere. The listing carries Google Play's "Contains ads" label.

KAYAK and momondo: one search across many sites

The KAYAK listing says it searches hundreds of travel sites to show your options, lists a flight tracker, mobile-only hotel rates and car sharing, and tracks search results with a notification when prices change. A Budget search, KAYAK Explore, shows flights for a set amount, its example being $300. A momondo listing from the same developer, KAYAK.com, uses almost the same text, so I count them as one choice and not two. Both carry the "Contains ads" label. Compared with Hopper, the framing is a search engine that shows you the options, not a booking app that tells you when to act.

Expedia: the full booking app, with flight price tracking

The Expedia listing describes one app for flights, hotels, cars, activities and vacation packages, with "Price Tracking" alerts when flight prices drop, Flight Deals on a map, a "Bundle & Save" offer and member prices. It says you can cancel for free on most hotels, and that trip alerts cover flight delays and gate changes. The savings figures on the listing carry footnotes I did not read, so I quote none. It is the closest like-for-like to Hopper as a place to book flights, hotels and cars together, and it too carries the "Contains ads" label.

Booking.com: for stays first

The Booking.com listing leads with hotels, apartments and homes, adds flights, car rentals and taxis, and lists free cancellation at most properties, 24/7 customer service in more than 40 languages and no booking or credit card fees. I saw no price tracking in the listing text, so for the Hopper job of watching a price it is the weakest fit here, and for finding a room it is the strongest on its own claims. The listing also says it has a Travel Communities forum and local-attractions discovery, which I did not test.

Hopper alternatives: how each one handles "when should I book"

This is the part that separates them, and the pages differ in kind, not only in degree. Hopper's pages promise a call: book now or wait. Google Flights' help article describes email updates with an estimate of a likely rise and a confidence level. Skyscanner, KAYAK and Expedia describe alerts when a price changes or drops, which tell you what happened, not what to do. Booking.com's listing describes none. I cannot say how accurate any of them is, because I ran no searches and compared no fares over time. If that accuracy is your reason to switch, test one tracked route in two apps for a few weeks before you rely on either.

A second split is where you pay. Hopper and Expedia describe booking inside the app. Skyscanner and KAYAK describe comparing providers and sending you to them. Google Flights' page says "find and book" without saying who takes the payment, so check that before you rely on it.

Which job needs which

  • Watch one flight price and be told: Google Flights, Skyscanner, KAYAK or Expedia per their pages; Hopper if you want the book-now-or-wait call.
  • Compare many sites in one search: Skyscanner or KAYAK.
  • Book flights, hotel and car in one app: Expedia, or Hopper itself.
  • Find a room: Booking.com first, then the hotel tabs of the others.
  • Decide what to do in the city once it is booked: not any of the above. That is NomadSwipe's job, and a different step. The trip planner comparison and swipe travel apps cover it, and TripIt alternatives cover keeping your bookings in one place.

When Hopper is still the better choice

On the pages I read, Hopper is the only one that pairs a price-watching feature with a stated book-now-or-wait recommendation, its own "Change for Any Reason" and "Cancel for Any Reason" plans and a flight disruption guarantee in one listing. Expedia and Booking.com describe free cancellation on most hotels, which is a different promise. If those are what you came for, none of the apps here replaces it, and that is the reason to stay.

The short version

For the flight-watching job, start with Google Flights, Skyscanner or KAYAK; for booking everything in one app, Expedia; for rooms, Booking.com. For the step after you book, which Hopper does not cover, I built NomadSwipe (Android) to pick the places and put them on a map. Each is a different job, so choose the job before the app.
