Is Citymapper worth it? Yes for city transit on its list; no for driving or choosing sights

Is Citymapper worth it? Yes, if you ride buses, trains or bikes in a city on its cities page, which names 124 entries. Its own news post says ads keep the app free. It will not choose what to see, and it is not built for drivers.

Key facts

  • What it is: a journey planner for city transport. Its home page offers a web app plus iPhone and Android apps, and its Google Play listing adds a Wear OS watch version.
  • Cost: the App Store listing shows Free with in-app purchases, and Google Play shows ads and in-app purchases. The one price on a Citymapper page I read is the ad-free CLUB tier in a news post: £1.49 a month.
  • Reach: its cities page lists 124 entries, 74 in Europe, 38 in the US and Canada and 12 under Asia and rest of world. Some entries are whole countries or regions, such as Belgium and Iceland.
  • Reviews: 4.9 on the App Store from 111,007 U.S. ratings per Apple's lookup record, 4.8 on Google Play with 10M+ downloads, but 2.4 from 66 reviews on Trustpilot.
  • Owner and upkeep: it joined Via per its own post, and version 11.54.2 came out on 10 November 2026 per Apple's record.

Cost: what is clear and what is not

Citymapper's CLUB post says the features once reserved for CLUB, including voice instructions, Walk Less routes, Lock Screen Navigation and ranking by speed or price, are open to everyone, and that an ad-free Citymapper costs £1.49 a month after a 50% cut. The post carries no date. Its terms page describes CLUB as "the paid version of our App that contains premium content", billed in advance at the start of a 12 month subscription period and not refundable except at Citymapper's discretion. Those two descriptions do not quite match, and the App Store listing shows no price list for its in-app purchases.

Independent reviewers give dollar figures. How-To Geek (published 3 December 2025) quotes $1.49 a month or $9.99 a year for the ad-free tier and calls the ads minimal pop-ups. I found no annual price on any Citymapper page, so treat that figure as unconfirmed, and read the number on your own purchase screen before you subscribe. If you cancel, the terms say you keep CLUB until the end of the paid period, and you cancel on the same platform where you paid, through Apple or Google Play.

Pros and cons, by source

Pros

  • Cost shown per mode. How-To Geek calls showing the fare for each option, including ride-hail estimates, a standout feature.
  • Mixed and gentler routes. The CLUB post lists Mixed, Walk Less, Simple and extra bus or train options, and says step-free routes are always free.
  • An AI pick, optional. A news post says a sparkle button shows the top two routes with a written reason.
  • Offline maps. The App Store text says official maps of some systems, such as the NYC subway, open offline.
  • Strong scores. 4.9 on the App Store from 111,007 U.S. ratings (Apple lookup).

Cons

  • Coverage. Six of my 19 destinations are not named on the cities page.
  • Bad Trustpilot score. 2.4 from 66 reviews, 54% of them one-star, on an unclaimed profile where the company has not invited reviews.
  • Accuracy complaints. One July 2026 Trustpilot reviewer says bus results and partial Underground closures confuse the app in London, and a 2024 App Store reviewer says bus times were only sometimes accurate.
  • Ads. The Play listing says it contains ads.
  • Android-only feature. The CLUB post says its best-time-to-leave planning is only on Android.

Two caveats on the reviews. The scores point opposite ways because they come from different groups: the store ratings cover far more people, while Trustpilot reviewers lean toward people with a complaint. And Pilot Plans, which rates Citymapper 4.8 out of 5, publishes its own trip planner, so read its verdict knowing that.

Is it still being worked on?

Yes. Apple's record shows version 11.54.2 released on 10 November 2026 and a 313 MB iPhone download that needs iOS 17, and the Play listing was updated on 4 November 2026. Ownership changed: its own post says it joined Via and that the consumer apps will continue, and Wikipedia dates the acquisition to March 2023. A ten-minute test before you rely on it

  1. Look up your destination on the cities page. If your city is not named, search for its country or region, and stop here if neither appears.
  2. Plan one real route you already know. Compare the time and the lines it suggests with what you would normally do. One reviewer reports poor bus results in London, so test the mode you will use most.
  3. Find the ad-free price on the purchase screen, then decide. If the ads do not bother you, you have no reason to pay.

The short version

As of 16 Nov 2026, Citymapper is worth installing for public transit, cycling and walking in the cities on its cities page, which names 124 entries. Its CLUB post says ads keep the app free and puts the ad-free tier at £1.49 a month, though the post is undated and its terms describe a 12-month billing period. It is not for drivers, for towns it does not list, or for choosing what to see. Ratings split: 4.9 on the App Store, 4.8 on Google Play, 2.4 on Trustpilot.

Questions about whether Citymapper is worth it

Is Citymapper free?

Mostly. Citymapper's own news post says ads keep the app free and that an ad-free version, called CLUB, was priced at £1.49 a month when that post was written. The post is undated and its terms page describes CLUB as billed per 12-month period, so read the price on your purchase screen.

Is Citymapper worth it for a short city trip?

Yes, if the city is named on its cities page and you will ride buses, trains, trams or ferries, or hire a bike or scooter. Check the list first, because it names 124 entries, and some cities travellers visit are not on it. Citymapper routes you between places; it does not suggest which places to visit.

Does Citymapper work in my city?

Look for your city or its country or region on Citymapper's cities page. On 16 Nov 2026 it named London, Paris, Tokyo and Mexico City, but not Bangkok, Mumbai, Cairo or Cape Town. The page says it is expanding, and the store listings let you vote for the next city.

Can Citymapper help me choose what to see in a city?

Not in the feature lists I read: its job is the route between places you already chose. For choosing places, NomadSwipe (Android, which I built) deals real places in a city as cards, you swipe to save, and the saved places appear on a map. NomadSwipe does no transit routing, so pair the two.
