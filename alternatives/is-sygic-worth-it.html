Is Sygic worth it? A verdict by type of traveler

Is Sygic worth it? For the Sygic Travel trip planner, now called Tripomatic, yes for day-by-day itineraries: its free plan covers 5 trip days a month and 25 places per trip, and Premium at $29.99 a year pays off mainly for offline maps and unlimited AI planning. Those are Tripomatic's own figures from its pricing page, read on 23 November 2026, and I did not test the app.

Which Sygic, and what it is called now

"Sygic" covers two kinds of app, and the answer differs. Sygic's own site lists Sygic GPS Navigation, Sygic Truck & Caravan GPS Navigation, Sygic Travel and Fuelio as separate apps. This page is about Sygic Travel, the trip planner. If you meant the driving navigator, read Is Sygic better than Google Maps instead.

Sygic Travel has a new name. Tripomatic's support site says: "In November 2024, Sygic Travel was rebranded to Tripomatic", with existing accounts migrated when you signed in to the new app, and that Tripomatic 26 launched on 4 February 2026. When I opened travel.sygic.com, my reader landed on tripomatic.com. The old sygic.com/travel page is still online and still describes "Sygic Travel Maps", so it can disagree with the new app. The Google Play listing reads Tripomatic Planner & Maps (formerly Sygic Travel), by Tripomatic s.r.o., with 1M+ downloads and an update dated 19 November 2026. For the shorter list of other planners, see Sygic Travel alternatives.

The limits and complaints to know about

  • Ratings: Google Play shows 4.4 from 1.27K reviews, and the U.S. App Store shows 4.6 from 3.3K ratings. The Play reviews visible on the listing page I read are dated 2019 to 2021, so they say little about the 2026 app.
  • The redesign: on an App Store review aggregator (published 26 August 2026), a paid user writes that saved sights from the previous version were gone, and another says Sygic "has now moved to being primarily a map application" with harder itinerary planning, done on a "legacy" website. These are two reviews, not a measured trend. The same page lists offline maps, a large database of sights and responsive developers as the positive takeaways.
  • No independent review base: Tripomatic's Trustpilot page shows 0 reviews. That aggregator's automated tally of 3,311 reviews (66.4% negative) I do not rely on, because I cannot see how it was built.
  • Data: the Google Play data safety section says the app may collect location, personal info and 3 other types, and may share app activity and device or other IDs with third parties. That is the developer's own declaration.
  • Not covered: I did not read Premium's cost in other currencies, the refund terms, or how well offline maps behave in practice.

Who should pick what

  1. Tripomatic (Sygic Travel): you want a day-by-day itinerary with drag-and-drop days, walking and transit times between stops, and a place database. Start on the free plan. Pay the $29.99 a year only after you hit the 5 days or 25 places cap, or if you need offline maps.
  2. Different need: NomadSwipe. I built NomadSwipe, an Android app for the step before the itinerary: you pick a city, swipe through real places one card at a time, save the ones you like and see the whole shortlist on a map. It works in any city worldwide. It is not an itinerary scheduler, an offline map app or a navigator, and iPhone is coming soon. See it on Google Play.
  3. Wanderlog: you want one app for the itinerary, the map and your bookings, with real-time editing. I did not find its Premium price, so read its pricing page before you rely on offline access.
  4. A dedicated offline map app: you mostly want maps without signal. Read Is Maps.ME worth it and Sygic Travel alternatives for what each one lists.

In short

As of 23 November 2026, Sygic Travel is now Tripomatic and is worth using as a day-by-day trip planner if your trip fits its free plan (5 trip days a month, 25 places per trip) or if you value offline maps enough to pay $29.99 a year. It is not the navigator Sygic is also known for, it is not built for swiping through places, and its ratings (4.4 on Google Play, 4.6 on the U.S. App Store) sit beside user complaints about the redesign. Use the 7-day trial to test your own destination offline before you pay.

Questions about whether Sygic is worth it

Is Sygic worth it?

For the trip planner, yes if your trip fits the free plan or you need offline maps. Tripomatic (formerly Sygic Travel) lists a free plan with 5 trip days a month and 25 places per trip, and Premium at $29.99 a year for unlimited days and places, offline maps and unlimited AI planning. I did not install or test it, so this is a reading of its own pages.

Is Sygic Travel still available?

It was renamed. Tripomatic's support site says Sygic Travel was rebranded to Tripomatic in November 2024 and that Tripomatic 26 launched on 4 February 2026 at maps.tripomatic.com. The Google Play listing now reads Tripomatic Planner & Maps (formerly Sygic Travel).

How much does Sygic Travel (Tripomatic) cost?

Tripomatic's pricing page, read 23 November 2026, lists a free plan at $0 and Premium at $29.99 a year after a 7-day free trial, with taxes added at checkout. Prices can differ by country and change, so check the pricing page before you buy.

Does Sygic Travel work offline?

Only with Premium, according to Tripomatic. Its pricing page lists saving trips and maps offline as a Premium feature, and its Google Play listing says Premium adds unlimited offline map downloads and walking navigation.

Is Sygic the same as Sygic GPS Navigation?

No. Sygic's own site lists them as separate apps: Sygic GPS Navigation is a driving navigator, and Sygic Travel is the trip planner this page covers. For the navigator, see Is Sygic better than Google Maps.

What if I want to choose places by swiping instead of building an itinerary?

That is a different job. NomadSwipe, which I built, is an Android app where you swipe real places in any city, save the ones you like and see them on a map. It is not an itinerary scheduler, an offline map app or a navigator.
