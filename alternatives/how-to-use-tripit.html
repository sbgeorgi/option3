How to use TripIt in 5 steps

How to use TripIt: forward each flight, hotel or car confirmation email to plans@tripit.com and it builds one itinerary per trip. Then check the trip it made, add by hand what email cannot carry, subscribe your calendar to its feed, and decide whether Pro is worth it. TripIt organises bookings you have already made. It is not a tool for finding things to do. For another planner walkthrough, see how to use Funliday.

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), a place-discovery app, and I wrote this page, so I have an interest in the last section. Every TripIt step below comes from TripIt's own help and product pages, which I read on 9 November 2026. I did not run TripIt for this page, so there are no screenshots of it and no opinions on how it feels to use. The full list of pages is in the Findings section.

Step by step

  1. 1. Forward a confirmation email to plans@tripit.com.

    This is the step TripIt's how-it-works page leads with: as soon as you book a flight, hotel, car or other reservation, forward it to plans@tripit.com and TripIt adds it to your trip. Its help centre adds the details that stop it failing. Send the original confirmation, because copied text, typed itineraries and screenshots do not count. Forward from the email address linked to your main TripIt account, since a different address creates a separate account. Forward from your own inbox, not from the airline or booking site, because TripIt identifies your account by the sender's address. Use only plans@tripit.com: support@tripit.com and other @tripit.com addresses will not post anything to your account.

    If you do not want to forward, the same help page lists Inbox Sync, which adds travel plans from your inbox automatically and works with Gmail, Yahoo or Microsoft accounts. Setup is on a separate TripIt page that I did not read, so I cannot describe it.

  2. 2. Check the trip TripIt made.

    After you forward, TripIt sends a notification email saying whether it created a new trip or added the plan to an existing one. One behaviour to know: if the travel dates overlap with a trip you already have, the plan goes into that trip instead of a new one, and you can move it afterwards. If you cannot find a booking, look at your upcoming trips, then at Unfiled Items, where confirmations that failed to process can sit until you move them into a trip.

  3. 3. Add what email cannot carry by hand.

    Manual entry is for bookings you have no email for, or for things such as a meeting or an activity. On the website: open the Trips page, click Add a Trip, enter the start date, end date and destination (a Trip Name is optional), and click Save. Then open the trip, click Add a Plan, pick a type such as flight, lodging or activity, fill in the details and click Save. A trip can have only one primary destination, so a two-city holiday needs two trips. The app has its own add-trip flow, which I did not read. To plan the journey between the two cities, see how to use Rome2Rio app.

  4. 4. Subscribe your calendar to the TripIt feed.

    Turn on the feed first. In the app that is Account, Settings, Calendar Sync, then the calendar feed toggle. On the website it is the profile icon, Settings, Calendar Feed, then the toggle. Copy the feed URL, then add it by URL: in Google Calendar click the plus next to Other calendars, choose From URL, paste it and click Add Calendar; in Apple Calendar choose File, New Calendar Subscription and paste it. iPhone and Android each have an in-app route under Calendar Sync as well.

    Two limits from TripIt's own page. The feed is read-only, so editing an event in your calendar does not change the trip. And it is slow: refresh time depends on the calendar, from 15 minutes to 24 hours, and Google Calendar can take up to a day. If it stops updating after you have waited, reset the feed URL in the same settings, remove the old calendar entry and add the new URL. Lotus Notes and Outlook 2013 or later are listed as not supported.

  5. 5. Choose free or Pro.

    TripIt's pricing page lists the free plan as covering the itinerary, adding plans from your inbox, calendar sync, sharing, area safety levels, airport and terminal maps, trip statistics and 3 uploaded documents per trip. Pro adds the flight and airport features below.

TripIt is a booking organiser, not a place-discovery tool

Read the three steps on TripIt's own explainer: forward the email, get one itinerary per trip, download the app. Every step starts from a booking that already exists. That is a strength for a flight-heavy trip and a gap for the other half of planning, which is deciding what to do once you land. TripIt's pricing page does list transport options and finding places near your hotel, but it does not describe browsing or ranking things to do in a city. Nothing on the pages I read says it does.

It does the part TripIt does not, and it does none of the parts TripIt does best: it does not read confirmation emails, it builds no itinerary from bookings and it sends no flight alerts. Questions about using TripIt

How do I add a booking to TripIt?

Forward the original confirmation email to plans@tripit.com, from the email address linked to your TripIt account and from your own inbox rather than from the airline or booking site. TripIt then emails you to say whether it made a new trip or added the plan to an existing one. If you would rather not forward, TripIt's Inbox Sync works with Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft accounts, and you can also add any plan by hand on the website or in the app.

Why is my forwarded email not showing up in TripIt?

TripIt's help lists the usual causes: the email went to support@tripit.com or another address instead of plans@tripit.com, it was forwarded from an address not linked to your main account (which makes a separate account), it was added to an existing trip with overlapping dates, it failed to process and sits in Unfiled Items, or it was sent straight from the airline or booking site. Check upcoming trips and Unfiled Items first.

How do I sync TripIt with my calendar?

Turn on the calendar feed (app: Account, Settings, Calendar Sync; website: profile icon, Settings, Calendar Feed), copy the feed URL and add it to your calendar by URL. The feed is read-only, and TripIt says refreshes take from 15 minutes to 24 hours depending on the calendar, with Google Calendar able to take up to a day.

Is TripIt a place-discovery app?

Mostly no. TripIt's own description is about organising bookings you have already made into one itinerary per trip. Its pricing page does list finding places near your hotel and transport options, but deciding what to see and eat in a city is not what its three-step explainer centres on. I built NomadSwipe (Android) for that part, and the two do different jobs.
