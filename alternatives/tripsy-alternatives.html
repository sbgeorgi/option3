Updated 2 November 2026. Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, which is Android only.

Tripsy lets you view a trip in a browser at my.tripsy.app, but its Android page says you create and edit trips in the Tripsy app. This page compares apps that run on Android, based on their websites and Google Play listings, read on 2 November 2026. What Tripsy does, and why Android users leave

The baseline: one trip built from your reservation emails

Tripsy's home page says you can "instantly turn your reservation emails into a complete trip": forward an email and the flights, stays and activities are added. It also lists flight alerts for delays, gates and baggage, expenses in any currency, and a place for reservations, notes, photos, links and documents per trip. Its listing on Apple's store says you can access the itinerary offline, invite people to edit a trip or follow along view-only, and it shows 4.7 out of 5 from 5.7K ratings.

The pricing page lists a free plan with unlimited trips, cloud sync, unlimited guests and flight search. Tripsy Pro adds forwarded reservations, smart import from files, links and text, expenses, flight update alerts, a calendar feed and documents, at $59 for one year or $299 as a one-time purchase, both shareable with family. Apple's store lists several in-app purchase prices, so the price you see may differ by offer.

The reason to look elsewhere is the platform. Its Apple store listing requires iOS 18, iPadOS 18, macOS 15, visionOS 2 or watchOS 10, and the Android page offers a waitlist instead of an app. On Android you can view a trip at my.tripsy.app, while editing happens in the Tripsy app. Tripsy also offers an AI-assistant connector for building itineraries; its pages do not say whether that works from Android.

NomadSwipe

Best for: picking what to do in a city, not filing bookings

NomadSwipe solves a different half of the trip than Tripsy. Tripsy organises what you have already booked; NomadSwipe helps you decide what to see once you are there. You open a city and get a deck of real places, one card at a time. Swipe right to save, left to skip. A card shows the category, rating, distance and whether it is open now, and every saved place lands on a map for that trip. The saved list sorts by rating, distance or A to Z.

  • Strength: you do not need a list of places before you start. The deck brings them to you and you keep the ones you choose.
  • Limit: it does not read reservation emails, track flights or store documents. If those are why you used Tripsy, pair it with TripIt or KDE Itinerary below.
  • Limit: no turn-by-turn navigation; directions, website and call are one tap from a saved place.
  • Limit: Android only today; iPhone coming soon.

The free plan includes 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip a month, 2 custom pins a month and a daily limit on saves. Premium comes as Monthly or Lifetime and removes those limits. Up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online; offline copies expire.

TripIt

Best for: the Tripsy email-forwarding habit, on Android

TripIt's Google Play listing says you forward confirmation emails to plans@tripit.com and it builds the itinerary, that your plans can be shared with anyone you choose, and that you can find them "even when you're offline". The listing shows 5M+ downloads and an update on 15 October 2026. Its Pro page offers a free 30-day trial, "then it's just $49 for a year", and an Inner Circle feature that shares your plans automatically with chosen people.

  • Strength: the forward-an-email workflow is the same idea as Tripsy's, and it runs on Android.
  • Limit: the pages we read do not list what the free tier includes, so check before relying on it. It organises bookings; it does not suggest places to visit.

Wanderlog

Best for: planning stops on a map, alone or with others

Wanderlog's Google Play listing calls it a "completely free travel app", says you can add bookings by forwarding emails or connecting Gmail, says friends can edit the plan in real time, and marks "Access your trip plans offline (Pro)". It shows 1M+ downloads and an update on 23 October 2026. Its home page links to both the App Store and Google Play.

  • Strength: bookings, a map of stops and shared editing in one plan.
  • Limit: offline access is a Pro feature, and the Pro price was not on any Wanderlog page we could read (its pricing URLs returned 404).

Tripomatic

Best for: a day-by-day plan with offline maps

Tripomatic (formerly Sygic Travel) says on its home page that the free plan has no time limit, allows up to 5 days with planned activities per calendar month and up to 25 places and hotels per trip, and shows ads. Its Premium page lists "Free for 7 days, then $29.99/year", says it runs on web, iOS, Android and Mac, and puts offline maps in Premium. A trip can be shared by link with edit or view rights. Its Play listing shows 1M+ downloads.

  • Strength: one plan you can open on an Android phone and a Mac, which suits someone leaving an Apple-only app.
  • Limit: offline maps and more than 5 planned days a month need Premium.

KDE Itinerary

Best for: booking extraction that stays on your phone

KDE Itinerary's official page says it "automatically extracts booking data from various input formats", can find reservations in your email, and does it all locally on your device. It is licensed LGPL-2.0-or-later and lists Android, Plasma Mobile and Linux. Its Play listing shows 5K+ downloads and an update on 18 October 2026.

  • Strength: the privacy-minded version of Tripsy's import, with no cloud account in the way.
  • Limit: it is built around tickets and bookings, so it has no deck of places to discover and no Apple apps.

FAQ

Is there a Tripsy app for Android?

No. Tripsy's Android page says there is no native Android app yet and offers a waitlist. You can view a Tripsy trip in a browser at my.tripsy.app, while editing happens in the Tripsy app, which runs on Apple devices.

Which Android app is most like Tripsy?

For forwarding reservation emails into one itinerary, TripIt is the closest, with KDE Itinerary as the on-device option. Wanderlog also takes forwarded emails or a Gmail connection.

How much does Tripsy Pro cost?

Its pricing page lists $59 for one year or $299 as a one-time purchase, both shareable with family. Apple's store shows several in-app purchase prices, so check the offer in the app.

Is Tripsy free?

Yes, there is a free plan with unlimited trips, cloud sync, unlimited guests and flight search, per its pricing page. Forwarded reservations, smart import, expenses, documents, flight alerts and the calendar feed are Pro.

Can an Android user see a trip planned in Tripsy?

Yes, through the web view at my.tripsy.app, per Tripsy's Android page. They cannot edit it there.

Does NomadSwipe import reservation emails like Tripsy?

No. NomadSwipe is for choosing places in a city by swiping and seeing them on a map. Use it next to an itinerary app if you need bookings in one place.

Which one to pick

  • You want Tripsy's email forwarding on Android: TripIt.
  • You want that import without a cloud account: KDE Itinerary.
  • You plan with others on a shared map: Wanderlog.
  • You want one day-by-day plan on Android and a Mac: Tripomatic.
  • Your bookings are done and you need to decide what to see in the city: NomadSwipe.
