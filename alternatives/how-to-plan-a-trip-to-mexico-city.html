How to plan a trip to Mexico City: 7 steps and a 3-day plan built on verified opening days

How to plan a trip to Mexico City: check your entry rules, choose a base, then build the days around Monday. Chapultepec Castle and the Frida Kahlo Museum are closed that day and Teotihuacan opens daily, according to the official pages I read on 16 November 2026.

Step by step

  1. Pick the month first.

    The Mexico City tourism site says the city can be visited at any time of year, and that spring (March to May) or fall (September to November) avoids the extremes of cold and rain, with May usually the hottest month. It adds that December to February nights and early mornings can drop close to freezing. Read the two official pages together, because they differ on the rain: the best seasons page speaks of heavy rain in the summer months, usually for a couple of hours at most, while the basics page puts the rainy season between May and October, with sunny mornings and cloudy, rainy afternoons. In practice that means plan the outdoor anchor, Teotihuacan, for the morning. If you want a festival, the seasons page lists Holy Week, when it says the city all but shuts down, and the 15 and 16 September Independence Day celebrations in the Zócalo.

  2. Check entry rules for your own passport.

    I read only two governments' pages in full, so this step covers British and US travellers, and everyone else should ask the Mexican embassy. The UK government's entry page says tourists from the UK need no visa, that your passport must be valid for your whole stay, and that the Mexican Embassy recommends at least 180 days of validity because 180 days is the longest tourist stay. You get a stamp showing how many days you may stay. The US State Department page says a visa is required only for stays over 180 days, that air travellers need one blank passport page per stamp, and that you must declare anything above 10,000 US dollars on entry and exit. For the immigration form on arrival by air, the Mexican immigration institute, INM, says on its digital migration form page (dated 2 May 2023) that air arrivals no longer fill in a paper form. You download the digital form after you enter, once, during the stay granted at the immigration desk, by scanning a QR code at the checkpoint or using the INM portal. The city's tourism guide still describes a physical card to be returned on leaving, so I would follow the INM page, which is the agency's own and the more specific, and keep the stamped passport page safe either way.

  3. Choose how many days, then a base area.

    The city's tourism site publishes itineraries for 1, 3 and 7 days. My view is that three days is the smallest stay that fits one outing beyond the city plus two city days, and the 3-day plan below is built that way. For the base, the same site groups its 100 must-see stops into eight routes. The table shows the four that matter most to a first visit, with the Metro or Metrobús lines the official pages name for each. Which one to sleep in is my judgment, not an official ranking: pick the area where your most fixed-time anchor sits, then keep each day to one or two areas.

  4. Plan the airport transfer and a daily transit habit.

    The airport's own Metrobús page says the public bus boards at gate 7 in Terminal 1 and gate 2 in Terminal 2, runs every day of the year, and operates Monday to Saturday from 4:30 to 24:00 and on Sundays and holidays from 5:00 to 24:00. The Mexico City tourism site's Metrobús page gives the airport fare, Line 4, as 30 pesos against 6 pesos on the other lines, and says you pay with the Integrated Mobility Card or by tapping a bank card or phone. Its Metro page says a Metro trip costs 5 pesos whatever the distance, that the Metro runs Monday to Friday from 5:00, Saturday from 6:00 and Sunday and holidays from 7:00, all until midnight, and that Line 5 connects directly with Terminal 1. The same page says the first three cars on most trains are reserved for women and children under 12. It lists two different prices for the transit card, so check the machine before you buy one. If you take a taxi instead, the UK government advises authorised pre-paid airport taxi services only.

  5. Sort money and pre-paid tickets.

    The Frida Kahlo Museum's visit page states that it has no ticket-office sales, so you book online before you go, and lists general admission at 320 pesos. Teotihuacan's INAH page lists 210 pesos, with 105 pesos for Mexican nationals and foreign residents. I did not find on those pages how the Teotihuacan gate takes payment, so carry some pesos for that day. The city's basics page suggests tipping about 10 percent, up to 15 for exceptional service, and carrying only a day's money plus a card. For cash, the UK advice says it is generally safer to use ATMs in daylight, inside shops or malls, and to avoid large withdrawals. It also warns that criminals have targeted people at ATMs and exchange offices.

  6. Learn the safety basics before you land.

    No single rating tells the whole story, so here are the three I read. The US State Department's advisory, issued 29 May 2026, rates Mexico overall at Level 2, exercise increased caution, and lists Mexico City at Level 2 too. It advises US citizens to use dispatched vehicles from regulated taxi stands or app-based services such as Uber or Cabify and not to wave down taxis on the street. Canada's advice, last updated 19 October 2026, says to exercise a high degree of caution across the country and does not list Mexico City among the states it advises against visiting. It says demonstrations are frequent in Mexico City and can disrupt traffic and public transport, and that the Mexico City metro is often very crowded and popular for pickpocketing. The UK government adds that unlicensed taxi drivers have robbed and assaulted passengers, including in Mexico City, that "express kidnapping" at ATMs happens in urban areas, and that there have been protests against gentrification in central and tourist areas since July 2025. The areas on the UK warnings page I read do not include Mexico City. On water, the UK health page says to drink only boiled or bottled water and avoid ice in drinks. It also says altitude sickness is a risk in parts of Mexico, but I did not find a Mexico City figure on it. The city's own security recommendations on the basics page are plain: keep jewellery out of sight, watch your wallet and phone, and avoid empty streets.

Questions about planning a trip to Mexico City

How many days do you need in Mexico City?

Three days covers the four anchors in this guide: Teotihuacan, the Chapultepec museums and the Frida Kahlo Museum, with the rest of each day left open. That is my own judgment, not an official figure. The Mexico City tourism site publishes itineraries for 1, 3 and 7 days, so it treats those as the usual lengths.

Which day are the museums closed in Mexico City?

Monday, for the three museums I checked. The Frida Kahlo Museum lists Monday as closed, the Chapultepec Castle museum says it never opens on a Monday, and the National Museum of Anthropology lists Tuesday to Sunday. The Teotihuacan archaeological zone is listed as open Monday to Sunday, 8:00 to 17:00. All read on 16 November 2026.

How do you get from Mexico City airport to the centre?

The airport's own page names Metrobús Line 4, which boards at gate 7 in Terminal 1 and gate 2 in Terminal 2 and runs every day. The Mexico City tourism site gives the airport fare as 30 pesos. It also says Metro Line 5 connects directly with Terminal 1. For taxis, the UK government advises authorised pre-paid airport taxi services only.

Is Mexico City safe for tourists?

Official advice is cautious but not a ban. The US State Department rates Mexico City Level 2, exercise increased caution, in an advisory issued 29 May 2026. Canada's advice for the whole country is a high degree of caution, and the UK government warns about unlicensed taxis, ATM crime and pickpocketing. Use the basics in step 6 and check the advisories again before you fly.

Findings, method and sources

Method. I read the official pages below on 16 November 2026, copied each opening day, hour, fare and rule as written, and built the 3-day plan so that every stop falls inside the listed hours. I made no visit and used no booking data. Where two official pages disagreed I say so in the text: the INM form page against the city guide, the two transit card prices on one page, and the rainy season between the basics and seasons pages. Where a page would not load for my reader, I used the national institute's listing and said so.

  • Monday is the closing day to plan around: the Frida Kahlo Museum and Chapultepec Castle are closed, the Anthropology museum lists Tuesday to Sunday, and Teotihuacan is listed as open daily.
  • The Frida Kahlo Museum lists Monday as closed, Wednesday from 11:00 and Thursday to 21:00, and has no ticket-office sales, so book ahead online.
  • Teotihuacan is listed as open 8:00 to 17:00 with last access at 16:30, and a 21 April 2026 bulletin kept the Pyramid of the Moon closed until further notice.
  • The airport Metrobús (Line 4) runs every day from gate 7 in Terminal 1 and gate 2 in Terminal 2, and the Metro runs every day of the year.
  • Official safety advice for Mexico City: Level 2 from the US, a high degree of caution for the country from Canada, and taxi, ATM and pickpocket warnings from the UK.

Limits: this guide is a desk read of official pages, not a trip report. I read only the UK and US entry rules, I give no transfer times, and I did not rank neighbourhoods. Hours, fares and advisories change, so check the linked pages on the day you book.
