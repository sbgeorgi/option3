What Lambus is today

Lambus is a trip planner from Lambus GmbH. Its Google Play description covers stops on an itinerary, GPX route import from devices such as Garmin, a route planner, travel documents "even offline", expense splitting, notes, a Discover section and ticket booking. The Lambus site adds a browser extension that adds stops from any website, an AI connector for ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, and booking partners GetYourGuide and Omio. The Play listing says Lambus is a Tourlane company, and shows 100K+ downloads.

What sits behind PRO

The Lambus PRO help page lists 15 PRO features. The ones people usually weigh when they look for a Lambus app alternative are Offline Mode, Extended Route Planning (travel time between waypoints), Google Maps place search for stops, PDF and GPX export, and unlimited data storage for photos, bookings and PDFs. The page does not say what the unpaid version limits, and I did not find a price on it or on the Lambus home page.

The U.S. App Store listing does show prices: in-app purchases named Monthly at $3.99, Yearly at $24.49, Pay Per Trip at $8.99, Lifetime at $89.99 and Yearly Upgrade at $24.99. It does not label them PRO, and I did not read the Google Play prices, so treat these as a guide only. An older developer reply on the Play listing says road distance and duration calculations need PRO because Lambus pays Google Maps for them.

How you add a stop in Lambus

These steps come from the Lambus help article on stops and nearby places, which I read on 12 November 2026. I did not follow them in the app.

  1. Open an active trip in the Lambus app.
  2. In the trip overview, tap the plus button next to Itinerary and choose Add stop.
  3. Type the place name, for example Paris, and pick it from the search results.
  4. Tap a stop to rename it. Nearby places are added the same way from a stop.

That flow starts from a place you already have in mind. The alternatives differ mostly in where the places come from: guides, forwarded emails, an AI prompt, or swiping.

Lambus alternatives, one at a time

Wanderlog: the closest match

Wanderlog's site shows "your itinerary and your map in one view", places added from user-shared guides in one click, reservations pulled in by forwarded email, expense splitting and live collaborative editing. Its Play listing calls the app completely free and also marks offline access as Pro, and the same text says plans are stored offline, so I could not tell which applies. It is larger than Lambus on Play (1M+ against 100K+). More in Wanderlog alternatives and how to use Wanderlog.

TripIt: for bookings, not discovery

TripIt builds the itinerary from confirmation emails you forward to plans@tripit.com, and its Play listing says reservation details are available even offline. It is for what you have already booked: it does not help you decide what to see. The listing says TripIt Pro renews at $48.99 a year. See TripIt alternatives.

Stippl: AI day plans

The Stippl site says you describe a trip and the AI builds a day-by-day itinerary, shows every stop on a map with distances and travel times, and says the core app is free forever. Its Play listing marks the AI trip planner as PRO, and shows a 3.7 average from 2,200 ratings. See Stippl alternatives.

Tripsy: Apple first

The Tripsy home page says to get it on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch, with one itinerary for flights, stays, activities and documents, plus email import and flight alerts. Its navigation links to an Android page that I did not read, so I left Tripsy out of the table. See Tripsy alternatives.

Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

For the step before the itinerary

I built NomadSwipe, so weigh this with that in mind. It is an Android app: pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time, swipe right or tap Save to keep one, and your saved places are pinned on a trip map where you build a trip or day plan. Cards show category, rating, distance and opening status, and the list sorts by rating, distance or A to Z. In the Play listing screenshots, a long weekend in Lisbon has 16 places, all picked by swiping.

It does not hold tickets, documents or expenses the way Lambus does, and it does not import GPX routes or email bookings. Offline support is narrow: up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online, and the copies expire. The plan without payment has 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip a month and 2 custom pins a month, plus a daily limit on saves. Premium is optional. iPhone is coming soon.

A workable split: choose places in NomadSwipe, then keep tickets and documents in Lambus or TripIt. FAQ

What is a good Lambus app alternative?

It depends on the part of Lambus you use. Wanderlog is the nearest for a shared itinerary on a map, TripIt if you mostly organise bookings, and Stippl if you want a day-by-day plan with a route. I built NomadSwipe, and it is narrower than all of them: it helps you choose places by swiping. For a head-to-head, see Lambus vs Wanderlog.

Is Lambus free?

It is free to download. The Lambus site says it is available for free on the App Store, Google Play and as a web app, and the Google Play listing shows in-app purchases. Its paid tier is called Lambus PRO, and its help page does not say what the unpaid version leaves out.

Does Lambus work offline?

In part. The Play listing says travel documents can be reached at any time, even offline, and the PRO help page lists an Offline Mode that opens the whole trip (itinerary, documents, expenses and photos) without a connection. The same page counts Offline Mode as a PRO feature, so check what your plan includes.

Who owns Lambus?

The Play listing names Lambus GmbH as the publisher and says Lambus is a Tourlane company, the online specialist for tailor-made travel. I did not find a second source for the ownership, so that is the company's own statement.

Is NomadSwipe a Lambus alternative?

Only for one job. I built NomadSwipe, an Android app where you swipe real places in a city, save the ones you like, see them on a map and build a trip or day plan. It does not store tickets or expenses the way Lambus does, and it does not replace its documents or booking side.

Does NomadSwipe work offline?

Partly. Its Play listing says up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online, and the copies expire. That is a saved trip, not an offline map.

Is NomadSwipe on iPhone?

Not yet. NomadSwipe is on Google Play for Android phones, and iPhone is coming soon.
