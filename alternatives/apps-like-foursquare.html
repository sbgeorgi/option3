Apps Like Foursquare: 7 Options for Finding and Keeping Places, and Where NomadSwipe Fits

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android). It is not a check-in app, so it is not a like-for-like swap for Swarm, but I say where it fits and where it does not. Facts checked on 16 November 2026. Updated 16 November 2026.

Apps like Foursquare depend on which Foursquare you used. Its City Guide app, for finding and saving places, closed on 15 December 2024, so Google Maps, Yelp, Mapstr, Beli, Apple Maps and Tripadvisor now cover that job; Swarm is still the check-in app. I built NomadSwipe (Android), which swipes real places to save but has no check-ins.

Key facts

  • Foursquare's own City Guide Sunset page says the City Guide app ran until 15 December 2024 and that Foursquare closed it to focus on Swarm.
  • Swarm is still on Google Play (10M+ downloads, updated 23 October 2026) and in Apple's store. It is the one to look at if you want the check-in log.
  • None of the six alternatives below is built around a check-in log with badges and mayorships; Yelp mentions check-ins only as one way places reach a list. They cover the other job: finding places and keeping them in lists on a map.
  • Yelp's My Lists, with built-in "Been Here" and "Want to Go" lists, is the closest list model to what Foursquare users kept. Mapstr's French page says it imports places saved on Foursquare or Swarm.
  • NomadSwipe is Android only and is not a check-in app. It is for deciding which places in a city to visit next.

Which Foursquare do you mean?

The name now covers two different things. The foursquare.com home page I read on 16 November 2026 opens its menu with Marketers, Developers, Places Data and Apps: it is a location-data company first, and its Places data is sold to businesses. This page is about the consumer side, the apps people used to find a coffee shop, leave a tip or check in. Comparison pages for the business data product, such as the Foursquare Places listings on software-review sites, answer a different question and I did not use them.

The consumer side itself split in 2014 into two apps. TechCrunch, in its October 2024 report on the closure, describes that as one app for exploration and discovery (City Guide) and Swarm for sharing your location. When people say "apps like Foursquare" they may mean either one, so I sort the options by the job they replace.

What happened to Foursquare City Guide, and what Swarm is now

The City Guide Sunset page in Foursquare's help center says the app would be available until 15 December 2024 at 11:59 PM ET, with web access continuing into early 2025, and gives the reason as focusing on Swarm. It says business listings stay discoverable on Swarm and that your lists are safe. On 16 November 2026 the old City Guide address in Apple's store returned a not-found page, and so did the Google Play address for it. That fits a closed app. I did not find a replacement City Guide app on foursquare.com.

Swarm is what is left. Its Google Play listing, titled "Swarm: Check In, Explore, Map", describes one-tap check-ins with photos and tagged friends, local search, custom lists such as "Tokyo Trip", a map of friends' check-ins, mayorships and badges. It shows 4.1 from 420K reviews, 10M+ downloads and an update on 23 October 2026. Its Data safety section says no data is shared with third parties and that it may collect location, personal info and six other types. Apple's listing shows 4.2 from 8.2K ratings and says it runs on iPhone, Apple Watch and iMessage. So if you want to keep checking in, Swarm is the answer, and the rest of this page is for the finding and saving half.

Three jobs people used Foursquare for

  • A log of where you have been. Check-ins, a timeline, badges. Swarm does this; none of the six apps compared here describes a check-in log with badges and mayorships.
  • Finding a good place nearby, with other people's opinions. The old City Guide job. Google Maps, Yelp and Tripadvisor are built around place pages with reviews.
  • Keeping your own list of places. Want-to-go lists, favorites, a city guide you build yourself. Google Maps, Yelp, Mapstr, Beli and Apple Maps all describe lists on their own pages, and NomadSwipe builds a shortlist per trip.

Google Maps: the default place to keep places

Google's Create a list of places page describes lists for "your favorite places or places you want to visit". On Android you tap You, then New list, name it and choose Private ("only you can view and edit") or Shared (a link to view or edit). To keep a place you open it, tap Save and pick a list. You can hide a list from the main map, and invite editors to a shared list. The Play listing adds place search with photos and reviews. Navigation is the headline, so this is a map that happens to keep lists, not a place-keeping app. More on lists: Google Maps lists alternatives.

Yelp: Been Here and Want to Go

Yelp's own My Lists article says every account has two built-in lists, "Been Here" for places you have tried and "Want to Go" for those you have not, and that the earlier Collections are now your personal lists inside My Lists. You can build them from the "Want to Go" icon in the home feed, the save button on a business page, or a search inside My Lists. You can rank a "Been Here" list from most to least favorite and share any list by link, which people can open without a Yelp account. The article says the feature is available in the U.S. and Canada on iOS and Android and that shared lists can be viewed on desktop. It also says places you have reviewed or checked in at on Yelp will already be in your "Been Here" list, so Yelp has check-ins of its own, but the article treats them as an input to lists, not as a Swarm-style log. The Play listing shows "Contains ads" and describes reviews, food delivery, reservations and home services, so it is a broader local-business app than Foursquare was. See Yelp alternatives for the other direction.

Mapstr: a map of your own places, with a Foursquare import

The Play listing describes bookmarking places on one map, sorting them by tags, adding comments and pictures, following friends' maps and checking your map without a connection. It shows in-app purchases, 4.0 from 4.45K reviews and 500K+ downloads. Apple's listing adds one-tap pins, "To try" and "Tried" marks, importing Google Maps, CSV or KML lists, and a version for iPhone, Apple Watch and iMessage. The reason Mapstr is on this page is its French import page, which says places saved on Foursquare or Swarm can be imported so you do not start from zero. It points to Profile, Settings, Data management, Import data, then choosing the source. Mapstr's home page and feature pages are in French; the store listings I read are in English. More: Mapstr alternatives.

Beli: Foursquare's restaurant half, ranked

Beli covers food only. Apple's listing says it keeps "organized ranked lists and maps of everywhere you've been and want to try", shows what friends are eating, adds tags, notes and favorite dishes, and offers a taste profile and a match score with friends. It is made for iPhone and iPad there, with 4.8 from 18K ratings. The Google Play listing carries the same description and shows 100K+ downloads and 4.0 from 1.89K reviews, updated on 14 November 2026. If your Foursquare use was mostly restaurants and what your friends ate, this is the closest match among the six. It does not cover sights, bars or hotels on the pages I read.

Apple Maps: guides and personal notes on iPhone

Apple's iPhone User Guide has two relevant pages. Organize places with custom guides says a guide called Favorites always exists, that you create your own with a name and an optional photo, and that you add a place from its card with Add to Guides. You can share a guide. Add places and notes to your Places says you can add notes visible only to you and pin frequently visited places. Both pages come from Apple's iPhone User Guide, and I read nothing about Android, so for an Android phone this is not an option I can confirm. It has no review or check-in layer on those pages.

Tripadvisor: reviews and booking, with saves

The Play listing for "Tripadvisor: Plan & Book Trips" says you can save favorite experiences, hotels and restaurants, get an itinerary planner with an AI trip builder based on your saves, book places to stay, and read traveler reviews. It shows "Contains ads", 4.5 from 1.47M reviews and 100M+ downloads. Its saves help page returned an error when I tried it, so how saving works is taken from the listing alone. It is the nearest thing here to City Guide's tips and reviews, with booking added. See Tripadvisor alternatives.

Taking your Foursquare and Swarm places with you

This was the most useful thing I found. Two pages describe it, and I followed neither, so treat both as what the companies say.

  1. Request your data from Foursquare. The City Guide Sunset page gives these steps on the web: log in at foursquare.com, open Settings from your name, choose Privacy Settings, click Initiate Data Download Request, confirm the email, then use the link in the second email. It says the export covers both City Guide and Swarm data, and that the option stays after the shutdown. There is a similar path inside the City Guide app, which no longer exists.
  2. Import into Mapstr if you want a map. Mapstr's French import page names Foursquare and Swarm as sources and gives the in-app path in the paragraph above. I do not know which Foursquare data it reads or how complete the result is, and Mapstr's page does not say.

The same Foursquare page says your City Guide lists "will remain available" and that you can still edit them on the web, while its first answer says City Guide as a whole will not be available after early 2025. I flag that under the disagreements below. The practical step is the same: export now.

Where the sources disagree

  1. Swarm's rating on one page. The Play header shows 4.1 from 420K reviews; the ratings block further down the same page shows 4.0 from 410K. I quote the header and say Play showed it.
  2. City Guide on the web. Foursquare's sunset page says the web version stays until "early in the 2025 New Year" and that after that City Guide "in its entirety" will be unavailable, yet it also says lists can be accessed and edited on the web after the sunset. The page is marked as updated a year ago, so I cannot tell which statement is current.
  3. Beli's score by store. Apple shows 4.8 from 18K ratings; Google Play shows 4.0 from 1.89K reviews. They are different stores and different audiences, and I do not average them.
  4. Reach figures. Mapstr's home page gives a count of places saved and "more than 4 million" users, Google Play shows 500K+ downloads, and Apple shows 1.1K ratings. They measure different things, and none confirms or refutes another.

Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

For choosing places in a city, not for logging where you have been

I built NomadSwipe (Android), so weigh this with that in mind. It is not a check-in app. There are no check-ins, no friends feed, no badges or mayorships, and no timeline of your past. It is a place-discovery and trip-building app, not a review site or a booking platform, and it does not replace Swarm.

What it does: pick a city and it deals real restaurants, sights, bars and stays one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep one, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Cards show the category, the rating, how far away the place is and, where hours are known, whether it is open. Saved places land in a shortlist for that trip and are pinned on a trip map, with a list view sorted by rating, distance or A to Z. You can add a custom pin for anything the cards miss, such as your hotel, and use destination search to explore another city. NomadSwipe works in any city worldwide, and the listing's sample trip is a long weekend in New York with 16 places, all picked by swiping. Google Maps and Yelp let you share lists; the NomadSwipe listing describes no sharing, so I make no claim about it.

The honest split: Swarm keeps the record of where you have been, while Google Maps, Yelp, Mapstr, Beli and Apple Maps keep the places you point at. NomadSwipe is for the step before that, when you have landed in a city and want a clear yes or no on each place. It does not import Foursquare data, and I make no claim that anything moves between them. iPhone is coming soon. Which one for which job

  • You still want to check in and keep a timeline: Swarm. Nothing else on this page describes a check-in log with badges.
  • A map you already own, with lists you can share: Google Maps, if you are fine with Save and a list name for each place.
  • Been-here and want-to-go lists next to reviews: Yelp, whose My Lists were designed around exactly those two states.
  • Bringing your Foursquare history across onto a map: Mapstr, per its own import page, after you check what it brings in.
  • Restaurants you rank against each other, with friends in the loop: Beli, which names restaurants only.
  • An iPhone with nothing extra to install: Apple Maps guides and Places, per Apple's own pages.
  • Tips, reviews and booking for a trip: Tripadvisor, with the listing's save feature.
  • Choosing what to do in a city you are about to visit: NomadSwipe (Android), alongside whichever app keeps your history.

The short version

  • For finding and keeping places, the options compared are Google Maps, Yelp, Mapstr, Beli, Apple Maps, Tripadvisor and NomadSwipe, and none of them is built around check-ins.
  • Foursquare says you can export City Guide and Swarm data from your account; Mapstr says it can import Foursquare and Swarm places.
  • NomadSwipe is Android only and is not a check-in app; it helps you choose and map places for a trip.

FAQ

Is the Foursquare City Guide app still available?

No. Foursquare's support page says the City Guide app was available until 15 December 2024 and that the web version would follow in early 2025. On 16 November 2026 Apple's store address for City Guide and the Google Play address I tried both returned a not-found page. Foursquare says it closed City Guide to focus on Swarm.

What is the closest app to Foursquare today?

For check-ins, Swarm, which is still made by Foursquare and is on Google Play and Apple's store. For finding places and keeping lists, the closest matches in this comparison are Google Maps, Yelp, which has Been Here and Want to Go lists, and Mapstr, which says it can import places saved on Foursquare and Swarm.

Can I move my Foursquare or Swarm places to another app?

You can request your data from Foursquare: Settings, Privacy Settings, then Initiate Data Download Request, and Foursquare says the export covers City Guide and Swarm data. Mapstr's French feature page says you can import places from Foursquare or Swarm into Mapstr. I did not run either, so I cannot say how complete the result is.

Is NomadSwipe like Foursquare?

Only in part. I built NomadSwipe (Android). It is not a check-in app: there are no check-ins, no friends feed and no badges. It is for choosing places in a city: swipe real restaurants, sights, bars and stays, save the ones you like and see them pinned on a trip map.
