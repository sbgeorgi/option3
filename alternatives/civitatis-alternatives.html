Civitatis: what the company says about itself

Civitatis’ About page calls it the leading company in online distribution of activities, excursions and guided tours in Spanish across the main tourist destinations of the world. Its Spanish home page repeats it as “Visitas guiadas y excursiones en español por todo el mundo” and lists final prices, 24/7 customer service and, for Rome, 117 activities with a 9.0 out of 10 score from 319,809 reviews. The English home page shows 102 Rome activities, so the count depends on the language version you open.

On Google Play, the Civitatis app says it offers more than 90,000 activities in about 4,000 destinations across more than 160 countries, and calls the company the leader in booking tours in Spanish since 2008. It adds that you can keep your bookings in one place and save tours or visits you want to do, then book quickly later. That saving step is the overlap with a planner, but what you save there is a bookable activity.

Free walking tours are the other part of the Civitatis identity. Its home pages list free tours in Madrid, Lisbon, Barcelona and Rome, labelled “¡Gratis!” in Spanish, and the Lisbon free tour shows a 9.9 out of 10 score from 105,885 reviews. The Spanish page calls the Lisbon one a “tour de precio libre”, a free-price tour. I did not read a matching offer on the rival pages.

Which apps like Civitatis offer tours in Spanish?

Only Civitatis states Spanish-language tours as its whole catalogue. For the others I confirmed what a Spanish-speaking shopper sees, not how many guides speak Spanish.

  • GetYourGuide: the site lists Español (España) and Español (México) among 44 language options, and its Play listing says support is available in several languages. That is the interface and support, not a count of Spanish-guided tours.
  • Tiqets: tiqets.com/es loads in Spanish and euros and offers “Switch to English”. It says more than 70 million travellers have used it.
  • Viator and Klook: I read their Spanish Google Play listings, which say nothing about Spanish-guided tours either way. Their web pages did not load for me, so I leave the language question open.

Practical rule: before you pay on any of them, open the activity and read its language line. Compare with GetYourGuide on the same tour in Viator or GetYourGuide, and read whether Viator is reliable if its reseller model worries you.

Free cancellation and pay later: Civitatis against the rest

This is where the pages differ most, so read the wording, not the logo.

  • Civitatis: its general conditions say all services confirm immediately and you pay when you book, by Visa, Mastercard or PayPal, and Klarna is mentioned among the payment methods. Some providers let you pay part at booking and the rest on the day. Section 7 says each activity has its own cancellation deadline and penalty, so there is no single rule to quote.
  • GetYourGuide: Play says free cancellation up to 24 hours before the activity on most bookings. Its English home page adds “the option to pay later” next to free cancellations on most activities.
  • Viator: Play lists free cancellation up to 24 hours before, plus a manage-my-bookings centre.
  • Tiqets and Klook: flexible cancellation on Tiqets’ home page, and flexible booking and cancelling on Klook’s listing, both without a deadline in the text I read.

If you want a hard 24-hour rule written in the app listing, GetYourGuide and Viator are the ones that state it. If you want Spanish-first support and a catalogue built for Spanish speakers, Civitatis is the one. Per-activity terms apply on all of them.
