Apps Like Trip.com: 4 Trip Planners to Use Instead or Alongside

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), and it is one of the apps discussed, so read its entry with that in mind. Trip.com, Wanderlog, TripIt, Tripomatic and Polarsteps facts checked on 11 November 2026. Last updated 11 November 2026.

Apps like Trip.com depend on which half of Trip.com you mean. For booking flights, hotels and trains, this page does not compare other sellers. For planning the trip, Wanderlog, TripIt, Tripomatic and Polarsteps do the itinerary job, and none of them needs to replace the booking app. NomadSwipe is a fifth, narrower option.

What Trip.com is, and what it is not

Trip.com is a booking platform first. Its Google Play listing (read 11 November 2026) calls it an “all-in-one travel app” with “thousands of flight routes and hotels”, plus car hire, tour tickets, train tickets in the UK, Europe and beyond, an e-sim and travel insurance. It adds Trip Coins credit, membership tiers and in-app calling to support. Google Play shows 50M+ downloads and a 4.7 rating from 994K reviews; Apple's public lookup for its U.S. listing shows 4.6 from 37,512 ratings.

The website's menu (read the same day on trip.com) lists Hotels & Homes, Flights, Trains, Cars, Attractions & Tours, Flight + Hotel, private and group tours, cruises, Deals, Travel Inspiration, a map and a link tagged “New”: Trip.Planner.

Trip.Planner, its itinerary feature

The launch press release (26 August 2025, read 11 November 2026) says planning starts with three questions: destination, trip duration and travel style. The itinerary can be previewed on a map with routes, edited in a canvas-style editor (rename, reorder, swap or remove items, add notes) and booked from the same view. It can import earlier Trip.com bookings and include flights, trains, hotels, restaurants and attractions. The same release says it is on “English-language Trip.com sites in select regions”. I did not use it, and the Play listing I read never mentions it, so I cannot say whether the app has it for you.

So the gap is not that Trip.com lacks a planner. The planner sits inside a store: it is built around booking what it suggests. If you want a planning tool that does not push you toward one seller, or one that also holds bookings you made elsewhere, that is where the apps below come in.

Trip.com alternatives for planning, one by one

Wanderlog: closest to a full planner

Its listing says it is for planning every kind of trip: build an itinerary, organize flight, hotel and car reservations, view places on a map and collaborate in real time. It also lists budgets, expense tracking and bill splitting, unlimited road-trip stops for free, and offline access as a Pro feature. Of the four it covers most of what Trip.Planner covers except buying the ticket. Pricing and the offline wording vary across its pages; Wanderlog alternatives goes through them.

TripIt: best when you book elsewhere

The TripIt listing describes the reverse of Trip.com's flow: you book somewhere else, forward the confirmation to plans@tripit.com and it lands in one itinerary, with PDFs, boarding passes and photos attached and details readable offline. The Pro tier adds flight status alerts, fare-drop refund alerts, reward tracking and airport maps, and the listing shows $48.99 a year. It keeps bookings tidy; it does not help you pick what to do. More in apps like TripIt.

Tripomatic: day-by-day with travel times

The Tripomatic listing describes a day-by-day itinerary with an AI trip planner, estimated travel times and walking distances, cost tracking, notes and inviting friends. It lists 50 million places and says offline maps and walking navigation need Premium. Its Play rating, 4.2, is the lowest of the four.

Polarsteps: plan, then record and share

The Polarsteps listing splits into Plan, Track, Share and Relive. Plan includes an AI itinerary planner, destination snapshots and a transport planner for each leg; Track records your route automatically; the rest is photos, stories and Trip Reels. Pick it if the memory of the trip matters as much as the plan.

NomadSwipe: the narrow option, and what it does not do

I built NomadSwipe, so weigh this section with that in mind. It is an Android app: you pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Cards show the category, rating, distance and, where hours are known, whether the place is open. Saved places land in your trip and are pinned on a map; you can sort the list by rating, distance or A to Z and add custom pins such as your hotel.

  • It books nothing: no flights, hotels, trains, cars or tours. If Trip.com's booking is what you use, keep it for that.
  • Its listing mentions no reservation import, budgets or route optimizer, which Wanderlog and TripIt list.
  • The free plan is limited to 3 trips, with 2 searched places saved to a trip a month and 2 custom pins a month, and a daily limit on saves. Premium is optional, Monthly or Lifetime.
  • Up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online, according to the Play listing; the copies expire.
  • It is Android today and an iPhone version is coming soon. It has 50+ downloads on Google Play, so it has no track record next to the apps above.

Where it fits: after you book, or before, when the open question is what to see and eat. The map trip planner guide shows the saved-places-to-map step.

FAQ

What are the best apps like Trip.com?

It depends on what you want from Trip.com. If you want a planner rather than a booking site, Wanderlog, TripIt, Tripomatic and Polarsteps are the four compared here. This page does not compare other booking sites, because I did not read their pages on 11 November 2026.

Is Trip.com a trip planner?

Mainly it is a booking platform: its Google Play listing leads with flights, hotels, car hire, tour tickets, trains, e-sims and travel insurance. It also has an AI planner called Trip.Planner, announced in August 2025, that builds an itinerary from a destination, a trip length and a travel style.

Does Trip.com have an itinerary planner?

Yes, Trip.Planner. Per the launch press release it generates an itinerary you can preview on a map, edit, and book from, and it can import existing Trip.com bookings. The press release says it is on English-language Trip.com sites in select regions, so check whether you see it. The Google Play listing I read does not mention it.

Is there a Trip.com alternative that keeps my bookings in one itinerary?

TripIt. Its Google Play listing says you forward a booking confirmation to plans@tripit.com and it is added to your itinerary, and that reservation details work offline. Wanderlog's listing also describes importing reservations by forwarding emails or connecting Gmail.

Can NomadSwipe book flights or hotels?

No. NomadSwipe books nothing. I built NomadSwipe: you swipe real places in a city, save the ones you like and see them on a map as a trip. Book flights, hotels and trains in whatever app or site you prefer.

Is NomadSwipe on iPhone?

Not yet. NomadSwipe is on Google Play for Android phones today, and an iPhone version is coming soon.
