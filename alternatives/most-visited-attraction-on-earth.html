Eight attractions and what each number counts

"Attraction" can mean a free park, a whole city, a ticketed museum or a theme park, and each is counted differently. The table keeps each figure next to its year, its counter and whether you pay to get in, so nothing is compared that should not be. The years differ, so read the rows as separate facts, not as a league table.

Why the lists disagree

  • Free versus ticketed. The Eiffel Tower alone has two figures: about 10 million at the free forecourt and 6.75 million who went up. A free park or street has no ticket office to count, which is why Central Park and Times Square sit in a different category from the Louvre.
  • A site versus a destination. Las Vegas's 38.5 million counts people who came to the city. The LVCVA release does not mention the Strip, so it cannot be read as a Strip figure.
  • Visits versus people, estimates versus counts. TEA says its report "combines these sources, including estimates" and that attendance measures visits, not unique people. Times Square's figure is pedestrians entering the area, which, in my reading, does not separate visitors from people who live or work nearby.
  • Who gets ranked. The Art Newspaper's 2025 survey only lists museums that supplied figures, says Chinese state museums' numbers "are not released in time", and leaves out the Natural History Museum in London, so its first place goes to the Louvre.
  • Numbers that do not fit. China Daily (29 Oct 2025) says the Palace Museum's daily cap was cut from 80,000 to 40,000. A 40,000 cap over 365 days is 14.6 million, below the 19 million reported. That is my arithmetic; the cap's start date is not given, so I could not reconcile the two.
  • Left out. I found no sourced single-site count for the Great Wall in the sources I read, so it is not in the table.

Should you go? Crowds and timing

A big number says a place is famous, not that your visit will be good. The sources give a few hard facts for planning. Times Square normally sees 200,000 to 250,000 pedestrians a day and up to 330,000 on the busiest days. The Louvre recommends booking a time slot but sells tickets on site when attendance is low. The Palace Museum uses real-name tickets under a daily cap, so book ahead through its official channel.

What follows is my own judgment, not a measurement. Decide what you want from the place. If it is the sight, the Eiffel Tower's forecourt is free and counts for most of its visitors. If it is the inside, book the slot first and build the day around it. If it is the atmosphere of a crowd, as with Times Square, you will have it whenever you go. Few people need all of them on one day, so pick one headline sight a day and leave the rest of the day open.

Questions about the most visited attraction

What is the most visited attraction in the world?

It depends on what you count. The highest single-site figure I found is Central Park's 42 million annual visitors, from the Conservancy's 2023 annual report, and it is a park with no gate. Las Vegas drew 38.5 million visitors in 2025, but that counts a whole destination. For ticketed sites, the 2025 TEA index has the Palace Museum at 19 million and Magic Kingdom at 17.8 million.

What is the most visited tourist attraction you pay to enter?

In the 2025 TEA Global Experience Index, the Palace Museum in Beijing's Forbidden City leads the museum list with 19 million visitors and Magic Kingdom leads theme parks with 17.8 million. The Eiffel Tower counted 6.75 million visitors to its levels in 2025 and about 10 million at its free forecourt, so the free and ticketed counts differ by half as much again.

What is the most visited museum in the world?

It depends on the list. TEA's 2025 index, as reported by Euronews, has the Palace Museum first with 19 million and the Louvre fourth with 9,046,000. The Art Newspaper's 2025 survey has the Louvre first with more than 9 million, because it only ranks museums that supplied figures and says Chinese state museums' numbers were not released in time.

Is Times Square or the Las Vegas Strip the most visited attraction?

I found no current, comparable site-level count for either. The Times Square Alliance reports about 220,000 pedestrians a day in 2024 and no annual total, and the Las Vegas figure of 38.5 million for 2025 covers the whole destination and does not mention the Strip. Older lists give Times Square about 50 million, a 2014 estimate with no current count behind it.

Findings, method and limits

  • Highest single-site figure found: Central Park, 42 million annual visitors (Conservancy annual report 2023). Largest in 2025 among ticketed sites: Palace Museum 19 million, Magic Kingdom 17.8 million (TEA, 2025).
  • Las Vegas drew 38.5 million visitors in 2025 (LVCVA), counting the whole destination.
  • Eiffel Tower 2025: about 10 million at the forecourt, 6.75 million up the tower (SETE).
  • Louvre 2025: 9,046,000 (TEA) or more than 9 million (Art Newspaper). Times Square 2024: about 220,000 pedestrians a day.

Method. I read each source page on 17 Nov 2026 and took figures only from those pages, with the year the source gives. TEA's index itself I read through Attractions Magazine and Euronews, not as the original report. I did not time or count anything myself and I show no NomadSwipe data on this page.

Limits. The figures come from different years and counters, some are estimates, and none is a like-for-like comparison. I did not verify the Summer Palace's entry rules. Opening rules, caps and prices change, so check each site's own page before you go.
