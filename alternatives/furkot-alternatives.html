Furkot vs Roadtrippers, row by row

How to read the two price models

The Furkot Pass gates things you use late in planning: offline caching of a whole trip map, elevation profiles, printed turn-by-turn directions, expense tracking and import. Roadtrippers gates how big a trip can be: its free plan stops at 3 stops per trip, and the first plan above it allows 20 stops for $35.99 a year. Those are the published caps, not a test of either app.

Furkot's published terms for free use are looser in time and tighter in the end state: it sets no time limit on trying the planner, but it says that without a pass you can keep viewing a trip and not change it. We could not find how much use triggers that request, so we do not state it.

Which one to pick

  • You want the lowest list price for a long route: Furkot, at $14 for 1 year, if browser-only suits you.
  • You want a store app on your phone: Roadtrippers, which links iOS and Android apps; Furkot runs in the browser.
  • You want RV routing and offline maps in one plan: Roadtrippers Premium lists both; Furkot's Pass lists RV routing and offline trip-map caching.
  • You want to invite a travel partner to edit: Furkot lets you invite with full or view-only access and its Pass list does not include sharing; Roadtrippers lists collaboration from Pro at $49.99 a year.

Furkot

Best for: planning a drive with overnight stops and hotels

Furkot calculates the driving time between the stops you pick, and you can set a daily limit on travel time. It shows distance and travel time for the whole trip, between stops and since the last overnight stop. It orders stops for efficiency, and you can drag a stop or reverse the itinerary. Furkot's home page says it suggests overnight stops from your schedule and preferences.

Furkot shows hotels, campgrounds and apartments on the map, and says it shows prices including taxes and service fees when they are available. When you are ready to book, it opens your preferred booking site with check-in and check-out dates pre-filled. You can also add trails from AllTrails, Wikipedia articles, ski resorts through Liftopia, meal stops, gas stations, museums and national parks, and it supports motorcycle rides, biking excursions and walking tours as well as driving.

You can plan as a guest or sign in with Facebook, Google or TripIt. If your plans currently live in TripIt, other trip planners besides TripIt are compared separately. Furkot says it earns commission on bookings and purchases made through partner links, and that it does not otherwise monetize traffic or sell data.

On price, Furkot's Pass page says you do not have to pay to start planning, and that a pass valid for 1 year costs $14. It can also be earned as a reward when a booking goes through Furkot's partners. The pass unlocks topo and satellite maps, caching a whole trip map for offline use, elevation profiles, configurable export formats, import, automatic refueling stops, RV park suggestions and RV-dimension routing, printed turn-by-turn directions, bulk adding of points of interest, tags and expense tracking. Exporting a basic GPX file is free for everyone.

On platforms, Furkot's mobile page says to open Furkot in a phone or tablet browser and add it to your home screen. It says you only need to connect to the internet once to load your trips, and that offline maps cover OpenStreetMap road maps only. The Google Play and Apple store searches we ran for Furkot returned no Furkot app.

  • Strength: it plans a multi-stop drive with overnight stops and hotel prices along the route, for a $14 pass and no stop cap stated.
  • Limit: its site describes routes and stops, not a swipe-based shortlist for one city.
  • Limit: browser-based, with no store app found, and without a pass you can view a trip but not modify it.

Roadtrippers

Best for: road trips with campgrounds and RV stops

Roadtrippers' home page shows four plans. Free gives 1 saved trip and 3 stops per trip. Basic is $35.99 a year or $11.99 a month for 3 saved trips and 20 stops per trip, and adds custom map styles and trip export. Pro is $49.99 a year or $16.99 a month for 5 saved trips and 50 stops, and adds limited ads, trip collaboration and navigation. Premium is $59.99 a year or $19.99 a month for unlimited saved trips and 150 stops, and adds RV GPS, route elevation and grades, overnight RV parking, member deals, live traffic and offline maps. The paid plans have a 7-day free trial, and the page calls its Autopilot trip planner its newest Premium feature.

The Apple store listing calls it a road trip planning app, free to download, and describes campground reviews and map overlays for public land, cell coverage and wildfire smoke. It shows a 4.6 average from 61,568 ratings. The home page links the iOS and Android apps and the web planner.

  • Strength: strong campground and RV coverage, with iOS, Android and web apps.
  • Limit: the free plan stops at 3 stops per trip, and navigation, collaboration and offline maps sit on higher tiers.

Wanderlog

Best for: one itinerary and map for any kind of trip

Wanderlog's Apple store listing calls it a free travel app with an optional Pro subscription. It says you can add unlimited stops for free on a road trip, optimize the route, see times and distances between places, and, with Pro, export a trip to Google Maps. The listing shows a 4.9 average from 36,340 ratings.

  • Strength: the itinerary, the map and the road-trip stops sit in one app, with an optional Pro plan for Google Maps export.
  • Limit: its listing covers flights, hotels and many trip types, so there is more to set up.

Roadie

Best for: multi-stop road trips with driving times

Roadie's Apple store listing describes a map-first route planner for roadtrippers, campers and vanlifers. You build an itinerary on an interactive map and add as many stops as you like, and each leg shows its distance and driving time. The listing shows a 4.6 average from 2,109 ratings, and the app is free to download.

  • Strength: a simple route planner that shows driving times between stops.
  • Limit: its listing is written for roadtrippers and campers, so it fits less well for a city stay.

Which to choose

  • You want to plan a drive with overnight stops and hotel prices along the route: Furkot.
  • You want a road trip with campgrounds, RV stops and a store app: Roadtrippers.
  • You want to shortlist real places in one city on Android: NomadSwipe, which I built.
  • You want a multi-stop road trip with driving times in a simple planner: Roadie.
  • You want one itinerary and map for any kind of trip: Wanderlog.

FAQ

Is Furkot free?

You can start planning without paying, and Furkot says it puts no limit on the time you can spend trying it before you buy. It sells a Furkot Pass for $14, valid for 1 year. Furkot says it will at some point ask you to buy one, and that without a pass you can keep viewing a trip but cannot modify it.

How much does the Furkot Pass cost?

Furkot's help page says a purchased pass costs $14 and is valid for 1 year. A pass can also be earned as a reward, for example for booking through Furkot's partner links, and a reward pass may last less than a year.

Is Roadtrippers free?

Roadtrippers has a free plan with 1 saved trip and 3 stops per trip. Its paid plans are Basic at $35.99 a year, Pro at $49.99 a year and Premium at $59.99 a year, with a 7-day free trial, according to its home page on 9 November 2026.

Is Furkot cheaper than Roadtrippers?

On list price, yes: the Furkot Pass is $14 for 1 year, while Roadtrippers' annual plans are $35.99, $49.99 and $59.99. The two do not gate the same features. Roadtrippers caps stops per trip at 3, 20, 50 or 150 by plan, while Furkot's help pages state no stop cap.

Does Furkot limit the number of stops?

We found no stop or trip cap on the 64 Furkot help pages we checked on 9 November 2026. That is not a promise of unlimited stops, because the pages do not say either way.

Is there a Furkot app?

Furkot's help page says to open furkot.com in a phone or tablet browser and add it to your home screen for an app-like experience. No Furkot app appeared in the Google Play or Apple store searches we ran on 9 November 2026.

Does Furkot plan driving time?

Yes. Furkot shows distance and travel time for the whole trip, between stops and since the last overnight stop, and you can set a daily limit on travel time.

Does NomadSwipe plan a driving route?

No. I built NomadSwipe, and its listed features are swipe, save, map and go. They do not include driving routes, drive-time limits or overnight stops.

Does NomadSwipe work offline?

Up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online. Offline copies expire, according to the Google Play listing.
