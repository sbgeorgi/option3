Apps Like GetYourGuide: 6 Options for Tickets and Day Trips, and Where NomadSwipe Fits

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android). It is not a booking marketplace, so it is listed second below only in the sense that it does a different job, and I say where it fits and where it does not. Facts checked on 16 November 2026. Updated 16 November 2026.

Apps like GetYourGuide are other booking marketplaces for tours, skip-the-line tickets and day trips: Tiqets, Civitatis, Klook, Musement and Headout all sell them. Key facts

  • GetYourGuide's Amsterdam page says its ranking weighs popularity, ratings, performance, availability, diversity and revenue, including supplier commissions.
  • I counted the first 24 cards on that page: 7 cruises, 7 attraction or museum tickets, 4 day trips of 5.5 to 8 hours, 4 walking or guided tours and 2 workshops. Going by titles, none is an open place you can simply walk into.
  • The Van Gogh Museum names GetYourGuide, Tiqets, Musement, Klook and Tours&Tickets as official resellers. The Anne Frank House says its tickets are only sold on its own website.
  • Tiqets, Civitatis, Klook, Musement and Headout sell tickets and tours too; they differ in focus, not in kind. NomadSwipe sells none, and is for deciding what to do with the hours between bookings.

What GetYourGuide is and what it does

GetYourGuide's about page describes a marketplace for tours, tickets and activities, founded in 2009 and made in Zurich and Berlin, with over 150,000 experiences in 12,000 cities and over 200 million tickets sold. It promises free cancellation up to 24 hours in advance, support around the clock, and a line of "Originals by GetYourGuide" it describes as its most immersive experiences. Those are its own figures and I could not check them.

The Google Play listing is titled "GetYourGuide: Travel & Tickets" and shows 4.9 from 385K reviews, 10M+ downloads and an update on 14 November 2026. It lists reserve now and pay later, instant confirmation, tickets you can open offline, and free cancellation up to 24 hours before for most bookings. Its Data safety section says it may share personal info and device or other IDs with third parties, and may collect personal info, financial info and three other types. I read the pages in a signed-out browser and installed no app.

Why people look beyond one marketplace

I did not survey reviews or forums, so I do not say what GetYourGuide's users complain about. What the pages themselves show is where a single marketplace leaves a question open:

  • The ranking is partly commercial, by its own account. The Amsterdam page's ranking note lists revenue, including supplier commissions, next to ratings and popularity, and adds that rankings are algorithmic with no guaranteed positions.
  • A list of things to book is not a list of things to do. Of the 24 cards I counted, none is a park, a square or a street. The nearest is a ticketed ice rink on the public Museumplein, which Wikipedia says is a public space that can hold a rink in winter.
  • Day trips eat the day. Four of the 24 cards are 5.5 to 8 hours long. In 48 hours, one 8-hour tour is a sixth of the whole time, before anyone sleeps.
  • The same ticket is sold in several places. The Van Gogh Museum's page lists five resellers, so which marketplace you open is partly a matter of which one shows the time slot you want.
  • Terms differ by attraction. GetYourGuide's own pages promise free cancellation up to 24 hours ahead, while the Anne Frank House says its tickets cannot be exchanged or refunded; Viator's matching 24-hour wording is set beside it in Viator or GetYourGuide, side by side.

Tiqets: the ticket specialist

The Tiqets site sells itself on museum and attraction tickets: its headline promises flexible cancellation options on all venues, skip-the-line entry on your phone and "more than 70 million happy travelers", which is its own marketing figure. The home page lists the Van Gogh Museum, Moco Museum Amsterdam and Amsterdam canal cruises among its top things to do, and names Barcelona, Paris, Rome, Milan and New York as top destinations. The Play listing says it covers over 500 destinations, stores tickets offline on your phone and sells audio guides. The site says the app has been downloaded by over 5 million travellers while Play shows 1M+, which are different measures, since Play counts only Android installs. Compared with GetYourGuide, it reads as narrower: attractions first, tours second.

Civitatis: guided visits and day trips

The Civitatis home page says "Fill your trip" and shows ratings out of 10, 9.10 over more than 5,000,000 reviews by its own count (same page). It lists Rome, Paris and Barcelona among its top destinations and labels several walking tours "Free!", including Rome, Madrid and Barcelona; I did not open those listings to see how the guide is paid. The Play listing says over 90,000 activities, lists free tours, day trips and multi-day excursions, skip-the-line tickets, food tours and cruises, and mentions saving ideas and building an itinerary in "My Trips". Civitatis and Klook are the two listings I read that describe saving ideas for later, though what they save is what they sell. Its Data safety section says it may share location, personal info and four other types.

Klook: activities plus getting around

The Klook listing puts activities next to hotels, car rentals, day tours and transport: trains, buses, cars and ferries. It says skip-the-line tickets are on selected attractions, that you can redeem e-vouchers or download them for offline access, and that you can save bucket-list ideas to a wishlist. It shows 4.5 from 150K reviews and 10M+ downloads. Its Data safety section says no data is shared with third parties. The Van Gogh Museum names Klook as an official reseller, but I could not load Klook's own site in my headless browser, so I did not read any Amsterdam page there. If your trip also needs a train or ferry seat, it is the one listing I read that says so.

Musement: tickets, with time slots

The Musement home page describes tickets to museums, attractions and activities and says it offers free cancellation on thousands of experiences. It shows an Amsterdam Van Gogh Museum ticket from $30, rated 4.38 of 5 (6,523), described as a time slot ticket with instant confirmation. That matches what the museum's own page says about start times. I could not find its Android listing under the identifiers I tried, so I say nothing about its app.

Headout: fewer choices on purpose

The Headout home page leads with "Curated, not crowded": it says it brings experiences worth your time rather than hundreds of options, that you can book anytime and that it vets every experience. Its destination list includes Amsterdam, Barcelona, Rome and Paris. If the sheer size of a marketplace's list is what tires you, a smaller catalogue is its stated answer; I did not compare catalogue sizes. I did not find its Android listing, so I say nothing about its app.

Where the sources disagree, and what they do not settle

  1. GetYourGuide's cancellation wording. The about page promises free cancellation up to 24 hours in advance with no qualifier. The Play listing says free cancellation up to 24 hours before for most bookings. I do not know which bookings are the exception.
  2. Who sells what. The Van Gogh Museum lists five resellers and the Anne Frank House lists none. A marketplace that is the right place for one attraction can be the wrong one for another, so the attraction's own page comes first.
  3. Prices. The museums show euros and GetYourGuide shows dollars. A €25 museum price next to a $30 marketplace price is not a markup, because the currencies, dates and what each ticket includes are not the same, and I do not call it one.
  4. Reach figures. Tiqets says over 5 million downloads on its site and shows 1M+ on Play. Civitatis says over 5,000,000 reviews while Play shows 17.1K for the app. Those are different things being counted.

For choosing the open hours, not for booking

  • Different need, picking what to see once you are there: NomadSwipe (Android), which sells no tickets and sits next to whichever marketplace you book through.

  • Guided visits and day trips with a 10-point rating scale: Civitatis, which also lists walking tours labelled Free! on its home page.
  • Activities and a train or ferry seat in one app: Klook, the only one of the listings I read that says so.
  • Fewer choices to wade through: Headout, going by its own wording; I did not compare catalogue sizes.

FAQ

What are good apps like GetYourGuide?

For booking tickets, tours and day trips: Tiqets, Civitatis, Klook, Musement and Headout, each described from its own pages above. Which one carries a given ticket depends on the attraction, so check the attraction's own page first. For choosing which open places to visit around the things you book, I built NomadSwipe (Android), which is not a booking marketplace.

Can I buy Van Gogh Museum tickets through GetYourGuide or a similar app?

Yes, according to the museum. Its ticket page names Tours&Tickets, GetYourGuide, Tiqets, Musement and Klook as official resellers and says all visitors need a ticket with a start time. It also says criminals are active and tells buyers to use its own ticket site or those resellers. Check that page for the current list before buying anywhere else.

Can I book the Anne Frank House through a ticket app?

Not according to the museum. Its own ticket page says tickets are only available through its website, are valid for one specific time slot, and cannot be exchanged or refunded. Tickets for a given day are released six weeks ahead. GetYourGuide's Amsterdam list shows an Anne Frank walking tour, which is a guided tour, not entry to the house.
