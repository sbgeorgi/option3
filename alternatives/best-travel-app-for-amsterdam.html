Best Travel App for Amsterdam: Which App for Which Job

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android). It is one row of six and I say where it does not fit. Every other fact comes from an official page or store listing I read on 15 November 2026. Published 15 November 2026. Updated 15 November 2026.

The best travel app for Amsterdam depends on the job, and I installed none of these to crown one. For trams, metro, buses and ferries, GVB's own travel app. For museum entry, the I amsterdam City Card app. For choosing what to see, a places app: I built NomadSwipe (Android), which works in any city worldwide and does only that.

Key facts

  • Amsterdam splits into four jobs: paying for transit, planning a trip across operators, getting into timed-ticket sights, and deciding what to see. No app I read does all four.
  • The I amsterdam transport guide says that with a contactless card or phone, GVB charges a maximum of €10.50 per day, and that you check in and out with no separate app.
  • GVB's home page carried a notice on 15 November: public transport disruption in the city on 14 and 15 November, with the new metro network being tried out and Sinterklaas arriving. It also listed 21 planned diversions and 10 lift and escalator disruptions. Live information matters more than a printed map this weekend.
  • The Anne Frank House sells tickets online only, for a set time slot, released six weeks ahead, and lists no I amsterdam Card discount.
  • The I amsterdam City Card pages disagree with each other on its lengths (24, 48 or 72 hours on one, up to 120 on another) and on whether you can get a physical card.
  • NomadSwipe works in any city worldwide. It does not sell tickets, plan transit or navigate.

What changes in Amsterdam itself

These are the local facts that decide which app you open, each from an official city, transit or museum page.

Language: English versions everywhere I looked

The GVB, NS, I amsterdam and Anne Frank House sites each had an English version and a switch to Dutch. The GVB app page says the app is fully available in Dutch and English, and the NS Play listing I read is in English. I found no official page that calls English a problem, but I did not open the apps, so I cannot say how much Dutch shows up inside them.

Transit: cashless, check in and check out

The I amsterdam getting-around page calls public transport there cashless and says you check in with a debit or credit card. Its transport guide lists what works: contactless debit cards from Dutch banks, Maestro, V PAY, Mastercard and VISA debit or credit cards, and a phone with Apple Pay or Google Wallet. You tap in when you board and out when you leave, including when you change trams or buses, and metro stations are gated. GVB's home page says a day out is possible by checking in and out with a debit card with no registration. Paying that way, GVB charges a maximum of €10.50 a day for all its lines.

The same guide lists GVB day and multi-day tickets, from one day to seven, as an instant ticket for Google or Apple Wallet, and the Amsterdam & Region Travel Ticket, one to three days across GVB, Connexxion, AllGo, R-net, EBS and NS in Amsterdam and the region. The GVB ticket page I tried returned not-found, so I give no GVB ticket prices.

Network shape: tram in the centre, metro and ferry for the north

The guide says trams run frequently across the city except Amsterdam Noord and NDSM, that four metro routes are often the fastest way to Zuidoost, Amstelveen and Noord, and that ferries across the IJ to Noord are free for pedestrians, cyclists and mopeds. The Central Station to Buiksloterweg ferry also runs through the night. Walking, the page's FAQ says, is the cheapest way to get around. A planner that knows ferries matters here, which is why the GVB app's page lists a ferry departure countdown.

Sights: museum clusters, one timed-ticket house and day trips

The I amsterdam top 20 page describes Museumplein as home to the Rijksmuseum, Moco Museum, Stedelijk Museum and the Royal Concertgebouw, the Rembrandt House Museum near Waterlooplein, over 800,000 bicycles in the city, and day trips: Haarlem is 20 minutes by train from Central, and Zaanse Schans is included in the City Card. The Anne Frank House is different. Its page gives the address as Prinsengracht 263-267 with the entrance at Westermarkt 20, a 20-minute walk from Central or tram 13 or 17 to Westermarkt, open daily 9:00 to 22:00. Tickets are online only, for a specific time slot, and are not sold at the door. That one sight needs planning before you arrive, not an app on the day.

Passes: City Card, GVB tickets and paying by card

Passes are not apps, but they decide what your apps have to do. The I amsterdam City Card page offers two versions. Total Experience gives unlimited access to all City Card offers with public transport included. Explorer lets you choose 3, 5 or 7 activities and has no public transport. The how-to page lists more than 70 museums and attractions, city-wide GVB metro, tram, bus and ferry for the card's duration, one canal cruise, a 24-hour bicycle rental and discounts. The countdown starts only when you activate it, and the card is valid for a year from purchase. Prices were not shown on the pages I read: the order calculator showed €0 until a card is chosen.

Two things the pages do not agree on. The getting-around FAQ describes the City Card's transport as 24, 48 or 72 hours, while the how-to page and the transport guide list 24, 48, 72, 96 or 120. And the how-to page says the digital card gives you no physical card, while the City Card page says the Total Experience card is available as a physical card at the I amsterdam Store at Central Station. The likeliest reading is that the store sells a physical version and online orders are digital, but I did not test an order. If you pick the digital card, the how-to page warns that a flat phone means no card.

To compare: the Anne Frank House gets no City Card discount, so its ticket (€16.50 for an adult including a €1.00 booking fee, €17.00 from 2027, per its page) comes on top. Add up the sights you will actually enter, then set that against paying by card at up to €10.50 a day for transit.

The apps, one at a time

GVB travel app: the operator's own

GVB's app page says you can plan a trip from anywhere in the Netherlands using the national 9292 planner, see the live location of a tram, bus, metro and train, get a countdown clock for ferries, buy a barcode ticket in the app (also for the night bus), view stop accessibility and lift and escalator faults, and set an Alert for disruptions on your usual route. The Play listing adds a crowd indicator, a bicycle as before and after transport, and a "Touch Swipe planner" for swiping from your location to key places of interest. That plans journeys to the sights, it does not choose them. The listing showed 4.7 from 9.32K reviews and 1M+ downloads, updated 13 October 2026. Because both the GVB app and 9292 use the 9292 planner, do not expect two different answers from them; the difference is tickets and Alerts.

9292: one planner for every operator

The Play listing says 9292 combines the timetables of train, bus, tram, metro and ferry for all Dutch public transport operators, with real-time data from NS, GVB and others, live vehicle locations, expected crowding and saved trips. It sells an e-ticket you pay for with iDeal, credit card or Google Pay and open gates with a QR code, and lists shared bikes and scooters including Donkey Republic, which you can rent in the app. It contains ads. I amsterdam's transport guide recommends it for planning in advance. If your trip leaves Amsterdam, for a day in Haarlem for example, this is the one that does not stop at the GVB network. 4.4 from 31.1K reviews, 5M+ downloads, updated 8 September 2026.

NS: trains, including Schiphol

The Play listing calls it the official planning app for the Dutch Railways and lists a real-time journey planner, departures per station, planned work and disruptions, station facilities and delay notifications for a saved trip. The text I read does not mention tickets, though the NS site lists single tickets, and says you can check in and out with a payment card. The listing showed 3.8 from 34.7K reviews and 10M+ downloads, updated 6 November 2026. Its use for a visitor is the airport: the I amsterdam page puts Schiphol to Central at 14 to 17 minutes for about €5.50.

I amsterdam City Card app: only if you buy the digital card

Per the how-to page, you order online, install the app, add the card to your account and activate it only once you are in Amsterdam. At a gate you hold the phone with the QR code under the scanner and do the same on exit; at attractions you show the phone at the ticket office; some museums need a time slot booked in advance. The app can transfer a card once and shows a map of locations and events. It is worth having only if the City Card pays off for the sights you plan. I did not find its Play listing under the identifier I tried, so I give no store figures.

Google Maps: the default planner

The Play listing describes directions for driving, walking, cycling and public transport, custom lists of favourite saved places, and reviews and photos, and adds that some features are not available in all countries or cities. Its help page says that when offline, transit, bicycling and walking directions are unavailable, which matters for a tram-and-walking city if your roaming data is off. I did not test transit in Amsterdam. Citymapper is the other mainstream option: its Amsterdam address redirected to a page for the whole Netherlands listing Amsterdam Metro (GVB) and Amsterdam Tram (GVB) lines, NS, bus and ferry, and its Play listing shows 4.8 from 392K reviews. More options in this class are in apps like Citymapper.

Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

For choosing places, not for getting around

I built NomadSwipe, so weigh this with that in mind. It is an Android app, and it works in any city worldwide. Pick a city and it deals real places one at a time: swipe right or tap Save to keep one, and every saved place is pinned on a trip map where you build a trip. Each place shows category, rating, distance and, where hours are known, whether it is open, and the list sorts by rating, distance or A to Z. You can add a custom pin for anything it does not show, such as your hotel. If you know the headline sights but not how to fill the rest of your days, picking places is the job it does.

What it does not do matters more for this query. It sells no tickets, so it does nothing for the Anne Frank House time slots or the City Card. It does not plan tram, metro or ferry journeys and it will not tell you about the weekend's disruption. Its listing says you can open a place and start directions, but I did not test that, and it is not a replacement for the GVB app or 9292. The listing says up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online, and the copies expire; it describes no downloadable maps. iPhone is coming soon.

Who should pick which

  • You do not know what to see yet: NomadSwipe to pick places, next to a transit app. I make no claim that its saved list moves into them automatically.
  • You will ride trams and the metro daily: the GVB app for its Alerts and ferry countdown, or just a contactless card, since the guide caps GVB travel at €10.50 a day.
  • Your trip goes beyond the city, to Haarlem or Zaanse Schans: 9292 or NS for the train leg, with the transport guide naming both.
  • You land at Schiphol: the NS app or 9292 for the 14 to 17 minute train to Central.
  • You plan three or more museum visits: price the I amsterdam City Card against separate tickets, and book time slots where the card list says they are needed. The Anne Frank House is outside that arithmetic.
  • You want one familiar app for walking directions and reviews: Google Maps, mindful of its offline limits.
  • You have a weekend and a fixed list of famous sights: you may need nothing beyond a contactless card, one ticket booking and a map.

The short version

  • No single app covers an Amsterdam visit: paying for transit, planning trips, timed-ticket sights and choosing places are separate jobs.
  • GVB charges a maximum of €10.50 a day for card or phone payers, per the I amsterdam guide, so a pass is not automatically cheaper.
  • The GVB app and 9292 share the national 9292 planner, per GVB's page; they differ in tickets and Alerts.
  • The Anne Frank House needs an online time-slot ticket and gives no City Card discount.
  • NomadSwipe, which I built, is Android only and covers choosing places, not tickets or transit.

FAQ

What is the best travel app for Amsterdam?

There is no single best one, because a visit needs separate jobs done. For trams, metro, buses and ferries, the operator's own GVB travel app is the one GVB's visitor page lists for planning and tickets. For museum entry, the I amsterdam City Card app. For choosing what to see, a places app. I built NomadSwipe (Android), which only does that last job, and I installed none of these apps, so this is what their pages say.

Which app do I use for trams and the metro in Amsterdam?

The GVB travel app is the operator's own. Its page says it plans trips with the national 9292 planner, shows live vehicle positions, a ferry departure countdown and lift and escalator disruptions, and sells barcode tickets in the app. You can also skip apps: the I amsterdam transport guide says to check in and out with a contactless bank card or a phone, and that GVB charges a maximum of €10.50 per day that way.

How do I pay for public transport in Amsterdam?

By tapping a contactless card or phone at the readers, according to the I amsterdam getting-around page, which calls public transport there cashless. You check in when you board and out when you leave, and the guide says no separate app is needed. The accepted cards it lists include Dutch bank debit cards and Maestro, V PAY, Mastercard and VISA debit or credit cards, plus phones with Apple Pay or Google Wallet.

How do I get from Schiphol Airport to central Amsterdam?

By train. The I amsterdam getting-around page says Nederlandse Spoorwegen runs a 14 to 17 minute ride from Schiphol to Amsterdam Central Station at approximately €5.50 per person. For train schedules, its transport guide names the NS and 9292 apps. The NS site also lists planning, single-ticket purchase and a note that a new timetable starts on 13 December.

Does the I amsterdam City Card cover the Anne Frank House?

Not as a discount, according to the museum. The Anne Frank House ticket page lists the I amsterdam Card as giving no discount, and says tickets are sold online only, for a specific time slot, with new tickets released every Tuesday at 10am CET for a visit six weeks later. The City Card page lists the Rijksmuseum, Moco and the Stedelijk among its highlighted attractions, so check each sight before you buy a card.

Do I need to speak Dutch to use travel apps in Amsterdam?

The pages I read suggest not. The GVB, NS, I amsterdam and Anne Frank House sites all offered English versions, and the GVB app page says the app is fully available in Dutch and English. I did not open the apps, so I cannot say how much Dutch appears inside them.
