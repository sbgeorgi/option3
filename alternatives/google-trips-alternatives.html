Google Trips Alternatives and What Replaced It (2026)

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, which is one of the apps compared, so read my entry with that in mind. Updated 3 November 2026.

Google Trips was shut down in August 2019, and Google pointed users to Google Search and Google Maps. Use Google Maps lists for saved places, Tripomatic or Wanderlog for itineraries, and NomadSwipe, which I built, for a shortlist of real places on Android.

The short version by need: Gmail reservations and an automatic itinerary belong to Maps and to email-forwarding planners, saved places belong to Maps lists or a swipe app, and offline use depends on which app you trust with a downloaded map. The table below sorts them, and the six sections after it give the detail.

Google Trips: what happened and what Google says now

  • Shutdown. Google's help page said support would end on 5 August 2019, and the sunset screen read “Many of your favorite features will live on in Google Search and Google Maps”, as reported by 9to5Google.
  • The 2019 plan. Google's post of 14 May 2019 said trip information would be “more accessible at google.com/travel, and in Google Search and Google Maps”, and that trips, including hotel and restaurant reservations, would come to Maps. In August 2019 Google said Maps would show flight and hotel reservations in one place, viewable offline.
  • Later change. Trip summaries on google.com/travel ended on 1 May 2023, according to 9to5Google, which noted that travel reservations might still appear in other Google services.
  • Today. Google's current Maps help pages describe saved lists, a You tab of recent and nearby saved places, and downloadable offline map areas. The You tab page does not mention reservations, so I could not confirm today where Gmail bookings appear in Maps.

1. NomadSwipe: for choosing places, not building a booked trip

Best for: travellers who want a shortlist of real places they chose themselves

I built NomadSwipe, a trip planner for Android. You pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Everything you save lands in your trip and is pinned on a map, and the list view can be sorted by rating, distance or A to Z. For a worked example, see the two-day Lisbon itinerary built from a 16-place sample trip, or read how to plan a trip on a map.

Where it fits the Google Trips job

  • You decide each place yourself, one card at a time, and each saved place is pinned on the trip map.
  • Each place shows its photos, directions, website and phone number, and you can sort saved places by rating, distance or A to Z.
  • It is on Google Play for Android phones, and Premium keeps up to 20 saved trips available offline.

Where it does not

  • It does not write an itinerary from a conversation, does not read your email, and the Google Play listing does not describe bookings for flights, hotels or reservations.
  • It is Android only for now (iPhone coming soon). The free plan is limited to 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip a month, 2 custom pins a month and a daily limit on saves.

Pricing

Free to download on Android. Premium is optional, comes as Monthly or Lifetime, and lifts the limits above. Google Play shows the current price before you pay.

2. Google Maps

Best for: saved places and offline map areas in an app you already have

Google Maps lets you create a list of places. In the You tab you choose New list, name it and save places to it, and a list can be private (“Only you can view and edit”) or shared by link for viewing or editing. The You tab also shows recently saved places and saved places near you. You can download map areas for offline use, and the help page says offline maps must be updated before they expire.

  • Strength: saved lists, shareable by link, and offline map areas in one app.
  • Weakness: it is a map and navigation app rather than an itinerary builder. Offline navigation needs the whole route inside the downloaded area, transit, bicycling and walking directions are unavailable offline, and neither help page says whether saved lists work offline, so check before you travel.

Pricing: the help pages I read do not state a price.

3. Wanderlog

Best for: itineraries with email forwarding

Wanderlog is a trip planner for itineraries and bookings. Its homepage says you can connect or forward your emails to add reservations to a trip plan, and it describes live syncing and collaborative editing for planning with others. It shows App Store and Google Play badges.

  • Strength: email forwarding for reservations, and shared editing, in one planner.
  • Weakness: the homepage I read lists “Offline access” only as a feature label and shows no Pro price, so the cost of the paid tier and the offline details are not confirmed here.

Pricing: check the app for current plans. Read our Wanderlog alternatives and Mindtrip alternatives comparisons for more.

4. Tripomatic

Best for: day-by-day city itineraries, with offline maps on Premium

Tripomatic lists web, iPhone and Android versions. Its free plan has no time limit and covers up to 5 days with planned activities per calendar month across all trips and up to 25 places and hotels per trip, with ads. Premium removes those limits and ads.

  • Strength: turns a list of places into a daily schedule, with offline maps in the mobile app and export to PDF, GPX or KML on Premium.
  • Weakness: the free plan has a monthly day limit and shows ads. Premium prices are not shown on the page I read.

Pricing: a free plan with the limits above; the Premium price is not listed on the page.

5. Roadtrippers

Best for: road trips with stops along the route

Roadtrippers describes itself as a road trip planner. It has a web planner and mobile apps for the App Store and Google Play, and its plans are shown in a comparison table on the homepage.

  • Strength: a planner for routes and stops, with web and mobile apps.
  • Weakness: the tool is built around road trips rather than city swiping, and the free plan allows 1 saved trip and 3 stops per trip.

Pricing: Free is $0 a year. Basic is $11.99 a month or $35.99 a year, with 3 saved trips and 20 stops per trip. Pro is $16.99 a month or $49.99 a year, with 5 saved trips and 50 stops. Premium is $19.99 a month or $59.99 a year, with unlimited saved trips and 150 stops.

6. Polarsteps

Best for: a record of a trip as you go, rather than a shortlist

Polarsteps offers a paid Plus tier. Its Plus page lists 3D maps, map styles, step stats, play mode, up to 10 Travel Buddies and 20% off Travel Books on the yearly plan, and says prices may vary by region.

  • Strength: a good fit if keeping a record of the trip matters as much as planning it.
  • Weakness: the Plus page does not describe offline use, place recommendations or bookings, so it does not replace a city shortlist or a booking tool.

Pricing: Plus costs €8.99 a month or €29.99 a year.

What to use now that Google Trips is gone

  • You want saved places and offline map areas in an app you already use: Google Maps.
  • You want a shortlist of real places on a map, on Android: NomadSwipe.
  • You want itineraries with email forwarding: Wanderlog.
  • You want a day-by-day city plan, with offline maps on Premium: Tripomatic.
  • You want a road trip with stops along the route: Roadtrippers.
  • You want a record of a trip as you go: Polarsteps.

Findings from the 3 November 2026 re-read

Sample and method: on 3 November 2026 I read three Google Maps help pages (lists, offline maps and the You tab), Google's May and August 2019 posts, three 9to5Google articles (two from 2019, one from 2023), a 2020 Beebom list, and the homepages or pricing pages of Wanderlog, Tripomatic, Roadtrippers and Polarsteps. This is a reading of public pages, not a hands-on test of every app.

  • Google's Maps offline help page says transit, bicycling and walking directions are unavailable offline, while driving directions still work without traffic information or alternate routes.
  • Neither the Maps lists page nor the offline maps page says that saved lists are available offline, so a Google Trips user who relied on offline lists cannot confirm the same behaviour from Google's current help pages.
  • The Maps You tab help page says nearby saved places are those within 25 km and that the recently saved section is unavailable if nothing was saved in the last 180 days, so Maps surfaces saved places by time and distance rather than by trip.
  • Of the four competitor pages I read for prices, two (Roadtrippers and Polarsteps) showed paid prices and two (Wanderlog and Tripomatic) did not, so price comparisons for those two have to be done inside the apps.
  • Both list articles I read for the keyword, 9to5Google (2019) and Beebom (2020), name TripIt and Roadtrippers among their picks and are dated, so their app lists predate the 2023 end of trip summaries.

FAQ

What replaced Google Trips?

Google did not replace it with one app. Its sunset screen said many features would live on in Google Search and Google Maps, and Google's May 2019 post said trip information would be reachable at google.com/travel, in Search and in Maps.

Is Google Trips still available?

No. Google ended support for the standalone Trips app on August 5, 2019. Trip summaries on google.com/travel were also set to end on May 1, 2023.

Where do my Gmail reservations show up now?

In August 2019 Google said Maps would show flight and hotel reservations in one place, under Your Places and then Reservations, and that you could open them offline. Google's current help page for the You tab in Maps does not mention reservations, so check your Maps app. Apps such as Wanderlog build a trip from forwarded confirmation emails.

Can Google Maps work offline for a trip?

Yes, for map areas you download. Navigation works offline only if the whole route is inside the downloaded area, driving directions work but transit, bicycling and walking directions do not, and downloaded maps expire unless updated. The help pages do not say whether saved lists are available offline.

Does NomadSwipe replace Google Trips?

Only for choosing places. I built NomadSwipe: it is Android only today, lets you swipe real places into a trip and see them on a map, and keeps up to 20 saved trips offline with Premium. It does not read your email or describe bookings, so it does not replace Gmail reservations.

Does Tripomatic work offline or export itineraries?

Tripomatic's website says its Premium plan adds offline maps in the mobile app and export to PDF, GPX and KML. Its free plan covers up to 5 days with planned activities per calendar month and shows ads.
