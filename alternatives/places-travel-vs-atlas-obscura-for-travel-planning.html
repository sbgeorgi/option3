Places.travel vs Atlas Obscura for Travel Planning: Which Helps More?

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), which appears once below as a third option. Facts about places.travel and Atlas Obscura checked on 14 November 2026. Updated 14 November 2026.

Places.travel vs Atlas Obscura for travel planning: neither is a planner. Places.travel is a publication of destination articles whose menu lists US states and cities; Atlas Obscura is a community database of unusual places with a map, lists and an app. Use Atlas Obscura to find and save stops with addresses, and places.travel to read US destination roundups.

Key facts

  • Places.travel calls itself a travel site of firsthand stories with four menu items: Destinations, Topics, Traveler and Travel TV. I found no app and no map on the pages I read.
  • Atlas Obscura's about page showed 33,803 unique places and foods, 15,013 stories and 1,163 experiences on 14 November 2026, and says the places are contributed by its community.
  • Its Google Play listing showed 4.8 from 2.67K reviews, 100K+ downloads and Contains ads; its App Store page showed 4.8 from 9.9K ratings on an iPhone-only listing.
  • For Chicago, Atlas Obscura's guide showed 169 entries with a Map tab; places.travel's Chicago page showed a short description and article cards.
  • The entry I opened on Atlas Obscura listed an address, coordinates, Get Directions and nearby places. The places.travel article I opened gave no addresses, hours, prices or ratings in its text.

What I did: one afternoon in Chicago, signed out

I wanted to see what each site hands you if you already know the city. I followed these steps on 14 November 2026, reading pages rather than planning a real trip.

  1. Places.travel: I opened Destinations, found Chicago under Midwest and Illinois, and opened its page. It gave a short paragraph about the lakefront, deep-dish pizza and museums, then article cards: Hit the Pavement on walkable cities, Evanston, North Shore lakefront gems, North Shore bakeries and a pizza guide.
  2. Atlas Obscura: I opened the Chicago guide. It was headed "169 Cool, Hidden, and Unusual Things to Do in Chicago, Illinois", updated May 14, 2026, with tabs for Attractions, Events, Food & Drink, Map, Stories and Lists, and Been Here, Want to Visit and Add to List under each entry.
  3. I opened one Atlas Obscura entry to see its anatomy. I picked a Lisbon bar-hotel, Pensão Amor, because it was listed on the New York City guide page, not because it is in Chicago. It showed a street address, coordinates, a Get Directions link, nearby places and related places. A prompt offered an account to read the rest of the story, though the page text I got back was complete, so I cannot say what a signed-out visitor sees. I saw no opening hours or rating on it.
  4. Places.travel: I opened a fresh article, Think You Know Tucson? These 12 Experiences Might Surprise You, dated 13 November 2026. It is 12 numbered places, each a short paragraph with a photo credit to Visit Tucson, and it ends with a link to a guide in Apple Maps, which needs an iPhone. The text I read gave no addresses, hours, prices or ratings.
  5. Result: with places.travel I ended with things to read and had to look up each stop elsewhere. With Atlas Obscura I ended with entries I could mark and open on a map, but they are tuned to the unusual rather than to a whole day, so the café and the train station still come from somewhere else.

Who each fits

Places.travel

Places.travel suits you if you are picking a destination in the United States and want readable roundups, with sections for solo, couple, family and accessible travel. Its Topics page also has a Guide on the Go tag and a Travel Itineraries category. It does not hold your saved places, and the pages I read show no total, map or app. It is a source of ideas, so you carry the list yourself.

Atlas Obscura

Atlas Obscura suits you if you want the odd stop: the guide to one city can run to hundreds of entries, each with an address and coordinates, and you can mark Want to Visit or add to a list. It is also the only one of the two with an app, and it runs events and trips. The Atlas Obscura alternatives page lists what else covers odd places. Atlas Obscura is built to make you curious about a place, not to say what is open on a Monday, so check hours before you go.

FAQ

Is places.travel an app?

I found no places.travel app. It is a website: a publication of destination articles, topic pages and a Travel TV section. A Google Play search for places.travel on 14 November 2026 returned other travel apps and none from places.travel, and the App Store was not checked. Some articles end with a link to a guide in Apple Maps, which needs an iPhone.

Is Atlas Obscura good for planning a trip?

It is good for finding and keeping unusual stops, less so for building a schedule. Its pages and Play listing describe a map, custom lists and a Been There tracker, and entries I read show an address, coordinates and a Get Directions link. On the pages I read I found no day-by-day schedule builder, and the entry I opened showed no opening hours or rating.

Which one covers more places?

Atlas Obscura publishes its size: its about page showed 33,803 places and foods collected, and its city guides show counts such as 169 for Chicago. Places.travel states no total on the pages I read, and its destination menu lists US states and cities. The two count different things, though: Atlas Obscura counts database entries, and places.travel publishes articles that each cover several places.

Is the Atlas Obscura app free?

Its App Store page showed Free on 14 November 2026. The Google Play page I read showed 4.8 from about 2.6K reviews, 100K+ downloads and Contains ads, and did not state a price. Check the current listing for in-app purchases.
