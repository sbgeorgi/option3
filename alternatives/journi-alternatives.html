What Journi is today

Journi is two Android apps from one developer, Journi - The Photo Book Makers, and both were updated on 12 November 2026, so it is still maintained. Journi Blog - Travel Diary is the journal. Its Google Play listing shows 4.7 from 6.36K reviews and 500K+ downloads, and describes timelines of photos, notes and maps that the app builds by grouping photos by time and location data, which you can turn into a photo book. It also lets followers track your location if you invite them. Journi Print - 5min Photo Book is the printing app: 4.6 from 19.8K reviews and 1M+ downloads, with a listing that says it designs a photo book in date order from as many as 1,200 photos.

The journiapp.com homepage leads with printing. It lists a Photo Book from €22.99 in five sizes, photo prints from €0.29, a photo calendar from €19.99 and a Travel Photo Book that uses automated maps, and it says Journi is trusted by over 11 million memory-makers. The homepage text I read does not describe the diary app. That appears on Google Play and in the help center, which has a separate Journi Blog section. So "Journi" can mean the journal, the printer or both, and which you want decides which alternative fits.

The Blog listing says Premium adds 16MP uploads, 1TB of image storage, backup to Dropbox or Google Drive, offline use and syncing across your devices, plus 10% off photo books on a 12 month plan, at €9.99 for one month, €43.99 for six or €53.99 for twelve. It also says photo books start at €22.99 including worldwide shipping, and that they are personalised products that cannot be returned or refunded, so you check the preview before you order. The help center calls the paid tier Journi PLUS and the Play listing calls it Premium; the pages do not say whether these are the same plan.

Why people look for Journi alternatives

I did not read reviews or forums, so I do not say why anyone leaves Journi. These are the places where its own pages leave a question, which is usually why a comparison like this gets opened:

  • One name, two apps. A search for Journi on Google Play returns a diary and a print shop. If you wanted only one of them, the other is clutter, and an alternative may cover just that half.
  • Offline use sits behind Premium. The listing ties offline use to the paid plan. If you travel without data, check that line before you rely on it.
  • Print orders are final. The listing says books are made for you and cannot be refunded, which makes the preview step matter more than the price.
  • Starting prices are not like for like. A price "from" depends on size, pages, cover and shipping, and the pages I read quote euros and dollars on different terms.
  • It starts from photos. Journi groups photos you already have. Some alternatives start from a recorded route or from written entries instead.

Polarsteps: the closest match for a trip record

The Polarsteps listing splits the app into plan, track, share and relive. It lists an AI itinerary planner, automatic route tracking on a world map, photos, videos and stories on each step, privacy controls (everyone, followers only or you only), Trip Reels and Travel Books. The website adds that it works offline, typically uses less than 4% battery a day while tracking and is ad-free. Its Travel Book page says books add weather, altitude and distances automatically, come as Premium or Lay-Flat Premium, and ship free around the globe through local printers. I saw no book price on that page. The Plus page says the paid tier adds 3D maps, more map styles, up to 10 Travel Buddies against 5 and, on the yearly plan, 20% off books.

Where it beats Journi: it also plans the trip, and its listing puts automatic route tracking at the centre. Where Journi is clearer: it states a starting price for a book, which Polarsteps does not on the pages I read. For more on this app, see Polarsteps alternatives.

Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

Different need: choosing places, not recording a trip

I built NomadSwipe (Android), so weigh this with that in mind. It is not a journal and not a photo book app. I make no claim that it records your route, stores your photos or prints anything. It is a place-discovery and trip-building app, so it belongs before the trip, where a journal belongs during and after it.

What it does: pick a destination and it deals real restaurants, sights, bars and stays one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep one, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Cards show the category, the rating, how far away the place is and, where hours are known, whether it is open. Saved places are pinned on a trip map, with a list view sorted by rating, distance or A to Z, and a custom pin for anything the cards do not show, such as your hotel. The listing's sample trip is a long weekend in New York with 16 places, all picked by swiping. It works in any city worldwide.

The honest split: if you want to remember a trip, Journi and its alternatives are the right tools and NomadSwipe is not. If you are still working out which places belong in the trip you will later write about, NomadSwipe is built for that step. iPhone is coming soon. FindPenguins: a tracker with a priced hardcover

The FindPenguins listing describes a travel tracker that logs your route in the background, battery-friendly and offline, posts with photos, videos and stories, and 3D flyover videos of the route. Its travel book page is the most detailed on prices of the apps here: a hardcover on 200 gsm paper from $40.99 for 24 pages at 21 cm, up to 400 pages, and a lay-flat book on 380 gsm paper from $59.99, up to 118 pages. The same page says "free shipping to many countries" and, in the line above it, that prices include VAT plus shipping, so the shipping terms are for you to confirm at checkout.

Compared with Journi, it offers a long printed book with a stated page range and a route-first record. Journi's lower starting price (€22.99 with shipping, per its listing) is for a different size and page count, so do not read the two numbers as a price comparison.

Day One: a journal first, travel second

The Day One listing pitches a journal for any part of life, with entries that capture location so you can revisit them on a map, end-to-end encryption, exports and prompts. The plans page lists Basic at $0 a year with one photo per entry, Silver at $49.99 with up to 30 photos and videos per entry, and Gold at $74.99, with a larger discount on printed journals at each step up. It does not describe a travel route or a trip book, so it fits if you write more than you photograph. It is a weaker match than Journi for turning one trip into a book.

Journey: diary features, a map view and a shared journal

The Journey listing (4.3 from 94.4K reviews) centres on writing: a journaling Pal you can chat with, mood tracking, templates, a shared journal, end-to-end encryption and the option to view entries on a calendar, in photos, in videos and on a map. The Journey site adds an Atlas view and says it runs on Android, iOS, macOS, Windows, Linux and the web. I found no printed book on the pages I read, and I did not read its prices. Pick it if you want a diary that also covers the trip, not a trip product.

Popsa: only the book

The Popsa listing says each order takes about 5 minutes, prints up to 600 photos across up to 150 pages and starts from $12. It lays pages out automatically, and also makes framed photo tiles and calendars. The part of the description I read mentions no journal, map or route. It is the nearest match to the Journi Print half and nothing like the diary half.

How I would choose: five questions

This is a method, not a trip report. I have not used these apps for a trip for this page, and the order of the questions is what matters.

  1. Do you already have the photos, or do you want the app to record as you go? If the photos are already on your phone, Journi Blog, Journi Print or Popsa can start from them. If you want the route captured while you walk, Polarsteps or FindPenguins describe that.
  2. Do you write more than you photograph? If yes, Day One or Journey are built around entries. If the trip is mostly pictures, they are a poor fit.
  3. Will a printed book be the end product? Then compare the same size and page count in each app's cart. Journi lists €22.99 from, FindPenguins $40.99 for a 21 cm hardcover with 24 pages and Popsa $12 from, and those numbers do not describe the same book.
  4. Who should see it? Polarsteps lists privacy by audience, FindPenguins says live location can be shared with a delay, and the Journi Blog listing offers four visibility levels. Check the setting before the first post.
  5. Is the trip still unplanned? Then the missing step comes before any journal. Swipe through the city in NomadSwipe, save only what you would cross town for and open the trip map; the listing's sample trip, a long weekend in New York, has 16 places, all picked by swiping. Then pick one of the apps above to record it.

When Journi is still the better choice

  • You want the diary and the book from one developer. The Blog listing describes building the timeline and turning it into a photo book inside the same family of apps, with the book's price stated.
  • You have a big pile of photos. The Print listing says as many as 1,200 photos go into one book, twice Popsa's 600.
  • You want other print products. The homepage lists prints from €0.29, cards and a calendar from €19.99, which a pure tracker does not sell.
  • You want a clear starting price with shipping. €22.99 including worldwide shipping is stated outright, where Polarsteps's page I read gives no book price.
  • You do not need planning. Polarsteps and NomadSwipe both help before the trip; the Journi pages I read do not describe planning.

Who should pick which

  • A travel diary with route tracking and a printed book: Polarsteps or FindPenguins.
  • Still choosing what to see in a city: NomadSwipe (Android), then a journal app for the trip itself.
  • Mostly writing, some travel: Day One or Journey.
  • Only a photo book from photos you have: Popsa, or Journi Print.

FAQ

Is Journi still available, and what does it do?

Yes. Journi is two Android apps from one developer, both updated on 12 November 2026 per Google Play: Journi Blog - Travel Diary, a journal that builds timelines from photos, notes and maps, and Journi Print - 5min Photo Book, which designs and prints photo books. Its website leads with photo books, prints and calendars.

What is the closest app to Journi?

It depends which Journi you mean. For the travel diary, Polarsteps and FindPenguins are the closest on their own pages: both record a route, collect photos and stories and sell a printed book. For the printing side, Popsa prints photo books and does nothing else. Day One and Journey are diary apps that are not built around travel.

Is NomadSwipe an alternative to Journi?

No. I built NomadSwipe (Android) and it is not a journal: it does not print books and I make no claim that it records your route or stores your photos. It is for choosing real places in a city, saving the ones you like and seeing them on a trip map. It comes before a journal app, not instead of one. iPhone is coming soon.

Can I use a journal app and NomadSwipe together?

Yes, as two separate tools. I built NomadSwipe (Android) to pick and map the places you want before you go, and a journal app is where you would record the trip as it happens. The two do not exchange data, so the link between them is you and a shortlist.
