How to use the weekend template

  1. Fix the frame first.

    Write the bed address, the check-in time and the time you must leave on Sunday. Those three facts set how long each day really is.

  2. Choose Saturday's anchors.

    Saturday is the one full day, so it gets three anchors: morning, afternoon and evening. Put anything with fixed hours or a timed ticket in the earliest block that fits.

  3. Keep Friday and Sunday light.

    Friday evening is arrival and dinner. Sunday gets a morning block and an afternoon block you are willing to drop.

  4. Add up to two optional places per block.

    Keep them in the same neighborhood as the anchor. An optional place across town is not optional, it is a second trip.

  5. Write the buffer.

    Put the real transit minutes between stops in the buffer cell, then keep a cushion on top for queues and detours.

  6. Fill the rainy-day row.

    Write one indoor swap for each outdoor anchor, so wet weather means swapping a row instead of replanning the day.

  7. Run the checklist the day before.

    Check Saturday and Sunday hours on each anchor's own page, confirm the bookings and set the swap decision.

The rules behind the template

How many places per block

One anchor plus up to two optional places, so a block never holds more than three places and an evening no more than two. Six blocks give six anchors. A realistic weekend delivers the anchors and roughly half the optional places, which is a judgment, not a measured figure. Two sources I read land close to this. Yopki's weekend template page says two or three activities plus meals fill a Saturday, and Stippl's weekend outline says "One must-do per half-day. Everything else is a bonus."

Why one anchor and two optional

The anchor is the place with a constraint: opening hours, a timed ticket or a reservation. Optional places are flexible, such as a park, a market stall or a coffee stop. When a block runs late you drop an optional place and the plan still holds. If every row is an anchor, one late train breaks the day.

Transit buffer

Stippl's template guide suggests 20 to 30 minutes per half-day as a cushion for transport delays and queues. I would write the real transit minutes between each pair of stops in the buffer cell and keep a cushion like that on top. If two places in one block are more than one short ride apart, move one of them to another block.

Rainy-day swap

Pick one indoor option for each outdoor anchor, in the same neighborhood. On Friday morning look at the forecast and decide whether the swap row replaces a block. That one row costs a few minutes and saves rebuilding the plan in a doorway.

Friday is arrival, not a day

Yopki's page treats Friday as arrival and dinner, Saturday as the full day and Sunday as the trip home. A template with three equal day grids hides that. I weight the rows the same way: three anchors on Saturday, one on Friday, and two lighter ones on Sunday.

The 16-place sample trip

The sample trip in NomadSwipe's Google Play listing is a long weekend in New York with 16 places, all picked by swiping. The material I have does not say which places they are, so I do not list any. What I can say is arithmetic on this template: six anchors would take six of the 16, and the other ten would be optional places or rainy-day swaps. That is the point of a shortlist. The pool you save is bigger than the plan, and the template picks from it. A long weekend that adds a Thursday or Monday needs one more block, so copy a Saturday row.

Checklist before you leave

  • Saturday and Sunday opening hours checked on each anchor's own page, because many places close one weekday. My weekend city break checklist counts how often that happens in city decks.
  • Timed tickets and reservations booked, with every reference saved in one note.
  • Bed address, check-in time and what to do if you arrive early.
  • The way home written down, with the time you must leave the last stop.
  • Each block's places in one neighborhood, with transit minutes in the buffer cells.
  • The rainy-day row filled and the forecast looked at on Friday morning.

What most weekend templates get wrong

I read three template pages on 18 November 2026: Yopki's weekend template, Stippl's template guide and a Notion trip itinerary template. Each is useful. These are the gaps I would fix, based on how the pages describe themselves.

  • They start from blank rows. Stippl's how-to says to pull in everything from your saved posts and notes, and Yopki tells you to pick your top two or three activities. Both assume you already hold a researched list. The hardest step, choosing, is left to you.
  • The weekend block has no transit or backup field. Yopki's page describes each time block as having fields for activity, location, reservation details and estimated cost. Travel time between stops and a plan B are not among them. The template above adds both.
  • They track logistics, not geography. The Notion template's description covers packing, flights, hotel details and confirmation numbers. A schedule that does not show where the places are lets you put two far-apart stops in the same block.
  • The sheet is awkward on a phone, says an app vendor. Stippl says a shared spreadsheet is awkward on mobile, where you need the plan. Stippl sells a planner app, so weigh that, but it is why the plain-text copy sits above.

Template or app: which saves time

A copyable template is enough when you already know the places, for example on a return trip or from a guide you trust. You need an order, buffers and a booking record, and a table does that. An app starts to save time one step earlier, when you have a city and no shortlist.

Questions

How many places should a weekend trip itinerary include?

Plan six anchors, one for each block from Friday evening to Sunday afternoon, and give each block at most two optional places. That is a ceiling of three places per block and two for an evening. Expect to do the anchors and about half of the optional places.

How do I plan a weekend trip itinerary starting Friday evening?

Treat Friday evening as arrival: a bed, dinner and a short walk. Put your detailed planning into Saturday, which is the one full day, and keep Sunday to a morning block plus an afternoon you can drop if the trip home needs the time.

Is a spreadsheet or an app better for a weekend itinerary?

A copyable template or spreadsheet is enough when you already know the places and only need an order and a record of bookings. An app helps earlier, when you do not yet have the places and need to choose them and see how far apart they are.
