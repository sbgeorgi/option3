Is Wanderlog worth it? Yes on the free plan; Pro only for offline and routes

Is Wanderlog worth it? The free plan is, because Wanderlog's own Pro page lists unlimited places, live collaboration and booking import at $0. Pro, shown at $39.99 a year, pays off only if you need offline access, route optimization, Gmail scanning or Google Maps export. Ratings split: 4.9 on the U.S. App Store, 1.8 on Trustpilot.

Updated 13 November 2026. Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), a narrower trip app, and I mention it in one short section near the end. I did not install or test Wanderlog. Every Wanderlog fact comes from a page I read on 13 November 2026, linked inline and listed under Sources.

Key facts

  • Free: $0 on the Pro page, with unlimited places, live collaboration with friends, flight, hotel and car import, smart recommendations, and an itinerary and map in one view. The help center adds that the number of trips and people you plan with is never restricted.
  • Pro: "Pro Annual" on the Pro page, starting at $39.99 a year, with a free-trial button. It adds offline access, route optimization, Google Maps export, automatic Gmail scanning, flight and car rental deals, unlimited attachments, dark mode, unlimited AI assistance and adding places from anywhere on the web.
  • Reviews: 4.9 from 36,399 U.S. App Store ratings, 4.8 on Google Play with 1M+ downloads, but 1.8 from 52 reviews on Trustpilot.
  • Maintained: version 2.228 came out on 4 November 2026, the seventh release since 24 September.

How to decide if Pro is worth it: five steps

This is the order I would follow, using only what the sources above state.

  1. Plan one real trip on the free plan. Add places, invite one person and forward one booking email. All three are in the free column, so you learn whether the planner suits you at no cost.
  2. Check whether the trip needs a Pro row. Offline access, route optimization, Google Maps export and Gmail scanning are the four people usually mean. If none applies, stop here: you have your answer.
  3. Price the need. The Pro page price of $39.99 a year is $3.33 a month by simple division. The App Store listing also shows monthly entries at $5.99 and $16.99. At $5.99, seven months costs $41.93, more than the annual price; at $16.99, three months costs $50.97. If you only need Pro for one trip, a monthly entry is cheaper, provided it is the one you are shown.
  4. Read the purchase screen before you tap the trial. The Pro page shows a free-trial button but no trial length. Reviewers on Trustpilot mention a one-week trial and, in one case, a charge for a full year after forgetting to cancel. Those are individual accounts I did not verify, and the point is only to look at the terms first.
  5. Know how to cancel before you subscribe. The help article says to go to wanderlog.com/settings/pro and click Unsubscribe if you subscribed on the website, or to open your profile page and tap the three-dots button if you subscribed on a phone.

Price: what is clear and what is not

Wanderlog's Pro page shows one plan, Pro Annual, "starting at" $39.99 a year. The word "starting" matters, because the U.S. App Store listing shows ten Pro in-app purchases: annual entries at $31.99, $39.99 (twice), $49.99 (three times) and $59.99, a "20% discount" entry at $39.99, and monthly entries at $5.99 and $16.99. The listing does not say why they differ, so the price you see may not be $39.99. The Google Play listing shows no price, only that it has in-app purchases. Prices also vary by country.

On a different axis, the help center says Wanderlog earns a small percentage from bookings made through the app and from Pro subscriptions, and that it shows no ads. That is Wanderlog's own answer to how the free plan is funded.

I cannot say which is right, but they measure different things. Trustpilot labels the reviews I read as unprompted, and several of them concern billing and trials more than the planner itself, while the store scores come from a far larger sample. Both can be true at once: a well-liked free planner and a Pro subscription that annoys some buyers.

The only detailed free-plan complaint in the sources is a January 2026 App Store review. The reviewer loved the free version but reported lag or refusals to load once one trip held a lot of stops (they suggested staying under about 100), could only sort 30 trips by recently viewed or upcoming date, and could not copy stops between trips. That is one person's experience on one device, but it is a reason to keep very large trips split up. A March 2025 four-star review calls Pro worth it for its auto-sort option, with the caveat that it sorts one day at a time.

Limits to know before you rely on it

  • Offline is the unclear one. Three first-party sources say Pro; two say automatic. Offline trip planners for Android compares what other apps keep on the phone.
  • The help center is old. Its articles on free use, cancelling and Gmail are dated about three years back, so button names may have moved.
  • The sign-up flow can push a trial. One Trustpilot reviewer says the app forces a free trial and the browser version pushes you to the app. I did not test the sign-up flow, so check what you are offered before you travel.
  • Very big trips may slow down, per the single review above. I did not test it.

Who should skip Wanderlog, or pair it with something simpler

If you want one app for bookings, budget, shared editing and routes, Wanderlog is built for that; its free plan covers places, a map, collaboration and booking import, while route optimization is Pro. If your hard part is deciding what to see, you may want a lighter app for the choosing step, then Wanderlog for the plan. The short version

As of 13 November 2026, Wanderlog is worth using for its free plan, which Wanderlog says has no cap on trips or collaborators and includes places, a map and booking import. Pro, shown at $39.99 a year on its Pro page, is worth paying for only if you will use offline access, route optimization, Google Maps export, Gmail scanning or unlimited AI help. The price you are offered may differ, since the App Store listing shows ten Pro entries from $5.99 a month to $59.99 a year. Expect split reviews: 4.9 from 36,399 U.S. App Store ratings, 4.8 on Google Play, and 1.8 from 52 on Trustpilot.

Questions about whether Wanderlog is worth it

Is Wanderlog free?

Mostly, yes. Wanderlog's Pro page lists unlimited places, live collaboration with friends and flight, hotel and car import at $0. Its help center promises no cap on trips or on the people you plan with. Offline access, route optimization, Google Maps export and Gmail scanning are listed under Pro.

How much does Wanderlog Pro cost?

Wanderlog's Pro page shows Pro Annual starting at $39.99 a year with a free-trial button. The U.S. App Store listing shows ten Pro purchases instead: annual entries from $31.99 to $59.99 and monthly entries at $5.99 and $16.99. The listing does not say which one you will be offered, so read the price on your purchase screen.

Is Wanderlog Pro worth it?

Only if you will use something that sits behind it: offline access on mobile, route optimization, Google Maps export, automatic Gmail scanning or unlimited AI assistance. At $39.99 a year on its Pro page that is about $3.33 a month. If you only build lists, a map and shared itineraries, the free plan already has those.

Does Wanderlog work offline?

Its sources disagree. The Pro page, the help center and the Google Play feature list put offline access under Pro, while a paragraph of the Play description and the App Store description say plans are stored offline automatically. Open your trip on a connection and test it on your own phone before you travel.

Is Wanderlog legit and safe to pay for?

It is an established app: Apple shows 36,399 U.S. ratings averaging 4.9 and Google Play shows 1M+ downloads. Trustpilot is far harsher at 1.8 from 52 reviews, and some of those describe trials that turned into a charge. Read the trial terms, and the cancellation steps, before you start one.

What is a simpler alternative to Wanderlog?

It depends on the job. If you only want to choose places in a city and see them on a map, NomadSwipe (Android, which I built) is a narrower option, and Wanderlog alternatives compares four more. NomadSwipe is not a full planner like Wanderlog.
