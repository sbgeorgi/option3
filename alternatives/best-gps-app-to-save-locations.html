Best GPS App to Save Locations: How Six Apps Save and Export Places

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android). It is one row of six, and I say where it does not fit. Every other fact comes from a help page, FAQ or store listing I read on 16 November 2026. Published 16 November 2026. Updated 16 November 2026.

The best GPS app to save locations is one whose saved places you can also take back out. By their own help pages, Google Maps, Mapstr, Organic Maps and OsmAnd document an export, while the two Apple Maps pages I read describe sharing only. I built NomadSwipe (Android), a trip-planning companion, not turn-by-turn GPS.

Key facts

  • NomadSwipe, which I built, swipes real places into a saved list and shows them on a trip map. Its Play listing describes no export, and it does not navigate turn by turn.
  • Saving a pin is the easy half. The half that decides which app you can leave later is export, and the six apps differ on it.
  • Google Maps' help page says saved lists are limited to 3,000 entries. Mapstr's FAQ says its base app holds up to 300 addresses and its paid tier lifts that.
  • Counting the steps Google's help pages list, saving one place in Google Maps on Android takes 4 and exporting all your lists takes 5. Mapstr's FAQ also lists 5 steps to export.
  • Organic Maps and OsmAnd both list GPX export, a format Organic Maps says it also imports from third-party apps. Organic Maps adds KMZ and GeoJSON; OsmAnd adds a full backup file.

What "GPS app" means on this page

The phrase covers three different jobs. A navigation app guides you along a route. A tracking app shows where a person or vehicle is. A place-saving app keeps a point you marked so you can find it again, in a list or on a map. The search results I looked at on 16 November were mostly navigation and location-tracking roundups, so if you want route guidance or to follow a person, this page is the wrong one. Here I compare only the third job: dropping or finding a place, keeping it, and being able to move the list somewhere else. I did not compare any app's navigation.

Walk-through: I followed two help pages step by step

Here is the walk-through I did on 16 November: I took the Google Maps help page for Android and the Mapstr FAQ, followed each page's instructions in order, and counted every action it lists. I did not run either app, so this tests how clear the documentation is, not how fast the app is. The two pages cover the same three jobs, which makes the counts comparable.

Google Maps on Android: save one place

  1. Open the Google Maps app.
  2. Search for a place, tap a marker or touch and hold a spot on the map.
  3. At the bottom, tap the name or address of the place.
  4. Tap Save and choose a list.

That is 4 steps, from the Save favorite places page. The same page says only you can find saved places unless you share a list, and lists touching and holding a spot on the map as one way to start, so you do not need a business listing to drop a pin.

Google Maps: get the whole list out

  1. Go to takeout.google.com.
  2. Make sure "Saved" is checked in "Create A New Export".
  3. Click Next Step.
  4. Select a destination, frequency and file format.
  5. Click Create Export.

That is 5 steps, from the Create a list of places page. Sharing one list inside the app is shorter than the export: open the app, tap You, open the list, tap Share, which is 4. The same page lists a computer version of each task, and on a computer the list-saving steps are 3 (search or click a place, click Save, choose a list).

Mapstr: export, and bring in a Google Maps list

  1. Open the Profile tab.
  2. Tap the settings icon at the top right.
  3. Scroll to the "Manage your data" section.
  4. Press the Export your data button.
  5. Receive the CSV and GeoJSON files at the email address on your account.

That is 5 steps, from the Mapstr FAQ, which adds that the file holds your addresses, tags and notes. Going the other way, the same FAQ says that in Google Maps you open a list, tap Share and pick Mapstr, and Mapstr detects the places and lets you add them. To add a single place, the FAQ lists tapping the plus button and choosing a search mode (name, address or coordinates); it does not number the steps after that, so I cannot give a total.

Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

For choosing places to save, not for driving there

I built NomadSwipe, so weigh this with that in mind. It is an Android trip planner that works in any city worldwide. You pick a city and it deals real places, one card at a time: swipe right or press Save to keep one, swipe left or press Skip to pass. The Play listing says each card shows the category, the rating, the distance and, where hours are known, whether the place is open. Every saved place is pinned on a travel map inside a trip, and you can sort the list by rating, distance or A to Z. Custom pins cover anything the cards do not show, such as your hotel.

For a query about GPS apps, the limits matter most. NomadSwipe is not a navigator. The listing says you can open a place and start directions, which I did not test, and I would not use it in place of a navigation app. Its listing describes no export and no import, so I cannot say your list could leave. It also lists limits that Premium removes: 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip a month, 2 custom pins a month and a daily limit on saves. The apps, one at a time

Google Maps: the default, with a cap to know about

The Play listing describes custom lists of favorite saved places that you can share with others. The help pages add that lists can be private or shared by link, that you can invite others to edit a shared list, that you can follow a list someone else made, and that Favorites, Want to go, Travel plans, Starred places and Saved places are default lists whose icons you cannot change. Saved lists are limited to 3,000 entries, and the page says a longer list may not display properly. If you want the detail on that limit and the lists, see Google Maps list limits and alternatives.

Apple Maps: Places, notes and guides

The two Apple pages I read are from the iPhone User Guide. They describe a Places area where pins, places, guides and routes sit together, personal notes that only you can see, and a pin option for places you visit often. A guide called Favorites always exists, you can create your own guides, and you can share a guide. Adding a place to a guide is 4 listed steps; sharing a guide is 3. Neither page mentions a file export or a size limit, so I cannot say whether either exists.

Organic Maps: offline maps, bookmarks as files

Its home page describes an offline maps app with GPS navigation and track recording, powered by OpenStreetMap data, and lists import, export and sharing of bookmarks and tracks in KML/KMZ, GPX and GeoJSON, plus importing Google Maps saved places from the Saved Places.json file in a Google Takeout export. The export entry says you can share a single bookmark with the Share button, or export a whole list as KMZ, GPX or GeoJSON from the three-dot menu on the Bookmarks and Tracks screen. Batch import is a button on the same screen. The Play listing showed 5M+ downloads and 4.1 from 24.4K reviews on 16 November. If you are weighing it against other offline map apps, see Organic Maps alternatives.

OsmAnd: favorites, folders and a GPX file

OsmAnd's Favorites page calls them bookmarks on the map, shown as yellow stars by default. You add one by short tapping a point of interest or long tapping a general map point and choosing the star, or by long tapping any location. Each has a name, optional address and description, and a folder, with nested folders on Android, and on Android you can attach photos, video or audio. The page says sharing a folder exports a Favorites GPX file, and that the full backup format also restores attached media. Its import and export page lists GPX, KML and KMZ as favorite import types, with KML and KMZ converted to GPX. It is the most configurable of the six, which also means more screens to learn.

Mapstr: a place memory with tags

The FAQ describes Mapstr as a map of the addresses that matter to you, available on iOS, Android and in a browser. You can save a restaurant, a bench or a plain point, organize with tags, notes, photos and a to try or already tested status, and choose what is private. Offline, the FAQ says your map stays viewable but adding a place needs a connection. Its Play listing shows 500K+ downloads and 4.0 from 4.45K reviews. For a longer head to head, see Mapstr vs Google Maps.

Who should pick which

  • You are planning one trip and have no list yet: NomadSwipe to choose places by swiping and see them on a map, next to a navigation app.
  • You collect places for years and want an exit: Google Maps or Mapstr, each with a documented 5-step export, keeping their 3,000 and 300 limits in mind.
  • You want your pins as a file in a standard format: Organic Maps or OsmAnd, which list GPX export.
  • You already live in Apple's ecosystem: Apple Maps guides, but check export yourself, because the pages I read do not describe one.
  • You need to share a list with other people: Google Maps lists, which the help page says can be shared by link.

FAQ

What is the best GPS app to save locations?

It depends on whether you need to export the list. By their own help pages, Google Maps, Mapstr, Organic Maps and OsmAnd each document an export, while the Apple Maps pages I read describe sharing a guide but no file export. I built NomadSwipe (Android), which saves places you swipe on but is a trip planner, not a navigation app.

Can I export my saved places from Google Maps?

Yes. Google's help page lists 5 steps: go to takeout.google.com, make sure Saved is checked, continue, choose a destination, frequency and file format, then create the export. I counted the steps from the page and did not run an export.

How many places can I save in Google Maps or Mapstr?

Google Maps' help page says saved lists are limited to 3,000 entries. The Mapstr FAQ says the base app holds up to 300 addresses and Mapstr plus lifts the cap. The Apple Maps, Organic Maps and OsmAnd pages I read state no limit.

Is NomadSwipe a GPS navigation app?

No. I built NomadSwipe, an Android app that works in any city worldwide: you swipe real places, save the ones you like, see them on a map and build a trip. Its listing says you can open a place and start directions, but I did not test that and it does not replace a navigation app.
