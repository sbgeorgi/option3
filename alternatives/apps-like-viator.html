Apps Like Viator: 5 Tour Booking Apps Compared, and Where NomadSwipe Fits

Key facts

  • Viator's Google Play listing describes an app for booking tours and attractions, with 300,000+ experiences, wishlists, tickets you can open offline, and reviews drawn from Tripadvisor and Viator travelers.
  • The five alternatives all sell bookings too. Tiqets leans toward museum and attraction tickets, Civitatis toward guided visits and day trips, Klook toward activities plus transport, and Airbnb toward experiences run by local hosts.

What Viator is and what it does

On Google Play the app is called Viator Tours & Attractions. Its description calls it an all-in-one vacation planner, itinerary tool and activity guide, and says you can browse tours and activities before and during your trip, open tickets offline, modify or cancel bookings, get same-day notifications with directions to departure and pickup points, contact tour guides directly and keep wishlists of what you want to book. It lists payment by card, PayPal or Apple Pay, reserve now and pay later, installments with Klarna, and a rewards scheme. The listing shows 4.9 from 85.4K reviews, 5M+ downloads, an Editors' Choice badge and an update on 6 November 2026.

Its Data safety section says no data is shared with third parties and that the app may collect location, personal info and five other types. I could not load viator.com itself in my signed-out headless browser, so everything about Viator on this page comes from that Play listing and from Viator's partner help article on cancellation policies, which is written for travel advisors, was last updated on 10 February 2022 and states the rule plainly: each experience has its own cancellation policy, shown in a section on the product page.

Why people look for an app similar to Viator

I did not survey reviews or forums, so I do not say what Viator's users complain about. What I can show is where the pages leave questions, which is usually why a comparison like this one gets opened:

  • One catalogue, one set of terms. A marketplace shows what it sells in your destination. If your city or the specific ticket you want is not there, another marketplace may carry it. I did not test any destination, so I do not say which app covers which city.
  • Cancellation terms vary by listing. Viator's Play listing says free cancellation up to 24 hours in advance, while its help page says each experience has a specific policy. That gap is the reason the table below quotes wording and the next section lists the differences.
  • Different declared data practices. Google Play shows what each developer says it shares. The five apps differ, and I list them below.
  • Different shapes of product. A skip-the-line museum ticket, a food tour and a home-hosted workshop are not the same purchase, and the apps lean toward different ones.
  • Booking is not planning. A catalogue answers "what can I buy here". The pages I read describe wishlists and, for some apps, itineraries, but not a swipe-by-swipe way to decide which places you want or a map of them.

GetYourGuide: the closest like-for-like

The Play listing pitches the same job as Viator: book tours, day trips and activities, with instant confirmation, reserve now and pay later, tickets you can open offline and customer service by email, phone and WhatsApp. It says over 100,000 experiences and names the Colosseum, Eiffel Tower and Sagrada Familia among the tickets. The GetYourGuide website repeats the cancellation line as "free cancellations on most activities" and shows individual tours with a rating, a review count and a "likely to sell out" tag on some. Its Data safety entry says personal info and device or other IDs may be shared with third parties.

Klook: activities plus transport

Klook's Play listing is the broadest in scope: activities, hotels, car rentals, day tours, and trains, buses and ferries to get around, with rewards tiers and a wishlist. That breadth is the difference from Viator, which describes tours and tickets. Its cancellation wording is the most structured I found: the help article on cancelling a booking says to check the activity's own policy, then describes three cases (free cancellation you can do yourself in the stated window, a conditional policy that needs customer support, and no cancellation or refund).

Tiqets: when what you want is a museum ticket

The Tiqets listing reads narrower: attractions in over 500 destinations, with guided tours, skip-the-line tickets, boat tours and city audio guides, and tickets stored offline. The Tiqets website front page is built around venues such as Sagrada Familia, Park Güell and the Vatican Museums, and carries the lines "Flexible cancellation options on all venues" and "Hassle-free cancellations and rescheduling". Neither line says what the flexibility is, so you still read the terms of the ticket you pick.

Civitatis: guided visits and day trips

Civitatis's listing says over 90,000 activities, founded in 2008, with guided tours, day trips, bar crawls, skip-the-line tickets, airport transfers and "Free Tours and guided visits". The listing text also says you can save favorite ideas and build an itinerary in "My Trips", and get personalised suggestions. On its Rome page I found a "Free cancellation" filter showing 80 matches, next to a home page tile that gives 103 activities for Rome; I did not check whether the two numbers count the same set. Its Data safety entry says location and personal info may be shared with third parties.

Airbnb Experiences: hosted by locals, a different model

The Airbnb listing describes experiences hosted by locals who are vetted on expertise, reputation and authenticity: cooking classes, food tours, workshops, plus "Airbnb Originals". It is an apps-like-Viator option only if you want that style of activity; most of the listing is about booking homes. For problems, the Refund Policy for Services and Experiences (effective 13 May 2025) says a guest automatically gets a refund when a host cancels, and that a host more than 15 minutes late or a delivered experience that differs substantially from the advert are covered issues. It also says this policy takes precedence over the reservation's own cancellation policy. What a guest can do before the day is not in that article, so I make no claim about it.

Where the pages differ

Reading the same facts across seven pages turned up mismatches worth knowing before you rely on any single line.

  1. Viator's headline versus its policy. The Play bullet says free cancellation up to 24 hours in advance. The help page says each experience has a specific cancellation policy on its product page. I read the bullet as typical, not guaranteed.
  2. "Most" versus "all". GetYourGuide says most bookings and most activities; Tiqets says options on all venues without saying what they are; Klook says it depends on the activity. Three apps, three different promises.
  3. Review counts inside one page. Klook's header showed 150K reviews and the ratings block lower on the same Play page showed 148K. I quote the header.
  4. Tiqets reach. Its Play page shows 1M+ downloads; its website says downloaded by over 5 million travellers. The site figure is presumably all platforms, but the page does not say.
  5. Civitatis counts. The Rome filter count (80) and the home tile count (103) are different numbers for what may be the same city catalogue.

Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

For deciding what is worth booking, not for booking it

I built NomadSwipe (Android), so weigh this with that in mind. It is not a booking marketplace. You cannot buy a ticket, a tour or a seat in it, there is no cancellation policy to read, and I make no claim that it connects to Viator or any other app. It is a place-discovery and trip-building app.

What it does: pick a destination and it deals real restaurants, sights, bars and stays one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep one, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. How I would combine a booking app with a map plan

This is a method, not a trip report: I have not booked anything through these apps for this page, and nothing here depends on the two kinds of app talking to each other. They do not.

  1. Pick the destination and the number of days first. Everything else is sized by that. A weekend has room for a handful of anchors, not a catalogue.
  2. Swipe before you shop. In NomadSwipe, go through the cards for the city and save only what you would walk across town for. The listing's sample trip, a long weekend in New York, has 16 places, all picked by swiping.
  3. Open the trip map. Look at where the saved places sit. Clusters become days; a lone pin on the far side is a decision to make now, not on the morning.
  4. Mark which picks need a ticket. Most restaurants, parks and streets do not. Museums, towers and timed-entry sights may. Check each venue's own page for its entry rules rather than assuming.
  5. Only then open a booking app. Search for just those few places or a tour that covers a cluster you already like. Compare two apps if a place appears in both, and read the cancellation section on the product page, not only the headline line quoted in the table above.
  6. Put the booked slot back on the map. Use a custom pin, which the listing describes as for anything the cards do not show, to mark the entrance or meeting point. The booking app will hold the voucher; your map holds the day.
  7. Leave gaps. A day with two fixed bookings and a saved list of nearby places is easier to live with than a day with six confirmations.

Who should pick which

  • You want the biggest catalogue and the closest match to Viator: GetYourGuide, going by its listing's scope and its 4.9 from 385K Play reviews, with the usual caveat about comparing ratings.
  • You want activities and transport in one app: Klook, the only listing here that also names trains, buses, ferries and car rentals.
  • You want a museum or landmark ticket and little else: Tiqets.
  • You want guided visits and day trips, with a saved-ideas itinerary: Civitatis, per its listing.
  • You want a workshop or class run by a local host: Airbnb Experiences.
  • The least data declared as shared on Google Play: Viator, Klook and Tiqets each declare none shared with third parties; check each app's current Data safety page.
  • You are still deciding what to see in a city: NomadSwipe (Android), then a booking app for the few things that need a ticket.

FAQ

What is Viator and what apps are like it?

Viator is a booking marketplace for tours, tickets and activities. Its Google Play listing describes travel experiences you can book, wishlist and manage in one app. Apps that do the same job are GetYourGuide, Klook, Tiqets, Civitatis and, for hosted experiences, Airbnb.

Do apps like Viator all offer free cancellation?

Not in the same words. Viator's listing says free cancellation up to 24 hours in advance, but its help page says each experience has its own policy. GetYourGuide says most bookings, Tiqets says flexible options on all venues, and Klook's help page describes free, conditional and no-cancellation activities. Read the policy on the product page before paying.

Is NomadSwipe an app like Viator?

No. I built NomadSwipe (Android) and it is not a booking marketplace: you cannot buy tickets or tours in it. It is for deciding which real places in a city are worth your time, by swiping them, saving the ones you like and seeing them on a trip map. iPhone is coming soon.

Can I use a booking app and NomadSwipe together?

Yes, as two separate tools. I built NomadSwipe (Android) to pick and map the places you want, and a booking app is where you would buy a ticket for the few that need one. The two do not exchange data, so the link between them is you, a shortlist and a map.
