Worldpackers

Best for: the nearest like-for-like swap for Workaway

Worldpackers (https://www.worldpackers.com/how_it_works) says you help your host for some hours per week and get accommodation and possibly meals and local activities. Hosts include social projects, NGOs, ecovillages, hostels and communities in more than 140 countries. Its plan page (https://www.worldpackers.com/get_verified) lists Solo Trips at US$59 for one year, Solo Pack at US$109 and Solo Pack Plus at US$139. The plans differ mainly in what its WP Safeguard repays for emergency accommodation if an experience does not go as agreed: up to US$59, US$199 or US$399. It also offers a refund if no host replies within the first 30 days, and says 97 percent of members who send 3 applications get replies.

Its home page quotes two different review totals (more than 530,000 in one place and 80,000 in another), so I give neither. At the base US$59 tier the price matches Workaway's list price, so the choice comes down to which site has hosts where you are going and which refund promise you prefer. Workaway's own promise, from its sign-up and how-it-works pages, is a refund guarantee if you get no response, and it says 99.7 percent of Workawayers who send 5 or more applications in the first 30 days get responses.

  • Strength: a written refund promise and a safeguard payout that grows with the plan.
  • Limit: the safeguard and support extras sit in the pricier plans; US$59 is the entry tier only.

WWOOF

Best for: learning organic farming, one country at a time

WWOOF's home page (https://wwoof.net/) says visitors take part in daily life with hosts and receive educational opportunities, meals and accommodation, with no money exchanged between hosts and WWOOFers. WWOOF USA's page says visitors spend about half of each day helping on a farm and that WWOOF is in more than 132 countries. The catch for Workaway users is the structure: each national WWOOF organisation is a separate entity, there is no international membership, and you buy a membership for every country with a national group. WWOOF Independents covers hosts in 80+ countries that have no national organisation, with fewer hosts per country.

WWOOF's help centre (https://help.wwoof.net/hc/en-us/articles/16654242038941-What-does-a-WWOOFer-membership-cost) lists one-year memberships such as 20 EUR for Portugal and Spain, 25 GBP for the UK, 45 USD for the USA, 59 CAD for Canada, 145 BRL for Brazil and 35 EUR for Independents, with dual memberships for two people at a higher price. Its refund policy grants a refund within 14 days of registering if you have not contacted any host; after that it is case by case, and WWOOF Italy cannot refund by law.

  • Strength: a clear focus on farms, with host approval steps and public feedback described on its how-it-works page.
  • Limit: a trip through three countries means three memberships.

HelpX

Best for: a low price and the long-running farm and hostel exchange

HelpX (https://www.helpx.net/) describes an online listing of organic farms, farmstays, hobby farms, homestays, ranches, lodges, hostels and sailing boats that invite helpers for short stays in exchange for food and accommodation. It says the typical arrangement is an average of 4 hours a day for free accommodation and meals, with some hosts asking for 2 hours for a bed only and others 6 hours with meals and extras. Helpers pick a Free or Premier membership and must upgrade to Premier to take full advantage: it costs 20 euros for 2 years and lets you contact all hosts and read all host reviews. The page names Australia, New Zealand, Canada and Europe including France, and does not state a host count, so I make no size claim.

  • Strength: the lowest paid price in this list, stated plainly: 20 euros for 2 years.
  • Limit: the page I read was still loading after 15 seconds and shows no refund or response guarantee, so I list none.

HelpStay

Best for: hostel, teaching and project stays, with the price shown up front

HelpStay (https://helpstay.com/) says it lists 2,500+ volunteer stays in 100+ countries, grouped under animals and environment, backpacker hostels, building and restoration, farming, community, teaching, internships and more. Its FAQ (https://helpstay.com/journal/faqs/) gives a yearly membership fee of either 42.99 euros or 51.99 euros; it does not say on that page what separates the two. One point worth knowing before you apply: the listings on its home page show some stays as Free and others as costing from 10 or 14 euros a day, so not every stay is a pure trade of work for a bed. HippoHelp (hippohelp.com), which links to HelpStay listings, says HelpStay is a work exchange platform connecting hosts with travellers who help for food and accommodation.

  • Strength: each listing states its cost, and some are free.
  • Limit: a paid stay is not the Workaway idea; filter for the free ones.

Nomador

Best for: pet and house sitting in cities

Nomador (https://www.nomador.com/) says travellers look after pets in exchange for a place to stay, with no money involved between them. It claims 50,000+ stays since 2015, 100+ countries and 3,000+ stays available, and its examples are apartments and houses in Berlin, Barcelona, Vienna, Montreal, Madrid and Buenos Aires. Its price page (https://www.nomador.com/prices) lists Discovery at 89 euros a year or 34 euros for 3 months, Standard at 149 euros and Premium at 189 euros, with the same price whether you are a sitter or a pet owner. The two upper plans add 7-day support and cancellation cover.

  • Strength: city stays in an apartment rather than a farm, and a 3-month option for a short trip.
  • Limit: you need to be comfortable with animals, and the work is care, not general help.

TrustedHousesitters

Best for: the biggest pet-sitting community

TrustedHousesitters (https://www.trustedhousesitters.com/) says sitters exchange their time and care for homestays and describes 16 years and 280k+ happy members. Its pricing page (https://www.trustedhousesitters.com/pricing/) shows sitter plans of US$129 a year (Basic), US$169 (Standard) and US$259 (Premium); Basic and Standard add US$12 per sit, and Premium has no booking fees. Standard and Premium include an accident and third party liability plan and free vet calls while sitting.

  • Strength: liability cover and vet calls are listed in the plans.
  • Limit: the cheapest sitter plan costs about double Workaway's, and per-sit fees come on top.

Trustroots

Best for: free stays with locals when you do not want a work schedule

Trustroots (https://www.trustroots.org/) is a different model. It describes travellers finding people who want to host them or meet them, says it is over 140,000 members strong, and states it is completely free to use and will remain so. It is run by the Trustroots Foundation, a non-profit registered in the United Kingdom since March 2015. There is no work trade, so it suits a night or two rather than a placement of several weeks.

  • Strength: no fee and no work expectation.
  • Limit: not a work exchange; it offers no work-for-a-stay arrangement.

Which one to pick

  • You want the same general mix as Workaway: Worldpackers, at the same US$59 entry price.
  • You want to farm and learn: WWOOF in one country, or HelpX if you want the lowest price and are happy with fewer written guarantees.
  • You want hostel or project work: HelpStay, checking each listing for a daily fee.
  • You love animals: Nomador or TrustedHousesitters; compare the per-sit fee against the annual price.
  • You want a bed with no work: Trustroots.
  • You want to plan what to do around the host you picked: NomadSwipe, then use the host site for the exchange itself. How to find things to do in a new city and how to save places for a trip cover the planning step.

What to check before you pay for any of them

Four things decide whether a membership is worth it, and every site above states them differently. First, the fee period: Workaway and Worldpackers sell one year, HelpX two years, Nomador also offers 3 months. Second, the number of memberships: WWOOF needs one per country. Third, the refund route: WWOOF refunds within 14 days only if you contacted no host, while Worldpackers and Workaway describe refunds if no host answers. Fourth, what is expected of you: HelpX states about 4 hours a day and WWOOF about half a day, while Workaway and Worldpackers leave the details to each host, so agree hours, days off and meals in writing before you travel.

FAQ

What is an app similar to Workaway?

The closest are Worldpackers (US$59 for a year, hosts in 140+ countries) and HelpStay (a yearly fee of EUR 42.99 or EUR 51.99, 2,500+ stays in 100+ countries). For farm stays only, WWOOF; for farms, farmstays and hostels with a lower price, HelpX (EUR 20 for 2 years). Prices were read on 27 November 2026.

How much does Workaway cost compared with its alternatives?

Workaway's sign-up page showed US$59 a year for one person and US$69 for two on 27 November 2026, with a Travel Tuesday code that ends on 1 December. Worldpackers starts at US$59 a year. HelpX Premier is EUR 20 for 2 years. WWOOF charges a separate fee per country, for example EUR 20 in Portugal or USD 45 in the USA.

Is there a Workaway alternative that is free?

Trustroots is free to use, but it is a hospitality network, not a work exchange. HelpX has a Free level, but its own site says helpers must upgrade to Premier to contact all hosts and read all host reviews. The work-exchange sites I read (Workaway, Worldpackers, WWOOF, HelpStay) all charge helpers a membership to contact hosts.

Is NomadSwipe an alternative to Workaway?

No. I built NomadSwipe, an Android app for choosing real places in a city by swiping, saving them and seeing them on one map. It does not list hosts, volunteering or work exchanges, so it only helps you plan what to do around the host you picked elsewhere.

Which alternative suits house and pet sitting?

Nomador and TrustedHousesitters are built for it: you look after pets, and sometimes a home, in exchange for a place to stay. Workaway's home page also lists house and pet sitting among its exchange types.
