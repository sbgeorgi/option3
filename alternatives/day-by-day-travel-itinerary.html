Day by day travel itinerary: how to build one in 5 steps

A day by day travel itinerary is one table with a row per day and three blocks in each row (morning, afternoon, evening), so a 3-day trip has 9 blocks and each block gets one main stop. What a day by day travel itinerary looks like

It is a grid, not an essay. Each row is a day, each cell is a block, and each block holds one anchor: the single place that block is built around. Beside the anchor you write its opening hours, a hop time from the previous block, and up to two optional places you drop first if a queue eats an hour. The finished sample for Florence is in the next section.

The first morning and the last afternoon are usually shortened by travel, so I treat them as the two blocks I am willing to lose. That leaves 7 solid blocks on a 3-day trip.

A filled 3-day sample: Florence, Tuesday to Thursday

I picked Florence because its big museums publish daily hours and two of them close on the same weekday. I chose Tuesday to Thursday on purpose: it keeps the Monday closures out of the trip. How to build a day by day travel itinerary in 5 steps

  1. Fix the frame.

    Write your dates, the weekdays they fall on, when you arrive on day 1 and when you must leave on the last day. A trip is the number of real blocks, not the number of days: 3 days are 9 blocks, minus whatever travel takes.

  2. List more candidates than you need.

    For 3 days I list 12 to 15 places, about 5 more than there are blocks. That is my own rule of thumb, not a standard: it gives you room to drop places without starting over. For how many stops a day really holds, see how many attractions per day is realistic.

  3. Write opening days and hours next to every name.

    Look for the weekday the place is closed, the last entry and any timed ticket. This step is what the Florence table above got right: without it, a Monday would have put two museums behind locked doors.

  4. Group by area, then put the hardest constraint first.

    One day is one connected stretch of the city. Place the earliest closing time or the timed ticket in the first block that fits, and let everything else bend around it. In Florence that was the Cathedral at 15.45.

  5. Fill the blocks and write one swap.

    One anchor per block, up to two optional places, and a 20 to 30 minute buffer after each block. Book only what has a timed ticket or a sell-out warning, and leave the rest open. Add one row for bad weather or a closed day, as the Monday row does above.

Questions

How many days should a day by day travel itinerary cover?

As many as the trip has, one row each. The grid works the same for 2, 3 or 7 days. Past about a week I would split it into one table per city or base, so each table stays short enough to read on a phone.

Should every block be filled?

No. Fill each block with one anchor and leave at least two or three blocks open. Open blocks are what you move a closed museum or a rainy afternoon into.

What is the first thing to check on a 3-day city itinerary?

Closing days. In the Florence sample, the Uffizi and the Accademia are both closed every Monday, so the weekday your trip starts changes the whole order.

Bottom line (26 November 2026)

  • A day by day travel itinerary is a grid: one row per day, morning, afternoon and evening blocks, one anchor in each.
  • Build it in 5 steps: fix the frame, list more candidates than blocks, write opening days beside every name, group by area with the hardest constraint first, then fill the blocks and add one swap row.
  • Check closing days first. In the Florence sample the Uffizi and the Accademia close every Monday, and the Cathedral closes at 15.45 on the date I read.
  • A spreadsheet, paper or a Google Maps list is enough to hold the table. An app is useful for choosing the places and seeing them on a map.
