What Travefy costs, and how long the free trial lasts

On Travefy's pricing page, one advisor pays $39 a month billed annually, which the page itself prices at $468 a year, or $49 a month on monthly billing. Each extra advisor is $20 a month on annual billing and $35 on monthly. The page's own example is 5 advisors at $119 a month billed annually. Agencies with 10 or more advisors get a quote. Prices are in US dollars.

The free trial is 10 days, includes every feature and needs no credit card, so nothing is charged when it ends, per the same page. The page describes a single plan with no features held back for a higher tier, and says its AI features, such as Smart Import, are included with no credits to buy.

One thing does not line up. An independent review on SaaS CRM Review, updated 28 October 2026, describes a Core plan at $39 and a Premium plan at $59 a month billed annually, and says Travefy printed no monthly price. The pricing page I read today shows one plan and a $49 monthly rate. Travefy's home page also names a Travefy Premium offer and a New Travel Agent Program at $25 a month that adds custom domain and email hosting. Plans and prices clearly move, so use the live pricing page, not any review, for the number you pay.

What you get for the money

Travefy's pricing page lists these as included for every advisor: itineraries and proposals, Smart Import (AI), 200+ supplier integrations, a CRM dashboard, contact management, automated workflows, a client mobile app, a website and landing page builder, commission tracking, booking management, custom forms, credit card authorization, branded invoices, tasks and document storage. It marks travel insurance and marketing campaigns as coming soon.

The page frames the price as a replacement for separate tools. Its cost calculator lists CRM, itinerary and proposal software, forms, a website builder and invoicing as the tools Travefy replaces, and says most advisors pay for four or five of them. Its sample shows $1,200 a year for three tools against $468 for Travefy, using list prices Travefy chose, so treat it as a sales illustration, not a measurement of your costs.

So the money question is simple. If you would otherwise pay for a CRM, an itinerary builder and a forms tool, $39 a month can replace them. If you need only one of those, it probably cannot.

Is Travefy free? Not any more

The only free route today is the 10-day trial. A PCMag review dated 25 February 2015 describes Travefy as a free online planner with expense splitting and a map, aimed at friends planning a trip together. That is a different product from the advisor platform on Travefy's site now, so do not read old reviews as a description of today's tool.

Travefy app for Android

Travefy's Trip Plans page says its client app is free for travelers on iOS and Android. An advisor builds the trip, emails it, and the client installs the app, creates a free account and sees the itinerary, documents and live flight updates, with offline access. The page shows your logo, not Travefy's. It also lists a separate Travefy Pro app for advisors, and the text I read does not name its platforms, so I cannot say whether it runs on Android.

Travefy alternatives and Travefy vs TravelJoy

Travefy's pricing page names TravelJoy, together with HoneyBook and Dubsado, as CRM tools that Travefy says it replaces, so for advisors the closest overlap is the CRM side. Decide in 3 minutes

  1. Do you sell trips to clients? If not, stop here. Travefy is built for advisors and shows no free plan.
  2. Count your advisors. One is $39 a month billed annually, or $468 a year. Five is $119 a month on Travefy's own example. Ten or more means a quote.
  3. Can you commit annually? Monthly billing is $49 a month for the first advisor, so annual saves $10 a month.
  4. List the tools you would cancel. Name your current CRM, itinerary builder, forms and invoicing tools and add up what you pay. If Travefy replaces fewer than two, the saving is thin.
  5. Use the 10 days on one real trip. Build one real client itinerary, send it, open it in Trip Plans on a phone and issue one invoice. No card is needed, so the test costs nothing.

Bottom line

As of 26 November 2026, Travefy is worth it for a travel advisor who would otherwise pay for a CRM, an itinerary builder and forms, at $39 a month billed annually after a 10-day trial. It is not worth it for a traveler planning their own trip, because it is advisor software with no free plan. Check the live pricing page first, since one review shows different plan names from the page I read.

Questions about whether Travefy is worth it

Is Travefy free?

No. Travefy's pricing page offers a 10-day free trial with every feature and no credit card, then $39 a month for one advisor billed annually. It shows no free plan. A PCMag review dated February 2015 called Travefy a free planning tool, but that describes an older consumer product, not today's advisor software.

How long is the Travefy free trial?

10 days. Travefy's pricing page says the trial includes every feature and needs no credit card, so nothing is charged when it ends.

Is there a Travefy app for Android?

Travefy's Trip Plans app, which clients use to open the itinerary an advisor built, is free for travelers on iOS and Android, according to Travefy's Trip Plans page. That page also mentions a separate Travefy Pro app for advisors, and the page text I read does not say which platforms it covers.

What is a Travefy alternative for a traveler who is not an advisor?

Travefy is built for travel advisors, so an ordinary traveler is better served by a trip planner app. If you only want to choose places and see them on a map, NomadSwipe (Android, which I built) does that one job. It does not build itineraries with AI.
