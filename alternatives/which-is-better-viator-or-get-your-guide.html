Pick Viator if, pick GetYourGuide if

Pick GetYourGuide if

  • You want a price promise in writing. Section 7 of its terms sets out how you claim the difference, and what is excluded.
  • You want the cancellation, payment and refund mechanics spelled out. The terms give the order of precedence for the 24-hour rule, how a refund is paid (original payment method or a refund gift card) and the exact reserve-now-pay-later timing.
  • You want to read how results are ranked. Section 3.3 says ranking uses popularity, ratings and revenue, including commission suppliers pay.

Pick Viator if

  • You value reviews you can cross-check: its Play listing says you can read reviews from Tripadvisor and Viator travellers, and its partner documentation describes a booker-only review rule for Viator's own reviews.
  • You want the larger catalogue by its own count: 300,000+ experiences on its listing, against 200,000+ on GetYourGuide's listing and 150,000 on its about page.
  • You like to pay in other ways: the listing names Klarna instalments, PayPal and Apple Pay, plus a rewards scheme. the GetYourGuide listing text I read names none of these.

If you care about the price-match promise, note that I could only read one side of it. That favours GetYourGuide on evidence, not necessarily on terms.

Where they are the same, and where it decides the choice

The 24-hour rule is a tie. The default on each side is the same, so it should not be what you pick on. What differs is how much of the catalogue the default covers. Viator's partner documentation gives numbers, so you can see that a minority of its tours sit outside it. GetYourGuide's pages say "most", with no share. That is a gap in what I could read, not proof that its exceptions are fewer or more.

The price promise is the one written difference. GetYourGuide wrote down a mechanism, with conditions that matter: it must be the same activity, with the same supplier, on the same date and for the same number of people. That narrows it a lot, because a cheaper offer that is a different tour or supplier is not covered. It is still more than I could find on the Viator side.

Reviews are built differently. A booker-only rule means each Viator review comes from someone who paid through Viator, but the Tripadvisor reviews on the same page do not carry that rule. GetYourGuide says reviews are of purchased activities and bans supplier-written ones. Neither page lets me say one set of stars is more honest than the other, and I did not measure review quality.

The seller answer matters when something goes wrong. GetYourGuide's terms say the contract is with the supplier, while GetYourGuide is your point of contact for the booking and payments (sections 3.1 and 8.4). Before you rely on either company to fix a problem, read the "who you buy from" row and the cancellation line on the tour page.

A five-minute check before you pay either one

  1. Open the tour page and find the cancellation line. It overrides the default on both sites, so an "all sales final" tour is a different decision from a standard one.
  2. Note the exact start time and time zone. Viator counts back 24 hours in local supplier time; GetYourGuide's terms do not say which time zone, so check the tour page.
  3. Look at the tour's supplier and reviews, then search for the same tour name once on the other site, and compare the date, group size and inclusions before the price.
  4. Choose how you will hold the ticket. If you need it offline, check whether it is a mobile voucher or a PDF.
  5. Save the confirmation email. On GetYourGuide the terms say the confirmation is your proof of a cancellation.

Questions about Viator and GetYourGuide

Which is better, Viator or GetYourGuide?

Neither is better for every trip. Both default to a full refund until 24 hours before the start, so the decider is the detail: GetYourGuide publishes a price-match promise in its terms, while Viator shows Tripadvisor reviews and lists more experiences by its own count. Read the cancellation line on the specific tour either way.

Is the cancellation policy different on Viator and GetYourGuide?

The default is the same: full refund more than 24 hours before the start, nothing after. The difference is how each says it. Viator's partner documentation splits tours into standard, custom and all sales final, while GetYourGuide's terms say its 24-hour rule applies unless the activity page, supplier terms or voucher say otherwise.

Does Viator or GetYourGuide match a lower price?

GetYourGuide's terms (version October 2025) promise to pay the difference if you find the same activity, with the same supplier and conditions, cheaper elsewhere, with exclusions for loyalty, corporate and group rates. I could not read any equivalent promise on Viator, because viator.com blocked every automated reader I tried, so I make no claim either way.

Are Viator and GetYourGuide reviews verified?

Both say they restrict who can review. Viator's partner documentation says only the person who booked through Viator can review there, and that Tripadvisor reviews shown on Viator need no booking. GetYourGuide's terms say reviews are of activities you purchased and ban supplier-written and paid reviews; its about page calls its reviews verified but I did not find the method.

Can I use tickets offline in the Viator and GetYourGuide apps?

Both Google Play listings, read on 25 November 2026, say you can open tickets offline. Whether a given tour sends a mobile ticket, a PDF or a voucher to print depends on the tour. GetYourGuide's terms say you receive a mobile voucher or a PDF where applicable, and Viator's partner documentation shows a ticket type per tour.
