How to write an itinerary for a trip, step by step

To write an itinerary for a trip, fix your dates and daily pace, list the places you want and group them by area, then give each day a morning, afternoon and evening block with one main stop in each. Add travel and meal buffers, check opening days, and write a backup. A two-day Edinburgh plan below shows it.

The seven steps

  1. Fix the frame: dates, pace and where you sleep.

    Write the arrival time, the departure time and the address of the bed. Those three facts decide how many usable hours each day has. Then choose a pace: I plan one main stop per half day for a relaxed trip, and two for a fast one.

  2. List the places you want, then mark one must-see per day.

    Dump every place you are curious about into one list. Then mark one must-see per day, the place you would regret missing. Everything else is optional and gets dropped first when time runs short.

  3. Cluster the list by area and walking distance.

    Put places that are close together on the same day. Look at a map, not the list. Two good places on opposite sides of a city belong on different days, or one of them goes.

  4. Give each day time blocks.

    Split every day into a morning, afternoon and evening block and put the must-see of the day in the block where it fits best. Anything with a fixed opening time or a booked ticket goes first, because it cannot move.

  5. Add buffers for transit and meals.

    Write the real travel minutes between stops, then add a cushion for queues, a wrong turn or a coffee. Put lunch and dinner in as blocks of their own. A plan with no meals in it is a plan that runs late.

  6. Check opening days and hours, and book what sells out.

    Open each place's own page and check the days and hours for the day you plan to be there. Many places close one weekday or have shorter winter hours. Book timed tickets that can sell out.

  7. Write a backup for bad weather and tired legs.

    Pick one indoor option for each outdoor must-see, close to it, and one stop per day you are happy to cut. Then the plan bends instead of breaking.

Worked example: two days in Edinburgh

This is an example plan I built to show the steps, not a trip I took. I picked Edinburgh because its main sights sit along one street. Wikipedia describes the Royal Mile as running between Edinburgh Castle and Holyrood Palace, almost exactly one mile long and downhill from west to east. That settles the clustering for me: Day 1 follows the street downhill, and Day 2 goes to the park at the east end and then the city centre.

The walking logic is my own arithmetic. One mile is about 1.6 km, so the Castle to the Palace is roughly 20 minutes of straight walking at an ordinary 5 km/h. In practice every stop on the street takes longer than the walk, so the buffers below are for stopping, not for walking. Wikipedia places Holyrood Park about 1 mile east of the Castle, which is why the park gets a morning of its own on Day 2.

What the example shows about the method

  • Geography does the sorting. The mile is downhill, so Day 1 has no backtracking.
  • Only one item is booked. The Castle is the only block I would book, because its own page warns that tickets sell out.
  • Weather sits in one block. The park is the one outdoor block with no easy shelter, so the backup row points there.

Questions

How far ahead should I write a trip itinerary?

Start the frame and the must-see list as soon as the dates are set, and finish the opening-hours check in the last days before you go. Hours and ticket availability change, so a check made months earlier can be out of date.

How many things should I plan per day?

I plan one main stop per half day and up to two optional ones near it, so a day holds three main stops. That is my own rule of thumb, not a measured figure, and a slow-paced trip can use fewer.

Should an itinerary include meals and travel time?

Yes. Give lunch and dinner their own blocks and write the travel minutes between stops. Those are the parts that make a plan run late when they are missing.

In short

Writing an itinerary for a trip comes down to seven steps: fix the frame, mark one must-see per day, cluster by area, set time blocks, add buffers, check opening days and write a backup. A table with a main stop, optional places and a buffer per block is enough. Use an app when the problem is choosing the places, not recording them.
