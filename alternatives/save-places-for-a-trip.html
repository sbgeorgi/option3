Best way to remember places you want to visit in a city, with the Google Maps steps

The best way to remember places you want to visit in a city is the one you can still open when you arrive. On Google Maps, tap the place's name or address, tap Save and choose a list, ideally one per trip. Saved places live under the You tab, on Android and iPhone alike. Below: 8 steps, then 4 methods compared and a test. If you want an app instead of a Google Maps list, see how to use Funliday.

Updated 10 November 2026. Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, an Android trip planner, and I wrote this guide. Every Google Maps step comes from the Google Maps Help pages for Android and for iPhone and iPad, read on 4 November 2026 and checked again line by line on 10 November 2026 (see Sources). A step the help pages did not list is left out.

The 8 steps for Android and iPhone

  1. Sign in to your Google Account.

    Google Maps Help says to make sure you are signed in if you want to save and find favorite places. The same page says you can save your favorite places to find them easily on any computer, phone or tablet.

  2. Find the place.

    Search for it by name, tap a marker on the map, or touch and hold a spot on the map. The last option is handy for an address with no name, such as a flat you rented.

  3. Tap the name or address, then Save.

    At the bottom of the screen, tap the place's name or address, tap Save, and choose a list. That is the whole save. Only you can find your saved places unless you create and share a list.

  4. Make one list per trip.

    Tap You, then New list, and enter a name and a description. On Android you also pick a list type, Private (only you can view and edit) or Shared (a link to view or edit), then tap Save at the top right. On iPhone, tap Save at the top right after the name and description. A name like "Lisbon, November" keeps a trip apart from your everyday places.

    To save a place to the new list straight away, tap New list in the Save sheet from step 3.

  5. Use the default lists as a rough "want to go" shortlist.

    Google Maps comes with default lists named Favorites, Want to go, Starred places, Labeled and Travel plans. The help page names them when it says their icons cannot be changed. If you only need one pile of maybes, save to Want to go. If you want a trip kept apart, use the list from step 4. You can also change the icon of your own lists: next to the list tap More, then Edit list, then Edit icon.

  6. Label addresses with no name.

    A private label puts a name of your choice on a place. These steps are from the Android page: search for an address, or touch and hold a spot on the map, tap the place's name at the bottom, then tap Label. For a business, open its page and tap More, then Add label. Labeled places show on your map, in search suggestions and in Google Photos. To see them again, tap You, then Labeled.

  7. Add a note to a saved place (iPhone page).

    The iPhone and iPad help page lists this: tap You, select a list, choose a place, tap Add a note about this place, write the note and tap Save. Notes can be up to 4,000 characters. The Android page I read did not list the steps, so check what your version shows. A note is the place to put an opening day, a booking number or "ask for the terrace".

  8. Find the places later.

    • By list: tap You, then pick a list under Your lists.
    • By name (Android page): tap You, then See all, then Search at the top left under Your places, type the place name or your note, and pick the best match.
    • By recency or distance (Android page): under You, Recently saved shows places added in the last few months (it is unavailable if you saved nothing in the past 180 days) and Nearby saved shows saved places within 25 km (about 16 miles) of you, and is unavailable if none are that close.
    • On the map: next to a list tap More, then choose Hide or Show to control whether its places appear on the main map. The iPhone page words it Hide on your map or Show on your map; the Android page words it Hide your saved places or Show your saved places.

    On Android you can also save several of your recent places to a list at once: tap You, then See all, touch and hold a place, tick the others, tap Save and choose a list.

5 Google Maps habits that keep saved places usable in a city

"Best" depends on how you travel, so here are the habits the help pages support, in the order I would use them. They keep a pile of saved places usable when you arrive.

  1. One list per trip. Name it with the city and month, as in step 4, so a city's places are not mixed with your everyday ones.
  2. A note on why you saved it. A note can hold up to 4,000 characters (iPhone page), and the Android You tab can search your saved places by place name or by your note.
  3. A label for addresses with no name. A private label shows on your map and in search suggestions, and you find it again under You, then Labeled.
  4. Hide the lists you are not using. Each list has a Hide or Show switch in its More menu, so the map shows one city.
  5. Keep the list short enough to act on. Saved lists are limited to 3,000 entries per Google Maps Help, but a trip needs far fewer: see how many places per day is realistic and how to cut a long list.

Saving remembers a place. It does not tell you which ones to visit. A 60-second test you can run on any of them

Put your phone in airplane mode, then open each place you have saved for your next city using the method you use today. Check three things and count how many you get.

  1. Name: can you read the place name and address?
  2. Why: can you read why you saved it, in your own words?
  3. Where: can you see it on a map next to the other places, with the map drawn and not blank?

Google Maps Help says saving a place is different from downloading an offline area, so do not assume the map will draw: run the test before you leave Wi-Fi, and if the map is blank, download the city's area and test again. A method that scores 3 of 3 with no signal is the one to trust on the trip. I have not run this test on every app, so the scores are yours to find.

What saved places look like on the map

Saved places are not a separate screen. The help pages describe the places on a list as appearing on your main map, and each list has a Hide or Show switch behind its More menu (worded Hide on your map on iPhone, Hide your saved places on Android). The same page says that if you save a list someone else made, its places also appear on your main map unless you hide the list. So the main map can fill up fast. Hide the lists you are not using before you plan a trip, so the map shows one city's places and not everything you have saved since you first opened the app.

Questions about saving places on Google Maps

How do I save a place on Google Maps?

Search for the place or tap it on the map, tap its name or address at the bottom of the screen, tap Save and choose a list. The steps are the same on Android and iPhone.

Where do my saved places go in Google Maps?

Open the You tab and look under Your lists. Each list can be shown or hidden on your main map from the More menu next to it. Labeled places have their own entry under You, then Labeled.

Can I see my saved places offline in Google Maps?

Saving a place is a separate thing from downloading an offline area, according to Google Maps Help. If you need maps without a signal, download the area for the city you are visiting as well as saving the places. For a My Maps map, see does Google My Maps work offline.

What is the best way to remember places you want to visit in a city?

Use the method you can still open with no signal. On Google Maps, save each place to a named list for the trip, add a note on why, and hide your other lists. Saving does not rank places, so cut the list before you go. Four methods are compared above, with an airplane-mode test.

Is there a limit to how many places I can save in a Google Maps list?

Google Maps Help says saved lists are limited to 3,000 entries and that lists with more entries may not display properly.

Is NomadSwipe available on iPhone?

Not today. NomadSwipe is Android only, on Google Play.

Findings

These findings come from reading the Google Maps Help pages for creating a list, saving favorite places, labeling a place and the You tab, for both Android and iPhone where a page existed, on 4 November 2026 and checked each one again on 10 November 2026. The method was to copy each step only if the page listed it, and to mark a step as Android only or iPhone only when the other platform's page did not list it. It is one app's help pages on one day, so treat it as a snapshot.

  • Saving a place takes three taps once you have found it, on both platforms: the place's name or address, then Save, then a list.
  • The Android and iPhone create-a-list pages differ: only the Android page lists a Private or Shared choice at creation and the Customize list order option, and only the iPhone page lists the note step.
  • Google Maps ships five default lists named on the help page: Favorites, Want to go, Starred places, Labeled and Travel plans.
  • The help pages describe lists, a main-map switch per list and a 3,000-entry cap, but none of the pages I read describe a day-by-day view or a way to cut a long list down.
