Lambus vs Wanderlog: Which Trip Planner App Fits You?

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), which appears once below as a third option. Facts about Lambus and Wanderlog checked on 13 November 2026. Updated 13 November 2026.

Lambus vs Wanderlog: choose Wanderlog if you plan around places on a map with friends and want bookings pulled in from email; choose Lambus if you want documents, group expenses and GPX routes in one app. Both are free to download, both sell a paid tier, and both cover Android, iPhone and the web.

Key facts

  • On Google Play on 13 November 2026, Lambus showed 4.4 from 2.32K reviews and 100K+ downloads; Wanderlog showed 4.7 from 37.9K reviews and 1M+.
  • Wanderlog's Pro page shows Pro Annual starting at $39.99 a year. Lambus sells PRO as a monthly, yearly or lifetime upgrade but its price article gives no amounts.
  • A Lambus PRO upgrade covers the whole trip, so travel companions need no upgrade of their own, according to that article. Wanderlog does not say this on the pages I read.
  • Whole-trip offline access is a paid feature in both apps, per their help pages.
  • Only Lambus mentions GPX route import; only Wanderlog lists reservation import from forwarded email or Gmail.

Five questions that decide it

I built this walk-through from the pages above, in the order I would ask the questions. Each step names the app the pages favour.

  1. Do you need email-forwarded bookings? Wanderlog's Play listing says to forward flight and hotel confirmations or connect Gmail, and its pricing page lists import of flights, hotels and cars on the free tier. Lambus's pages describe a documents section and booking partners (GetYourGuide, Omio), but not email import. Wanderlog.
  2. Do you have a recorded route? Lambus says you can import .gpx files from makers such as Garmin, and its help article gives the steps: open the itinerary, open Map Settings, tap GPX files, then email the file to the address shown for your trip. I found no GPX import in the Wanderlog pages I read. Lambus.
  3. Is the trip mostly one big group chat? Both cover groups. Wanderlog's help article says you invite people and set whether they can view or edit. Lambus's sharing article covers a link or embed for people outside the trip, and its site names chat and push notifications for groups. Roughly even, so decide on the next two.
  4. Will you pay once for a group? Lambus's price article says a PRO upgrade benefits the whole trip, so friends do not each need one. Wanderlog's Pro page does not say. If one person is paying for a group of six, that sentence matters. Lambus, on what is stated.
  5. Is it mostly a road trip? Wanderlog's Play listing talks about unlimited stops for free, a route optimizer and times and distances between places, and the Pro page lists route optimization and export to Google Maps under Pro. Lambus has a route planner, but its PRO page lists travel time between waypoints as a paid feature. Wanderlog, if you will pay for Pro; otherwise even.

Where each app's own pages disagree with themselves

The offline answer is the clearest example. The Wanderlog help article and its Pro page say offline access is a Pro feature, while the Play description says all plans are stored offline automatically. Lambus's Play listing says documents are available offline, and its PRO page lists Offline Mode for the whole trip as a PRO feature. I did not test either app, so I cannot say which sentence holds in practice; check the app before relying on offline access.

Dates also differ. Wanderlog's help articles show "3 years ago" while its Pro page and Play listing are current for November 2026, so where they conflict I trusted the Pro page. Lambus's help pages show a current date on every article, which tells me little about when the wording was written. Ratings need care too: Lambus has 4.4 on Google Play from 2.32K reviews, and the U.S. App Store page shows 4.7 from 83 ratings. A 4.7 from 37.9K reviews on Wanderlog's side and a 4.7 from 83 ratings on Lambus's are not the same evidence.

What each does better

Lambus

Lambus is built around the paperwork of a trip: documents, notes, expenses and tickets in one place, plus GPX import for bike, motorbike and hiking routes. Its PRO page lists 15 features, including calendar sync, a private documents store protected by Face ID or fingerprint, PDF and GPX export, and real-time flight updates. Its site adds a browser extension and an AI connector for ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. If your trip is complicated by logistics rather than by choosing places, Lambus is built for that.

Wanderlog

Wanderlog is built around places on a map. Its site calls it "your itinerary and your map in one view", the Play listing adds user-shared guides you can add from in one click, and its help center says the free tier has no cap on trips or the people you plan with. It is also the much larger app on Google Play. For deeper reading, see how to use Wanderlog, how to add places on Wanderlog, what happened to Wanderlog and Wanderlog alternatives. FAQ

Is Lambus or Wanderlog better?

Neither wins outright. Wanderlog is the stronger fit if you plan around places on a map, edit with friends and want reservations pulled in from email. Lambus is the stronger fit if you want travel documents, group expenses and imported GPX routes for bike, motorbike or hiking tours in one app. I did not test either app, so this is based on what their own pages say.

Is Lambus free?

It is free to download. The Lambus site says it is available for free on the App Store, Google Play and as a web app, and its help page calls PRO an extension of the free basic platform. PRO is sold as a monthly, yearly or lifetime upgrade, and the price article gives no amounts.

Is Wanderlog free?

Its help center says the website and app stay free for as many trips as you want to plan. Wanderlog Pro is the paid tier, and wanderlog.com/pro showed Pro Annual starting at $39.99 a year on 13 November 2026.

Which one works offline?

Both put whole-trip offline access behind their paid tier according to their help pages: Lambus calls Offline Mode a PRO feature, and Wanderlog says Pro members can save trip plans in the app. Lambus's Play listing also says travel documents are reachable offline, and Wanderlog's Play description says plans are stored offline automatically, so each has one sentence that points the other way.

Which is better for a group trip?

Both support groups. Wanderlog describes real-time editing with view or edit permissions, and its pricing page lists live editing under the unpaid tier. Lambus describes real-time updates, push notifications and chat for groups, and adds expense splitting. Wanderlog lists budgets and bill splitting too, so neither has a clear edge on the pages I read.

Can I import a GPX route into Lambus or Wanderlog?

Lambus says yes: its Play listing mentions importing .gpx files from makers such as Garmin, and its help article says to email the file to your trip's own address. I found no GPX import on the Wanderlog pages I read. Wanderlog lists export to Google Maps instead.

Is NomadSwipe a Lambus or Wanderlog alternative?

Only for one job. I built NomadSwipe, an Android app where you swipe real places in a city, save the ones you like, see them on a map and build a trip. It does not hold tickets, documents or expenses the way Lambus does, and it does not import email bookings the way Wanderlog does.
