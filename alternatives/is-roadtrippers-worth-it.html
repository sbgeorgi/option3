Is Roadtrippers worth it? A verdict by type of trip

Is Roadtrippers worth it? Yes for a US or Canada road trip with more than 3 stops, if you will pay for a plan, because the free tier saves one trip with 3 stops. No for a one-city break, travel outside North America, or anyone unwilling to pay $59.99 a year for offline maps and Autopilot.

Three cost points from the same pages. First, the free trial: monthly plans do not get one, the 7-day trial is one-time, and if you already used it you are charged the annual price at once. The trial converts to a paid year unless you cancel at least 24 hours before it ends, and cancelling during the trial ends your access immediately. Second, a sale: on 24 November 2026 roadtrippers.com opened on a Fall Savings Event page with a countdown of about 2 days and 50% off for new members: Basic $17.99, Pro $24.99 and Premium $29.99 a year. That is temporary, so judge by the regular prices above. Third, the Roadtrippers support site says auto-renewal is on by default for all subscribers.

What I did on the Roadtrippers site, 24 November 2026

I did not create an account or plan a trip, so this is what is readable without one, in a signed-out text reader. Treat it as a look at the front door, not a test of the planner.

  1. I opened maps.roadtrippers.com with no login. A map of the United States loaded, with category filters for Favorites, Hotels & Unique Stays, Sights & Attractions, Campendium, The Great Outdoors, Bars & Restaurants, Places to Camp and Activities & Experiences, a Remove ads button, and tabs for Explore, Itinerary, My trips and Start Trip, with a Log in link.
  2. Below the map were Nearby guides (Route 66 and I-40 road trips among them, each with a mileage such as 2,316 MI) and Must-See Extraordinary Places with ratings, such as Brooklyn Bridge at 4.5. So browsing places and guides needs no payment.
  3. I read the support site's website walkthrough. You enter a starting point and destination, then choose Plan with Autopilot (Premium) or Plan on your own, add the stops you already know, optionally avoid highways, and launch the trip to the map.
  4. I read the article "What Can I Do on Roadtrippers for Free?" (dated 13 November 2026). Without a membership you can discover places on the map, read and post reviews, view photos and browse guides, and the free plan saves one trip with up to 3 stops.

What this told me: the free tier lets you look around and try one very short trip. The cap you hit first is stop 4, which is why a road trip with a few overnight stops needs a plan.

The honest cons

  • The free plan is small: 1 saved trip and 3 stops. A Google Play reviewer (October 2025) calls it "way too restrictive" and objects that more than 3 waypoints and inviting people to a trip need a membership; Roadtrippers replied with the three plan prices.
  • Subscription and cancellation complaints: on Trustpilot, Roadtrippers shows 2.3 from 125 reviews, 40% of them one-star and 25% five-star. Recent reviews (October 2026) complain about the trial turning into a yearly charge and about cancelling late. Trustpilot says it does not fact-check reviews, and only 15 were posted in the last 12 months, so read it as a signal, not a measurement. Roadtrippers' own page says 4.8 from 60K+ reviews, which is its own claim, and I could not see how it is counted.
  • Mixed Android reviews: the Google Play listing text I read (the listing is linked from roadtrippers.com) shows 3.2 from 615 reviews, under a tablet label, and the listing was updated 17 November 2026. A review dated 16 November 2026 says "Gone down hill" and that information is gone. One dated 10 June 2026 says it did not tell gasoline from diesel stations and did not work in Canada, although the listing says Canada is covered. Those are two reviews, not a trend.
  • North America only: the Play listing says it is designed for the USA and Canada only, and RV routing is U.S. only.
  • Older reviews of the same type: Pilot's review (updated 23 December 2025) lists outdated place information, navigation only in portrait mode with crashes, and limited countries. Its prices and 7-waypoint free limit do not match Roadtrippers' pages today, so I use it only for the kind of complaint.
  • Sources that disagree: Upper, a route-software vendor (updated 27 October 2026), says the free version allows 7 waypoints. Roadtrippers' own pages today say 3 stops, so I trust the official page.
  • Not covered: I did not install the app, read its iPhone store listing (the page I opened returned a not-found message), test offline maps, Autopilot or navigation, or read Reddit threads.

The short version

As of 24 November 2026, Roadtrippers is worth it for a US or Canada road trip with more than 3 stops, if you pay for a plan: Basic is $35.99 a year for 20 stops, and Premium is $59.99 a year for 150 stops, Autopilot, offline maps and RV tools. It is not worth it for a one-city break or travel outside North America, and its Trustpilot score of 2.3 from 125 reviews is mostly about subscriptions and cancelling. Use the 7-day trial, and cancel at least 24 hours before it ends if you do not want the charge.

Questions about whether Roadtrippers is worth it

Is Roadtrippers worth it?

Yes for a US or Canada road trip with many stops, if you will pay: the free plan saves one trip with 3 stops, Basic ($35.99 a year) allows 20 stops, and Premium ($59.99 a year) adds Autopilot, offline maps and RV tools. Not for a one-city break. I did not test the app, so this is a reading of Roadtrippers' own pages, read 24 November 2026.

Is Roadtrippers free?

Partly. Roadtrippers' support site says you can browse places on the map, read and post reviews, view photos and browse guides without a membership, and save one trip with up to 3 stops. Everything past that needs a paid plan or the 7-day trial.

How much does Roadtrippers cost?

Roadtrippers' membership page, read 24 November 2026, lists Basic at $35.99 a year or $11.99 a month, Pro at $49.99 a year or $16.99 a month, and Premium at $59.99 a year or $19.99 a month. The 7-day free trial is one-time and not offered on monthly plans. A 50% off sale banner was also showing that day, so check the current price.

Does Roadtrippers work offline?

Only on Premium, according to Roadtrippers' own table. Its membership page shows offline maps and live traffic as Premium features, and its Google Play listing says offline maps come with the Premium membership. I did not test how well they work.

Does Roadtrippers work outside the US and Canada?

Its Google Play listing says the app is designed for discovering and planning road trips in the USA and Canada only, and its RV routing features are listed as U.S. only. I did not test it abroad, so for any other country check with the trial before you pay.

What if my trip is one city, not a road trip?

That is a different job. NomadSwipe, which I built, is an Android app where you swipe real places in any city, save the ones you like, see them on a map and build a trip. It is not a road-trip route planner, an RV tool or a navigator.
