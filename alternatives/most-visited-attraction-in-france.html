Most visited attraction in France: what each 2025 figure counts

The most visited attraction in France, on the figures I read, is Notre-Dame de Paris: officials report more than 11 million visitors in its first year after reopening in December 2024, with free entry. Among ticketed sites, Disneyland Park in Paris counted 10.8 million visits in 2025 (TEA), ahead of the Louvre at 9 million.

For context, the culture ministry's Patrimostat summary (11 Sept 2026) says the CMN's monuments passed 12 million visitors for the first time in 2025, and national museums and exhibition venues recorded 32.8 million entries. France as a whole drew 102 million international visitors in 2025 per the tourism minister, a country count, not an attraction.

Why the lists disagree

  • Free versus ticketed. The Eiffel Tower has two figures for one monument: about 10 million at the free forecourt and 6.75 million who went up. Notre-Dame is free, so there is no ticket office to count at, and its figure is an official's total. Even the Louvre's 9 million includes about 3 million free admissions.
  • A site versus a destination. Mont-Saint-Michel is 2.794 million for the site and 1.62 million for the ticketed abbey. Versailles is counted as a "domaine". France itself is 102 million, which is a country, not an attraction.
  • Visits versus people. TEA counts visits to each park, so Disneyland Paris is two rows, 10.8 million and 5.919 million, and a visitor to both is two visits. I did not find a unique-visitor figure for any row.
  • Different windows. Notre-Dame's figure runs from the 7 Dec 2024 reopening to December 2025, Puy du Fou's to 31 Oct 2025, and the rest are calendar 2025. So Notre-Dame's figure is reported higher than Disneyland Park's, but over a longer window.
  • One source, two roundings. The Arc de Triomphe is 1.9 million in one report of the CMN's release and 1.85 million in another, and the Louvre is 9 million by its own announcement and 9,046,000 in TEA. Counts also move: the Louvre had 10.2 million in 2018.
  • Left out. Sacre-Coeur, Pont du Gard and Futuroscope are not in the table: I found no operator or ministry count I could read today. The Paris city page I read on Sacre-Coeur gives no visitor figure, and the 10 to 11 million figures I saw in search results came from sites that cite no count.

Plan a visit: tickets and time slots

A big number says a place is famous, not that your visit will be good. The sources give a few hard facts. All were read on 19 Nov 2026, and prices and rules change, so check the official page before you buy.

  • Louvre. Time-slot booking is recommended, including for free-admission visitors, though tickets may be bought on site when attendance is low. The posted rate is EUR 22 for EEA residents and EUR 32 for others. Reservations are required for everyone from 19 Dec 2026 to 4 Jan 2027, with a list of exemptions. The museum has a daily limit of 30,000 entries, which AFP says it set after the 2018 peak.
  • Eiffel Tower. The forecourt is free. The official fares page lists an adult ticket at EUR 23.50 for the second floor by elevator and EUR 36.70 for the summit by elevator, with new 2027 fares from 11 Jan 2027. Both the Eiffel Tower and the Louvre warn about fake ticket sites, so buy only from their own pages.
  • Notre-Dame. Entry is free with or without a reservation, per the Adnkronos report. The same report says the cathedral is considering regulating entry during services when it is crowded, so expect a queue at busy times. I did not check the current reservation page.

Questions about the most visited attraction in France

What is the most visited attraction in France?

On the figures I read, Notre-Dame de Paris: officials say it welcomed more than 11 million visitors in the year after its December 2024 reopening, and entry is free. The Eiffel Tower's free forecourt drew about 10 million in 2025. Among ticketed sites, Disneyland Park in Paris counted 10.8 million visits in 2025. The windows and counters differ, so this is not a strict league table.

What is the most visited paid attraction in France?

In the 2025 TEA index, Disneyland Park in Paris leads with 10.8 million visits, ahead of the Louvre (9 million announced by the museum), Versailles (8.4 million) and the Eiffel Tower (6.75 million who entered its levels). Disneyland Paris's second park, Walt Disney Studios, added 5.919 million visits, so the two parks together are 16.7 million park visits, by my sum.

What is the most visited museum in France?

The Louvre: it announced 9 million visitors for 2025, and the TEA index lists 9,046,000, fourth among museums worldwide. The Palace of Versailles follows with 8.4 million in the same TEA list. About 3 million of the Louvre's 2025 visits were free admissions, per the museum's figures reported by AFP.

Is the Eiffel Tower the most visited monument in France?

Not on the lists I read. The operator counted about 10 million people at the free forecourt and 6.75 million who went up, both for 2025. Notre-Dame's reported 11 million (December 2024 to December 2025) is higher, and Disneyland Park's 10.8 million visits are higher than the 6.75 million ticketed Eiffel visits.

How many tourists visit France?

France recorded 102 million international visitors in 2025, up from 100 million in 2024, per the tourism minister's review reported by L'Echo Touristique and the economy ministry's page. That counts a whole country, not an attraction, so it cannot be used as a site figure.

Findings, method and limits

  • Highest figure found: Notre-Dame de Paris, more than 11 million visitors from the Dec 2024 reopening to Dec 2025, free entry (officials via Adnkronos).
  • Highest ticketed single site: Disneyland Park (Paris), 10.8 million visits in 2025 (TEA); Louvre 9 million (the museum); Versailles 8.4 million; Eiffel Tower 6.75 million up the tower and about 10 million at the free forecourt (SETE).
  • Smaller, well counted: Puy du Fou 3 million (2025 season), Mont-Saint-Michel 2.794 million (abbey 1.62 million), Arc de Triomphe 1.9 million (CMN).

Method. I read each source page on 19 Nov 2026 and took figures only from those pages, with the year the source gives. TEA's index I read through Attractions Magazine, L'Echo Touristique and Euronews, not as the original report. The Louvre, Versailles, Arc de Triomphe, Mont-Saint-Michel and Puy du Fou figures reach me through press reports (L'Echo Touristique, AFP), not through each operator's own release, and the Notre-Dame figure through a single wire report that quotes officials. I summed Disneyland Paris's two parks myself. I did not count anything and I show no NomadSwipe data on this page.

Limits. The years, windows and counters differ, some figures are estimates, and none is a like-for-like comparison. I did not read the Patrimostat tables or any operator's annual report, so a figure here can differ from the operator's final one. Prices and rules change.
