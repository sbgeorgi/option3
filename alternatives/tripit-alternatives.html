6 Best Alternatives to TripIt in 2026

NomadSwipe wrote this page and is one of the apps compared, so read our own entry with that in mind. Updated 10 November 2026.

People look for alternatives to TripIt when its one itinerary per trip, built from forwarded confirmation emails, is not enough: they want free instead of Pro at $49 a year, help deciding what to do on arrival, or a tool for one kind of trip. Six are compared below; free planners lists free tiers.

Triply and TripExpert: which one you mean

Checked 2 November 2026 from each official page or store listing. Triply (triplypro.com) compares and books airport parking, so it is not a trip planner or a TripIt alternative, and this page does not compare parking sites. TripExpert AI is a travel planner on the App Store, by Andreas Bardram. It creates a plan from your dates, location and interests, where NomadSwipe has you choose each place by swiping. Tripexpert.com is a review aggregator for hotels, restaurants and attractions, not an app for organizing a trip.

1. NomadSwipe: for deciding what to do, not tracking bookings

Best for: travellers who have booked the trip and now need a plan for the city

NomadSwipe is an Android trip planner. You pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Each card shows the category, rating, distance and whether the place is open. Everything you save lands in your trip and is pinned on a map.

Where it beats TripIt

  • It helps you find places to visit. TripIt organizes what you have booked; NomadSwipe deals cards for food, sights, bars or stays, ranked in the order you choose.
  • Each place has photos, directions, its website and phone number.
  • Sort your saved list by rating, distance or A to Z, and add custom pins such as your hotel.
  • Up to 20 saved trips are available offline, according to the Play listing.

Where it does not

  • It does not read confirmation emails or build an itinerary from your bookings, and it sends no flight alerts.
  • Its Google Play listing is for Android, and it lists no trip import from other apps.
  • The free plan is limited: 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip and 2 custom pins a month, and a daily limit on saves.

Pricing

Free to download. Premium is optional, comes as Monthly or Lifetime, and removes the limits above. Check the current Premium price on Google Play.

The two apps solve different problems, so you could use both. You gain a quick way to build a shortlist of places in a city. You give up TripIt's booking itinerary and flight alerts. If your main need is keeping flights and hotels in order, stay with TripIt or try KAYAK Trips. If it is working out what to do when you land, try NomadSwipe.

What TripIt does that NomadSwipe does not

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, so read this as the honest half of a comparison I have a stake in. Everything about TripIt below comes from tripit.com and its Google Play listing, read on 10 November 2026. The NomadSwipe side is limited to what the app does today.

  • It builds the itinerary from your email. TripIt says you forward confirmation emails to plans@tripit.com and it creates an itinerary for each trip, whichever site you booked on. It lists flights, hotels, rental cars, trains, airport shuttles, cruises, travel agency bookings and events from sites such as OpenTable, Eventbrite and StubHub. NomadSwipe reads no email and has no booking import.
  • It sends flight alerts, on the Pro plan. TripIt's Pro page lists Flight Status (“real-time flight alerts throughout your trip”), Go Now (when to leave for the airport), Risk Alerts for potential disruptions, Alternate Flights, a Terminal and Gate Reminder, Baggage Claim Info and a Check-in Reminder 24 hours before your flight. It also lists Seat Tracker, Fare Tracker and Point Tracker. The pricing page shows these under “Everything in TripIt (free), plus”, so the free plan does not include them. NomadSwipe sends no flight alerts.
  • Its free plan covers the basics. The pricing page lists a comprehensive itinerary, access across devices, syncing plans with your calendar, sharing plans with others and 3 documents per trip (Pro: 25). TripIt also says you can open your itinerary offline.
  • Airport help. Pro lists searchable airport maps, directions to your connecting gate and security wait times. NomadSwipe is about the city you land in, not the airport.
  • An employer perk. The Play listing says SAP Concur users may get TripIt Pro at no charge if they are eligible, so a work traveller may not pay the $49 at all.
  • More platforms. TripIt's pricing page says its app is on iOS (including Apple Watch) and Android. NomadSwipe is on Android only.

Who should stay on TripIt

  • You fly often and rely on gate changes, delay alerts or Go Now to get to the airport on time.
  • You book on many different sites and want every reservation in one itinerary without typing it in.
  • Your employer uses SAP Concur and you may already get Pro free.
  • You are on an iPhone or an Apple Watch.
  • You need the itinerary synced to a calendar or shared with family or colleagues.

NomadSwipe fits next to TripIt rather than in place of it: keep TripIt for the flights and hotels, and use NomadSwipe to swipe through places, save the ones you want and see them on a map once you know where you are going. If a free app with flight alerts is the whole reason you are looking, read the KAYAK Trips entry below first.

2. Wanderlog

Best for: itineraries, guides and bookings in one place

Wanderlog lets you create detailed itineraries, browse guides shared by other users and manage bookings. Like TripIt, you can connect or forward your emails to add reservations to the plan, and it also recommends places to visit based on your itinerary.

  • Strength: combines booking import with place recommendations, which TripIt does not focus on.
  • Weakness: its Play listing is unclear on offline use: one line marks offline trip plans as Pro, another says plans are stored offline automatically.

Pricing: free to start with a paid Pro tier; check the current price in the app. Read our comparison of travel apps like Wanderlog for more.

3. KAYAK Trips

Best for: organizing bookings without paying

KAYAK Trips is part of KAYAK. It keeps your travel plans in one place and offers shared itineraries with editing permissions. KAYAK says Trips is free no matter where you book, can add your bookings automatically, and gives real-time updates on cancellations, delays or gate changes. That last point matters if flight alerts are why you pay for TripIt Pro. I have not tested KAYAK's alerts against TripIt's.

  • Strength: the closest free match to TripIt's core job.
  • Weakness: it lives inside a larger travel search app, and the page asks you to sign in to plan.

Pricing: free.

4. Tripomatic

Best for: day-by-day city itineraries

Tripomatic, formerly Sygic Travel, helps you discover places to visit, plan a day-by-day itinerary and navigate. Its Play listing describes it as a way to plan trips and create your own travel guide.

  • Strength: turns a list of places into a daily plan.
  • Weakness: its Play listing puts unlimited offline map downloads, walking navigation and AI trip planning behind Premium.

Pricing: free to install; Premium is paid and starts with a free trial, see the Google Play listing for current pricing.

5. Polarsteps

Best for: tracking a trip as it happens

Polarsteps describes itself as a travel tracker and trip planner for the countries and U.S. states you have been to. Its paid Plus tier adds 3D maps, step stats and map styles.

  • Strength: a good fit if the record of the trip matters as much as the plan.
  • Weakness: the pages we read do not mention booking import, so it does not replace TripIt's itinerary from emails.

Pricing: free; Plus is paid, and Polarsteps' Plus page says pricing is shown in the app's settings menu, so check it there.

6. Roadtrippers

Best for: planning a road trip

Roadtrippers is built around driving routes and the stops along them. Paid plans raise the number of stops you can add to a trip. If you are weighing other route planners, Furkot compared with Roadtrippers and other road trip tools is a separate page.

  • Strength: purpose-built for drives, which TripIt is not.
  • Weakness: it is a road-trip planner, not a general booking organizer.

Pricing: free plan; Basic $35.99 a year or $11.99 a month, Pro $49.99 a year or $16.99 a month, Premium $59.99 a year or $19.99 a month. The 7-day free trial applies to annual plans only; monthly plans are billed immediately.

Match the app to the problem you have

  • You have booked and want to decide what to do in the city: NomadSwipe.
  • You want bookings and place ideas in one planner: Wanderlog.
  • You want a free booking organizer: KAYAK Trips.
  • You want a day-by-day schedule: Tripomatic.
  • You want to track the trip as you go: Polarsteps.
  • You are planning a drive: Roadtrippers.
  • You fly often and want flight alerts: TripIt Pro is still worth a look, and KAYAK Trips says it sends real-time flight status alerts for free.

FAQ

Is TripIt free?

Signing up for TripIt is free. TripIt Pro is the paid tier: its official page offers a free 30-day trial, then $49 a year. The Google Play listing shows $48.99 a year for Pro in the US (checked 10 November 2026), so the price you see in the app can differ slightly from the website.

Is TripIt Pro worth $49 a year?

It depends on how often you fly. Pro adds real-time flight alerts, Seat Tracker, Fare Tracker, check-in reminders and airport maps. If you rarely fly, the free plan or a free alternative may be enough.

What are Triply and TripExpert?

Triply (triplypro.com) is an airport parking comparison and booking site, not a TripIt alternative. TripExpert AI is an App Store travel planner that creates a plan from your dates and interests. Tripexpert.com is a hotel and restaurant review aggregator.

What is the best free TripIt alternative?

KAYAK Trips says it is free no matter where you book and that it gives real-time updates on cancellations, delays or gate changes, so it is the closest free match for organizing bookings. NomadSwipe is free to download if you mainly want to decide what to see and eat in a city.

What happened to TripCase?

TripCase looks discontinued. Its website redirects to a Sabre page at sabre.com/tripcase-sunset/, which showed a page-not-found error when we checked on 10 November 2026, so we do not list it as an option.

What does TripIt do that NomadSwipe does not?

TripIt builds an itinerary from confirmation emails you forward to plans@tripit.com, and its Pro plan ($49 a year on tripit.com) adds real-time flight alerts, Go Now, Risk Alerts, Seat Tracker and Fare Tracker. NomadSwipe reads no email, has no booking import and sends no flight alerts; it is for choosing and saving places. Sources: tripit.com/web/pro and tripit.com/web/pro/pricing, read 10 November 2026.

Can I import my TripIt trips into NomadSwipe?

No. NomadSwipe does not list a trip import feature and does not read booking emails. Search your destination and save the places you want by swiping.

Is NomadSwipe available on Android?

Yes. NomadSwipe is free to download on Google Play for Android phones.
