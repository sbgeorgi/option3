5 Best Wanderlog Alternatives in 2026

1. NomadSwipe: the lighter, swipe-first option

Best for: people who want to decide quickly where to go in a city

NomadSwipe is an Android trip planner. You pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Each card shows the category, rating, distance and, where hours are known, whether the place is open. Everything you save lands in your trip and is pinned on a map.

Where it beats Wanderlog

  • Less to learn. There is no itinerary builder to set up: swipe, save, open the map.
  • You can rank what matters to you (food, sights, bars or stays) and the deck follows that order.
  • Offline: up to 20 saved trips are available offline, according to the Play listing.
  • Sort the saved list by rating, distance or A to Z, and add custom pins such as your hotel.

Where it does not

  • Its listing does not mention reservation import from email, budgets or expense tracking, route optimisation or a road-trip mode. Wanderlog's listing covers all of these.
  • Its Google Play listing is for Android, and it lists no trip import from other apps.
  • The free plan is limited: 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip a month, 2 custom pins a month and a daily limit on saves.

Pricing

Free to download. Premium is optional, comes as Monthly or Lifetime, and removes the limits above. Google Play shows the price before you pay.

What Wanderlog does better than NomadSwipe, and where it falls short

I built NomadSwipe, so weigh this section with that in mind. I have not installed or tested Wanderlog. Every Wanderlog statement here is what its own pages said when I read them on 10 November 2026 (listed under Sources), and every NomadSwipe statement is what its Google Play listing says.

Where Wanderlog does better

  • Bookings. You can forward flight, hotel and rental car confirmation emails into a trip, or connect Gmail (the App Store listing marks linking Gmail as Pro). NomadSwipe's listing mentions no reservation import.
  • Money. Wanderlog lists budgets, expense tracking and bill splitting for groups. NomadSwipe's listing mentions none of them.
  • Planning with other people. Wanderlog lists real-time collaboration with edit or view permissions. NomadSwipe's listing does not describe sharing a trip.
  • Drives. Wanderlog lists unlimited road-trip stops for free, times and distances between places, a route optimizer (a Pro feature per its help center) and export to Google Maps. NomadSwipe's listing mentions none of these.
  • No trip cap on the free plan. Its help center says it will never restrict the number of trips you plan. NomadSwipe's plan without Premium stops at 3 trips.
  • Track record. Wanderlog's Play listing shows 1M+ downloads and a 4.7 rating from 37.9K reviews, and its App Store listing shows 4.9 from 36,349 ratings. NomadSwipe has 50+ downloads on Google Play, too few for a rating to mean anything.
  • Platforms. Wanderlog runs on Android, iOS and the web. NomadSwipe is on Android.

Where Wanderlog falls short

  • The price is hard to find. The Pro page shows no price and the Play listing shows none. The App Store lists ten Pro entries in seven name-and-price combinations, annual from $31.99 to $59.99, and does not say which one applies to you.
  • Its pages disagree about what Pro includes. Offline access is Pro in the help center and the Play feature list, automatic in the Play description, and unmarked in the App Store list. Export to Google Maps is Pro in one App Store sentence and unmarked in the road-trip paragraph beside it.
  • The Pro help articles are old. The articles on offline access, route optimization and downloading the app are dated 2 and 10 March 2023, while the store listings were updated in November 2026. Without testing, I cannot say which wording is current.
  • No swipe deck. The listings describe adding things to do from guides with one click and building an itinerary, but none describes dealing places one card at a time or ranking food, sights, bars or stays to order suggestions. That is the narrow job NomadSwipe is built around.

Where NomadSwipe is narrower than it may sound

  • Its offline feature is up to 20 saved trips, available after you open them online, and the copies expire until you open the trip online again.
  • Its free plan limits are tighter than Wanderlog's: see the table below.

2. TripIt

Best for: keeping every booking in one itinerary

TripIt builds an itinerary from confirmation emails: forward a booking to its address and it is added to your plan. You can also upload PDFs, boarding passes and photos, and details stay available offline.

  • Strength: bookings in one place, with map and directions tools.
  • Pro adds flight status alerts, refund alerts when a fare drops, reward-program tracking and airport maps.
  • Weakness: it organizes trips you have booked more than it helps you find places to visit.

Pricing: Pro auto-renews at $48.99 a year on the Google Play listing; TripIt's own page offers a 30-day free trial, then $49 a year (both read 4 November and again on 10 November 2026).

3. Roadtrippers

Best for: road trips and camping in the USA and Canada

Roadtrippers focuses on routes: millions of places along the way, pre-made trip guides, and camping and RV features such as campground reviews and map overlays.

  • Strength: purpose-built for driving trips.
  • Paid plans raise the stop limit up to 150 a trip, and its Play listing adds RV-friendly GPS navigation, offline maps and trip collaboration to Premium.
  • Weakness: the free plan starts with up to three stops, and the listing says it is designed for the USA and Canada only.

Pricing: Roadtrippers' own plan table (read 10 November 2026) lists Free at $0 with 1 saved trip and 3 stops per trip; Basic at $35.99 a year or $11.99 a month (3 saved trips, 20 stops); Pro at $49.99 a year or $16.99 a month (5 saved trips, 50 stops); and Premium at $59.99 a year or $19.99 a month (unlimited saved trips, 150 stops). It offers a 7-day free trial. If you plan a long road trip, Wanderlog's free unlimited stops may be the better deal.

4. Tripomatic

Best for: day-by-day city itineraries

Tripomatic, formerly Sygic Travel, builds a day-by-day itinerary with estimated travel times and walking distances, and offers AI trip planning. Its Play listing lists 50 million places and multiple map styles; its own site says millions of places.

  • Strength: realistic daily schedules in unfamiliar cities.
  • Weakness: offline maps and walking navigation need Premium, and the free plan is limited to 5 days with planned activities a month and 25 places and hotels per trip, with ads.

Pricing: free with the limits above and no time limit, according to Tripomatic's own site. That site shows no Premium price, and the Play listing shows in-app purchases without a single price, so check the price in the app. On the free plan the AI assistant answers preset questions; custom prompts and follow-up questions are Premium (read 10 November 2026).

5. Polarsteps

Best for: recording a trip and sharing it afterwards

Polarsteps tracks your route automatically, lets you add photos and stories to each step, and shares updates with people who follow your trip. Afterwards you can make a Trip Reel video or order a printed Travel Book. It also has an AI itinerary planner.

  • Strength: the best fit here if the memory of the trip matters as much as the plan.
  • Weakness: planning is only one part of a larger tracking and sharing app.

Pricing: free to install. Polarsteps' Plus page says planning, tracking, reliving and following trips are in the free plan, with 5 Travel Buddies; Plus (monthly or yearly) adds 10 Travel Buddies, 3D maps, more map styles and step stats, and yearly Plus members get 20% off Travel Books. It shows no price: it says to check the Plus page in the app settings (read 10 November 2026).

Picking by the job you need done

  • You want a quick shortlist of places in a city: NomadSwipe.
  • You have lots of bookings to organize: TripIt, or stay with Wanderlog.
  • You are planning a drive or camping trip in the USA or Canada: Roadtrippers.
  • You want a daily schedule with travel times: Tripomatic.
  • You want to record and share the trip: Polarsteps.

FAQ

Is Wanderlog free?

Mostly. Wanderlog's help center says its website and app stay free for as many trips as you want to plan, with a paid Pro tier for power-user features (that article is dated March 2023). Its Pro page showed no price when read on 10 November 2026, and its U.S. App Store listing shows Pro in-app purchases from $31.99 to $59.99 a year and at $5.99 and $16.99 a month. Its help center and Google Play feature list mark offline access as Pro, and the App Store listing marks linking Gmail and exporting to Google Maps as Pro.

What does Wanderlog do better than NomadSwipe?

I built NomadSwipe, so weigh this with that in mind. Wanderlog covers more of the planning job: reservation import from forwarded emails or Gmail, budgets and expense splitting, real-time collaboration, a route optimizer, unlimited road-trip stops, no stated cap on trips on its free plan, and iOS and web as well as Android. It also has a far larger user base: 1M+ Google Play downloads against NomadSwipe's 50+. NomadSwipe is narrower: swipe real places, save them and see them on a map. See the full comparison.

How much does Wanderlog Pro cost?

Wanderlog's own Pro page and its Google Play listing showed no price when we read them on 10 November 2026. Its U.S. App Store listing showed Pro in-app purchases at several price points: annual plans from $31.99 to $59.99 and monthly plans at $5.99 and $16.99. Check the price in the app before you subscribe. See the findings for how we read it.

What is the best free Wanderlog alternative?

It depends on the job. NomadSwipe is free to download and covers discovering places by swiping and seeing them on a map. TripIt's basic itinerary organizer, Roadtrippers (up to three stops free) and Polarsteps also have free entry points, so check each one's current plan before you rely on it.

What is a simpler alternative to Wanderlog?

If you only want to find places and see them on a map, NomadSwipe has fewer features than Wanderlog and a shorter learning curve: swipe real places, save the ones you like, and open the trip on a map.

Which Wanderlog alternative is best for road trips?

Roadtrippers is built for road trips in the USA and Canada, including camping and RV features. Wanderlog itself also supports road trips and says unlimited stops are free.

Can I import my Wanderlog trips into NomadSwipe?

NomadSwipe does not list a trip import feature, so plan to re-save the places you want by swiping or searching the destination.

Does Wanderlog work offline?

Wanderlog's sources disagree. Its help center says storing your trip plan and documents offline is a Pro feature, and its Google Play feature list marks offline access as Pro, but the same listing also says plans are stored offline automatically and its App Store listing carries no Pro marker. Test it on the free plan before you rely on it. NomadSwipe's Play listing says up to 20 saved trips are available offline.

Is NomadSwipe available on Android?

Yes. NomadSwipe is on Google Play for Android phones.

Findings: what each app's own pages say (read 10 November 2026)

What we compared and how. On 10 November 2026, six days after the 4 November pass, I re-read Wanderlog's own pages rather than rely on older write-ups: the wanderlog.com/pro page, its Google Play listing, its U.S. App Store listing (the public page plus Apple's public lookup API for the rating count) and five help center articles. I fetched raw HTML with curl for the Pro page, the Play listings and the help center, because a page-summary tool saw no content on the Pro page or the Play listing and was refused (HTTP 403) by the help center. I also re-read each alternative's own pages: TripIt's Pro page and Play listing, Roadtrippers' plan table and Play listing, Tripomatic's site and Play listing, and Polarsteps' Plus page and Play listing. Wanderlog prices and Pro wording were unchanged since 4 November; the Play rating reads 4.7 from 37.9K reviews (the 4 November version of this page said 4.8 from 37.7K) and the App Store count is 36,349 ratings (was 36,249). I did not install Wanderlog, subscribe, or test any feature, so everything below is what the pages say, not what the app does.

  • The Pro page shows no price. The raw HTML of wanderlog.com/pro, 50,386 bytes, contains no dollar amount and no plan text, and a read through a page-summary tool also found no prices or plan names. The Google Play listing shows no price either; it only says the app has in-app purchases.
  • Only the App Store listing shows prices, and they vary. It lists ten Wanderlog Pro in-app purchases that come to seven distinct name-and-price combinations: Annual Plan at $31.99, $39.99, $49.99 and $59.99, Monthly Plan at $5.99 and $16.99, and a “20% discount” entry at $39.99. The highest annual entry is about 1.9 times the lowest. The listing does not say why the entries differ, so I cannot say which one applies to you.
  • Offline access is described three ways. The help center articles on downloading the app and on downloading a trip plan call storing the plan and documents offline a Pro feature (both dated March 2023). The Play feature list says “Access your trip plans offline (Pro)”, yet a later paragraph of the same listing says plans are automatically stored offline. The App Store feature list says “Access your trip plans offline” with no Pro marker.
  • The listings disagree on export to Google Maps. The Play listing mentions it next to unlimited road-trip stops with no Pro marker. The App Store listing says that with Pro you can export your trip to Google Maps and link your Gmail account. The help center says the route optimizer is a Pro feature.
  • The free tier has no trip cap. The help center says the website and app stay free for as many trips as you want to plan, with Pro adding power-user features. The Play description also calls the app “completely free”. A second help article says Wanderlog earns a small percentage from bookings made through the app and from Pro subscriptions, and shows no ads.
  • Wanderlog is far larger than NomadSwipe. Its Play listing shows 1M+ downloads and a 4.7 rating from 37.9K reviews; its App Store listing shows a 4.9 average from 36,349 ratings. NomadSwipe has 50+ downloads on Google Play, and I do not quote a NomadSwipe star rating because there are too few ratings for it to mean anything. If a large user base and a long track record matter to you, that favours Wanderlog.
  • Roadtrippers publishes its prices. Its plan table lists four tiers, Free, Basic, Pro and Premium, from $0 to $59.99 a year, with saved trips rising from 1 to unlimited and stops per trip from 3 to 150, and a 7-day free trial.
  • Tripomatic publishes limits, not a price. Its site lists the free plan limits (5 days with planned activities a month, 25 places and hotels per trip, ads) and says offline maps are Premium, but shows no Premium price.
  • Polarsteps shows no price outside the app. Its Plus page lists what Plus adds and says to find pricing in the app settings.

Limits: the store listings are the U.S. English versions, prices differ by country and over time, and the Wanderlog help center articles are older than the listings. Treat the prices as a snapshot, and check the number the store shows you before you pay.
