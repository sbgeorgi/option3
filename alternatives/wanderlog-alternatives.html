1. NomadSwipe: the lighter, swipe-first option

Best for: people who want to decide quickly where to go in a city

NomadSwipe is an Android trip planner. You pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Each card shows the category, rating, distance and, where hours are known, whether the place is open. Everything you save lands in your trip and is pinned on a map.

Where it beats Wanderlog

  • Less to learn. There is no itinerary builder to set up: swipe, save, open the map.
  • You can rank what matters to you (food, sights, bars or stays) and the deck follows that order.
  • Offline: up to 20 saved trips are available offline, according to the Play listing.
  • Sort the saved list by rating, distance or A to Z, and add custom pins such as your hotel.

Where it does not

  • Its listing does not mention reservation import from email, budgets or expense tracking, route optimisation or a road-trip mode. Wanderlog's listing covers all of these.
  • Its Google Play listing is for Android, and it lists no trip import from other apps.
  • The free plan is limited: 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip a month, 2 custom pins a month and a daily limit on saves.

Pricing

Free to download. Premium is optional, comes as Monthly or Lifetime, and removes the limits above. Google Play shows the price before you pay.

2. TripIt

Best for: keeping every booking in one itinerary

TripIt builds an itinerary from confirmation emails: forward a booking to its address and it is added to your plan. You can also upload PDFs, boarding passes and photos, and details stay available offline.

  • Strength: bookings in one place, with map and directions tools.
  • Pro adds flight status alerts, refund alerts when a fare drops, reward-program tracking and airport maps.
  • Weakness: it organizes trips you have booked more than it helps you find places to visit.

Pricing: Pro auto-renews at $48.99 a year on the Google Play listing; TripIt's own page offers a 30-day free trial, then $49 a year (both read 4 November 2026).

3. Roadtrippers

Best for: road trips and camping in the USA and Canada

Roadtrippers focuses on routes: millions of places along the way, pre-made trip guides, and camping and RV features such as campground reviews and map overlays.

  • Strength: purpose-built for driving trips.
  • Premium adds more stops and further features; see the listing for details.
  • Weakness: the free plan starts with up to three stops, and the listing says it is designed for the USA and Canada only.

Pricing: free to install with in-app purchases; see the Google Play listing for Premium pricing. If you plan a long road trip, Wanderlog's free unlimited stops may be the better deal.

4. Tripomatic

Best for: day-by-day city itineraries

Tripomatic, formerly Sygic Travel, builds a day-by-day itinerary with estimated travel times and walking distances, and offers AI trip planning with Premium. It lists 50 million places and multiple map styles.

  • Strength: realistic daily schedules in unfamiliar cities.
  • Weakness: offline maps and walking navigation need Premium.

Pricing: free to install with in-app purchases; see the Google Play listing for Premium pricing.

5. Polarsteps

Best for: recording a trip and sharing it afterwards

Polarsteps tracks your route automatically, lets you add photos and stories to each step, and shares updates with people who follow your trip. Afterwards you can make a Trip Reel video or order a printed Travel Book. It also has an AI itinerary planner.

  • Strength: the best fit here if the memory of the trip matters as much as the plan.
  • Weakness: planning is only one part of a larger tracking and sharing app.

Pricing: free to install with in-app purchases; see the Google Play listing for current options.

Picking by the job you need done

  • You want a quick shortlist of places in a city: NomadSwipe.
  • You have lots of bookings to organize: TripIt, or stay with Wanderlog.
  • You are planning a drive or camping trip in the USA or Canada: Roadtrippers.
  • You want a daily schedule with travel times: Tripomatic.
  • You want to record and share the trip: Polarsteps.

FAQ

Is Wanderlog free?

Wanderlog says its website and app stay free for as many trips as you want, with a paid Pro tier for some power-user features. Its help center and Google Play listing mark offline access to your plans as a Pro feature.

How much does Wanderlog Pro cost?

Wanderlog's own Pro page and its Google Play listing showed no price when we read them on 4 November 2026. Its U.S. App Store listing showed Pro in-app purchases at several price points: annual plans from $31.99 to $59.99 and monthly plans at $5.99 and $16.99. Check the price in the app before you subscribe. See the findings for how we read it.

What is the best free Wanderlog alternative?

It depends on the job. NomadSwipe is free to download and covers discovering places by swiping and seeing them on a map. TripIt's basic itinerary organizer, Roadtrippers (up to three stops free) and Polarsteps also have free entry points, so check each one's current plan before you rely on it.

What is a simpler alternative to Wanderlog?

If you only want to find places and see them on a map, NomadSwipe has fewer features than Wanderlog and a shorter learning curve: swipe real places, save the ones you like, and open the trip on a map.

Which Wanderlog alternative is best for road trips?

Roadtrippers is built for road trips in the USA and Canada, including camping and RV features. Wanderlog itself also supports road trips and says unlimited stops are free.

Can I import my Wanderlog trips into NomadSwipe?

NomadSwipe does not list a trip import feature, so plan to re-save the places you want by swiping or searching the destination.

Does Wanderlog work offline?

Wanderlog's sources disagree. Its help center says storing your trip plan and documents offline is a Pro feature, and its Google Play feature list marks offline access as Pro, but the same listing also says plans are stored offline automatically and its App Store listing carries no Pro marker. Test it on the free plan before you rely on it. NomadSwipe's Play listing says up to 20 saved trips are available offline.

Is NomadSwipe available on Android?

Yes. NomadSwipe is on Google Play for Android phones.

Findings: what Wanderlog's own pages say (read 4 November 2026)

What we compared and how. On 4 November 2026 I re-read Wanderlog's own pages rather than rely on older write-ups: the wanderlog.com/pro page, its Google Play listing, its U.S. App Store listing (the public page plus Apple's public search API for the rating count) and four help center articles. I fetched the raw HTML with curl and read the text, because the Pro page is built in the browser and a summary tool saw no content on it. I also re-read TripIt's Play listing and Pro page to confirm the TripIt prices in the table above. I did not install Wanderlog, subscribe, or test any feature, so everything below is what the pages say, not what the app does.

  • The Pro page shows no price. The raw HTML of wanderlog.com/pro, about 50 KB, contains no dollar amount and no plan text, and a second read through a page-summary tool also found no prices or plan names. The Google Play listing shows no price either; it only says the app has in-app purchases.
  • Only the App Store listing shows prices, and they vary. It lists ten Wanderlog Pro in-app purchases that come to seven distinct name-and-price combinations: Annual Plan at $31.99, $39.99, $49.99 and $59.99, Monthly Plan at $5.99 and $16.99, and a “20% discount” entry at $39.99. The highest annual entry is about 1.9 times the lowest. The listing does not say why the entries differ, so I cannot say which one applies to you.
  • Offline access is described three ways. The help center article on downloading the app calls storing the plan and documents offline a Pro feature (the article is dated March 2023). The Play feature list says “Access your trip plans offline (Pro)”, yet a later paragraph of the same listing says plans are automatically stored offline. The App Store feature list says “Access your trip plans offline” with no Pro marker.
  • The listings disagree on export to Google Maps. The Play listing mentions it next to unlimited road-trip stops with no Pro marker. The App Store listing says that with Pro you can export your trip to Google Maps and link your Gmail account. The help center says the route optimizer is a Pro feature.
  • The free tier has no trip cap. The help center says the website and app stay free for as many trips as you want to plan, with Pro adding power-user features. A second help article says Wanderlog earns a small percentage from bookings made through the app and from Pro subscriptions, and shows no ads.
  • Wanderlog is far larger than NomadSwipe. Its Play listing shows 1M+ downloads and a 4.8 rating from 37.7K reviews; its App Store listing shows a 4.9 average from 36,249 ratings. NomadSwipe has 50+ downloads on Google Play, and I do not quote a NomadSwipe star rating because there are too few ratings for it to mean anything. If a large user base and a long track record matter to you, that favours Wanderlog.

Limits: the store listings are the U.S. English versions, prices differ by country and over time, and the help center articles are older than the listings. Treat the prices as a snapshot, and check the number the store shows you before you pay.
