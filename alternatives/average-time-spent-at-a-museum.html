Average time spent at a museum: about 1 to 3 hours

The average time spent at a museum is roughly one to three hours, depending on the museum and on how the time was measured. Tracked visitors averaged 57.6 minutes at an art museum in Nancy and 47 minutes at a science center, and the Van Gogh Museum says two to three hours. A single exhibition averages about 20 minutes.

Published 16 Nov 2026. Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), and I wrote this page. The visit-length figures come from sources I read on 16 Nov 2026 and link below. The opening-hours figures come from my own read-only queries on the app's stored place data on the same day.

Two smaller numbers sit under the visit averages. In the Nancy data, each visitor spent an average of 29 seconds in front of the artworks they selected. In the science center study, the mean of the average holding times across 61 interactive exhibits was 1.6 minutes, with the quickest exhibits at 30 seconds and the slowest at 5.6 minutes. Sandifer also cites a third figure from earlier work: family groups at two other science centers averaged a little over two hours in total, a number I read only second-hand in his paper.

Where the figures disagree, and why

  • Different units. About 20 minutes is the time in one exhibition, 47 to 58 minutes is a whole visit to a mid-size institution, and two to three hours is a whole visit to a famous one. None of these contradicts the others. The same Serrell article reports 13 minutes for the Smithsonian's Hall of Mammals, a vast hall that visitors crossed quickly.
  • Different sets of exhibitions. Sandifer quotes Serrell's earlier summary of 34 exhibitions as 13 minutes on average, while the 110-exhibition database in the 2010 article gives about 20. Both are Serrell's numbers, from different groups of exhibitions, which is a reminder that an "average" depends on what was put in the pool.
  • Different ways of counting. The Van Gogh Museum states that its two to three hours include the cafe and shop. Sandifer's 47 minutes leaves out film time. I found no statement in the Nancy paper on how breaks were treated. Those choices can move an average by tens of minutes.
  • Different people. The Nancy visitors were volunteers who agreed to wear glasses, and the science center sample was balanced by sex and age and left out school groups. Neither is a random draw of everyone who walks in, and an institution's own estimate may describe the visitor it plans for.
  • Individuals vary more than averages do. In Nancy the range ran from 11 minutes to almost 2 hours, in the science center from 12 minutes to over 2 hours, and the Louvre data contains both short-stay (under 1.5 hours) and long-stay (over 6 hours) visitors. The MIT team found that short-stay and long-stay visitors visit a similar number of key locations, with the long-stay visitors covering them more extensively.

How many museums fit in a day

No source I read counts museums per day, so this section is my own arithmetic on the figures above, not a finding. It also assumes you want to look at things, not tick them off: a fast pass is possible, since the Smithsonian hall averaged 13 minutes.

Two things the arithmetic leaves out decide most real days. First, travel and queues between buildings, which no study here measured. Second, your own pace: if you tend to stay longer than the average, use the long end of each range. Since visits ran from 11 minutes to almost two hours in a single three-floor building, plan with a range for each stop and cut the weakest one if the morning runs long. Visit length is not opening hours: what the stored hours say

A museum's opening hours are the window you can visit in, which is a different thing from the time visitors spend. To show how far apart the two are, I counted the weekly hours stored in NomadSwipe's place data on 16 Nov 2026. This is opening span, not time visitors spend, and it pools every city deck together.

The median span of 7 hours is longer than every whole-visit average in the table above, including the two to three hours the Van Gogh Museum gives. So a museum that is open for most of a day does not need most of your day. The practical use of the opening span is the other way round: it tells you the latest you can arrive. Two examples I read today show how the pieces fit. The Prado's visit page lists 10:00 to 20:00 Monday to Saturday and 10:00 to 19:00 on Sundays and public holidays, with entry until 30 minutes before closing. The Met's plan-your-visit page shows 10 am to 5 pm on four days of the week, 10 am to 9 pm on Friday and Saturday, and Wednesday closed. Those are opening spans of 10 hours (9 on Sundays and public holidays) at the Prado and 7 or 11 hours at the Met, against visits that last one to three hours.

The 68 split-opening places matter for planning in a different way: a midday break can cut your window in half, which is easy to miss when you only look at the opening and closing times. Which weekday a museum is closed is a separate question, covered with its own data in which day are museums closed.

Questions about museum visit length

How long does the average person spend in a museum?

In the figures I read, whole visits average between about 47 minutes and three hours. Tracked visitors averaged 57.6 minutes at the Nancy Museum of Fine Arts (51 volunteers) and 47 minutes at the Reuben Fleet Science Center (47 visitors), while the Van Gogh Museum says two to three hours including its cafe and shop. Treat that as a range, because the samples are small and the methods differ.

How long do people look at one painting?

About half a minute in the one study here that timed it. In the Nancy Museum of Fine Arts dataset, visitors spent an average of 29 seconds in front of the artworks they selected. In a science center, the average holding time per interactive exhibit was 1.6 minutes, so a painting and a hands-on exhibit are not comparable.

How many museums can you visit in one day?

That is my own arithmetic, not a measured figure. At the two to three hours the Van Gogh Museum gives for a visit, two large museums take four to six hours. At about an hour each, close to the Nancy average, three mid-size museums take just under three hours. Add travel, meals, queues and each museum's own opening hours.

Is a museum's opening time the same as how long visitors stay?

No. Opening hours are the longest a visit could be, not the time people spend. In NomadSwipe's stored hours, 1,749 museum and gallery places with a single continuous block of open hours have a median open span of 7 hours a day, while the visit averages above run from 47 minutes to three hours. Check the last-entry time too: the Prado admits visitors until 30 minutes before closing.

Findings, method and limits

  • Whole-visit averages in the sources read: 57.6 minutes (Nancy Museum of Fine Arts, 51 volunteers, 2019), 47 minutes (Reuben Fleet Science Center, 47 visitors), and two to three hours stated by the Van Gogh Museum.
  • Exhibition-level average: about 20 minutes across 110 tracked exhibitions in Serrell's database; 13 minutes in the Smithsonian's Hall of Mammals.
  • Per object: 29 seconds per selected artwork in Nancy; 1.6 minutes mean holding time per interactive exhibit in the science center.
  • Spread within one building: 11 minutes to 1 h 55 (Nancy) and 12 minutes to 2 h 23 (science center).
  • Opening span in NomadSwipe's stored hours (read 16 Nov 2026): median 7 hours a day for 1,749 museum and gallery places with one continuous block of open hours, middle half 6 to 8 hours; 68 of 1,879 places (3.6%) have a split opening on at least one open day, 39 on every open day.

Method. I read each source page or paper on 16 Nov 2026 and took figures only from those pages. I did not time any visitors myself. The opening-hours counts come from read-only SQL on the NomadSwipe app's place data, counting museum and gallery places once per Google place ID and using their stored weekly hours. A place counts as "a single continuous block" when its open days each show one opening period, and as "split" when an open day shows a midday break.

Limits. The visit-length studies are small, some are decades old, and none is a random sample of all museum visitors. The Van Gogh figure is an institution's planning guidance with no stated method. Averages from a science center, an art museum and a famous national museum are not the same thing, and I do not claim they combine into one number. The stored hours are a snapshot of what the app holds, not live opening times, so holidays and seasonal hours are not modelled; check each museum's own page before you go. The opening-span figures pool every city deck in the app, so they describe the places this app holds, not museums everywhere, and they say nothing about how long anyone stays.
