The apps, one at a time

1. Visited: the closest match for ticking off places

Visited's Play listing (developer Arriving In High Heels) says you can mark countries, states and cities you have been to on a world map, US map and scratch map, keep a journal and use country and state counters. Its site adds that you can build a map of countries you have been to, want to visit and have lived in, with city and regional views, and sorts travel lists into categories such as world heritage sites and national parks. The site lists the Cities Map under Premium features, so the city view may not be in the basic version; I did not test that. Play shows 100K+ downloads, a 3.9 score on a small number of reviews, Contains ads, updated 2 October 2026, and a data-safety line that no data is shared with third parties.

2. NomadSwipe: a different need, not a visited tracker

Different need: places you want to go, pinned on one trip map

I built NomadSwipe (Android), so weigh this with that in mind. It is a trip planner, not a visited-countries tracker. You pick cities, it deals real places one at a time, and you swipe right or tap Save to keep one. Every saved place is pinned on one trip map where you build the trip. The Play listing describes no visited-countries counter, no scratch map and no travel stats, so if you want to see a count of countries on a map, use one of the apps around it in this list.

The listing does say its six colour labels can be renamed, and gives Day 1 and Must see as examples. If you already use it for planning, you could name one label "Been there" and filter by it. That is my suggested workaround, not a feature built for tracking, and I claim nothing more than the listing says. NomadSwipe is Android only today.

3. been: track visited countries (Prostachki Ltd)

This is the Play title and developer exactly as listed, so it is easy to confuse with other apps named Been. The listing says you can mark countries you have visited, view detailed maps, keep a customizable logbook and share your tracked countries. Play shows 1M+ downloads, 4.8 from 12.4K reviews, updated 4 November 2026, an In-App Purchases section, and a data-safety line that it may share app activity, app info and performance, and device or other IDs with third parties. A first guess at the address com.beenapp returned not-found, so I do not mean that app.

4. Travel Tracker (Countries Been): the most checklists

Developer Changzhi Ai. Its listing lists six maps in one app (world countries, US states, European countries, Chinese provinces, Indian states, US national parks) with progress percentages, a short introduction for almost every place, nine languages, cloud sync, image and PDF export, and CSV export of your data. The text says it is built for iPhone and iPad as well. Play shows the lowest download bracket and updated 25 September 2026, so it is a small app; I read no ratings.

5. Polarsteps: automatic route and journal

Polarsteps' Play listing describes automatic route tracking on a digital world map, photos, videos and stories added to each step, travel stats with countries visited and distance, trip reels and printed travel books. Its site says tracking keeps working when you are offline and calls the app ad-free. It is a journal first, so it suits people who want a record of a trip rather than a tick-list. Play shows 10M+ downloads, 4.7 from 200K reviews, In-app purchases, updated 20 November 2026.

6. Google Maps: Timeline and saved lists

Google's Timeline help page says Timeline automatically saves your visits and routes on each signed-in device, is off by default and can only be turned on if you opt in, and is not available on a computer. A separate help page says you can create lists of places such as favourites or places you want to visit. Neither is a countries counter. The Play listing shows Contains ads, 10B+ downloads, 4.4 from 19.6M reviews, updated 18 November 2026.

7. Mapstr: favourite places on one map

Mapstr's Play listing says you can save favourite places on one map, sort them by tags, add comments and pictures, plan trips, follow friends' maps and check your map offline. That makes it a fit for places worth returning to rather than countries you have been to. Its site is in French and links a web version and a Mapstr pro page. Play shows 500K+ downloads, 4.2 on a small number of reviews, updated 31 October 2026, and a data-safety line that it may share app activity and app info and performance with third parties.

8. Wanderlog: a different need, a trip planner

Wanderlog's Play listing calls itself a trip planner with itineraries, reservations and places pinned on a Google Maps-based map, and says offline access to trip plans is a Pro feature. It does not describe a visited-places tracker. Play shows 1M+ downloads, 4.8 from 38.1K reviews, updated 16 November 2026.

Who should pick which

  • You want a map of countries you have been to: Visited, been: track visited countries or Travel Tracker. Travel Tracker has the most checklists; Visited adds states, cities and themed lists.
  • You want a record of each trip with photos: Polarsteps, whose listing describes routes, steps and printed books.
  • You want it to log itself: Google Maps Timeline, if you accept turning on location history, or Polarsteps' route tracking.
  • You want a map of places you loved: Mapstr, or Google Maps lists.
  • You are choosing places for the next trip: NomadSwipe (I built it, Android only today) or Wanderlog. Both are planners, not trackers.

FAQ

What is the best app to keep track of places visited?

It depends on what you count. For ticking off countries, states and cities, Visited, been: track visited countries and Travel Tracker are built for it. For an automatic route and journal, Polarsteps. For an automatic visit history, Google Maps Timeline, which is off until you opt in. For saved addresses, Mapstr. I built NomadSwipe (Android), which does a different job: it saves places you want to go. I installed none of these, so this is what their pages say.

Is there an app that tracks the places I visit automatically?

Two of the pages I read say so. Polarsteps lists automatic route tracking and a countries-visited count, and its site says tracking continues offline. Google's help page says Timeline automatically saves your visits and routes on your signed-in devices, that it is off by default, and that it only works in the Google Maps app, not on a computer. I did not test either.

Which app counts countries, US states and national parks?

Travel Tracker (Countries Been) lists six checklists on Google Play: 241 countries and territories, 50 US states, 51 European countries, 34 Chinese provinces, 36 Indian states and 63 US national parks, with percentages. Visited and been: track visited countries both describe country tracking on their Play listings, and Visited adds US states and cities.

Can NomadSwipe track the countries I have visited?

No. I built NomadSwipe (Android), and it is a trip planner: you swipe real places, save the ones you want to go to and see them pinned on one trip map. Its listing describes no visited-countries counter, scratch map or travel stats. The listing says its six colour labels can each be renamed, so you could name one Been there, but that is a workaround I am suggesting, not a feature built for tracking.

Can I keep a list of places I want to visit and places I have been?

Yes, with a saved-places app. Google's help page says you can create a list of places such as favourites or places you want to visit, and Mapstr's Play listing says you can pin places on your own map, sort them by tags and add comments and photos. Neither page describes a visited counter.

Do these apps work offline?

Some say so. Polarsteps' site says tracking keeps going off the grid, Mapstr's listing says you can check your map offline, Wanderlog's listing marks offline access to trip plans as Pro and the Google Maps listing mentions offline maps. The Visited, been: track visited countries and Travel Tracker pages I read did not mention offline use, and I make no offline claim for NomadSwipe here.
