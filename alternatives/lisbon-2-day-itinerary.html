Lisbon itinerary for 2 to 4 days, planned from 29 real places

A Lisbon itinerary for 2 to 4 days: 16 places from the NomadSwipe store-listing sample trip, picked by swiping and pinned on a map, plus 13 more stored places for days 3 and 4. The stops are grouped by area, so each day mostly stays on one side of the city.

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, and I wrote this guide. The places come from that sample trip. Hours and prices change, so check each place's website before you go. Updated 10 November 2026.

Before you start

  • Stay in or near the old town if you want to walk between day one's stops.
  • Alfama and the castle are steep. Wear shoes you can climb in.
  • Check each site's own opening days before you go. Days 1 and 2 list no hours. For days 3 and 4 the table says which places have stored hours, and 5 of the 13 have none.

Day 1: castle, old town and river

Most of day one is on foot. Alfama is Lisbon's oldest district. It runs down the slope between the castle above and the Tagus below, with a maze of narrow streets and small squares.

  1. Núcleo Arqueológico do Castelo de São Jorge: the archaeological area and museum inside the castle walls, at Rua de Santa Cruz do Castelo. Start here, while the morning is cool.
  2. Miradouro de Santa Luzia: a viewpoint on Largo de Santa Luzia.
  3. National Pantheon: on Campo de Santa Clara.
  4. Miradouro da Graça: a viewpoint on Calçada da Graça.
  5. Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara: a viewpoint on Rua São Pedro de Alcântara.
  6. Praça do Comércio: a square facing the river. The area was rebuilt after the 1755 earthquake, and the square was remodelled in that rebuilding (Wikipedia).
  7. Sail in Lisbon: a sailing trip on the Tagus. We have not checked its departure point or schedule, so check the operator before you go.
  8. Casa Lundum | Live Fado Show Alfama: live fado in Alfama, at Travessa do Almargem. Best kept for the evening.

Day 2: museums, the park and the far side

The day two stops are spread across the city. Plan a metro or taxi hop between the far ones: the Palácio Nacional da Ajuda, the Oceanário and the zoo. The two Gulbenkian stops share one park, so they make a good morning.

  1. Calouste Gulbenkian Museum: a collection that runs from ancient Egypt to the early 20th century (Wikipedia).
  2. Centro de Arte Moderna Gulbenkian (CAM): modern art, in the same park as the museum, on its southern edge.
  3. Palácio Nacional da Ajuda: a royal residence in the Ajuda parish. It was built after the 1755 earthquake and is now a museum, with the Royal Treasure Museum of crown jewels since 2022 (Wikipedia).
  4. Oceanário de Lisboa: in Doca dos Olivais, in the Parque das Nações area. Its permanent exhibit is One Planet, One Ocean (official site).
  5. Jardim Zoológico: Lisbon's zoo, at Sete Rios. It opened in 1884 (Wikipedia).
  6. Cinemas NOS Amoreiras: a cinema at Amoreiras, a good indoor pick for the late afternoon.
  7. Cervejaria Ramiro: a seafood restaurant on Avenida Almirante Reis, for dinner.

Also on the sample list: National Museum of Ancient Art, at Rua das Janelas Verdes. Wikipedia says the museum has been closed for renovation since 29 September 2025 (Wikipedia), so check its status before you plan around it.

Lisbon itinerary for 4 days: what days 3 and 4 add

Days 3 and 4 are built only from stored places that are not already on this page. That is 13 places in four groups: the old town and Baixa, the park and gardens to the west, the river to the far west, and one outlier to the east. I add no descriptions beyond each place's stored name, rating and review count, because I read no outside source for them. Distances are straight lines between the stored coordinates, not walking routes.

Which of these have opening times. Eight of the 13 have weekly opening hours stored in the app: the Fado Museum, Aljube Museum, Money Museum, GNR Museum, Pharmacy Museum, Reservatório da Mãe d’Água, Museu da Carris and the National Coach Museum. The other five have no stored hours, so I have no verified opening times for them and you must check them yourself: Museu Nacional do Azulejo, Praça Dom Pedro IV, Jardim da Estrela, Eduardo VII Park and Ponte 25 de Abril. Stored hours are a snapshot from October 2026 and can be out of date.

Day 3: Alfama, Baixa and Chiado, plus one outlier

Day 3 is the most compact day in the plan. In the order below, the five hops between the six stops add up to about 2.4 km in a straight line, so it is mostly a walk.

  1. Fado Museum (4.1 from 650 reviews, stored hours).
  2. Aljube Museum (4.6 from 334 reviews, stored hours), 0.45 km from the Fado Museum.
  3. Money Museum (4.5 from 201 reviews, stored hours), 0.58 km on.
  4. Praça Dom Pedro IV (4.2 from 2,176 reviews, no stored hours), 0.58 km on.
  5. Museu da GNR (4.3 from 136 reviews, stored hours), 0.21 km on.
  6. Pharmacy Museum (4.1 from 175 reviews, stored hours), 0.61 km on.

Outlier: the Museu Nacional do Azulejo (4.5 from 4,599 reviews, no stored hours) is 1.96 km east of the Fado Museum, away from everything else. Add it as a seventh stop at the start of day 3, or swap it in for one of the smaller museums.

Which weekday. My how many days Lisbon needs page, which read the stored hours on 9 November 2026, has the Fado, Aljube and Money museums closed on Monday, the Money Museum also closed on Tuesday, and the Pharmacy and GNR museums closed on Sunday. Wednesday to Saturday is the only run of days when all five of those stored-hours stops show as open. The two places with no stored hours could not be checked this way.

Day 4: the park, Estrela and the river west

Day 4 is spread out: the five hops add up to about 6.5 km in a straight line, with one long gap of 2.3 km between Jardim da Estrela and the river. Use the metro, a tram or a taxi for that gap. Take it as two halves and drop the second if you are tired.

  1. Eduardo VII Park (4.2 from 2,081 reviews, no stored hours).
  2. Reservatório da Mãe d’Água das Amoreiras (4.4 from 171 reviews, stored hours), 0.83 km from the park.
  3. Jardim da Estrela (4.4 from 378 reviews, no stored hours), 0.78 km on.
  4. Museu da Carris (4.6 from 277 reviews, stored hours), 2.32 km on.
  5. Ponte 25 de Abril (4.3 from 2,953 reviews, no stored hours), 0.9 km on.
  6. National Coach Museum (4.4 from 2,000 reviews, stored hours), 1.69 km on, the far west end of day 4.

Which weekday. The Reservatório is closed on Monday and the Museu da Carris on Sunday in the stored hours, so Tuesday to Saturday suits day 4. I did not read which days the Coach Museum is closed, so check it.

Sample and method for days 3 and 4

I built NomadSwipe, and these 13 places come from its stored Lisbon data, read in November 2026 with read-only queries. I counted distinct Google place IDs inside a rectangle from 38.68 to 38.76 N and 9.20 W to 9.10 W, kept the sights rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews, and got 19 places. Six of the 19 are already on days 1 and 2: the Calouste Gulbenkian Museum, the Centro de Arte Moderna Gulbenkian, the National Pantheon, the Palácio Nacional da Ajuda, the National Museum of Ancient Art and the Jardim Zoológico. The other 13 are days 3 and 4. Of the 19, 12 have stored hours and 7 do not. Of the 13 added places, 8 have stored hours and 5 do not.

Limits: my how many days Lisbon needs page counts 23 sights in a 5 km circle with a written list of sight types, and this page uses the 19 from a rectangle, so the two lists overlap but are not the same. That page also puts LX Factory and Doca de Santo Amaro in its river west day, and they are not in the 19, so they are not here. Ratings, review counts and hours were stored in October 2026 and may have changed. The order of stops is my own grouping of the stored coordinates and is not a tested route. The National Museum of Ancient Art is in the 19 but sits on day 2 and may be closed (see above).

Lisbon itinerary for 3 or 4 days: how to stretch this plan

Days 3 and 4 above add 13 places to the 16 on the sample trip. If you would rather not travel that far, there are two other ways to stretch the first two days.

  • For 3 days, split day 2. The Gulbenkian Museum, the CAM and the Amoreiras cinema make one day (my how many days Lisbon needs page groups Amoreiras and Gulbenkian into one area). The Palácio Nacional da Ajuda, the Oceanário and the zoo are far from each other, so give them their own day with metro or taxi hops, or drop one.
  • For a fourth day, add an area this plan skips. That page counts 23 sights rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews in the app’s Lisbon data, about 4 days at five a day, or 3 if you stay central. Its river west day adds LX Factory and Doca de Santo Amaro, which are not in this plan, next to the Ponte 25 de Abril and the Museu da Carris, which are on day 4 above.

Getting around

  • Metro: Lisbon's metro has four lines, run by Metropolitano de Lisboa. A contactless Viva Viagem card works on it (Wikipedia).
  • Trams, buses and funiculars: Carris runs the buses and trams, the Graça and Bica funiculars, and a lift (Carris).
  • On foot: the old town is steep. Use a tram or taxi for the hills between stops.
  • In the app: every place card shows its distance, and the list sorts by rating, distance or A to Z.
