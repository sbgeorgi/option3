Is AllTrails good? Yes for trails, not for a city trip

Is AllTrails good? Yes, if you hike or bike on trails: it pairs trail maps with community reviews, and its Plus tier adds offline maps. It is not built for choosing restaurants, sights or neighbourhoods on a city trip, and Trustpilot reviewers are harsh about subscriptions. Plus showed at $29.99 a year on its plans page on 20 November 2026.

Key facts

  • What it is: its Google Play listing calls it a "companion and guide" for hiking, biking, running and walking, with "over 500,000 trails" and reviews from a community of trailgoers. Its About page says 500K+ curated trails.
  • Price: the plans page showed Plus as "7 days free, then $29.99/year" with "Cancel anytime in settings". It offered a Peak trial too but showed no Peak price.
  • Behind Plus: unlimited map downloads for offline use, trail conditions, wrong-turn alerts, Garmin and COROS sync and Live Share; the feature-by-feature split is in AllTrails paid vs free.
  • Ratings: 4.9 from 1M ratings on the U.S. App Store, 4.5 from 389K reviews and 50M+ downloads on Google Play, but 1.4 from 244 reviews on Trustpilot.
  • Maintained: the Play listing was updated on 18 November 2026.

What AllTrails does well

  • Trail discovery with filters. The Play listing says you can search by location, interest and skill level, and find dog-friendly, kid-friendly, stroller-friendly or wheelchair-friendly trails. The U.S. App Store text adds filters for difficulty and elevation gain and trails with waterfalls or hot springs.
  • Community reviews and conditions. Both store listings lead with "detailed reviews and inspiration from a community of trailgoers". In its June 2024 review, TechRadar called it "a great community app" where every trail is vetted by a human before being shared.
  • Offline maps in Plus. AllTrails' help center says Plus lets you download a single trail, a whole park or your own defined area, so content and navigation work out of cell service. That is the feature the reviews single out as worth paying for.
  • Safety tools. Wrong-turn alerts and Live Share (friends watch your activity in real time) are both in Plus per the plans page.
  • Scale and polish. The U.S. App Store listing shows 4.9 from 1M ratings and an Editors' Choice badge. Google Play shows 4.5 from 389K reviews and 50M+ downloads.

Where it falls short for a city trip

  • The unit is a trail, not a place. Its navigation lists Trails, Parks and Points of Interest. On 20 November 2026 its homepage "Top cities" were Bryce, Springdale, Sedona, Paradise, Estes Park and Yosemite Valley: gateways to parks, not city-break destinations. If you are choosing between a food market and a museum, nothing I read says AllTrails helps.
  • The best features are for the backcountry. Offline downloads, trail conditions, wrong-turn alerts and Garmin sync matter when you leave the road. On a street map with signal they add little.
  • Paywalls. TechRadar's 2024 cons were "some filters, offline maps and other features locked behind paywall". Building your own route is a Peak feature per the help center and the Play listing; two Trustpilot reviewers (February and June 2026) complain that route plotting now costs more.
  • Ads and data. The Play listing is marked "Contains ads" and says a subscription removes "occasional ads". Its data safety section says the app may share location, personal info and three other types with third parties. That is the developer's own declaration and I did not check it against the app.
  • User-written data can age. TechRadar's tester found one route that led across a short stretch of private land and says user-driven information can be outdated. That review is from June 2024, so treat it as a pattern to watch, not a current defect.

The short version

As of 20 November 2026, AllTrails is good at what it was built for: finding and following reviewed trails, with offline maps behind a Plus plan that its plans page showed at $29.99 a year after a 7-day trial. It is the wrong tool for choosing places in a city. Expect split reviews: 4.9 from 1M ratings on the U.S. App Store, 4.5 from 389K on Google Play and 1.4 from 244 on Trustpilot. Questions about whether AllTrails is good

Is AllTrails good for hiking?

Yes, by its own pages and the reviews I read. Its Google Play listing describes more than 500,000 trails with reviews, filters and turn-by-turn navigation, and its U.S. App Store listing shows 4.9 from 1M ratings. A June 2024 TechRadar review called it well worth a free download even for occasional hikers.

Is AllTrails Plus worth paying for?

If you hike where signal is poor, yes: downloading maps for offline use is the headline Plus feature. AllTrails' plans page showed 7 days free, then $29.99 a year, on 20 November 2026. If you stay on busy, well-signed paths with coverage, the base app may be enough. I did not test either tier.

Is AllTrails good for city trips?

Not for choosing places. Its pages describe trails, parks and points of interest such as waterfalls and photo spots, and its top cities on 20 November 2026 were park gateways such as Bryce, Springdale and Sedona. For picking restaurants, sights and neighbourhoods in a city, a different kind of app fits better.

How much does AllTrails cost?

The plans page showed Plus at 7 days free, then $29.99 a year, on 20 November 2026. It showed a Peak trial button but no Peak price, so I quote none. A June 2024 TechRadar review gave Plus as $34.99, so prices have moved; read the amount on your own purchase screen.

Is AllTrails legit?

It is an established app: 50M+ downloads on Google Play and 1M ratings on the U.S. App Store. Trustpilot is far harsher at 1.4 from 244 reviews, mostly about subscriptions, trial charges and pop-ups. Check the trial terms before you start one.

What should I use instead of AllTrails for a city?

It depends on the job. To pick real places in a city and see them on a map, NomadSwipe (Android, which I built) is a different kind of app; it has no trails. For trail alternatives, see apps like AllTrails.
