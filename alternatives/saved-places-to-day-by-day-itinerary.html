How to sort a list of saved places into a day-by-day city trip itinerary

Saved places are a pile of pins, not a plan. Here is how to turn them into a day-by-day city trip itinerary: put pins that are close together on the same day, check which days each place is open, then put each day in order. Five steps, with screenshots.

Updated 9 November 2026. Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), and I wrote this guide. The screenshots are from the app's Lisbon sample trip.

1

Swipe and save the places you like

Open a city in NomadSwipe and swipe through real places. Each card shows the place, its category, distance and rating. Tap Save on the ones you want, Skip on the rest. Every save goes into your trip.

Using Google Maps instead? Tap a place and choose Save (click by click).

2

See all your saved places on one map

Open your trip and switch to the map. Every saved place is a pin, so you can see at a glance what sits near what. Tap a pin to open that place. A pin far from all the others is either a mistake to drop or a day trip to keep apart.

3

Put pins that are close together on the same day

This step is your call, and it is simple: places that sit near each other go on the same day. Day 1 is the pins you can walk or ride between in a few minutes, day 2 is the next group of pins, and so on. NomadSwipe does not build the days for you. It shows you the map so you can.

Does looking at the map really work? I took five of my 2-day city plans, hid the day labels and sorted the stops into two days by coordinates alone. It matched my own day for 62 of 77 stops. So where the stops are is most of the answer; adjust the rest by hand.

4

Check each place is open that day

Open a place and tap Website to check its opening days for the weekday you will be there. In my five plans, 11 of 58 stops with stored hours show Monday as closed and 6 show Tuesday as closed. If a place is closed, swap it for another stop near it, not for something across town.

5

Put each day in order and go

Start with the place that has the tightest hours, add lunch or coffee close to it, then move in one direction so you never double back. Anything you are unsure is open goes late in the day, where a closed door costs the least. Your trip also works as a list that you can sort by rating, distance or A to Z, and every place has a Directions button.

How many attractions per day is realistic

I counted it instead of guessing. Every numbered day list in my city guides on this site is one data point: 26 guides, 56 days, 443 stops. A stop is one numbered item in a day list, so it includes meals, a drink or an evening show where the guide has one. I built NomadSwipe, and these guides are plans I made from its saved places, so the numbers show how full I made each day, not how many a visitor enjoys.

Rule of thumb, from this data only: plan 7 or 8 stops a day. 39 of the 56 days fall there, and 50 of the 56 fall between 6 and 9. The 9 and 10-stop days I measured had stops about 1 km apart, and the two 6-stop days I measured had medians of 3.4 and 5.6 km between stops (see the method notes below). I did not measure walking time or how long anyone stays at a stop, so this is a count of what a built plan holds, not a limit.

When your list is too long for your days

Multiply your days by 8 to get a ceiling, and by 7 for a lighter pace. Three days is about 21 to 24 stops. If you saved 34 places, you need to lose about 10 to 13, or add a day.

Cut by cluster, not by rating. This is my reasoning, not something the counts prove. Take away the lowest-rated pin in every area and each day still has the same trips across town, with one stop fewer. Take away a whole cluster of pins, the one farthest from the rest, and a trip across town goes with it. So:

  1. Sort the pins into clusters on the map, as in step 3.
  2. Count each cluster. Fill each day with the clusters that add up to 7 or 8 stops.
  3. Drop the cluster that is left over, preferring the one farthest from the others. Drop places closed on your days next.
  4. Use rating only inside a cluster, to choose between two stops that do the same job.

The same counts answer 3 days versus 4: divide your list by 8. Up to 24 stops fits 3 days, and 25 to 32 needs 4. For a count by sights only, see my guides to how many days you need in Lisbon, Porto, Athens and Budapest. They assume 4 to 6 sights a day, so I did not mix them into this table.

Questions about turning saved places into a city trip itinerary

How many attractions or places should go in one day?

Across 56 days in my 26 city guides a day holds a median of 8 stops, counting meals and a drink; 50 of the 56 days hold 6 to 9. That is what I planned, not a rule. The closer the stops, the more fit: the 9 and 10-stop days sit about 1 km apart, the two 6-stop days 3 to 6 km apart. The table is in the section on how many attractions per day is realistic.

What if I have too many places on my list for 3 days?

Three days at the median of 8 stops hold about 24 stops with meals, fewer sights. Cut whole clusters first (the ones farthest from the rest), then places closed on your days, then doubles in one area. Use rating only inside a cluster. For Lisbon the app has 23 well-rated sights, and at six a day three days hold 18 of them.

How do I prioritize must-see places in a city trip?

Mark each place must, want or maybe. Put places with fixed hours or a closed day in first, keep every must, then fill the days with wants. Use rating only as a tiebreaker. Of the 23 well-rated Lisbon sights in the app, 13 have stored hours and 5 of those show Monday as closed.

Should I spend 3 days or 4 days in a city?

Count the well-rated sights, divide by how many you can enjoy a day, and check how close together they are. By that counting rule the app's data gives Lisbon 4.6 days at five sights a day, Porto 3.8, Athens 3.8 at four a day and Budapest 2.2. Where most sights are close, as in Porto, the lower number can work. It is a counting rule, not a measured fact. See the guides for Lisbon, Porto, Athens and Budapest.

What if a saved place is closed on the day I planned?

Swap it for another stop near it, not for something across town. In my five plans, 11 of 58 stops with stored hours show Monday as closed and 6 show Tuesday as closed, so a Monday or Tuesday trip usually needs a swap or two. Check the place's own page for the final answer.

How do I save places on Google Maps for a trip?

Per Google's help page: tap the place, tap its name or address at the bottom, tap Save and pick or create a list. A Private list is only visible to you. I did not find grouping by day on that page. Click by click: how to save places on Google Maps for a trip.

Method, sample and sources

Data. The NomadSwipe app's stored place data, read with read-only queries on 7 November 2026. The five plans are my published 2-day guides for Chicago, Lisbon, Minneapolis, Sedona and Yerevan (79 stops; 77 matched a card). A stop counts as having stored hours only if all seven weekdays are listed, and as closed if that day reads Closed.

  • A coordinates-only split into two days (two-group k-means from the two farthest stops, straight-line distance) matched my hand-made split for 62 of 77 stops: Chicago 16 of 16, Yerevan 14 of 17, Sedona 12 of 15, Minneapolis 11 of 15, Lisbon 9 of 14.
  • 58 of 77 stops have stored hours. Of those, 11 show Monday as closed and 6 show Tuesday as closed.
  • Across eight days in four plans (Chicago, Minneapolis, Sedona, Yerevan) a day holds 6 to 10 stops; the 6-stop days have medians of 3.4 and 5.6 km between stops.
  • Of 73 sight cards in six decks (rating at least 4.3, at least 300 reviews), 9 sit more than 50 km from the deck's stored centre: 5 of 17 in Sedona and 4 of 7 in Vienna, none in Chicago, Lisbon, Minneapolis or Yerevan.
  • The 3-versus-4-day figures and the Lisbon counts (23 well-rated sights, 13 with stored hours, 5 of those closed Monday) come from my four how-many-days guides. A sight counts as well-rated at 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews.
