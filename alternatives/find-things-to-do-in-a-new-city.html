How to find things to do in a new city, in 7 steps

How to find things to do in a new city: pick a centre point, keep places within walking range, judge each by rating and review count together, and check unrated ones before you cut. In the Brussels data I tested, 39 sights near the Grand-Place narrowed to 15. The same filter works on a rainy day and in your own city. If you would rather start from a review site's lists, apps like Tripadvisor compares the options.

Updated 10 November 2026. Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, an Android trip planner, and I wrote this guide. The numbers come from the NomadSwipe app's stored Brussels place data, read on 4 November 2026 with read-only queries. The rainy-day counts for five more cities were read on 10 November 2026. Outside facts come from the Wikipedia pages listed under Sources.

The 7 steps

  1. Pick one city and one centre point.

    A city name alone does not guarantee that every result is in the city. The Brussels deck holds 545 place cards, and 25 of them sit more than 50 km from the Grand-Place. The eight farthest are between 7,345 km and 9,046 km away, and the stored addresses of three of the gardens that surfaced are in the Bronx, Brooklyn and Washington, DC. Choose one anchor, such as the main square, and a radius you would walk or ride. I used the Grand-Place and 3 km, which kept 330 of the 545 cards.

  2. Separate sights from food, drinks and hotels.

    Mixed lists bury the things you came to see. Inside the 3 km ring the deck holds 143 restaurants, 73 hotels and 39 cards stored as a museum, park, garden, zoo, historical site or tourist attraction. Sights are about one card in eight. Filter by category first, then do the same pass for food. The stored label is loose, so read the name: four karaoke venues in the ring are typed as tourist attractions.

  3. Read rating and review count together.

    A rating says how happy reviewers were, and the review count says how many of them there were. You need both. Across the whole Brussels deck, 409 cards have a rating, the median is 4.1, and only 81 are 4.5 or higher. Of those 81, 28 have fewer than 100 reviews. Eight cards are rated a perfect 5.0, and seven of those have fewer than 20 reviews. My first-pass line is 4.0 or higher with at least 100 reviews. It is my own rule of thumb, not a standard, and the findings below show why I use two numbers.

  4. Check the unrated places before you cut.

    An empty rating means unknown, not bad. In the ring, 15 of the 39 sights have no stored rating, including Leopold Park and Parc de Forest. Just outside it, 5.4 km from the Grand-Place, the Atomium card has no rating either. Wikipedia says the Atomium was built for the 1958 Brussels World's Fair and stands 102 metres tall, so a rating filter alone would have dropped it. Scan the unrated cards by name and decide on what you know.

  5. Swipe fast and save only the clear yes answers.

    Going card by card is quicker than reading lists, because each place is one decision. In NomadSwipe a card shows a photo, a category tag, the distance, the rating and Skip and Save buttons. Swipe right or tap Save and the place goes into the trip's shortlist. Skip is a no. Keep the shortlist small enough to do in the days you have. To save places in Google Maps instead, read how to save places in Google Maps.

  6. Put the shortlist on a map and remove outliers.

    Open the trip and every saved place is pinned. A pin that lands in another country is a data slip, not a bargain, so drop it. Clusters show you which places can share a day. For turning clusters into days, use the 8-step method in how to plan a trip on a map. If your list came from a chatbot, mapping a ChatGPT itinerary walks through pinning and checking those places.

  7. Check opening hours the day before.

    Hours are stored for 371 of the 545 Brussels cards, and for 23 of the 39 sights in the ring, so 16 sights have none. A card shows whether a place is open where hours are known, but hours change, so confirm on the place's own page before you travel.

What to do on a rainy day in a new city

A rainy day changes one rule: filter by stored type before anything else. Of the 15 sights in the second table, 13 are stored as a museum, one as a tourist attraction (The Music Village) and one as a park (Parc du Cinquantenaire). Eight of the 13 museums are within 0.7 km of the Grand-Place by straight line: Illusiemuseum Brussel, Museum of Illusions Brussels, the Musical Instruments Museum, Brussels COMICS Figurines Museum, the Magritte Museum, BELvue museum, Brussels City Museum and the Museum of Erotics and Mythology (MEM). They are short hops apart, not walking times.

Going alone does not change the filter. The app's place data has no field for whether a place suits a solo visitor, so this page ranks on rating, review count and distance only. A “museum” type is also not a promise that a place is indoors or open, and the data holds no weather information, so check hours (step 6) and read the unrated cards by name (step 4).

Explore your own city like a tourist

The seven steps work in a city you already live in, with two changes. For step 1, use a square you rarely visit as the anchor instead of the main one. For step 4, you can judge the unrated cards from what you know, because an empty rating means unknown, not bad. Then keep it a tourist's list: sights only, within about 3 km, rated 4.0 or higher with at least 100 reviews. In the Brussels data that rule cut 39 sights to 15.

Check that the deck has enough sights before you rely on it. In Brussels, sights were 39 of the 330 cards inside the 3 km ring, so most of a city's cards are food and hotels. A rainy-day plan in four moves

  1. Start the shortlist the evening before. Open the city's deck and swipe only the cards whose category looks indoors. Save the clear yes answers and skip the rest, as in step 5 above.
  2. Apply the same two-number rule. Keep places rated 4.0 or higher with at least 100 reviews, my own first-pass line, and read unrated cards by name.
  3. Open the map and keep one cluster. In the trip map every saved place is pinned. Pick the cluster where the pins sit closest together, so a wet day is spent inside the places rather than between them.
  4. Sort by distance and check the open status. The list can be sorted by rating, distance or A to Z, and a card shows whether a place is open where hours are known.

Stored hours matter more on a rainy day than on a dry one. On a dry day a closed museum costs you a detour, because the park next door is still there. On a wet day it can empty the whole afternoon. A card with no stored hours cannot tell you whether it is open, so the count in the last column is the number I would plan around, not the loose one. Hours still change, so confirm on the place's own page before you leave. The data has no weather field and no field for solo travellers, so none of this is specific to being alone. Going alone only means the plan has one person's taste to satisfy, and the shortlist can be as narrow as you like.

Explore your own city like a tourist: one new place a week

I built NomadSwipe to swipe real places in a city, and the same deck works on a city you live in. Search for your home city as the destination, then run this method.

  1. Pick one category. The app ranks food, sights, bars and stays separately. Choose one, such as museums, and ignore the rest for now.
  2. Swipe your own city's deck. Save only places you have never been to. Skip anything you already know, and skip anything that is simply not your taste.
  3. Check the map. Look at where the saved pins fall. A neighbourhood you rarely visit with two or three pins is a better first trip than a spread of pins across town.
  4. Go to one new place a week. Take it from the saved list, starting with the one closest to your usual route.

The arithmetic is simple. At one place a week, 12 saved places is 12 weeks. By the counts above, Athens holds 12 museum, gallery or aquarium cards that pass the stored-hours and rating rule, Lisbon and Porto hold 10 each, Budapest 5 and Chiang Mai 3. That is the length of the museum-only version of this method in each city, counting only cards that pass those rules. I have not run this method for a whole season, so I make no claim about what it changes. The count only tells you whether your city's deck is deep enough to try it.

Questions about finding things to do

How do I find things to do in a new city quickly?

Pick a centre point and a radius, separate sights from food and hotels, read rating and review count together, check unrated places by name, and save only the clear yes answers. Then look at the saved places on a map.

Is a high rating enough to pick a place?

No. In the Brussels data I read, 81 of 409 rated cards were at 4.5 or higher, and 28 of those had fewer than 100 reviews. Eight cards were rated 5.0 and seven of those had fewer than 20 reviews, so a rating from a handful of reviews tells you little.

Why can a famous place be missing from a ranked list?

Some places have no stored rating. The Atomium card in the Brussels data has none, so any filter on rating would drop it. Check unrated places by name before you cut.

How many reviews should a place have before I trust its rating?

There is no standard number. I use 100 as a first-pass line, which is my own rule. In the Brussels data the median rated card had 392 reviews, so 100 is a low bar there.

What can I do on a rainy day in a new city alone?

Filter by stored type first. In the Brussels data, 13 of the 15 sights that passed the rating and review rule are stored as museums, and 8 of those 13 are within 0.7 km of the Grand-Place. The data has no weather field and no solo-visitor field, and a museum type is not a promise that a place is indoors or open, so check hours before you go.

How many indoor places can I find for a rainy day in a new city?

Findings, method and sample

Sample and method. I read the NomadSwipe app's place data on 4 November 2026 with read-only queries. The destination named Brussels holds 545 cards, with 536 distinct normalized titles, so a few places appear twice. A separate destination named City of Brussels holds 17 more cards, which I left out. The stored rows were all last updated on 9 May 2026, so ratings and review counts are a snapshot. I measured straight-line distance from the Grand-Place at 50.8467 N, 4.3525 E, the coordinates Wikipedia gives for it. I counted a card as a sight when its stored type is museum, tourist attraction, park, garden, zoo or historical site. The 3 km radius, the 4.0 rating line and the 100-review line are my own choices.

  • 409 of 545 cards (75%) have a stored rating. The median is 4.1 and the mean is 4.09. 280 are 4.0 or higher and 81 are 4.5 or higher.
  • Review counts are skewed: among rated cards the 25th percentile is 116 reviews, the median is 392, the 75th percentile is 1,543 and the maximum is 43,752. 64 rated cards have fewer than 50 reviews.
  • 28 of the 81 cards rated 4.5 or higher have fewer than 100 reviews, and 19 have fewer than 50. Of the eight cards rated 5.0, seven have fewer than 20 reviews.
  • Rating and review count point in different directions. Among the 24 rated sights in the ring, the three highest-rated have 75, 859 and 152 reviews, and none of them is among the ten most reviewed. The three most reviewed are Manneken Pis (16,937 reviews, rated 3.3), the Musical Instruments Museum (4,150, rated 4.4) and the Parlamentarium (3,555, rated 4.2).
  • In the ring, 15 of 39 sights have no stored rating. 19 of the 28 tourist-attraction cards in the whole deck are unrated, and so are the Atomium, Leopold Park and Parc de Forest.
  • The deck is mostly not sights. In the 3 km ring, 143 cards are restaurants, 73 are hotels and 39 are sights, and 25 cards in the whole deck are more than 50 km from the centre.
  • Price level is empty on all 545 cards, so this guide says nothing about cost.
