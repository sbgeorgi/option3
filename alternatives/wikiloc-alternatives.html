Apps like Wikiloc: 4 trail apps compared, and where NomadSwipe fits

Key facts

  • Wikiloc is a trail-sharing and GPS route app. Its home page shows 22,396,635 members sharing 85,697,983 trails and 155,845,146 photos, and its Play listing says it covers hiking, cycling and over 80 other outdoor activities.
  • The four alternatives are the same kind of app. They differ mainly in how their trail catalogue is built (community, reviews, official routes or none), and in which parts sit behind a paid tier.
  • Wikiloc lists free offline maps on its own pages. AllTrails lists offline map downloads under its paid Plus plan (see AllTrails paid vs free), and OsmAnd lists unlimited map downloads under paid Maps+.
  • The Wikiloc pages disagree with each other on the size of its library (85.7 million trails on the site, 50 million on Google Play). I list that and one more mismatch below.
  • NomadSwipe does not record or navigate trails. Its Google Play listing describes no track recording, GPX files, topographic maps or turn-by-turn navigation.

What Wikiloc is and does

The Wikiloc site calls itself a place to discover the best outdoor trails for hiking, cycling and many other activities, with a list that runs from mountain bike and hiking to kayak, snowshoe and sail boat. Members upload their own GPS tracks from the phone app, and the home page promotes offline maps at no charge, live tracking, search by passing area and sending trails to your GPS. The Google Play listing adds that routes are recorded by community members, that you can send them to Garmin, Suunto, COROS and Wear OS devices, and that the phone can guide you with a direction indicator and sound alerts if you leave the path. It shows 4.6 from 162K reviews and 10M+ downloads, updated 13 November 2026.

The line between free and paid comes from two Wikiloc pages. The help article says the free app can discover trails, record your own, follow your own trails and the trails of Wikiloc ORG accounts, and download offline maps. The Play listing puts the route planner, 3D maps, advanced search filters, search by passing area and a weather forecast under Premium. So Wikiloc is not a case of offline maps behind a paywall; it is a case of planning tools behind one.

Why people look for apps like Wikiloc

I did not survey anyone, so these are the reasons the pages and a few dated reviews actually show, not a ranking.

  • Where the paywall sits. One 2019 review on the Play listing complains that following a trail with GPS needed payment, and the developer's reply says you can follow your own trails and ORG trails without paying and that the contribution is optional. That reply matches the help article above. Premium still holds the planner and filters, so if those are what you want, the cost comparison is worth doing with current prices.
  • The interface and joining hikes together. A 2022 review on the same page says the interface is not great, that it was hard to connect short hikes into longer ones, and that the reviewer would export the GPX files to another app to navigate. The developer replied asking for feedback. It is one review among the handful shown, so treat it as a lead, not a verdict.
  • Community routes against curated ones. Wikiloc says its routes are recorded by members. Outdooractive says it offers officially approved routes. I did not assess how reliable either kind is on the ground.
  • Data sharing. Wikiloc's Data safety section says the app may share device or other IDs with third parties, and may collect location and personal info. OsmAnd's section says no data is shared with third parties. I did not read this section for the other three apps.
  • Not a trail trip at all. If what you wanted was a city break, Wikiloc and its four alternatives all answer a different question from the one you had. That is where NomadSwipe fits, below.

Where the sources disagree

  1. How many trails Wikiloc has. The home page says 85,697,983 trails and 22,396,635 members. The Play listing text says "50 million" hiking, trekking and biking routes. They may have been written at different times; I cannot tell which is current.
  2. Wikiloc's review count. The Play header showed 162K reviews while the ratings block lower on the same page showed 159K. I quote the header figure and say Google Play showed it.
  3. What "free" means at Komoot. The Komoot home page says a komoot account at no cost lets you find, customise and navigate adventures. The Play listing says your first region costs nothing, permanently, and that offline trail maps, turn-by-turn navigation and the tracker in other areas come through single regions, region bundles or the World Pack. Read both before assuming you can navigate anywhere without paying. Step by step: how to use Komoot offline.

Alternatives to Wikiloc, one at a time

AllTrails: the biggest catalogue, with reviews

The AllTrails Play listing shows 4.5 from 389K reviews and 50M+ downloads, Editors' Choice, with ads and in-app purchases. It says you can search over 500,000 trails, with reviews, conditions and filters for dog-friendly, kid-friendly, stroller-friendly and wheelchair-friendly trails, and navigate with turn-by-turn guidance. The paid Plus plan adds offline maps, wrong-turn alerts, live sharing, 3D maps and ad removal, and the newer Peak plan adds creating a route from scratch and heatmaps of recent trail activity. The AllTrails site shows "Build custom route" and "Create a trip" in its navigation, and its own top-parks list is mostly U.S. national parks, which hints at where the catalogue is deepest but is not a coverage claim. One June 2026 reviewer on the listing says waypoint and photo-location editing moved behind the paid plan, and the developer asked them to email support; I did not verify that. Pick it if reviews and conditions matter more than a larger raw library, and if you will pay for offline maps. For a fuller verdict on AllTrails, see whether AllTrails is worth it.

Komoot: route planning by sport and surface

The Komoot Play listing shows 4.5 from 443K reviews and 10M+ downloads, Editors' Choice. It is built around the planner: pick road cycling, mountain biking or hiking and it shows surface, difficulty, distance and elevation profile, with turn-by-turn voice navigation and downloadable topographic maps. Community "Highlights" appear as red dots on the map, and the site adds curated Collections and filters by distance, difficulty or public transport links. It syncs with Garmin, Wahoo, Sigma and Bosch devices per the listing. The catch is the regional model described above. Pick it if you want to build a route to a spec instead of choosing from other people's recorded tracks.

Outdooractive: officially approved routes and map partners

The Outdooractive Play listing shows 4.5 from 78.2K reviews and 5M+ downloads, updated 5 November 2026, with ads and in-app purchases. It describes officially approved routes for hiking, cycling, ski touring and more, a route planner, saving individual plans or whole areas offline, and tracking on a Wear OS watch. The site lists Pro and Pro+ memberships, specialist maps (the German and Austrian Alpine Club maps, ADFC cycling maps, KOMPASS and others), GPX download, and activity types that include city walks and pilgrimages. The pages I read do not say which of those features are paid-only. One reviewer on the listing found the map selection underwhelming and the U.S. maps dated, so its strength may be regional; I did not check coverage. Pick it if you hike or ski in the Alps or want routes with an official stamp.

OsmAnd: the offline map first, trails second

The OsmAnd Play listing shows 4.5 from 255K reviews and 10M+ downloads, updated 18 October 2026. It is an offline OpenStreetMap app with navigation profiles for car, bicycle, pedestrian and more, GPX recording and import, route planning point by point, and map styles including topographic. Paid Maps+ adds unlimited map downloads, contour lines and offline Wikipedia and Wikivoyage; the OsmAnd Pro subscription adds cloud backup, weather and sensor support. The OsmAnd site says navigation works totally offline and lets you tap a hiking or cycling route on the map to see its elevation profile. What the pages I read do not describe is a shared library of other people's trails like Wikiloc's, so you bring or import your own GPX files. Pick it if the offline map and data control matter more than a trail catalogue. For a similar OpenStreetMap-based app, see our Organic Maps page, which covers track recording and hiking routes on that app's own site.

Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

For choosing places in a city, not for walking a trail

I built NomadSwipe, so weigh this with that in mind. It is an Android app, and it is not a Wikiloc alternative in the way the four above are. Its listing describes no track recording, no GPX import or export, no topographic or trail maps and no turn-by-turn navigation. If you want to record a hike, follow a mountain trail or share a route, use one of the apps above.

What it does: pick a city and it deals real restaurants, sights, bars and stays one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep one, and your saved places are pinned on a trip map, where you can see which ones sit near each other. Cards show category, rating, distance and, where hours are known, whether the place is open, and the list sorts by rating, distance or A to Z. You can open a place to see its photos and start directions. The plan the listing describes limits you to 3 trips and 2 searched places saved to a trip a month, with optional Premium on a Monthly or Lifetime plan. Up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online, and the offline copies expire. iPhone is coming soon.

A fair use alongside Wikiloc: the trail from the trailhead town. Pick the trail in a trail app, then use NomadSwipe to choose where to eat and what to see in the nearby city. I make no claim that the two share data. For the place-picking side, see swipe travel apps, saving places for a trip and planning a trip on a map.

FAQ

What are the best apps like Wikiloc?

AllTrails, Komoot, Outdooractive and OsmAnd are the four compared here. Each one lists GPS route recording or following and offline maps in some form on its own pages. AllTrails has the largest trail catalogue and reviews, Komoot has a route planner built around sport type, Outdooractive leans on officially approved routes, and OsmAnd is the most customisable offline map. I did not install any of them.

Is Wikiloc free?

Partly. Wikiloc's help article (last updated 10 March 2025) says the basic app lets you discover trails, record your own, follow your own trails and the trails of Wikiloc ORG accounts, and download offline maps. Its Google Play listing names the route planner, 3D maps, advanced search filters, search by passing area and weather forecast as Premium features. I did not read current prices.

What is the difference between Wikiloc and AllTrails?

Both let you find, record and follow trails. On their own pages, Wikiloc leans on a very large community library across 80 or more activities and lists offline maps at no charge, while AllTrails lists over 500,000 trails with reviews and conditions and puts offline map downloads in its paid Plus plan. Both list paid tiers; I did not test either app.

Is there an app like Wikiloc that works offline?

Yes. Wikiloc itself lists offline maps at no charge. OsmAnd describes a fully offline map and navigation app with GPX recording, Komoot lists offline trail maps for the regions you have, Outdooractive lets you save plans or whole areas offline, and AllTrails lists offline map downloads in its paid Plus plan. Which parts are paid differs by app, so check the listing before a trip.

Is NomadSwipe an app like Wikiloc?

No. I built NomadSwipe, an Android app where you swipe real places in a city, save the ones you like, see them on a map and build a trip. Its listing describes no track recording, no GPX files, no topographic maps and no turn-by-turn navigation, so it does not replace Wikiloc, AllTrails, Komoot, Outdooractive or OsmAnd on a trail.

Can NomadSwipe record or navigate a hike?

Its Google Play listing describes neither. You can open a saved place to see its photos and start directions, but I did not test what that does on a trail, and I make no claim that it works as hiking navigation.

Is NomadSwipe on iPhone?

Not yet. NomadSwipe is on Google Play for Android phones, and iPhone is coming soon.
