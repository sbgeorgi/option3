5 Days in NYC Itinerary: A Day-by-Day Plan Matched to Opening Days

A 5 days in NYC itinerary that works: day 1 Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island, day 2 9/11 Memorial and Brooklyn Bridge, day 3 MoMA and the High Line, day 4 Natural History Museum and Central Park, day 5 the Met. Avoid Tuesday for the 9/11 Museum and Wednesday or Thursday for the Met.

The plan at a glance

  • Before day 1, check your start weekday: the Met is closed Wednesday and Thursday, so some start days need a reorder (table below).
  • Different need, the open evenings: NomadSwipe (Android), which I built, for swiping real food and drink places and saving them to a map. The sights below come from official pages, not the app.
  • Day 1, the harbor: Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island by ferry from Battery Park, then the Staten Island Ferry at dusk.
  • Day 2, Lower Manhattan to Brooklyn: 9/11 Memorial and Museum, a walk over the Brooklyn Bridge, DUMBO and Brooklyn Bridge Park.
  • Day 3, Midtown to Chelsea: MoMA, then the High Line.
  • Day 4, Upper West Side: American Museum of Natural History, then Central Park.
  • Day 5, Fifth Avenue: the Metropolitan Museum of Art, then the park toward Bethesda Terrace, with the afternoon left open.

Which days of the week to avoid

Three official closures decide the order: the Met is closed Wednesday and Thursday, the 9/11 Museum is open Wednesday to Monday (“plus select Tues & holiday hours”), and four of the sights close on Thanksgiving and Christmas. I tested all seven start weekdays against the plan as written, and for each failing start a script found the reordering that moves the fewest days.

  • Tuesday: do not put day 2 on a Tuesday, because the 9/11 Museum is not open that day except on select dates. The Memorial outdoors is open daily 8 AM–8 PM per the same footer, so a Tuesday visit can still see the Memorial.
  • Wednesday and Thursday: the Met is closed both days. Put day 5 on a Friday, Saturday, Sunday, Monday or Tuesday.
  • Holidays: the Statue of Liberty (NPS), the Met, the Natural History Museum and MoMA list Thanksgiving Day, 26 November 2026, and 25 December 2026 as closed; the Met also lists 1 January and the first Monday in May. If your five days contain one of those dates, swap that day’s museum for a park, the bridge or the ferry.
  • Late openings: MoMA is open until 8:30 PM on Fridays and the Met until 9 PM on Fridays and Saturdays, so a Friday is the best day to put either one last.

Transit fare and what moving around costs

  • Subway and local bus: $3 for most riders, per the MTA fares page read on 25 November 2026. Express buses are $7.25.
  • Weekly cap: the OMNY fares page says that once you have paid $35 on subways and local buses within seven days, you ride free for the rest of that period, and your first tap starts the period.
  • Boats: the Staten Island Ferry has no fare and the Statue of Liberty ferry is part of its ticket, so day 1 needs no subway fare beyond getting to Battery Park.
  • In this plan: exactly one leg is over my 2 km line and not a ferry (MoMA to the High Line). The rest of your rides are between your hotel and the first stop of each day.

Questions about 5 days in NYC

Is 5 days in NYC enough?

Yes for the big sights, if you accept one anchor per half day. By straight line no day covers more than 6.5 km, and most of day 1 is a ferry crossing. It leaves no room for most of Brooklyn, Queens or the Bronx, so treat the evenings and the day 5 afternoon as your flex time.

Which days of the week should I avoid in NYC?

By the official pages I read on 25 November 2026, the Metropolitan Museum of Art is closed Wednesday and Thursday, and the 9/11 Museum is open Wednesday to Monday plus select Tuesdays, so Tuesday is the day to avoid it. The Statue of Liberty, the Met, the Natural History Museum and MoMA are also closed on Thanksgiving Day (26 November 2026) and Christmas Day (25 December 2026).

How do you get around NYC for 5 days?

The MTA page I read on 25 November 2026 puts the fare at $3 for most riders on subways and local buses, and the OMNY page says you ride free for the rest of a seven-day period once you have paid $35. The Staten Island Ferry has no fare, and the Statue of Liberty ferry is part of that attraction's ticket. I did not check station names or lines, so use the MTA's own trip planner for each ride.

What should I do in the evenings of a NYC trip?

Keep the sights for the day and use the evenings for food, drinks and views you pick yourself. The plan names one open-late anchor most evenings (for example the High Line until 10 PM before 1 December) and leaves the rest to your own shortlist. Method, sample and limits

Sources read on 25 November 2026. I read every hour and price in the tables with a headless browser on the day, from the official pages listed below. Where a page did not state a fact, the table says “not read” and I did not fill it in from memory. Coordinates come from each place’s English Wikipedia article (the High Line, which has no coordinate in the API, from its infobox). I computed distances with the haversine formula in Python on those points; the Brooklyn Bridge point is the middle of the span, so the legs on either side of it cover only half the bridge. The weekday table is a brute-force test of all 120 day orders against the closures.

  • Closed-day rules used: the Met closed Wednesday and Thursday, the 9/11 Museum not open on Tuesdays except select dates. Four of seven start weekdays (Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday) work as written; Monday, Saturday and Sunday each need a reorder.
  • Day distances by straight line: day 1 6.5 km, day 2 3.1 km, day 3 2.8 km, day 4 0.8 km, day 5 0.9 km; the farthest pair of stops on any one day is 3.0 km (day 1, across the harbor).
  • The restaurant ratings and review counts are stored place data from a read-only look at the app on 25 November 2026. I did not check which of the sights above have cards there.

Limits: hours change on holidays and by season, and I read each page once on one day. The 9/11 Museum’s “select Tuesdays” are not listed in the footer I read. I did not read the Staten Island Ferry timetable, the High Line, Central Park or 9/11 Museum prices, or any restaurant hours. Straight lines are not routes or times. The ratings above are the stored ratings of places, read from the app, and are not ratings of NomadSwipe.
