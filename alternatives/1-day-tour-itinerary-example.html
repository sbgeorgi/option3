The example: Rome in one day, hour by hour

I built this day backward from the earliest closing time, not forward from breakfast. In winter the Colosseum park closes at 16:30 and admits its last visitors at 15:30, so the park stops go first and the stops with later hours (the Pantheon until 19:00, the Trevi Fountain until 22:00) take the afternoon. Distances are straight-line figures I worked out from the coordinates on each stop's Wikipedia page, so the real walk is longer. I did not convert them into walking minutes.

What the official pages changed in my plan

  • The Colosseum has a compulsory entry slot. The official page says ticket sales, with a compulsory reservation of the time slot, open 30 days before the visit. That makes it the anchor: it goes first, and the rest of the day moves around it.
  • The last admission is an hour before the park closes. From 25 October 2026 to 28 February 2027 the park runs 8:30 to 16:30 with last admission at 15:30. The Forum and Palatine open at 9:00, so a 10:15 start sits inside that window.
  • One ticket covers three areas. The Colosseum, Roman Forum and Palatine ticket is valid for 24 hours, for a single access to the Colosseum without the arena and a single access to the Forum and Palatine. Tickets are issued in the holder's name, and an identification document must be kept visible at the entrances.
  • The Pantheon keeps long hours. Roma Turismo lists it daily from 9 to 19 with last entry at 18:30 and the ticket office closing at 18:00. Hours may vary during religious services, so check the day before.
  • The Trevi Fountain now has a ticketed basin. Roma Turismo says that from 2 February 2026 non-residents pay two euros to enter the basin, valid 9:00 to 22:00. Wikipedia gives the ticket hours as 11:00 to 22:00 on Mondays and Fridays and 9:00 to 22:00 on other days, with access free after 22:00. The two sources differ on Mondays and Fridays, and 15:00 is safe under both.
  • Left out for lack of a verified source. I did not find a Colosseum ticket price on the official page, a plain Pantheon entry price (only audio-guide bundles were listed), transit fares or walking times. Add them from the operator on the day.

My own read: the park stops fill the first half of the day and leave a long, loose afternoon, which is what a winter day needs. Whether five stops and a lunch is comfortable depends on you, and the first thing I would drop is the Trevi Fountain basin. Hours and prices change, so recheck each official page before you go.

How to build your own 1 day tour itinerary

  1. Find the earliest closing time first.

    Open each stop on the official page and write down the earliest closing time and the last admission. In this Rome day it is the Colosseum park at 16:30, with last admission at 15:30, so everything else is placed around it.

  2. Book the timed slot.

    If one stop needs a reserved entry time, book it first and put it in the earliest row that fits. In Rome that is the Colosseum, where the slot is compulsory.

  3. Group the stops by area.

    Put stops that sit near each other in consecutive rows. A stop in another part of town is a second trip, not a stop.

  4. Give every row a time allowance and a backup.

    Write how long you plan to spend, how you get there, and one swap for rain or a closed door.

  5. Check hours and closures on the day before.

    Open the official pages again. Hours change by season, and one-off closures are posted as news.

Check before you leave

  • The earliest closing time and last admission are written at the top of the plan.
  • The season is right: these hours are winter ones and change after 28 February 2027.
  • The timed slot is booked and the reference is saved in one note.
  • One-off closures are read on each official news page. The Colosseum park, for example, posted a Roman Forum Museum closure from 11:00 to 14:00 on 11 November 2026.
  • Free-entry days are noted: the park lists the first Sunday of each month, and the Pantheon site lists the first Sunday of the month too. The Domus Aurea is closed on the first Sunday of each month.
  • Every row has a rainy-day swap or is marked as the first to drop.

Questions

What is a good 1 day tour itinerary for Rome?

A day that starts with the booked Colosseum slot at 08:30, continues to the Roman Forum and Palatine on the same ticket, breaks for lunch near the Pantheon, then visits the Pantheon and the Trevi Fountain in the afternoon. The table above has the times, and the park hours in it are the winter ones, valid from 25 October 2026 to 28 February 2027.

How many stops fit in a 1 day tour itinerary?

Plan one timed anchor, two or three other stops and one meal slot. The Rome example has five stops and a lunch, which already fills about eight hours at the allowances I chose. Mark one stop as the first to drop.

Why does the Rome example start with the Colosseum?

Entry to the Colosseum is by reserved time slot, and the park closes at 16:30 in winter with last admission at 15:30. Putting the stop with the fixed slot and the earliest closing time first means a late morning cannot cost you the one visit you cannot swap.

Can I reuse this 1 day tour itinerary example for another city?

Yes. The plain-text block is not tied to Rome. Write the earliest closing time of the day first, add your timed anchor, group the other stops by area, and give each row a time allowance, a way to get there and a rainy-day swap.
