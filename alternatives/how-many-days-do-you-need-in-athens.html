How many days do you need in Athens? 57 sights counted

How many days do you need in Athens? About four. The NomadSwipe app's Athens data has 15 sights rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews, which is 3.8 days at four a day. The Acropolis hill is not in the data, so add a day for the ancient sites. It is a counting rule, not a measured fact.

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), and I wrote this page. Every app number comes from the NomadSwipe app's stored Athens place data, read with read-only queries on 9 November 2026. The few outside facts come from Wikipedia and are labelled. Hours, prices and visit times are left out, and the reason is under Findings.

The counting rule

The data has no visit times, so I cannot measure how long a sight takes. What I can do is count, and I state the rule so you can change it: days = sights worth seeing ÷ sights per day. My 2-day Athens itinerary plans 16 stops over two days, and 7 of them are sights (five museums, a gallery and a garden), so about 3.5 sights a day with meals and drinks between them. The Budapest count used 5 a day, but the Athens deck is museum-heavy (12 of the 15 well-rated sights are museums), and I assume a museum takes more of a day than a bridge or a statue. So I use 4 a day as the middle case. That is my choice, not a measured fact, and the sensitivity table below shows 3, 5 and 6.

Which sights count is also a choice. I counted the 15 sights stored with a rating of 4.0 or higher and 100 or more reviews as the "well-rated" set, and all 28 sights with any stored rating as the "everything rated" set. There are 57 real sight cards in all, but 29 of them have no rating, so I left them out of the day maths.

How many days: what 1 to 5 days hold

I sorted the rated sights by stored review count and cut them into days of four. This is a count of the app's data, not a route: the stops in a row are the next most-reviewed ones, so they are not always near each other. Allou Fun Park (stored rating 5.0, review count 55,043) is set aside as an outlier, because it sits 4.7 km from my centre point and is a fun park, so it would take a day of its own shape. The 75 sight-typed cards are not 75 sights. Four are not sights by name (a tour guide typed as a museum, a hotel called Park Inn typed as a park, a business typed as a garden and a park-management company), and 14 are duplicate copies of a place already counted, such as three cards for the Old Parliament museum and two each for the Acropolis Museum, the Benaki Museum and the War Museum. That leaves 57 real sights. Their stored types are 22 museums, 21 art galleries, 7 parks, 4 amusement places, 2 gardens and 1 beach. All 16 tourist-attraction cards fail the check as well: seven are clubs, bars or karaoke by name, four are tourism offices or tour companies, and five I could not classify from the title.

Of the 57 sights, 28 have a stored rating, 15 are rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews, and 30 have stored opening hours, which leaves 27 without. Of the 15 well-rated sights, 14 have stored hours. The deck is also lopsided: there are 336 places to eat and drink against 57 sights.

What the Athens deck does not have

This is the biggest limit on the count. Searching the card titles of both Athens rows found no card for the Acropolis hill, the Parthenon, the Temple of Olympian Zeus, Hadrian's Library, the Panathenaic Stadium, Anafiotika, Philopappou Hill or the Zappeion. Lycabettus and Kerameikos only turned up inside restaurant, hotel and bar names. The Ancient Agora appears only as a museum card, the Stoa of Attalos, which has no stored rating. Wikipedia describes these places this way:

  • The Acropolis is a rocky hilltop about 150 m above sea level with a summit of roughly 3 hectares, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987, with the Parthenon, the Propylaea, the Erechtheion and the Temple of Athena Nike on it (Wikipedia).
  • The Ancient Agora lies northwest of the Acropolis, and the reconstructed Stoa of Attalos in it now houses the Museum of the Ancient Agora (Wikipedia).
  • The Temple of Olympian Zeus is about 500 m southeast of the Acropolis and about 700 m south of Syntagma Square, and was dedicated in 132 AD (Wikipedia).
  • The Panathenaic Stadium is in Pangrati, east of the National Gardens, is described as the only stadium in the world built entirely of marble, and hosted the opening and closing ceremonies of the first modern Olympics in 1896 (Wikipedia).

If you add those four as your own pins, that is four more sights, or about one more day at four a day. So by my rule a first visit that includes the ancient sites is about five days, not four. I have no source for how long any of them takes, so that day is arithmetic, not a measured visit time.

Who needs fewer days, who needs more

  • Two days is enough if you want the headline museums and a garden. Two days at four a day holds 8 of the 15 well-rated sights, and my Athens 2-day guide plans 7, all from the well-rated 15.
  • Three days holds 12 of the 15 at four a day, or all 15 at five a day, if you keep days light on museums.
  • Four days is the default if you want all 15 well-rated sights without rushing: 15 ÷ 4 is 3.8.
  • Five or more days if you add the Acropolis, the Agora, the Temple of Olympian Zeus and the stadium as pins, or you walk through museums slowly (3 a day is 5 days for the 15).
  • Food and nightlife do not change the number. The deck has 336 places to eat and drink and 75 bars and clubs within 6 km, 120 of them rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews. At about three food stops a day you use a handful.
  • Allou Fun Park is the outlier. It is stored 4.7 km from my centre point by straight line, and every other well-rated sight sits within 1.8 km of it, so a day that includes the fun park is a different shape.

What this count cannot tell you

  • How long a sight takes. The data has no visit times, so four a day is my assumption, not a measurement.
  • Prices, tickets, opening days and getting around. I left them out. The visitgreece.gr Acropolis page I read for this page lists no hours or prices, and the Wikipedia pages I read do not cover them. Check each place's own site for hours and tickets.
  • Stored hours. 30 of the 57 sights have stored hours, and they date from a snapshot between May and July 2026. Check the day you plan to go.
  • Sights the deck lacks. A deck holds only the places the app has, so a famous sight with no card is not counted, and in Athens that includes most of the ancient sites.

Questions about how many days to spend in Athens

Is 3 days enough in Athens?

By my counting rule, it covers the museum-led sights the app's data rates well. Three days at four sights a day is 12 slots, which holds 12 of the 15 sights rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews, and five a day (15 slots) holds all 15. It is not enough if you add the monuments the deck lacks, such as the Acropolis hill, the Ancient Agora and the Temple of Olympian Zeus, which by my rule would need about one more day.

Is 2 days enough in Athens?

Two days holds 8 sights at four a day, which covers 8 of the 15 well-rated ones. My Athens 2-day guide plans 7 sights across two days, mixed with meals and drinks, and every one of them is in the well-rated 15. Two days fits if you want the headline museums and a garden and are happy to leave the rest.

How many days do you need in Athens for the Acropolis and the ancient sites?

The Athens data has no card for the Acropolis hill, the Ancient Agora site, the Temple of Olympian Zeus or the Panathenaic Stadium, so the counts here leave them out. Add each as your own pin. Four added pins is about one more day at four a day by my rule. I found no hours or visit times I could cite, so check each site's official page.

Do restaurants and bars change how many days I need in Athens?

Not in this data. Within 6 km the deck has 336 places to eat and drink, 102 of them rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews, plus 75 bars and clubs. At about three food or drink stops a day, four days use 12 stops, so sights set the day count and food does not.

Findings, method and sample

Sample and method. I read the NomadSwipe app's place data on 9 November 2026 with read-only queries. Athens is stored as two destination rows (country code GRC), both named Athens and sharing one centre point (37.9842 N, 23.7281 E), holding 499 and 248 cards. Together that is 747 cards with 739 distinct Google place IDs. I removed repeated place IDs, measured distance by straight line from the stored centre point, and kept the 593 cards within 6 km, which carry 541 distinct titles. I counted a card as a sight if its stored type was museum, art gallery, garden, park, amusement place or beach and its title was not obviously something else, and I merged cards with the same or near-identical title into one. "Well-rated" means a stored rating of 4.0 or higher with 100 or more stored reviews. I computed the days myself as sights divided by sights per day.

  • 747 cards, 739 distinct place IDs, 593 within 6 km, 541 distinct titles within 6 km, 291 of the 593 with stored opening hours.
  • 75 sight-typed cards become 57 real sights: 22 museums, 21 art galleries, 7 parks, 4 amusement places, 2 gardens and 1 beach. That is six stored categories, but museums and galleries make up 43 of the 57.
  • 28 of the 57 sights have a stored rating, 15 are rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews (12 museums, Taf The Art Foundation, the National Garden and Allou Fun Park), 30 have stored hours and 27 do not. 14 of the 15 well-rated sights have stored hours; the Benaki Museum of Islamic Art does not.
  • All 16 tourist-attraction cards fail the sight check (7 clubs, bars or karaoke by name, 4 tourism offices or tour companies, 5 unclassifiable from the title).
  • Days by rule: the 15 well-rated sights need 2.5, 3.0, 3.8 or 5.0 days at 6, 5, 4 or 3 sights a day, and the 28 rated sights need 4.7, 5.6, 7.0 or 9.3.
  • The 14 well-rated sights other than Allou Fun Park sit within 1.8 km of the centre point; Allou Fun Park is 4.7 km out.
  • Within 6 km there are also 336 places to eat and drink (102 rated 4.0 or higher with 100 or more reviews), 75 bars and clubs, 73 hotels and 18 other non-food cards.
  • No card matched the Acropolis hill, the Parthenon, the Temple of Olympian Zeus, Hadrian's Library, the Panathenaic Stadium, Anafiotika, Philopappou Hill or the Zappeion. The Ancient Agora matched only the unrated Stoa of Attalos museum card.

Limits: this is a counting rule, not a measured visit time, and four sights a day is my assumption, set from my own 2-day guide (3.5 sights a day) and rounded up. Ranking by review count favours popular cards and puts the unrated cards last. All the cards in the 6 km set were last updated between 2 May and 16 July 2026, so ratings, review counts and hours may have changed. Merging duplicate cards by title and judging a card a non-sight from its title were my own calls and a different reader could count a few differently. The 6 km radius and the centre point are my choices, and stays are counted but never limit the day count. I read no page that gave ticket prices or visit times, so the page has none: the visitgreece.gr Acropolis page lists neither, and I did not source the official Greek museum and monument sites.
