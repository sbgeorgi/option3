What GPSmyCity costs

The numbers that decide "worth it" are on the U.S. App Store listing (read 28 November 2026). It lists three yearly options, each with a 3-day trial: $14.99 for the walks in 670+ cities, $14.99 for travel articles in 1,300+ cities, and $20.99 for all of it across 1,800+ cities. At those prices the walks plan is about $1.25 a month and the all-in plan about $1.75 a month. The text says each subscription renews automatically unless you cancel at least 24 hours before the trial or period ends, and that you turn renewal off in your App Store account.

Two cautions. The first is Android: Google Play shows "Contains ads" and "In-app purchases" and describes a "small payment" to get route maps, but the text I read states no amount, so I cannot say the Android prices match the iPhone ones. The second is the older pay-per-city model. A 2018 review on the App Store listing says the upgrades were "less than $5 at the time" before the yearly subscription arrived. The current listing text does not mention per-city purchases, so I could not confirm whether they still exist. The earlier is GPSmyCity free page has the feature-by-feature split of free and paid; this page is about whether the paid part earns its price.

What paying changes in practice

On the free side, the listings say you can open each walk, see its attractions, use the offline map and read travel articles. After upgrading, the listings add walking tour maps, turn-by-turn directions, creating your own walk and no ads; the App Store text calls the directions "voice guided". An Android user wrote on Google Play on 12 October 2026 that they upgraded "to get the dialogue commentary and GPS locator" and that it helped "even when not doing a tour". That is one person, but it matches what the blog review describes: navigation plus narration with history of each stop, address, hours and whether entry is free or paid.

The practical test is whether you will follow a route. If you like being told where to turn next and what a building is, the upgrade is the product. If you plan by choosing a few places and wandering between them, the route layer adds little, and the free offline map may already be enough.

What users say, and how current the app is

Store scores are decent and the app is maintained. Google Play showed 4.4 from 2.96K reviews (a lower section of the same page says 2.91K), 500K+ downloads and an update on 27 October 2026, though the "What's new" text only says "New features and performance improvement". The App Store showed 4.0 from 1.3K ratings, and justuseapp lists the iPhone version as published on 2026-09-23. The developer replies on Play: the three reviews shown on the page I read, dated 20 September to 19 October 2026, each carry a reply from GPSmyCity.com, Inc.

Praise and complaints both sit in the reviews. A Play reviewer on 20 September 2026 said the number of sites "competes with" DK Travel and Lonely Planet guidebooks, and another called it "cheaper than even one guide book". The complaints on justuseapp, which reproduces App Store reviews, are about reliability and depth: one reviewer could not download maps until the developer fixed the purchase restore, one said the walk map did not show all the places an article named, and one could take only one neighborhood tour in a city. The blog review's own improvement list is smaller: navigation and narration audio overlap, and you have to check in at each stop by hand. I read 4 complaints and 3 praise reviews on that page, far too few to estimate how common any problem is. The Trustpilot page I opened showed 2 reviews, so I did not use it.

Two practical limits come from GPSmyCity's own tutorial page. Turn-by-turn depends on built-in GPS hardware, so it warns that cheap Android phones and Wi-Fi-only iPads may lack it, and it suggests carrying a power bank because GPS drains the battery.

Decide in 3 minutes

  1. Find your city. Open the GPSmyCity site's city list and see whether it has walks there. Walk guides are in 670+ cities; articles are in 1,300+. If your city only has articles, the $14.99 walks plan will not give you a route.
  2. Try the unpaid part on that city. The listings say you can see the attractions and use the offline map for each walk before paying.
  3. Pay only if you want to be led. If you want directions to the next stop, the trial is the cheap way to find out, as long as you cancel at least 24 hours before it ends.
  4. Pick the plan by content. Walks and articles are separate $14.99 plans, and the $20.99 plan has both. Do not buy articles if you wanted routes.
  5. Check the price on the day. These numbers are from 28 November 2026 and the Android prices were not stated in the text I read.

The short version

As of 28 November 2026, GPSmyCity is worth paying for if you want ready-made walking tours with turn-by-turn directions in a city that has a walk guide: $14.99 a year on the U.S. App Store for walks, or $20.99 with articles, with a 3-day trial. It is not worth paying for if you only want to see what a walk visits and use an offline map, because the listings say that part costs nothing, or if you would rather choose your own places. Check that your city is among the 670+ with walk guides, and check the Android price, which I could not confirm.

Questions about whether GPSmyCity is worth it

Is GPSmyCity worth paying for on a short city break?

Yes, if you want to be walked along a route: on 28 November 2026 the U.S. App Store listing showed $14.99 for a year of the walks plan, with a 3-day trial. No, if you only want to see what a walk visits and carry an offline map, because the listing says that part costs nothing.

How much is GPSmyCity?

On 28 November 2026 the U.S. App Store listing showed $14.99 a year for city walks, $14.99 a year for travel articles and $20.99 a year for both. Google Play marks the app "In-app purchases" but the text I read gave no amounts, so I cannot confirm the same prices on Android.

Does GPSmyCity work offline?

Yes. Both store listings say the walks and articles work without a data plan, and the GPSmyCity site says "No Roaming. No Data Plan Needed." Its tutorial page adds that the turn-by-turn function depends on the phone having built-in GPS hardware, and that GPS use drains the battery.

Can I build my own tour in GPSmyCity without paying?

No, going by the listings. The App Store and Google Play both list creating your own walk under the features you get after upgrading. The GPSmyCity home page says custom walks are offered in 670+ cities.

What if I only want to pick places, not follow a route?

Then a walking-tour app is more than you need. NomadSwipe (Android, which I built) lets you swipe real places in a city, save the ones you like and see them on a map. It has no turn-by-turn directions or narration, so it is a different tool, not a GPSmyCity replacement.
