What happened to Maps.me? A sourced timeline from 2011 to 2026

What happened to Maps.me? It was sold twice: Mail.Ru Group bought it in 2014, then announced on 2 November 2020 the sale to Daegu Limited for RUB 1,557 million. A redesign and a community fork, Organic Maps, followed. The app is still on sale, now under Convexity Holdings AG.

What changed for people who used the app

Read in order, the sources describe a product that went from paid to free to monetised. The 2014 move to a free app came with a corporate owner; by 2016 the app showed Booking.com hotels, per the OSM wiki history. The 2020 sale notice shows why an owner would push for revenue: the notice gives 2019 revenue of RUB 159 million against an EBITDA loss of RUB 25 million.

After 2020 the changes that users complain about are mostly about access. The OSM wiki says the developers moved more features behind a subscription, capped free map downloads at 10 regions in 2023 and again from spring 2024, and changed the editing functions in 2023, after which editing existing places was no longer possible. A U.S. App Store reviewer dated 24 June 2023 describes the same wall, in their words asking for money to download more than a certain number of offline maps; that is one person's account on the listing, not a measurement.

The current App Store listing shows a mix of the old and new product. It still leads with offline maps and turn-by-turn navigation for driving, walking and cycling, and it adds travel guides, of which the listing says some are in the subscription and others sold separately. Its privacy label says purchases, financial info, location, contact info and other data may be collected and linked to your identity; Organic Maps, by contrast, says on its own site that it has no ads, no tracking and no data collection. Each is the developer's own claim, and I tested neither.

The Russia-related story is about a wallet, not the map

Two things are easy to blur here, so this section keeps them apart. First, the old ownership trail: the app was built by a team that moved to Moscow after the 2014 purchase, and the seller in 2020 was a Russian internet group (renamed VK in 2021). Second, a payments story in 2022 and 2023, which is what the Guardian covered.

The September 2022 release announced a Maps.me wallet with a Mastercard prepaid card, Apple Pay for iPhone wallet users and a waiting list of over 1 million. Six months later the Guardian reported on 26 March 2023 that Alex Grebnev, who it says runs the payments, mapping and cryptocurrency app Maps, had a problem: at least 111 Russian users had been found signed up for the card, business partners worried it may have breached EU sanctions imposed after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and Monavate ended its deal amid concerns over checks on users' location. The article also says Grebnev bought Maps.me from Mail.ru Group in 2020 for about $20m.

The article reports a response too: N2, a company it names, rejects the claims and the allegations; the article says it is understood N2 blamed a technical fault by a payment processor and said the situation was quickly rectified and no sanctions were breached. I can only pass on what a newspaper reported. I read the article through a Yahoo UK copy that cut off in my text tool, so part of this detail came from a fetch reader that could see more of the page. I found no page that says how the map app itself changed for users in Russia, and I did not read any Russian-language source, so I make no claim there.

Where the sources disagree, and where they are silent

  • Who the buyer was. The 2020 notice and Wikipedia name a company, Daegu Limited, part of Parity.com Group. The Guardian names a person, Grebnev. The two could be the same deal (East-West Digital News lists Grebnev as a Parity co-founder), but no page I read spells out the link, so I do not.
  • Current owner. The App Store lists Convexity Holdings AG as seller, and the listing's copyright line reads MAPS.ME (CYPRUS) LTD; the official site carries a 2026 Convexity copyright. No page ties Daegu or Parity to either name, or says when the seller changed. That is a gap, not a finding.
  • When it became free. November 2014 on Wikipedia; December 2014 on the OSM wiki history.
  • When Organic Maps started. Wikipedia's Organic Maps article says a fork began on 20 December 2020 and joined the community effort in January 2021; the Maps.me article says January 2021. Both can be true.
  • When CoMaps started. June 2025 on the Maps.me article; early 2025 on the OSM wiki; the Organic Maps article puts a governance letter in April 2025 and says the lack of a resolution led to the new project.
  • When the card launched. 6 September 2022 on the Finextra release; 18 September 2022 in Wikipedia's text.
  • User numbers are not a trend. 1.8 million active users in 2014 (TechCrunch), 10 million monthly active users in the 2020 notice, and 60 million annual active users in the 2022 release count different things.
  • Money. The notice gives the EBITDA loss as RUB 25 million. Wikipedia converts it to US$0.39 million and East-West Digital News to $3.9 million, a tenfold gap, so I use rubles only.
  • Stale pages. Wikipedia's infobox lists version 12.0.1 from January 2021, the OSM wiki lists 17.5, and both carry maintenance or date caveats. Wikipedia's Maps.me article has tags for notability and reliance on primary sources, so I used it only where a primary page agrees or nothing better exists.

Summary: what to remember

As of 16 November 2026, Maps.me was sold in 2014 and again on 2 November 2020, was redesigned in a way community pages say removed features, spawned the Organic Maps fork, and is still on sale under Convexity Holdings AG, version 18.1.1. The Guardian's 2023 Russia and sanctions reporting concerns its payment card, and the company it names disputes it. The sources disagree on several dates and say nothing on how Daegu and Convexity relate, so treat those as open.

Questions about what happened to Maps.me

Is Maps.me still available?

Yes. On 16 November 2026 it was listed on the U.S. App Store (version 18.1.1, released 11 November 2026, per Apple's lookup record) and on Google Play, whose listing showed 50M+ downloads and an update dated 14 September 2026. No page read for this article announces a shutdown.

Who bought Maps.me in 2020, and is that the owner today?

Mail.ru Group announced on 2 November 2020 that it had sold 100% of Maps.me to Daegu Limited, a member of Parity.com Group, for RUB 1,557 million, per its notice. Today the App Store lists Convexity Holdings AG as seller and the official site carries a 2026 Convexity copyright. No page I read links Daegu or Parity to Convexity, so I do not say whether the owner is the same.

Did the Maps.me Russia and sanctions story involve the map app?

The reporting I read is about a payments, mapping and crypto app called Maps and its prepaid card. The Guardian reported in March 2023 that at least 111 Russian users had signed up for the card and that a Mastercard partnership ended; a company named in the article rejects the claims and says no sanctions were breached. I found no page about how the map app itself changed for Russian users.

When did Maps.me start limiting free offline map downloads?

The OpenStreetMap wiki, a community page and not the vendor, says a limit of 10 free map regions arrived in summer 2023, was removed a few months later and was back in spring 2024. I could not confirm the limit on an official page, so test your destination's maps before travelling.
