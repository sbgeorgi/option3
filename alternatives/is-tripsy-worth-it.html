What Tripsy costs, and what is free

Tripsy is free to download. Its App Store listing says some features need Tripsy Pro, sold as a subscription or a one-time lifetime purchase. On the pricing page, the Individual tab on 27 November 2026 showed Yearly at $19 for 1 year of Tripsy Pro, with $39 struck through and a 50% off label, and Lifetime at $149 as a one-time purchase, with $199 struck through and a 25% off label. The home page carries a “Black November Sale” banner reading “Up to 50% Off”.

That is the number to weigh. The $19 on the pricing page is a sale price, and the page does not say when the sale ends, so the usual price is probably the struck-through $39 a year, which is my reading of the page rather than a stated fact. The page also has a Family tab that I could not open, so I do not know what a family plan costs. The earlier Tripsy alternatives page quoted a different figure from a read on 2 November 2026; it now carries today’s numbers. I cannot tell from the text whether the older figure was a Family price, an earlier list price or something Tripsy has since changed, so check the live page before you buy.

The free plan is narrower than the word suggests. The pricing page has a feature grid with a check or an empty circle per row. The Free column has a check for four rows: unlimited trips, cloud sync, unlimited guests and flight search. Every other row has an empty circle in the Free column: forwarded reservations, advanced travel stats, flight update alerts, documents, a calendar feed, a 10-day weather forecast, timezone changes, data export and expense management. I read the icons from the page markup, not from labelled text, so treat that split as my reading. The App Store listing independently says Smart Import “requires Tripsy Pro”. One 2023 App Store review titled “Great UI, poor customer service, limited free version” says the same about the free tier; a 2025 review on the same listing says the free version is “great for those who only take a few trips”. Both are one person each, so they show how the free plan reads, not how most people feel.

What you get, and how current it is

Tripsy’s home page and App Store listing describe one trip that holds flights, stays, activities, reservations, documents and expenses. You forward a reservation email and it appears in the trip. It sends flight updates for delays, gates, terminals and baggage claims, shows a 10-day weather forecast, syncs the itinerary to your calendar and works offline. You can invite people to edit or to follow along view-only, and set what each guest can see. It handles expenses in several currencies and exports to CSV. The listing also mentions routes between stops with estimated times, and a connector for AI assistants. The pricing page lists Claude, Cursor, Codex, ChatGPT, Grok Bot, Gemini and Raycast under Integrations; I did not test any of them.

It is actively maintained. The Tripsy updates page lists version 3.10.10 on 23 November 2026, preceded by 3.10.9 on 18 November, 3.10.8 on 11 November, 3.10.7 on 31 October, 3.10.6 on 27 October and 3.10.5 on 23 October. The entries read as fixes and refinements, such as a crash fix, more reliable email syncing and faster startup. That cadence suggests a product being worked on, though it does not show the app is bug-free. Awards, per Tripsy’s home page: an App Store Awards 2024 finalist for iPhone App of the Year, and the App Store listing shows an Editors’ Choice badge.

The limits are in the listing too. It requires iOS 18 or later, and the same text says Smart Insights runs on your device and requires Apple Intelligence. Because Tripsy has no Android app, it has no Google Play rating either, so its only store score is the Apple one.

Tripsy for Android

This is where “worth it” ends for many readers. Tripsy’s Android page reads: “There’s no native Android app yet. You can already view your Tripsy itineraries in a browser at my.tripsy.app.” Under it: “View online. Create and edit in the Tripsy app.” and an invitation to join an Android waitlist. So an Android phone can open a trip someone on iPhone built, but cannot start one. The App Store listing lists iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. The page has no date for an Android release, so do not buy Pro now in the hope of one. Decide in 3 minutes

  1. What phone do you carry? If it is Android, stop here: Tripsy cannot create trips on it. Pick TripIt or Wanderlog from the table, or a trip planner app for Android.
  2. Do your bookings arrive by email? If yes and you are on Apple devices, forwarding is Tripsy’s best trick, and it is a Pro row on the pricing grid. Count how many trips a year you would forward before paying.
  3. Try the free plan on one real trip. It has unlimited trips, cloud sync, unlimited guests and flight search, so nothing is lost by starting there.
  4. Choose lifetime or yearly by your trip count. At the pricing page sale prices, $149 once equals about eight years of the $19 plan, and $19 a year is the cheap way to find out whether you use it. If the sale ends, the struck-through $39 and $199 are the likely figures, per my reading above.
  5. Re-read the live pricing page on the day you buy. My own 2 November reading differed from today’s.

Is Tripsy free?

To download and use, yes: the App Store listing says “Free · In-App Purchases” and the free plan has no trip limit. For the features most people name as the reason to choose Tripsy (forwarded reservations, flight alerts, documents), no: those need Pro. If you searched “is the Tripsy app free”, the honest answer is that the free plan is a real way to organise trips by hand, and Pro is what automates them.

Bottom line

As of 27 November 2026, Tripsy is worth trying for anyone on iPhone, iPad or Mac who wants flights, stays and documents in one trip: the free plan costs nothing, and Pro was $19 a year or $149 once in a sale. It is not worth it on Android, because there is no native Android app and the browser view cannot create trips. Check the live pricing page first, because the sale numbers differ from what I read on 2 November.

Questions about whether Tripsy is worth it

Is Tripsy free?

Partly. Tripsy is free to download, and its pricing page lists unlimited trips, cloud sync, unlimited guests and flight search in the Free column. Forwarded reservations, Smart Import, expenses, flight alerts, documents and a calendar feed are marked Pro. On 27 November 2026 the page showed Pro at $19 for a year (struck-through $39) or $149 once (struck-through $199).

Is there a Tripsy app for Android?

No. Tripsy's Android page says there is no native Android app yet, and offers a waitlist. You can view itineraries in a browser at my.tripsy.app. Creating and editing happens in the Tripsy app, which runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch.

How much is Tripsy Pro?

On 27 November 2026 the Individual tab of Tripsy's pricing page showed $19 for 1 year of Pro, down from $39 under a 50% off label, and $149 for a one-time lifetime purchase, down from $199 under a 25% off label. The home page carries a Black November sale banner, so the price may change. I could not open the Family tab.

What is a good Tripsy alternative on Android?

TripIt has an Android app and forwards-to-itinerary email import like Tripsy, and Wanderlog has an Android app with a map-first planner. If you only want to choose places in a city, NomadSwipe (Android, which I built) swipes real places and saves them to a map. It does not keep confirmation emails, so it is not a Tripsy replacement.
