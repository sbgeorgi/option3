How to use Mindtrip in 9 steps

How to use Mindtrip: open mindtrip.ai or the iOS app, click Set up your travel assistant and answer a few questions about your travel style, then chat with the assistant for ideas, save places, build an itinerary and book when you are ready. Mindtrip says it is free for travelers, and its paid product, Mindtrip for Business, is for tourism boards and hotels.

Key facts

  • What it is: Mindtrip's FAQ calls it a travel platform that uses AI plus human insight to turn inspiration into flexible plans. You can save and share ideas, ask questions, build itineraries and book, all in one place.
  • Where it runs: on the web and in the iOS app, per its FAQ. I found no Android app mentioned.
  • Price: free for travelers, per the FAQ. Mindtrip for Business is the paid product.
  • How you start: Set up your travel assistant on the homepage, which asks a few questions about your travel style.
  • The sections: the top bar of mindtrip.ai lists Stays, Flights, Eats, Experiences, Events and Trips.

Step by step

This walk-through is based on Mindtrip's own documentation, not on my own use of the app. Mindtrip's help area is a short list of FAQs rather than a numbered tutorial, so I put its statements in the order a new user meets them.

  1. 1. Open Mindtrip on the web or in the iOS app.

    The Traveler FAQs say Mindtrip is available on the web and in the iOS app, and the footer of mindtrip.ai has a Get iOS app link. The homepage top bar shows Stays, Flights, Eats, Experiences, Events and Trips, plus Log in and Set up assistant.

  2. 2. Set up your travel assistant.

    The homepage button reads Set up your travel assistant, and its closing section says: answer a few questions to help Mindtrip get to know your travel style. The page describes an assistant that remembers how you like to travel so you do not start from scratch each time, and that gets better at finding places the more you use it. Its example preferences are phrases like foodie spots but kid-friendly, street food over fine dining and local gems only.

  3. 3. Tell the chat about yourself.

    The FAQ answers "How do I get Mindtrip to match my travel style?" like this: tell it about yourself in the chat. When you ask for ideas or build an itinerary, share what you love doing, what you would rather skip, your budget, who you are traveling with and any pet peeves. The more you share, the more personalized the recommendations and plans become. The FAQ says it stores the travel preferences you share plus your signup and profile information, and does not store credit card details.

  4. 4. Ask for ideas.

    Per the FAQ, you can ask anything: it pulls from thousands of sources to surface ideas, places and events that match your interests, whether you are planning a big trip or just looking for something fun to do nearby. The homepage says the assistant weighs the tradeoffs and shows only options that fit, and a customer quote on it says the assistant explains why a place is a good fit, but that quote is Mindtrip's marketing, not a test.

  5. 5. Optionally, start from a link, video or screenshot.

    In July 2024 both TechCrunch and Axios reported a Mindtrip feature called Start Anywhere. TechCrunch says you can insert a link such as a Reddit post or blog and ask the assistant to build an itinerary from the places mentioned, or use a YouTube, TikTok or Instagram link, with a catch: TikTok only worked for videos with text overlays, and YouTube worked through transcripts. Axios adds that you can upload a photo or start from a copy of a hotel or flight reservation. This is two-year-old press coverage. Mindtrip's current homepage does not name the feature, so check whether it is still there under that name. Its FAQ does say you can save inspiration from socials, blogs and videos.

  6. 6. Save places into collections and look at the map.

    The FAQ says you can save inspiration from socials, blogs, videos or wherever it strikes into collections you share with friends or turn into a trip. TechCrunch's report describes seeing the mentioned places on a map and saving them to favorites or a trip list. The FAQ also says that once you are on the ground you get interactive maps.

  7. 7. Build the itinerary and adjust it.

    Per the FAQ you build flexible itineraries and tweak them as you go, or borrow ideas from other travelers and make them your own. The homepage's Build tile says the same in other words: create a trip around how you travel and keep your itinerary, ideas and bookings in one place. Public guides and collections on Mindtrip come from its creators and destination partners, so there is material to borrow from.

  8. 8. Compare, book and share.

    When you are ready, the FAQ says you can book flights, places to stay, restaurants and things to do without jumping between tabs, and keep your bookings and receipts in one place. The homepage says you can book directly on Mindtrip or have the assistant handle the details. If a booking goes wrong, the FAQ says to contact whoever you booked with, such as Priceline or Booking.com, because your confirmation email lists their details. To plan with others, it says to share ideas and invite people to collaborate.

  9. 9. Use it during the trip.

    The homepage says the assistant watches details such as price drops, flight changes and restaurant availability, and offers on-trip advice, for example indoor ideas when rain is forecast. Its Watch tile mentions automatic alerts and agents that keep tabs on what matters to you. The FAQ adds interactive maps and real-time recommendations once you are on the ground, and an easy way to save and share the places that made the trip memorable afterwards.

What Mindtrip runs on, and what it costs

On cost, the FAQ's answer to "Is Mindtrip free?" is plain: yes, for travelers, with planning, saving, questions, recommendations and itineraries free on the web and in the iOS app. Mindtrip for Business is a separate paid product for tourism boards and hotels that want Mindtrip trip planning on their own websites. On data, the FAQ names OpenAI for the conversation and partners including TripAdvisor, Viator, Priceline, Agoda, Google and HotelBeds. In 2024 Axios reported that the company gets a cut when users book through the travel search engines and aggregators it works with, which is worth knowing when you read its recommendations. I did not find a current statement on that in the FAQ.

Where Mindtrip stops

Mindtrip is a chat-led assistant with booking attached. You describe what you want in words, and it answers. The pages I read do not describe a way to flip through candidate places one by one and keep or drop each, and they do not mention an Android app. Neither is a flaw: the product is built around a conversation and a booking flow. It does mean that if you already know your city and just want to sort through real places quickly, typing questions is a different job from swiping.

Mindtrip, NomadSwipe and Wanderlog compared

Questions about using Mindtrip

How do I use Mindtrip?

Open mindtrip.ai or the iOS app and click Set up your travel assistant, then answer a few questions about your travel style. Chat with the assistant for ideas, save places into collections, build an itinerary and tweak it, and book flights, stays or restaurants when you are ready. This comes from Mindtrip's FAQ and homepage, and I did not run the app.

Is Mindtrip free?

For travelers, yes, according to Mindtrip's own FAQ: you can plan trips, save ideas, ask questions, get recommendations and build itineraries on the web and in the iOS app at no cost. Mindtrip for Business is a paid product for tourism boards and hotels. Read on 23 November 2026.

Is there a Mindtrip app for Android?

Mindtrip's FAQ says it is available on the web and in the iOS app, and its homepage offers a Get iOS app link. I found no Android app on the pages I read, so on an Android phone you would use the website. NomadSwipe, the app I built, is Android only today, with iPhone coming soon.

How is Mindtrip different from ChatGPT?

Mindtrip's FAQ says ChatGPT is great for answering questions generally, while Mindtrip combines conversational AI with its own travel knowledge base, trusted travel data and insights from people who have been there, and adds tools to curate, share, plan, book and explore. That is Mindtrip describing itself, not a test I ran.

Where does Mindtrip get its information?

Its FAQ says it works with OpenAI for the conversational side and partners with travel companies including TripAdvisor, Viator, Priceline, Agoda, Google and HotelBeds. Many public guides and collections come from its community of creators and from destination partners.

Can more than one person work on a Mindtrip trip?

Mindtrip's FAQ says you can share ideas, invite others to collaborate and get recommendations that balance everyone's vibe. I did not test how that works in practice.

In short

To use Mindtrip, open mindtrip.ai or the iOS app, set up your travel assistant by answering a few questions, tell the chat how you like to travel, ask for ideas, save places into collections, build and tweak an itinerary, then book flights, stays or restaurants and share the trip with others. Mindtrip says it is free for travelers. It is a chat-and-booking assistant, so it answers what to do and where to book, and the pages I read describe no Android app and no card-swiping. If you want to swipe real places and see them on a map on Android, that is the job NomadSwipe, which I built, does.
