What AllTrails actually is

The AllTrails homepage opens with a search box for a city, park or trail name, and lists three things next to it: record your activities with live tracking, download maps for trails and parks to use without service, and build or edit your own route. Its navigation names trails, parks and points of interest as things to browse, plus print maps and a route converter under "Custom routes & maps".

The Google Play listing is more specific. It calls the app a companion and guide for hiking, biking, running and walking, built around reviews and conditions from a community of trailgoers, and says it can filter for dog-friendly, kid-friendly, stroller-friendly or wheelchair-friendly trails. It lists "over 500,000 trails", turn-by-turn navigation, points of interest such as waterfalls and photo spots, and Wear OS support. It is the biggest in this comparison on Play: 4.5 from 389K reviews and 50M+ downloads, updated 11 November 2026, marked "Contains ads".

The listing splits paid features into two tiers. Plus is described as offline maps, wrong-turn alerts, live sharing of your activity, weather, air quality and UV conditions along a trail, printed maps and ad removal. Peak adds creating your own route from scratch or modifying an existing trail, plus heatmaps of recent trail activity. So AllTrails is first a trail guide with reviews, and second a recorder and navigator.

Why people look for apps like AllTrails

I did not survey anyone, so these are the reasons the pages themselves point to, not a poll.

  • The useful part sits behind a subscription. Per the Play listing, offline maps, wrong-turn alerts and ad removal need Plus, and route-from-scratch needs Peak (what Plus and Peak add is laid out in AllTrails paid vs free). If you only ever wanted those, you can compare how the other four tier theirs below.
  • Ads. The listing carries the "Contains ads" label and says a subscription removes "occasional ads".
  • Data sharing. The listing's data safety section says the app may share location and personal info, among other types, with third parties, and may collect location, personal info and seven other types. That is the developer's own declaration, and I have not checked it against what the app does.
  • A different style of route. AllTrails leads with a database of reviewed trails. If you want a route generated for a road bike, a track another hiker recorded, or an official route over a national topo map, the apps below start from there instead.
  • Individual complaints. One reviewer wrote on 2 June 2026 that waypoints and a photo-location feature were removed, and that editing places now needs a paid plan. Another, in July 2025, said trails vanished and reappeared in the new map view. These are single, unverified reviews of one app version each, so read the current ones before deciding.

Alternatives to AllTrails, one at a time

AllTrails: the reference point

Covered above. Pick it when the reason to open the app is "which trail?" and you want reviews and conditions from other people on one page. It has the largest Play footprint of the group, and the paid tiers add the offline and planning tools.

Komoot: when you want the route built for your sport

The Komoot site says it generates sport-specific routes: smooth asphalt for a road bike, singletracks for a mountain bike, peaceful trails for hiking. You can filter by distance, difficulty or public transport links, and it offers voice turn-by-turn and offline maps. The Play listing adds surface, difficulty and elevation profile, community Highlights shown as red dots in the planner, and sync across phone, desktop and Wear OS. The model is regional: your first region costs nothing, and offline maps, bike routes and voice navigation elsewhere come through single regions, bundles or the World Pack. The listing does not show those prices. A reviewer in the same listing said some trails near them were missing, and the developer replied that planning depends on OpenStreetMap data, so coverage varies by area.

Wikiloc: when you trust tracks other people actually walked

The Wikiloc homepage says it has 22,396,635 members sharing 85,697,983 trails and 155,845,146 photos, and lists live tracking, offline maps and a Premium plan. The Play listing says routes are recorded with GPS by community members, covers more than 80 activity types, and lets you send routes to Wear OS, Garmin, Suunto or COROS devices. It puts the route planner, 3D maps, advanced search filters, search by passing area and weather forecast under Premium. Its trail count is quoted two ways, 85.7 million on the site and "50 million" on Play. It is a library of recorded tracks, so quality will vary by track and I did not sample any.

Outdooractive: when official routes and national topo maps matter

The Outdooractive site offers a route finder, route planner and travel guide, and says its routes are recommended by mountain guides, trail wardens and other industry professionals. Its Play listing says "officially approved" routes, names official topo maps in 25 countries (Ordnance Survey in the UK, USGS in the USA, IGN in France, Swisstopo in Switzerland among them), lets you save plans or entire areas offline, and includes a live-location sharing feature called BuddyBeacon. It also contains ads. It is the most map-source-heavy of the group, and the one to open if you hike with a national map in mind.

Gaia GPS: when you are leaving the marked trail network

The Gaia GPS site sells maps more than trails: Premium lists the Gaia Hike and Gaia Overland maps, more than 300 layers such as cell coverage and private land, and offline maps, with snap-to-trail and straight-line route modes for off-trail planning, plus weather, wildfire and avalanche layers. The Play listing mentions backpacking, public land trails, ski and snowboard trails and 4x4 planning. Its Play rating is the lowest of the five shown here, 3.4 from 22.4K reviews. The site showed a promotional first-year price and a trial, which I leave out because it is a promotion that will change.

Where the sources disagree

  1. Tiers. The AllTrails homepage lists "Create your own route" and map downloads without naming a tier, while the Play listing puts create-from-scratch under Peak and map downloads under Plus. I cannot tell from these pages exactly what the base app lets you do, and the Plus page that would settle it would not load.
  2. Review counts. The AllTrails Play header showed 389K reviews and the ratings block lower on the same page showed 383K. I quote the header.
  3. Wikiloc's size. The site counts 85.7 million trails, Play says 50 million. They are probably different measures, and neither says what it counts.
  4. Where a feature sits. Wikiloc's homepage lists "Search by Passing Area" beside live tracking and offline maps without naming a plan, while the Play listing places it under Premium. Check the plan before relying on it.

Where NomadSwipe fits (and where it does not)

For the city part of a trip, not the trail

I built NomadSwipe (Android), so weigh this with that in mind. It is not a trail app. It has no trail database, no activity recording and no route navigation, no topo maps and no downloadable maps. If your question is "which trail, and how do I follow it", use one of the four apps above.

What it does is the planning around a trip. You pick a city and it deals real restaurants, sights, bars and stays one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep one, and every saved place is pinned on a travel map where you build a trip. Cards show category, rating, distance and, where hours are known, whether the place is open, and the list sorts by rating, distance or A to Z. The listing's sample trip is a long weekend in New York with 16 places, all picked by swiping. Its listing says you can open a place for photos and start directions, which I did not test.

Offline is narrow: up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online, and the offline copies expire. A workable split for a hiking trip with a town at each end is to track the trail in one of the apps above and use NomadSwipe to choose where to eat, sleep and look around before and after. I make no claim that anything moves between the apps automatically. iPhone is coming soon. NomadSwipe had 50+ downloads on Google Play when I last checked, so it is new, and the apps above are far more established. FAQ

What are the best apps like AllTrails?

From their own store listings and sites, Komoot, Wikiloc, Outdooractive and Gaia GPS are the four closest. Each lists trail or route discovery, GPS navigation and offline maps. Which is best depends on what you want from it: reviewed trail pages, community-recorded tracks, official routes with national topo maps, or backcountry map layers. I have not used any of them in the field.

Which app like AllTrails has offline maps?

All four listed here mention offline maps on their own pages. Komoot's Play listing ties offline maps to regions beyond your first one. Wikiloc's Play listing describes offline GPS navigation through topographic maps. Outdooractive's listing says you can save single plans or whole areas offline. Gaia GPS lists offline maps under Premium. AllTrails itself puts map downloads under its paid Plus tier, per its Play listing.

Why do people look for alternatives to AllTrails?

The AllTrails Play listing gives the structure behind most reasons: offline maps, wrong-turn alerts and ad removal sit under paid Plus, route-from-scratch and heatmaps sit under Peak, and the listing says the app contains ads. Its data safety section also says location and personal info may be shared with third parties. Individual reviewers add their own complaints, such as a changed map view, so check recent reviews yourself.

Which app is best for planning my own route instead of picking a listed trail?

Komoot, Outdooractive, Wikiloc and Gaia GPS all describe a route planner on their pages. Komoot generates routes by sport. Outdooractive plots routes over its official map catalog. Wikiloc's planner is listed under Premium and favours popular community stretches. Gaia GPS offers snap-to-trail and straight-line modes on its site. AllTrails lists building a custom route too, and its Play listing places create-from-scratch under Peak.

Is NomadSwipe an app like AllTrails?

No. I built NomadSwipe (Android). It is for choosing real restaurants, sights, bars and stays in a city: you swipe places, save the ones you like, see them on a map and build a trip. It has no trail database, no activity recording and no route navigation, so it does not replace AllTrails, Komoot, Wikiloc, Outdooractive or Gaia GPS on a trail.

Does NomadSwipe record hikes or work offline?

It does not record hikes. I built NomadSwipe (Android), and its Google Play listing describes saved trips, not activity tracking. On offline, the listing says up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online, and the copies expire. It describes no downloadable maps.
