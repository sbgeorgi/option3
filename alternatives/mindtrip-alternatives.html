6 Best Mindtrip Alternatives in 2026

Updated 25 October 2026. I built NomadSwipe, which is one of the apps compared, so read my own entry with that in mind.

Mindtrip is a personal travel assistant. It learns how you like to travel, compares flights, stays and restaurants, can book them for you, and sends price and change alerts. That suits people who want an assistant to handle the details. If you want a simpler way to choose places yourself, on a map and without a booking step, here are six apps worth comparing.

Mindtrip at a glance

  • Assistant. It learns your travel style and compares flights, stays and restaurants, then lets you book directly or have the assistant handle the details.
  • Alerts. It sends alerts for price drops, flight changes and restaurant availability, and you can create agents to track what you choose.
  • On the trip. It offers suggestions during a trip, such as indoor ideas when rain is forecast.
  • Platforms. The Mindtrip site we read lists an iOS app. Android is not mentioned. Check their site for current platforms.
  • Pricing. Not stated on the site we read. Check their site.

The right choice depends on whether you want an assistant that books and tracks for you, or a map of places you pick yourself.

1. NomadSwipe: for choosing places, not handing over the trip

Best for: travellers who want a shortlist of real places they chose themselves

NomadSwipe is a free Android trip planner. You pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Everything you save lands in your trip and is pinned on a trip map.

Where it differs from Mindtrip

  • There is no assistant to set up or wait for. You decide on each place yourself, one card at a time.
  • You can sort saved places by rating or by distance.
  • It is free to download, and the free plan is enough to try a trip.

Where it does not

  • It does not write an itinerary from a conversation, and the Google Play listing does not describe bookings for flights, hotels or restaurants.
  • It is Android only. The free plan is limited to 3 trips, 2 destination searches and 2 custom pins a month.

Pricing

Free to download, with no card needed. Premium is optional, comes as Monthly or Lifetime, and removes the free-plan limits. Google Play shows the current price before you pay.

2. Layla

Best for: a planned trip with advisors and bookings

Layla plans a trip through a chat with an AI, then offers a human travel advisor to refine the plan and a team to book it. Planning is free, and you pay for the travel arrangements in your booking. Travolution reported on 3 August 2026 that Layla is now part of Expedia Group.

  • Strength: someone can plan and book the whole trip for you.
  • Weakness: it is a planning service with bookings, so it is less suited to a quick, self-chosen shortlist of places.

Pricing: planning is free. Layla's own blog and a competitor report different premium prices, so check the app. Read our Layla alternatives comparison for more.

3. Wanderlog

Best for: itineraries with booking import

Wanderlog is a trip planner for day-by-day itineraries, bookings and sharing plans with travel companions. You can connect or forward confirmation emails, and flight and hotel reservations are added to the trip. It also suggests attractions and nearby places based on your itinerary.

  • Strength: email import and place recommendations in one planner.
  • Weakness: the page we read gives no price for either tier, and offline access is a Pro feature.

Pricing: free to start, with a paid Pro tier. The current price is not stated on the page read, so check the app. Read our Wanderlog alternatives comparison for more.

4. Tripomatic

Best for: day-by-day city itineraries; offline maps with Premium

Tripomatic lets you organise your trip into days with drag-and-drop scheduling, see routes between stops and discover attractions. Offline maps for your trip places are a Premium feature in the mobile app.

  • Strength: turns a list of places into a daily schedule with routes.
  • Weakness: the free plan allows up to 5 days with planned activities per calendar month across all trips, and Premium pricing is not stated on the page read.

Pricing: free plan with the monthly limit above; Premium removes the limits and the ads.

5. Polarsteps

Best for: recording a trip as you travel

Polarsteps is a travel tracker and trip planner that records where you go. The free plan includes planning, tracking, reliving and following trips, plus up to 5 Travel Buddies. The paid Plus tier adds 3D maps, map styles, step stats and play mode.

  • Strength: a good fit if the record of the trip matters as much as the plan.
  • Weakness: the Plus page does not describe place recommendations or bookings.

Pricing: free; Plus costs €8.99 a month or €29.99 a year. The page says prices may vary by region.

6. TripIt

Best for: organising booked flights and hotels

TripIt is built around the trip you have already booked. Its Pro tier adds flight status alerts, Seat Tracker, Fare Tracker, check-in reminders, airport maps and terminal and gate updates.

  • Strength: strong alerts and airport tools if you fly often.
  • Weakness: the Pro page lists no place recommendations, so it does not help you choose what to do in a city.

Pricing: free sign-up; Pro is $49 a year after a 30-day free trial. Read our TripIt alternatives comparison for more.

Which Mindtrip alternative is right for you?

  • You want an assistant that books and watches prices: Mindtrip, if it fits your platform. Check their site.
  • You want a free shortlist of real places you chose yourself, on a map: NomadSwipe, on Android.
  • You want someone to plan and book the whole trip with an advisor: Layla.
  • You want itineraries with booking import and place ideas: Wanderlog.
  • You want a day-by-day plan, with offline maps on Premium: Tripomatic.
  • You want a travel journal as you go: Polarsteps.
  • You want flight alerts for trips you have booked: TripIt Pro.
