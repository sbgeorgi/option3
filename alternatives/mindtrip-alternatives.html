Is Mindtrip Free? What I Found, Plus 6 Alternatives

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, which is one of the apps compared, so read my entry with that in mind. Mindtrip facts last checked 9 November 2026.

The Mindtrip app is free to install, according to its own App Store listing. The Mindtrip website pages read on 9 November 2026 show no plan prices. The best alternatives are Wanderlog for itineraries, Layla for AI chat planning, Roadtrippers for road trips and NomadSwipe, which I built, for an Android shortlist of real places. If you used Inspirock before, see what happened to Inspirock. For a list of replacements, see apps like Inspirock.

Mindtrip's app is for iPhone only, and a full AI assistant may be more than you need if you only want a shortlist of places. After the free-or-not answer, the six apps below cover other jobs: a free card-based shortlist, itineraries with email import, AI chat planning with an advisor, road trips, recording a trip and day-by-day city plans.

Is Mindtrip free?

For the iPhone app, yes according to Mindtrip's own App Store listing, which shows the price as Free and calls the app “totally free”. The Mindtrip website pages read on 9 November 2026 show no plan prices, and none says in plain words that the whole service is free. So treat it as free to install and start, and check the app itself before you rely on a feature. If you also use review sites, see trip planner apps like Tripadvisor.

What you may still pay for

Bookings. Mindtrip's Terms say reservations are made with a third-party booking partner, that the partner's own terms govern the booking, and that payments run through the partner or a payment processor. Prices shown may leave out taxes, fees or other charges. A free app is therefore not a free flight, stay or table.

What I could not confirm

  • Whether any paid tier exists. I found none on the pages above. Third-party directory pages that turned up in my search disagree with each other about Mindtrip's pricing and none is official, so I did not use them.
  • Android. mindtrip.ai links only to the App Store, and its pages do not mention Android. A Google Play search for Mindtrip showed no Mindtrip-titled app in the part of the results that loaded, which does not prove none exists.
  • Which iPhones. The App Store listing says iPhone only and requires iOS 26.0 or later, so older iPhones may not run it.

1. NomadSwipe: for choosing places, not building a booked trip

Best for: travellers who want a shortlist of real places they chose themselves

NomadSwipe is an Android trip planner. You pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Everything you save lands in your trip and is pinned on a map.

Where it beats Mindtrip

  • There is no AI chat and no booking step. You decide each place yourself, one card at a time.
  • It is on Android; Mindtrip's App Store listing is for iPhone only.
  • Each place shows its photos, directions, website and phone number, and you can sort saved places by rating, distance or A to Z.

Where it does not

  • It does not write an itinerary from a conversation, does not watch prices and does not describe flight or hotel bookings.
  • It is Android only. The free plan is limited to 3 trips, plus 2 searched places saved to a trip and 2 custom pins a month, plus a daily limit on saves.

Pricing

Free to download, with no card needed. Premium is optional, comes as Monthly or Lifetime, and removes the limits above. Google Play shows the current price before you pay.

2. Wanderlog

Best for: itineraries with email import

Wanderlog is a trip planner for day-by-day itineraries and bookings. Its homepage says you can connect or forward your emails so that reservations are added to the trip, and that you can plan along with friends with live syncing and collaborative editing. Its homepage also shows download badges for the app stores.

  • Strength: email import and shared editing in one planner.
  • Weakness: the homepage states no Pro price and does not say which features sit in Pro, so check the app for what the paid tier adds.

Pricing: free to start, with a paid Pro tier mentioned. The homepage states no Pro price, so check the app. Read our comparison of apps like Wanderlog for more.

3. Layla

Best for: planning with an AI chat and an optional advisor

Layla plans a trip through a chat with an AI, then offers an optional travel advisor and a team that handles bookings. That suits people who want the whole trip handled rather than a shortlist they build themselves.

  • Strength: the whole trip, from plan to bookings, can be handled in one service.
  • Weakness: it is a heavier service than a shortlist, and it is built around bookings.

Pricing: planning is free, with no subscription or planning fee, according to Layla's FAQ. You pay for the travel arrangements in your booking, and the homepage says nothing is added on top when its advisors book the trip. Read our comparison of apps like Layla AI for more.

4. Roadtrippers

Best for: road trips with stops along the route

Roadtrippers describes itself as a road trip planner that plans your trip for you. You can plan on the web or in its apps for iPhone and Android, and anything you plan or save syncs between them.

  • Strength: a planner for routes and stops, with web and mobile apps that stay in sync.
  • Weakness: the free plan allows 1 saved trip and 3 stops per trip, and the tool is built around road trips rather than city swiping.

Pricing: Free ($0 a year, 1 saved trip, 3 stops per trip). Basic is $11.99 a month or $35.99 a year, Pro is $16.99 a month or $49.99 a year, and Premium is $19.99 a month or $59.99 a year. Plans can be tried free for 7 days.

5. Polarsteps

Best for: recording a trip as you travel

Polarsteps is a travel tracker that records where you go. Its FAQ says travellers can plan, track, share and relive their adventures for free. A paid Plus tier is available.

  • Strength: a good fit if the record of the trip matters as much as the plan.
  • Weakness: the Plus page does not describe offline use, place recommendations or bookings, so it does not replace a city shortlist or a booking tool.

Pricing: free; Plus costs €8.99 a month or €29.99 a year. The page says prices may vary by region.

6. Tripomatic

Best for: day-by-day city itineraries, with offline maps on Premium

Tripomatic lets you discover places, plan your day-by-day trip and navigate. Its homepage says you can plan on a computer and navigate with your phone, and it lists web, iPhone and Android apps.

  • Strength: turns a list of places into a daily schedule, with offline maps and export to PDF, GPX or KML on Premium.
  • Weakness: the free plan allows up to 5 days with planned activities per calendar month across all trips and up to 25 places and hotels per trip, and it shows ads. Premium prices are not listed on the page read.

Pricing: free plan with no time limit, with the monthly limit above. Premium removes the limits and the ads, and adds offline maps and export.

FAQ

Is Mindtrip free?

For the iPhone app, yes according to Mindtrip's own App Store listing, which shows the price as Free and calls the app “totally free”. The mindtrip.ai pages read on 9 November 2026 show no plan prices and do not say in plain words that the whole service is free. Flights, stays and restaurants are booked with partners, and the partner's price and terms apply.

Is Mindtrip free on Android?

I could not confirm an Android app. mindtrip.ai links only to the App Store, and a Google Play search showed no Mindtrip-titled app in the part of the results that loaded. The site itself is a web app.

Does Mindtrip have paid plans or a subscription?

None appears on the pages read: the homepage, welcome page, about page, Terms of Service and App Store listing show no plan prices, and the Terms do not mention subscriptions. Mindtrip lists business offerings for hotels and destinations, which are separate from traveller use. That is not proof that no paid tier exists.

Is NomadSwipe available on Android?

Yes. NomadSwipe is free to download on Google Play for Android phones.

Does NomadSwipe build an itinerary from a chat?

No. NomadSwipe deals real places one card at a time. You save the ones you want and see them pinned on a map of your trip.

Can NomadSwipe book flights or hotels?

The Google Play listing does not describe bookings. From a place's page you can get directions, visit its website or call ahead.

Does NomadSwipe work offline?

Up to 20 saved trips are available offline, according to the Google Play listing.

Does Mindtrip work offline or export itineraries?

Mindtrip's App Store listing says version 18.1 added offline access to your itinerary, photos and trip details; the pages read do not say whether chat or maps work offline, and none mention export. Tripomatic's website says its Premium plan adds offline maps and export to PDF, GPX and KML.

Findings, method and sources

Sample and method. On 9 November 2026 I requested 11 Mindtrip URLs as text. Five gave usable content: the homepage, the welcome page, the about page, the Terms of Service and the App Store listing. Four returned only a login prompt or no text (the pricing page, the older /terms URL, the /homepage URL and the business packages page), one help-centre host did not resolve, and one short link redirected to the App Store listing. The usable pages were checked for the words free, price, subscription, plan, Android and iPhone, and a fact counted only if it appeared on a page that loaded. I used no third-party directory, review or press page for any Mindtrip fact.

  • The App Store listing shows the price as Free and calls the app “totally free”; it says iPhone only and requires iOS 26.0 or later.
  • None of the 4 Mindtrip website pages I could read (homepage, welcome, about, Terms) gives a plan price; the only prices are sample flight and hotel fares in the preview.
  • The Terms say bookings are made with booking partners, and they neither say whether the service is free nor mention subscriptions.
  • The homepage links only to the App Store and does not mention Android; the Google Play search showed no Mindtrip-titled app in the visible results, which were cut off.
  • Offline: the App Store listing says version 18.1 added offline access to your itinerary, photos and trip details.

Limits: a text fetch can miss content that loads after the page opens, and a login gate hid some pages, so a paid tier could exist on a page I could not read. Mindtrip can change its pricing at any time. The Android finding rests on a search whose results were cut off. For the other five apps I re-read each one's own page on the same day; Wanderlog's page was too long to read in full and I read its first part, and Layla's FAQ page returned an error, so I used its homepage, which quotes the FAQ.
