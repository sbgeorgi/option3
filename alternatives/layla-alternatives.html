Layla Alternatives in 2026: 6 Apps With Prices Checked 9 November

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, which is one of the apps compared, so read my entry with that in mind. Last checked 9 November 2026.

The best Layla alternatives depend on the job: Wanderlog for itineraries, Mindtrip for an AI assistant that books, Tripomatic for day-by-day plans, and NomadSwipe, which I built, for a lighter shortlist of real places on Android. For reference, Layla lists Premium at $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year on its App Store listing, has iPhone and Android apps, and is now part of Expedia Group, per Travolution. For a step-by-step walkthrough of a different planner, see how to use Funliday.

Layla at a glance

  • Planning is free on the site. Layla's site says there is no subscription or planning fee, and you pay for the travel arrangements in your booking. The app's App Store listing also shows optional Premium subscriptions.
  • Minimum budget. Layla recommends at least €100 per person per night for its tailor-made trips.
  • Premium price. The App Store listing shows $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year, and Pilot, a competitor, quotes about $49 a year. The Google Play listing I read shows no price, so check your store.
  • Platforms. iPhone and iPad (iOS 16.6 or later) on the App Store, and Android on Google Play. Google Play shows 10K+ downloads.
  • Ownership. Travolution reported on 3 August 2026 that Layla is now part of Expedia Group. Layla is based in Berlin.
  • Reviews. Trustpilot shows 4.3 out of 5 from 98 reviews, 83 of them in the last 12 months. Reviewers praise smooth booking, personalised trips and responsive human agents. Some say the AI forgot earlier instructions, that they needed several attempts to get a usable plan, or that confirmations were slow. Another trip planner that changed owners is covered in what happened to Inspirock. If you used Inspirock, here is an Inspirock alternative comparison.

None of this makes Layla a bad choice. It is a different kind of tool: a planning service with advisors and bookings. The options below are lighter, and some of them are better for a specific job. If a chatbot already gave you a list, checking a ChatGPT itinerary on a map in six steps is a lighter next step.

1. NomadSwipe: for choosing places, not building a booked trip

Best for: travellers who want a shortlist of real places they chose themselves

NomadSwipe is an Android trip planner. You pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Everything you save lands in your trip and is pinned on a map.

Where it beats Layla

  • There is no AI chat-to-itinerary step and no advisor to wait for. You decide each place yourself, one card at a time.
  • Up to 20 saved trips are available offline, according to the Google Play listing.
  • Each place shows its photos, directions, website and phone number, and you can sort saved places by rating, distance or A to Z.

Where it does not

  • It does not write an itinerary from a conversation, and the Play listing describes no hotel or flight booking.
  • It has no advisor and no customer care line on the pages we read.
  • It is Android only. The free plan is limited to 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip and 2 custom pins a month, plus a daily limit on saves.

Pricing

Free to download, with no card needed. Premium is optional, comes as Monthly or Lifetime, and removes the limits above. Google Play shows the current price before you pay.

The two apps solve different problems. NomadSwipe gives you a quick, self-chosen shortlist of places and a map, with no booking funnel. You give up the advisor, the booked itinerary and the bookings Layla handles. If you want someone to plan and book the whole trip, Layla is the better fit.

2. Wanderlog

Best for: itineraries with booking import

Wanderlog is a trip planner for day-by-day itineraries, bookings and sharing plans with travel companions. You can connect or forward confirmation emails, and flight and hotel reservations are added to the trip. It also suggests attractions and nearby places based on your itinerary, and the map and itinerary share one view.

  • Strength: email import and place recommendations in one planner.
  • Weakness: its own site pages show no price, and the App Store lists many Pro price points, so what you pay depends on your storefront and offer.

Pricing: free to start, with a paid Pro tier. Wanderlog's site shows no price. Its US App Store listing shows Pro annual plans at $31.99, $39.99, $49.99 and $59.99 and monthly plans at $5.99 and $16.99, with a 4.9 rating from 36K ratings. The Wanderlog site says it has 8M+ trips planned. Read our comparison of travel apps like Wanderlog for more.

3. Mindtrip

Best for: an AI assistant that books and watches prices

Mindtrip calls itself a personal travel assistant. It learns how you like to travel, compares flights, stays and restaurants, and lets you book directly or have the assistant handle the details. It also tracks price drops, flight changes and on-trip conditions such as rain forecasts.

  • Strength: the assistant watches for price drops and flight changes.
  • Weakness: its booking and price-watching focus means it sits closer to a booking funnel than NomadSwipe does, and the site shows no plan prices.

Pricing: not stated on the site pages read. The price examples on the page are illustrations of price drops, not a plan. The site links to an iOS app and does not mention Android.

4. Tripomatic

Best for: day-by-day city itineraries; offline maps with Premium

Tripomatic lets you organise your trip into days with drag-and-drop scheduling, see routes between stops and discover attractions. Offline maps for your trip places are a Premium feature in the mobile app.

  • Strength: turns a list of places into a daily schedule with routes.
  • Weakness: the free plan allows up to 5 days with planned activities per calendar month across all trips, and the site states no Premium price.

Pricing: free plan with the monthly limit above and up to 25 places and hotels per trip; Premium removes the limits and the ads. The US App Store listing shows Premium Yearly at $23.99, Monthly at $3.99, 3 Months at $14.99 and an AI Assistant Upgrade at $19.99, with a 4.6 rating from 3.3K ratings. The site lists web, iOS and Android.

5. Polarsteps

Best for: recording a trip as you travel

Polarsteps is a travel tracker and trip planner that records where you go. The free plan includes planning, tracking, reliving and following trips, plus up to 5 Travel Buddies. The paid Plus tier adds 3D maps, map styles, step stats and play mode.

  • Strength: a good fit if the record of the trip matters as much as the plan.
  • Weakness: the Plus page does not describe place recommendations or bookings, so it does not replace a city shortlist or a booking tool.

Pricing: free; Plus costs €8.99 a month or €29.99 a year, per its Plus page. The page says prices may vary by region, and it offers no free trial. Free includes up to 5 Travel Buddies and Plus up to 10. Polarsteps says 23M+ travellers use it.

6. TripIt

Best for: organising booked flights and hotels

TripIt is built around the trip you have already booked. Its Pro tier adds flight status alerts, Seat Tracker, Fare Tracker, check-in reminders, airport maps and terminal and gate updates.

  • Strength: strong alerts and airport tools if you fly often.
  • Weakness: the Pro page lists no place recommendations, so it does not help you choose what to do in a city.

Pricing: free sign-up; Pro is $49 a year after a 30-day free trial, per its Pro page. Read our comparison of apps like TripIt for more.

FAQ

Is Layla free?

Layla's site (layla.ai) says planning a trip is free, with no subscription or planning fee, and you pay for the travel arrangements in your booking. The Layla app is also free to download, and its App Store listing shows optional Premium subscriptions at $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year (read 9 November 2026, source).

How much does Layla Premium cost?

On the App Store listing read on 9 November 2026, Layla lists a monthly subscription at $9.99 and a yearly one at $49.99 (source). The Google Play listing I read shows no prices, so check the in-app screen for your store and region.

Does Layla have an Android app?

Yes. Layla - AI Trip Planner is on Google Play from Layla AI GmbH, shown with 10K+ downloads and last updated on 5 November 2026. It is also on the App Store for iPhone and iPad.

Does Layla work offline?

Layla's Google Play listing says vouchers and booking details stay in the app, even offline. It does not say the planner itself works offline. Pilot, a competitor, reports no offline mobile access for Layla, so treat full offline planning as unconfirmed.

Is Layla the same as layla.eco?

No, not as far as the pages I read show. layla.eco sells a property monitor for noise, smoke and occupancy aimed at rental hosts and hotels, and its page does not mention the Layla AI travel planner at layla.ai.

What do Trustpilot reviewers say about Layla?

Trustpilot showed 4.3 out of 5 from 98 reviews on 9 November 2026, 83 of them in the last 12 months. Reviewers praise smooth booking, personalised trips and responsive human agents. Others say the AI forgets earlier instructions, that they needed several attempts to get a usable plan, or that confirmations were slow.

Who owns Layla?

Travolution reported on 3 August 2026 that Layla is now part of Expedia Group. Layla was founded in Berlin in 2023, and its Google Play listing names Layla AI GmbH. The Layla site pages I read do not mention Expedia.

Does NomadSwipe build an itinerary from a chat?

No. NomadSwipe deals real places one card at a time. You save the ones you want and see them pinned on a map of your trip.

Can NomadSwipe book hotels or flights?

The Google Play listing does not describe bookings. From a place's page you can get directions, visit its website or call ahead.

Is NomadSwipe on iPhone?

Not yet. It is on Android today, free to download on Google Play, and iPhone is coming soon.

Does NomadSwipe work offline?

Up to 20 saved trips are available offline, according to the Google Play listing.

Findings, method and sample

Sample and method. On 9 November 2026 I re-read 15 public pages for this update. They were the Layla site, its App Store and Google Play listings, Travolution's report, Trustpilot, Pilot's comparison, the official pages of Wanderlog, Mindtrip, Tripomatic, Polarsteps and TripIt Pro, the App Store listings of Wanderlog and Tripomatic, and layla.eco. Prices are the ones each page showed that day, in the currency it showed. I did not install or pay for any of the apps, and I used no NomadSwipe app data for the comparison, so every competitor figure is a listing claim, not a test. Pages that returned an error (Layla's FAQ, About and blog pages returned HTTP 429) are not used for any fact.

  • Layla: free to download on the App Store and Google Play, with App Store subscriptions at $9.99 a month and $49.99 a year, 4.6 from 216 App Store ratings, 10K+ Google Play downloads, 4.3 from 98 Trustpilot reviews (links in Sources below).
  • The $49.99 App Store price matches the $49 a year Pilot, a competitor, quotes, so the earlier gap in Layla's price came from older pages.
  • Of the 6 alternatives, only Polarsteps (€8.99 monthly, €29.99 yearly) and TripIt Pro ($49 yearly) state a price on the official page I read. Wanderlog and Tripomatic show prices only in App Store listings, and Mindtrip shows none.
  • Tripomatic puts offline maps behind Premium, and NomadSwipe offers up to 20 saved trips offline per its Play listing. I did not find an offline claim on the other pages I read.

Limits: store prices vary by country and promotion, so the price in your app can differ. Ratings are the numbers the stores and Trustpilot showed on the day and move often. Pilot sells a competing planner, so I used it only to cross-check Layla's price and import limits. Layla's own site says nothing about Expedia, so the ownership fact rests on one trade-press report.
