Google Maps offline for a trip: see saved places without internet while travelling

Can you see saved places in Google Maps without internet while travelling? Google's help pages do not say. Saving a place is separate from downloading an offline area, and a downloaded area gives driving navigation only: walking, transit and bicycling directions are unavailable offline. Test your list in airplane mode before you go.

Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe (Android), and I wrote this page. Every statement about Google Maps comes from Google's own Maps Help pages, which I read on 7 November 2026 (see Sources). Where those pages were silent, this page says so, and silence does not mean a feature is missing. Published 7 November 2026. Updated 9 November 2026: the offline maps, create-a-list and Save favorite places pages were read again that day and the statements below still held.

The map itself is a separate question. Downloaded maps expire, and Google Maps tries to refresh one that has 15 days or less left when your phone is on Wi-Fi, per the offline maps page.

How to see saved places without internet while travelling

No Google page I read says a saved list loads with no signal, so do not rely on it. What the pages do say is that downloading an area and saving a place are two different things, so a downloaded map does not by itself carry your list. Here is what I would do before leaving Wi-Fi:

  1. Test the list. Put the phone in airplane mode, open Google Maps, then open your list and a saved place. If it loads, you know for that phone and that list; if not, go to step 3.
  2. Download the area for the map. The offline maps page gives the steps; it covers the map and driving navigation only.
  3. Keep your own copy of the stops. Write down the names, addresses and the order of the day somewhere that opens without a connection.
  4. Refresh before you fly. Check the Offline maps list the day before, since downloaded maps expire.

If you save places in NomadSwipe (Android) instead, the listing says up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online. Offline copies expire, so open the trip online again to refresh it.

The catch for city trips: the walking gap

Most of the stops in a city break are reached on foot, and walking is on the unavailable list. Google's directions page says you can add up to 9 stops, final destination included, and that multiple destinations work for every mode except public transit and flight. Put together with the offline page, a walking day with 9 stops can be planned online in Google Maps, but nothing I read says the walking guidance for it carries over offline. That is my reading of two pages, not a sentence Google wrote, so test it yourself.

In practice, the downloaded area still shows you the map, so you can see where you are relative to the places you chose. What you lose is turn-by-turn walking guidance, transit routes and bike routes. If your trip is mostly driving, the offline map is a much better fit than it is for a trip on foot, and options for planning a drive with several stops covers the planning side. How a saved-places trip fits

A trip in Google Maps usually starts as a list of saved places. Google's page on creating lists covers making a list, saving places to it and sharing it, and it does not mention offline use. The offline maps page does not mention lists either. So the honest answer is that I do not know whether a given list loads with no signal, and I will not guess. If you rely on lists, test in airplane mode before you travel, and read what Google Maps lists do and where they stop for the wider picture. For a My Maps map, see does Google My Maps work offline.

Before you lose signal: a short test

  1. Download the area on Wi-Fi. Do it signed in on Android, outside Incognito on iPhone, and cover the whole area you will drive through.
  2. Check the Offline maps list. Look for anything expired or close to expiring and update it by hand rather than waiting for the 15-day automatic attempt.
  3. Switch on airplane mode. Open the map, search for a place you saved, and ask for walking directions. Based on Google's pages, the walking request should be unavailable, so you know before you travel.
  4. Open your list. If your places do not load, write down the names and addresses of the stops that matter.
  5. Plan the walking order in advance. Decide the sequence of stops while online and keep it somewhere that opens without a connection.

Questions about Google Maps offline for a trip

Does Google Maps give walking directions offline?

No. Google's help page for downloaded areas says walking, transit and bicycling directions are unavailable offline. Only driving navigation works, and only while the entire route is inside the downloaded area. I read this on both the Android and the iPhone version of the page on 7 November 2026.

How long does a downloaded Google Maps area last?

The page I read does not give a number of days for how long a map is valid. It says you must update downloaded maps before they expire, and that Google Maps tries to update a map automatically when it has 15 days or less left and your phone is on Wi-Fi. You can also update by hand from the Offline maps list, so check the list the day before you travel.

Can I download offline maps for every country?

No. The help page says you cannot download offline maps in some countries or regions because of contractual limitations, language support, address formats or other reasons. If your destination is one of them, you need a different offline map app or a plan that does not depend on a downloaded map.

How do I see my saved places in Google Maps without internet while travelling?

Google's pages do not say whether saved lists load offline. The create-a-list page and the offline maps page do not mention each other, and the Save favorite places page says saving a place is different from downloading an offline area. Test your list in airplane mode while you still have Wi-Fi, and keep the names and addresses of the stops that matter in your own copy.

What we checked: findings, method and sample

Sample and method. On 7 November 2026 I fetched four Google Maps Help articles with a web-fetch tool that returns the page text, five fetches in all: the offline maps article on its Android and its iPhone version, the article on creating lists of places, the article on getting directions, and the article on planning a commute or trip (the last was returned only in part, so I treat it as a weak source). For each I recorded only what the text said about offline use, and marked silence as silence. I did not test Google Maps in airplane mode, did not read Google's changelogs, and the text I received showed no revision date, so this is a snapshot of what Google's pages said on the read date, not a test of the app.

  • Both versions of the offline maps article (Android and iPhone) give the same three limits: walking, transit and bicycling directions are unavailable offline, navigation needs the entire route inside the downloaded map, and driving offline has no traffic information or alternate routes. Both give the same 15-days-or-less, Wi-Fi rule for automatic updates. The steps differ in one detail: Android says signed in, iPhone says not in Incognito mode.
  • Of the four articles, one (the offline maps article) states offline rules. The lists article and the trip-planning article contain no mention of offline use in the text I received, and the directions article only links to the offline article and carries an app promotion that mentions downloading offline areas.
  • The directions article lists seven travel modes (driving, public transit, walking, ride sharing, cycling, flight and motorcycle), up to 9 stops including the final destination, and no multiple destinations for public transit or flight. The offline article names three modes as unavailable offline (transit, bicycling and walking) and I recorded no statement about the other four.
  • Silence list, the questions the pages I read do not answer: whether saved lists load offline, whether search, hours, reviews or photos work inside a downloaded area, how many days a downloaded map stays valid, and whether a multi-stop route planned online keeps working offline.
  • Of the ten rows in the table above, four are stated limits (traffic and alternate routes, walking, transit and bicycling), one is a stated condition for a working feature (driving navigation), two describe maintenance and regional availability, and three are silences (lists, search and place details, multi-stop routes).

Limits: the text came through a fetch tool that may shorten pages, Google can change its help pages at any time, and the walking-day reading in this page is my inference from two pages. NomadSwipe statements come from its Google Play listing as checked on 2 November 2026 and are limited to what the sibling offline page already says. I did not copy any third-party review text.
