6 Best Roadtrippers Alternatives in 2026

Updated 26 October 2026. Written by NomadSwipe, which is one of the apps compared, so read our own entry with that in mind.

Roadtrippers is a road trip planner with stops and paid navigation, and a free plan limited to one saved trip and three stops per trip. If you want a free place shortlist you swipe through on Android, NomadSwipe is an option, though it is not a route planner.

Roadtrippers is strongest when the trip is a route. The six apps below cover other jobs: a route planner for the car, driving directions with offline maps, a day-by-day itinerary planner, an itinerary with email import, a travel record, and a free shortlist of places you choose yourself.

Roadtrippers at a glance

  • Plans. Free ($0 a year, 1 saved trip, 3 stops per trip). Basic ($35.99 a year or $11.99 a month, 3 trips, 20 stops). Pro ($49.99 a year or $16.99 a month, 5 trips, 50 stops). Premium ($59.99 a year or $19.99 a month, unlimited saved trips, 150 stops).
  • Trial. A 7-day free trial applies to annual plans only. Monthly plans are billed immediately.
  • Autopilot. The homepage says Autopilot “creates your itinerary based on what we've learned from over 42 million trips” and calls it “our newest Premium feature”.
  • Features by plan. Custom map styles and trip export start on Basic. Trip collaboration and navigation start on Pro. RV GPS, overnight RV parking, member deals, live traffic and offline maps are Premium only.
  • Platforms. A web planner, plus apps from the Apple and Google Play download links. The homepage says “Anything you plan or save automagically syncs with the apps”.

What reviewers say about Roadtrippers

  • Pilot's review (last updated 23 December 2025) says some users report “maps being outdated with suggestions on restaurants and hotels that have been shut down or relocated”. It says navigation “is only available in portrait mode”, and lists “Buggy navigation mode” and “Pricey annual fee” among its cons.
  • Upper is named as an alternative on its own page, so treat its criticism as a rival's view. It says the free version “includes invasive ads”, that “Frequent app crashes, image loading issues, and freezes are reported by users”, and that Roadtrippers is “Tailored mainly to the US, Canada, New Zealand, and Australia”.

These are dated, partly competitor-written reviews, so treat them as signals to check, not settled facts.

1. NomadSwipe: for choosing places, not routing a road trip

Best for: a free shortlist of real places you choose yourself, on a map

NomadSwipe is a free trip planner for Android. You pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Everything you save is pinned on your trip map.

Where it beats Roadtrippers

  • You do not need a route to start. Pick a city and swipe through real places one card at a time.
  • Each place shows its photos, directions, website and phone number, and you can sort saved places by rating, distance or A to Z.
  • The free plan covers 3 trips, and up to 20 saved trips are available offline.

Where it does not

  • It does not plan a driving route between stops. Roadtrippers plans routes and offers RV GPS and overnight RV parking on its Premium plan.
  • It is Android only, and the Google Play listing does not describe trip export or live traffic.

Pricing

Free to download, with no card needed. The free plan includes 3 trips, 2 destination searches and 2 custom pins a month, plus a limited number of swipes. Premium is optional, comes as Monthly or Lifetime, and removes those limits. Google Play shows the current price before you pay.

The two apps solve different problems. Roadtrippers is built to route a trip with stops along the way. NomadSwipe gives you a free shortlist of places you chose yourself, on Android, with no route planning. If your trip is a route, choose Roadtrippers or Furkot below.

2. Furkot

Best for: planning a road trip route by car

Furkot calls itself a “road trip planner” with the tagline “Miss the turns on your terms!” Its homepage says it “attempts to plan a route for your trip in the most efficient way”, and covers day trips, weekend getaways and trips across a continent.

  • Strength: built around planning a route, which is the job NomadSwipe does not do.
  • Weakness: its homepage lists an RV tour among its uses and campgrounds among its lodging options, but does not describe RV-specific routing. Offline use and export are not mentioned on the page we read.

Pricing: no price is stated on the homepage we read, so check the current price on its site.

3. Google Maps

Best for: driving directions with offline maps on Android

Google's Maps page says Gemini can help “while driving, walking, or cycling to help you find places on your route”, and mentions “skipping the tolls for a scenic path”. Google's Android help page explains that you can download an offline map from a place's details, or pick a custom area under Offline maps.

  • Strength: driving directions and offline maps in one app you may already have.
  • Weakness: offline maps are not available in every country or region, and downloaded maps expire and must be updated. The pages we read do not describe planning a multi-stop road trip, so we have not compared that.

Pricing: the pages we read do not state a price, so we have not given one.

4. Tripomatic

Best for: day-by-day itineraries, including multi-stop road trips, with offline maps on Premium

Tripomatic is a trip planner for web, Apple and Android. Its free plan has no time limit, but allows up to 5 days with planned activities per calendar month across all your trips, up to 25 places and hotels per trip, and shows ads. Its FAQ says you can plan a road trip with multiple stops, and that Tripomatic “shows you the route between stops so you can plan your driving time”.

  • Strength: turns a list of places into a daily schedule, with offline maps and export to PDF, GPX and KML on Premium.
  • Weakness: the free plan's monthly day limit is tight, and the page we read shows no Premium price.

Pricing: a free plan. Premium removes the limits and the ads, and adds offline maps in the mobile app and export. Its price is not shown on the page we read.

5. Wanderlog

Best for: itineraries with email import and a map view

Wanderlog's homepage says “One app for all your travel planning needs”, lists map view and route optimization, and says you can “Connect or forward your emails to get it magically added into your trip plan.” It also lists offline access as a Pro feature and “live syncing and collaborative editing”.

  • Strength: an itinerary and map in one view, with email import, and a homepage section on planning road trips with your stops, distances and times.
  • Weakness: offline access is a Pro feature and the homepage states no Pro price, so check the current price before you plan around one.

Pricing: its homepage calls it a free vacation planner, but states no price for a paid tier.

6. Polarsteps

Best for: recording a trip as you travel

Polarsteps' homepage describes “One travel app for all your adventures.” It says it will “Capture your route automatically”, can “Transform your trip into a Travel Book”, and calls itself “Proudly ad-free”.

  • Strength: records your route automatically and turns a trip into a book.
  • Weakness: its homepage has a trip planner for stays and activities, but it does not describe planning a driving route between stops.

Pricing: a free plan. Plus is €8.99 a month or €29.99 a year, and prices may vary by region. Its Plus page says “Everything you’ve come to know and love about Polarsteps stays free to use.”

Which Roadtrippers alternative is right for you?

  • You want a route with stops, navigation and RV features: Roadtrippers.
  • You want a road trip route planned by car: Furkot.
  • You want driving directions and offline maps on Android: Google Maps.
  • You want a free shortlist of real places you chose yourself, on a map, on Android: NomadSwipe.
  • You want a day-by-day itinerary, including multi-stop road trips, with export on Premium: Tripomatic.
  • You want an itinerary with email import and a map view: Wanderlog.
  • You want a travel record of your trip as it happens: Polarsteps.

FAQ

Is Roadtrippers free?

Roadtrippers has a free plan at $0 a year, with 1 saved trip and 3 stops per trip. Its paid plans start at $11.99 a month or $35.99 a year, and the 7-day free trial applies only to annual plans.

Does NomadSwipe plan a driving route?

No. NomadSwipe deals real places one card at a time and pins the ones you save on a map of your trip. It does not plan a driving route between stops.

Is NomadSwipe available on Android?

Yes. NomadSwipe is free to download on Google Play for Android phones.

Does Roadtrippers work offline?

Offline maps are included only in the Premium plan ($59.99 a year or $19.99 a month), according to its plans page.

Is Furkot free?

The Furkot homepage we read does not state a price, so we cannot say. Check the current price on its site before you decide.

Does NomadSwipe work offline?

Up to 20 saved trips are available offline, according to the Google Play listing.
