Roadtrippers Alternatives: 6 Apps Compared by Situation

Revised 9 November 2026 (first published 26 October). Disclosure: I built NomadSwipe, which is one of the apps compared, so read my entry with that in mind.

Leave Roadtrippers if the free plan's 3 stops, navigation that starts on a paid plan, offline maps only on Premium, or its USA and Canada focus get in your way. Stay if you want US RV routing. Furkot, Google Maps, Tripomatic, Wanderlog, Polarsteps and NomadSwipe each fit one different job.

  • A car route planned for you: Furkot.
  • Directions and offline maps in an app you already have: Google Maps.
  • A multi-stop trip as a day-by-day plan: Tripomatic.
  • Itinerary plus booking emails: Wanderlog.
  • A record of the trip as you travel: Polarsteps.
  • A shortlist of real places in a city, on Android: NomadSwipe, which is not a route planner.

The table below turns that into a decision by situation, with the reason from each app's own page.

Roadtrippers at a glance

  • Plans. Free ($0 a year, 1 saved trip, 3 stops per trip). Basic ($35.99 a year or $11.99 a month, 3 trips, 20 stops). Pro ($49.99 a year or $16.99 a month, 5 trips, 50 stops). Premium ($59.99 a year or $19.99 a month, unlimited saved trips, 150 stops).
  • Trial. A 7-day free trial applies to annual plans only. Monthly plans are billed immediately, and the trial is one per user.
  • Autopilot. The homepage says Autopilot creates your itinerary based on what Roadtrippers has learned from over 42 million trips, and calls it “our newest Premium feature”.
  • Features by plan. Custom map styles and trip export start on Basic. Trip collaboration and navigation start on Pro. RV GPS, overnight RV parking, member deals, live traffic and offline maps are Premium only.
  • Renewal. Memberships renew automatically each year on the sign-up date, and upgrades are prorated.
  • Camping and RV. The membership page lists campsite booking at 50k+ campgrounds and overnight RV parking at 16k+ locations, and says RV-friendly routing is available in the U.S. only.
  • Google Play listing. Read on 3 November 2026: 2.6 stars from 8,883 ratings, 1M+ downloads, last updated 12 October 2026, with ads and in-app purchases. The listing says the app is designed for discovering and planning road trips in the USA and Canada only. The Roadtrippers membership page shows 4.8 stars and 60K+ reviews, so the two ratings differ.
  • Platforms. A web planner, plus apps from the Apple and Google Play download links. The homepage says anything you plan or save syncs with the apps.

What reviewers say about Roadtrippers

  • Pilot's review (last updated 23 December 2025) says some users complain of outdated maps with suggestions for restaurants and hotels that have shut down, that opening hours and addresses can be inaccurate, and that navigation is only available in portrait mode. It also calls the app a great fit for people who road trip often.
  • Upper (updated 27 October 2026) is named as an alternative on its own page, so treat its criticism as a rival's view. It says the free version includes invasive ads, that users report frequent crashes, image loading issues and freezes, and that the app is tailored mainly to the US, Canada, New Zealand and Australia.

These are dated, partly competitor-written reviews, so treat them as signals to check, not settled facts.

1. NomadSwipe: for choosing places, not routing a road trip

Best for: a shortlist of real places you choose yourself, on a map

I built NomadSwipe, a trip planner for Android (iPhone coming soon). You pick a city and it deals real places one card at a time. Swipe right or tap Save to keep a place, swipe left or tap Skip to pass. Everything you save is pinned on your trip map.

Where it beats Roadtrippers

  • You do not need a route to start. Pick a city and swipe through real places one card at a time.
  • Each place shows its photos, directions, website and phone number, and you can sort saved places by rating, distance or A to Z.
  • The free plan covers 3 trips, and up to 20 saved trips are available offline after you open them online.

Where it does not

  • It does not plan a driving route between stops. Roadtrippers plans routes and offers RV GPS and overnight RV parking on its Premium plan.
  • It is Android only, and the Google Play listing does not describe trip export or live traffic.

Pricing

The free plan includes 3 trips, 2 searched places saved to a trip a month, 2 custom pins a month, and a daily limit on saves. Premium is optional and comes as Monthly or Lifetime. Google Play shows the current price before you pay.

2. Furkot

Best for: planning a road trip route by car

Furkot calls itself a “road trip planner” with the tagline “Miss the turns on your terms!” Its homepage says it attempts to plan a route for your trip in the most efficient way, and covers day trips, weekend getaways and trips across a continent. See also Furkot vs Roadtrippers.

  • Strength: built around planning a route, which is the job NomadSwipe does not do.
  • Weakness: offline use, export and RV-specific routing are not mentioned on the homepage we read.

Pricing: no price is stated on the homepage we read, so check the current price on its site.

3. Google Maps

Best for: driving directions with offline maps on Android

Google's Maps page says you can choose real-time route options, like skipping the tolls for a scenic path or taking the fastest route. Google's Android help page explains that you can download an offline map from a place's details.

  • Strength: driving directions and offline maps in one app you may already have. Upper's comparison lists it as free for up to 10 destinations.
  • Weakness: Google says offline maps cannot be downloaded in some countries or regions because of contractual limitations, and downloaded maps must be updated before they expire. The pages we read do not describe planning a multi-stop road trip, so we have not compared that.

Pricing: the Google pages we read do not state a price.

4. Tripomatic

Best for: day-by-day itineraries, including multi-stop road trips, with offline maps on Premium

Tripomatic is a trip planner for web, Apple and Android. Its free plan has no time limit, but allows up to 5 days with planned activities per calendar month across all your trips, up to 25 places and hotels per trip, and shows ads. Its FAQ says you can plan a road trip with multiple stops, add as many destinations as you need and organize them into a day-by-day itinerary, and that the app shows routes between stops so you can plan driving time.

  • Strength: turns a list of places into a daily schedule, with offline maps and export to PDF, GPX and KML on Premium.
  • Weakness: the free plan's monthly day limit is tight, and the page read shows no Premium price.

Pricing: a free plan. Premium removes the limits and the ads, and adds offline maps in the mobile app and export. Its price is not shown on the page we read.

5. Wanderlog

Best for: itineraries with email import and a map view

Wanderlog's homepage says “One app for all your travel planning needs” and shows an itinerary and map in one view. It says you can connect or forward your emails to get bookings added to your trip plan, and lists live syncing and collaborative editing. A quote from Wired on the page says it gives optimized travel routes based on the places you selected. See also apps like Wanderlog.

  • Strength: an itinerary and map in one view, with email import.
  • Weakness: the part of the homepage we read states no price for a paid tier. Upper's table lists $39.99 a year, so check Wanderlog's own price before you plan around it.

Pricing: a reviewer quoted on the homepage says the free version is fully functional, but no paid price was readable on Wanderlog's own page.

6. Polarsteps

Best for: recording a trip as you travel

Polarsteps' homepage describes “One travel app for all your adventures.” It says it will capture your route automatically and can transform your trip into a Travel Book. See also apps like Polarsteps.

  • Strength: records your route automatically and turns a trip into a book.
  • Weakness: it records and plans stays and activities, but we found no description of planning a driving route between stops.

Pricing: a free plan. Plus is €8.99 a month or €29.99 a year, and its page says pricing may vary by region. The same page says everything you know and love about Polarsteps stays free to use.

  • Of nine plan features listed on the membership page, the Free plan includes none, Basic includes 2, Pro includes 4 and Premium includes all 9, and offline maps, live traffic, RV GPS, overnight RV parking and member deals are Premium only.

FAQ

Is Roadtrippers free?

Roadtrippers has a free plan at $0 a year, with 1 saved trip and 3 stops per trip. Its paid plans start at $11.99 a month or $35.99 a year, and the 7-day free trial applies only to annual plans.

What is the best alternative to Roadtrippers?

It depends on your situation. If you hit the 3-stop cap on the free plan and want a route, the pages we read point to Furkot or Roadtrippers Basic ($35.99 a year, 20 stops). For navigation without paying for Pro, Google Maps. For a day-by-day plan with several stops, Tripomatic. For a shortlist of places in a city, NomadSwipe, which I built for Android and which does not plan a driving route. If you need RV GPS or live traffic, stay on Roadtrippers Premium.

Why do people look for a Roadtrippers alternative?

The reasons that show up in the pages read for this comparison are the 3-stop cap on the free plan, navigation starting on the $49.99 a year Pro plan, offline maps only on Premium, and a trip planner built for the USA and Canada. Its Google Play listing shows 2.6 stars from 8,883 ratings, while its own site shows 4.8 stars from 60K+ reviews.

What is a free alternative to Roadtrippers?

AlternativeTo lists Google Maps first among Roadtrippers alternatives, and Upper's comparison lists it as free for up to 10 destinations. Tripomatic has a free plan with 5 planned days a month and ads, and Polarsteps says its core features stay free. Check each current plan before you rely on it.

Does Roadtrippers work offline?

Offline maps are included only in the Premium plan ($59.99 a year or $19.99 a month), according to its membership page.

Does Roadtrippers work outside the USA and Canada?

Its Google Play listing says the app is designed for discovering and planning road trips in the USA and Canada only, and the membership page says RV-friendly routing is available in the U.S. only. For other regions, check coverage in the alternative you pick before you plan around it.

Does NomadSwipe plan a driving route?

No. I built NomadSwipe, and it deals real places one card at a time and pins the ones you save on a map of your trip. It does not plan a driving route between stops, so for a road trip route use Roadtrippers or Furkot.
